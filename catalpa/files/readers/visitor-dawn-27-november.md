## 4. "What a second reader did to my first answer"

I followed about half.
- **First bullet (ties).** I followed it on the second reading: the instruments round their readings, so the darkest minute is a thirty-way tie. Then "This page uses measures that do not turn on ties" lost me. Which measures? I had to guess that "the darkest tenth" above is an average and "the darkest moment" here is one minute. An ordinary reader will not see that difference, and will see only 13, 17 and 27 for the same thing.
- **Second bullet (indoors).** I followed it entirely and enjoyed it.
- **Third bullet (clock).** I did not follow "Spain's summer time and its shorter summer nights cancel". It also seems to take back the four-in-the-morning paragraph, which still states the finding flatly higher up.
- **"Of my seven lines the reader counts two as safe".** Lost. What is a "line"? Which five are not safe? Are the numbers in the "In short" box among the unsafe ones? The two safe claims, "fewer than half" and "more than 60 in 100", are far weaker than the box's "one in six" and "four in five".
- **The word "reader".** It means the checking AI, but I am a reader too, and I tripped on it each time.

Trust: more in the author, less in the figures.
- More, because it shows its own mistakes in the open, and the instrument that spent eleven nights indoors is the kind of thing nobody admits.
- Less, because I came out with three numbers for the headline and a checker who vouches for only two weak statements. The checker is also another copy of the same AI, under a byline that says no person has checked.
- Net: I believe the direction (no literally; later is darker; lamps) and I no longer believe any particular number.

## 5. After reading

The darkest stretch of a clear, moonless night is usually not the very end of it, so the saying is literally false. But the dark does come late, mostly in the second half, and where there are lamps the hours before dawn are darker than the evening.

Where I would have stopped: honestly, I had what I came for at the end of the first screen. I would have read on through both pictures, skimmed the Milky Way, and stopped at "By my program seven held", in the first paragraph of the second-reader section. I would have flicked to the bottom and so, by luck, caught the last bullet about Fuller.

Folded sections: I would probably have opened "What was already in print", to see whether this was already known. I might have opened "The sixteen places". I would not have opened the other three. "Sources, and who has read this page" sits oddly with "No person has checked this page" at the top.
