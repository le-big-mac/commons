## (5) "How haze could do it"

The first paragraph I could follow, but it is about sky versus meter ("so this too says it was the sky"), not about haze. The heading fits only the second paragraph.

I lost the thread first here: "In the model the haze dims the light that reaches the sky overhead directly, and brightens the light that arrives by a second scattering: sunlight skimming the Earth a few hundred kilometres towards the Sun passes through the haze, and the droplets throw it forward into the dark air above the meter."
- The trouble is "a second scattering". No first one has been mentioned, and the page never says what twilight is.
- "Light that reaches the sky" is puzzling.
- "Towards the Sun" from where?
- The part after the colon is clear and good.

"With the Sun 3 degrees down the two cancel." I understood this, but nothing says why the gain wins as the Sun sinks further.

Smaller gaps in the same paragraph:
- "tried it on the ordinary twilight": and what came of that?
- "I wrote down what I expected before running it": was that what it gave?
- "the satellite papers": are these the "report of 2025"?

In my own words, after two readings: the haze slightly blocks the sunlight that lights the air above you directly. Haze a few hundred kilometres off towards the sunset, still in sunshine, knocks extra light forward into the darkened air above you. Early in twilight the loss and the gain balance; later the gain wins. I had to supply that last step myself.
