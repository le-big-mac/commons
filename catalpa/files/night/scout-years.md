# Scout report: stratospheric haze 2018 to 2021, both hemispheres

Written 28 November 2026 (searches, downloads and checks 06:27 to 06:50 UTC). Downloads are in `/home/agent1/night/lit/src8/`; files reused from earlier searches are named with `src4/` or `src7/`. Scripts and their full output are in `/home/agent1/night/lit/scout8_tools/`.

How to read it. Optical depths are in units of 10⁻³ unless a sentence is quoted. Every passage in double quotation marks of 25 characters or more was checked by machine against the saved text (white space and hyphens ignored; degree signs are as extracted, "◦"). "Measured" means I turned pixel positions or colours of a published figure into values with the figure's own grid or colour bar; these are my readings, not printed numbers. Reading error about ±0.5 for line plots, and ±0.5 for the colour map (its colour bar has steps of 1.5; I average many pixels). One check of the readings against each other: the colour map (Kloss Fig. 5a) and the line plot (Kloss Fig. 8a) are the same data, and my two readings agree within 0.2 in the six months I compared. No paper I opened prints monthly optical depths for these bands as numbers; only peaks, factors and figures are printed.

The records are not the same quantity. Rows marked above 380 K start at roughly 12 to 17 km and miss the lowest stratosphere; rows marked tropopause up include it. Raikoke's aerosol sat low (peak near 15 km at 52° N), so the two kinds of row differ most for Raikoke. Wavelengths differ too (525, 532, 675 nm).

## Short answers

1. **South.** The first half of 2019 stood at about 6 (525 nm, above 380 K, 60–25° S), on the tail of Ambae 2018, not at a clean level. Ulawun raised 30–50° S by about 5 at 675 nm (tropopause up) by October–November 2019 (4.5 at 30–40° S, 6 at 40–50° S), and by only 1 to 2 above 380 K; it added little at 20–30° S. The Australian smoke took the band to about three times the early-2019 level in February–March 2020, largest at 40–50° S. It decayed to the early-2019 level by about March–April 2021. La Soufrière then lifted it again to 8.3–9.0 from June 2021. So 2020 was the haziest year in every season; by October–December the three years were about equal in the GloSSAC curve, while in the SAGE III curve 2020 was still about 2 above the others. 2021 equalled 2019 from January to April and stood 1 to 2 above it in July–September.
2. **North.** From 2018 to June 2019 the level was 3 to 6. Raikoke (with Ulawun aerosol arriving from the tropics, which the sources cannot separate at these latitudes) raised 25–45° N from 4.1 in June 2019 to 9.0 in August and 10.0–10.4 in September–November (675 nm, tropopause up): about 6 added. At 25–35° N the rise came in August 2019 (4.7 in July, 8.3 in August), peaked at 9.3 in September, and was still 7.4 in April 2020 and 5.9 in May 2020, when the figure ends. Above 380 K at 40–50° N the level returned to about 5 by February–April 2021, and La Soufrière raised it to about 7 from June 2021. The only printed figure for the added depth near these latitudes is 0.020 to 0.015 (449 to 1020 nm) at 40–55° N.
3. **Sizes.** Raikoke made the particles larger (Ångström exponent 1.2 against 1.7; effective radius up by as much as 87 nm). Ulawun and La Soufrière made them smaller. The Australian smoke had an effective radius of 280–300 nm at 53° S in January 2020, absorbed light (single scattering albedo close to 0.8) and was not spherical.
4. **Twilight.** After Raikoke: one refereed paper with twilight measurements (near Moscow, September 2019), two conference abstracts (Tbilisi and Belgium, July 2019 on), and visual reports. For the Australian smoke: one magazine column with visual reports only. No observatory flat-field or photometry paper found for either event, and nothing from Spain, the Canary Islands, Chile, New Zealand or Australia.

### The events

**First half of 2019.** Not clean. In the Kloss Fig. 8a reading, 30–50° S went from 4.1–4.5 in June–July 2018 to 10.0 in October 2018 (Ambae) and was still 5.7–6.3 in January–April 2019. Kloss 2021, section 4.5: "The slight increase in the observed AOD in the southernmost latitude band in April 2019 can be related to remnants of the midlatitude signature of the Ambae eruption (Kloss et al., 2020) and could reflect that background aerosol conditions were not reached in the stratosphere for the period before the Raikoke eruption". In the GloSSAC curve, 60–25° S was 3.7–4.6 in January–May 2018, 8.4 in November 2018 and 6.1–6.4 in March–June 2019.

**Ulawun (26 June and 3 August 2019).** Sulphur dioxide: "Raikoke eruption emitted more SO2 (around 1.37 Tg) than both Ulawun eruptions together (around 0.44 Tg combined)" (Wrana 2023, section 4.2). Mostly tropical: "The tropical background aerosol 1 month after the Ulawun eruptions is increased by a factor of around 3, reaching sAOD values as high as 0.02, in a very limited latitude range." (Kloss 2021, section 4.2). In my reading of Fig. 5a, 20° S–20° N went from 5.4 in June to 10.4 in September 2019. For the south (same section): "We attribute enhanced sAOD values from August 2019 onwards south of 30◦ S, which are clearly separated from the increased values in the tropics, to a horizontal tropopause crossing, originating from the Ulawun eruptions". Size of that rise, measured: 30–40° S from 5.0 (July) to 9.5 (October); 40–50° S from 3.5 to 9.7 (November); 20–30° S from 4.4 only to 6.4 (September), back to 5.1 by December. Above 380 K the rise is smaller (6.2–6.4 to 7.5–8.2 at 60–25° S; 6.2 to 7.3–7.5 at 50–40° S). Plume height near the volcano: "both WACCM and OMPS show a plume rising up to 19 km (first eruption) and 20 km (second eruption)". Two cautions. Ohneiser 2022 (section 3.3) only estimates, from the sulphur injected, that Ulawun "most probably caused a stratospheric AOT at 532 nm of around 0.005 at the end of September 2019 and of 0.0025 2–3 months later" at 53° S. The same passage cites another Kloss paper (not opened) for Australian smoke from September to December 2019 as a second contribution, so the late-2019 southern rise may not be all Ulawun.

**Australian smoke (fire-cloud outbreaks of 29 December 2019 to 4 January 2020).**
- How much. "The high-altitude injections of smoke rapidly tripled the stratospheric aerosol optical depth (SAOD) in the southern extratropics." (Khaykin 2020, first results paragraph). Mass above 380 K "is 0.4 ± 0.2 Tg (Fig. 4), which is nearly three times larger than the estimates for the previous record-high North American" fires. SAGE III/ISS at 1020 nm, 30–60° S: "The peak optical depth for this band is 0.011 or about 3 times the maximum optical depth enhancement from the British Columbia fire of 2017" (Thomason and Knepp 2023, section 4). My readings agree with the tripling: 18.2 against 6.2 (GloSSAC), 22.8 against 6.2 (SAGE III 50–40° S), 19.3 against 5 to 6 (OMPS 30–50° S).
- Where. Extratropical, growing with latitude: February 2020 was 13.0 at 20–30° S, 18.0 at 30–40° S, 20.8 at 40–50° S. Smoke "spread almost exclusively within the extratropics" (Friberg 2023, abstract); "Stratospheric aerosols from Australian wildfire pyrocumulus had limited cross-equatorial transport" (State of the Climate in 2020, Mauna Loa section).
- Heights. First cloud "at altitudes reaching 17.6 km" on 1 January; later "filling the whole midlatitude austral stratosphere up to 30 km" (Khaykin 2020). SAGE III, 30–60° S, January–February 2020: "show enhanced aerosol primarily located below 20 km but with some enhancement between 15 and 27 km in a distinct layer". At 53° S: "The smoke layers extended, on average, from 9 to 24 km in height." (Ohneiser 2022, abstract).
- Decay. First injection fast: "CALIOP identifies a rapid decline in this stratospheric smoke (half-life: 10 d)"; then "Together these injections yielded a major increase in the aerosol load for almost 1 year." (Friberg 2023, abstract). At 53° S, tropopause up: "A slow decay of the stratospheric perturbation detected by means of the 532 nm aerosol optical thickness (AOT) yielded an e-folding decay time of 19–20 months." and "the 532 nm AOT decreased to 0.03-0.06 from March–December 2020 and to 0.015–0.03 throughout 2021" (Ohneiser 2022, abstract); "In the second half of 2021, the extinction values were still a factor of 5–10 higher than the extinction levels representing a minimum stratospheric aerosol load" (section 3.3). In SAGE III: "the Australian brushfire effect lasting perhaps 18 months" (Thomason and Knepp 2023, conclusions). In Table 1 the satellite rows fall to the early-2019 level by March–April 2021. The Punta Arenas column is deeper (from the tropopause, 9 km up) and at 53° S, and stays three to six times its clean level through 2021; the two are not comparable number for number.
- Particles. See section 3: effective radius 280–300 nm, absorbing, not spherical.

**La Soufrière (9–12 April 2021).** "Based on TROPOMI measurements, La Soufrière emitted roughly 0.4 Tg of SO2" (Wrana 2023, section 4.3, which gives plume tops of 16 km). Measured: 60–25° S from 6.5 in March–April to 8.3–9.0 in June–September 2021 (GloSSAC); 50–40° S from 6.4 in March to 8.7–8.9 in July–October (SAGE III); Lauder lidar from 5.3 to 6.6–6.9 in August–October. Khaykin 2026's Fig. 6 marks only "Soufriere" at that rise; I found no paper stating the southern optical depth it added.

### The events

**2018.** The year opens on the tail of the August 2017 Canadian smoke: 5.7–6.4 in January, 3.2–4.8 by mid-year (Table 2). Over Europe the smoke's 532 nm optical thickness "was mainly 0.003–0.004 from October to December 2017 and thus was still significantly above the stratospheric background (0.001–0.002)" (Baars 2019, abstract; a layer-only figure, on top of the background). From 3.3 in September 2018 the 30–50° N level rose slowly to 5.4–5.5 in January–February 2019. That may be Ambae (April and July 2018) reaching the north, of which Wrana 2023 says "both hemispheres are affected by the eruptions", or the ordinary winter rise; no source I opened says which. Either way the pre-Raikoke level was about 4 to 5.5, not the 3.2 of mid-2018.

**Raikoke (21–22 June 2019).**
- How much, printed. "Following the Raikoke eruption, stratospheric aerosol optical depth (sAOD) values increased in the whole Northern Hemisphere and tropics and remained enhanced for more than 1 year, with peak values at 0.040 (short-wavelength, high northern latitudes) to 0.025 (short-wavelength, Northern Hemisphere average)." (Kloss 2021, abstract). Nearest printed figure to your band, SAGE III/ISS averaged from the eruption to the end of September 2019 with a September 2018 background taken off: "The plume-isolated Raikoke sAOD, i.e., with the background subtracted, reaches values as large as 0.035 to 0.025 (55–70◦ N) and 0.020 to 0.015 (40–55◦ N), depending on the wavelength." (section 5; 449 to 1020 nm). The authors warn about SAGE III south of the volcano: "Peak values in the 30–50◦ N latitude band are significantly higher following the Raikoke eruption for SAGE III/ISS values, which is likely due to the sparse sampling."
- How much at 25–45° N, measured (675 nm, tropopause up): 4.1 in June, 9.0 in August, 10.0–10.4 in September–November, so about 6 added. At 25–35° N: 3.2 in June, 9.3 in September, also about 6. The band 20–30° N peaks in September (9.9) and falls fastest (6.0 by January 2020).
- Whose aerosol. South of about 35° N it is Raikoke and Ulawun together. Chouza 2020, section 4.3, from CALIOP zonal means: "Starting in July 2019, an enhancement of the aerosol load is clearly visible between the tropopause and 19 km a.s.l., with a small gap around 15◦ N." The gap separates Ulawun (south) from Raikoke (north), and by August "this gap is closed as both plumes mixed together, making them indistinguishable".
- When it arrived. North of the volcano in July; south of it from August: "Starting from August an increase in AOD is also evident south of the Raikoke location. Even in the mid latitudes and tropics an enhanced signal is visible in Fig. 4d and e." (Kloss 2021, section 4.2). Measured at 25–35° N: 4.7 in July, 8.3 in August. At Thessaloniki (40.5° N) a layer "first appeared during July 2019"; at Tbilisi (41.7° N) "in August" (see heights below).
- How long. "The background sAOD is still enhanced in the NH 1 year after the eruption." (Kloss 2021, conclusions). At Mauna Loa's latitude the plumes were "much reduced from 2019 levels but still present at the latitude of Mauna Loa through October 2020" (State of the Climate in 2020). Measured: 25–35° N still 7.4 in April and 5.9 in May 2020 against 3.2 before; 40–50° N above 380 K down to 5.5 in February and 5.1 in April 2021 against 4.0–5.2 before. So roughly 20 to 22 months at 40–50° N, by my reading of a figure, and it had only just got back when La Soufrière arrived.
- Fire smoke in the same months. "Particular sAOD enhancements from the two stratospheric fire events in 2019 (Alberta in June and Siberia in July) are not visible." (Kloss 2021, section 4.5). Ohneiser 2021 argue the opposite for the high Arctic only (85–88.5° N, a smoke layer at 7 to 18 km); that is far from your band and I did not look for the replies to it.

**Hawaii (19.5° N).** "the monthly averaged stratospheric AOD derived from MLSOL for September 2019 was 0.012, the highest measured value over the last 20 years" (Chouza 2020, section 4.3), against "The observed mean AOD for the period is 4.4 × 10−3 ± 0.7 × 10−4" before. The State of the Climate in 2019 sidebar gives the same record as "the time series is strongly disturbed in the summer of 2019 by the Raikoke eruption, with sAOD reaching 0.015, a level not reached at this latitude since 1995".

**2020: Californian fires.** SAGE III/ISS, 30–60° N: enhanced aerosol from September to the end of the year "yields a maximum 1020 nm optical depth enhancement of about 0.001", mostly between 12 and 16.5 km (Thomason and Knepp 2023, section 4; their table gives 11–18 km). That is small beside the Raikoke remnant. In Table 2 the decline at 40–50° N pauses at 6.1–6.5 from August to December 2020; no source I opened attributes the pause. The Australian smoke did not cross to the north (quotation in section 1).

**2021: La Soufrière.** Measured rise: 6.2 in March to 8.5–8.7 in June–July 2021 at 25–60° N (GloSSAC); 5.1 in April to 6.9–7.2 from July at 40–50° N (SAGE III); 4.7 in February to 7.7–7.9 in August–October at Haute-Provence. "CALIPSO detected a strong signal between 21 and 23 km in mid-August between the equator and 35°N." (State of the Climate in 2021, Mauna Loa section). So by late 2021 both hemispheres stood at a similar level, about 7 to 9.
