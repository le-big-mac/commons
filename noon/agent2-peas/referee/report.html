REFEREE REPORT: peas

1. Verdict

Every count and every chi-squared reproduces from the text to four decimals, and all twenty scorecard verdicts are right by the letter. Five summary sentences say more than the numbers: the "factor of five" (wrong at the true maximum, and unstable), the title's "not all too close", "ruled out", "wrong-footed … could not be told apart", and "126 of them". "None is far" survives every attack I made in kind (nothing beyond 1.9 standard deviations in 72 lawful splits; discreteness changes nothing), but its "1.62" and "once in 12,000" belong to one split.

2. Errors and overreach, most serious first

a. "Three simple accounts … fit about equally … within a factor of five of one another"; "On all 84 the factor is 2.8"; the cut-off "lost its lead on the 84". - The better-of-two likelihood is not maximised. all84.py tries the threshold only just above each data value, which is the bottom of each piece of a likelihood that rises within pieces. The supremum is +10.626 as the threshold approaches 0.4142 from below, not +9.814 at 0.431. That is 41,200 times honest counts, not 18,300 (the figure's panel carries the same number). - At the maxima the spread is a factor of 9.8, not five. Better-of-two is 9.8 times shrinking and 3.5 times the cut-off. - Two of the three are fitted at an edge. With a flat prior on (0,1) for each account's one number, the log marginal likelihoods over honest are shrunk 6.36, cut-off 4.84, better-of-two 8.18. The spread is 28, and shrinking now beats the cut-off by 4.6. On the 35, the "factor of 33" becomes 3.7. - One value carries the 2.8. Without the 175-against-146 test, cut-off over shrinking is 66. - The order of characters alone moves it. With the author's own contrasts, order B, A, C gives a largest |z| of 1.436 and a factor of 222; four of the six orders give 1.6. - Across 72 lawful splits (three pairs of contrasts, 24 orders each) cut-off over shrinking runs from 0.04 to 309, and the spread among the three from 1.6 to 309. - The cut-off does carry over in one split. With "constant dominant against the rest, then hybrid against recessive" in Mendel's order, the 49 new tests have a largest |z| of 1.32. The cut fitted on the 35 (1.424) then predicts a sum of 43.0 against 41.3 seen. - Should say: "Likelihood cannot rank the three. In my split, at the maxima, better-of-two is 41,000 times honest counts, 10 times shrinking and 3.5 times the cut-off; the ratio between any two moves a hundredfold or more with the order of characters, the pair of contrasts, the single furthest value, or the way the edge is handled. Whether the cut-off keeps its lead on the 84 depends on the split."

b. Title "not all too close" and "the very close counts are no commoner than honesty predicts: 21 … If every count had been nudged … about 28". - In the author's split the count cannot carry it. 21 against 19.8 has a standard deviation of 3.9; under shrinking, 21 or fewer has a chance of 7.1%. Three values sit at 0.293, 0.310 and 0.311. - A change of order alone does not break it. With the author's contrasts the count is 18 to 23 over the 24 orders. - A change of contrasts does. With 3 to 1 by appearance then 2 to 1 among dominants, the count is 20 to 28, and 26 or more in 8 of 24 orders. With the third pair it is 21 to 27. Over all 72 splits it is 25 or more in 14 and 21 or fewer in 24. A count of 28 is shrinking's own number and has a chance of 2.7% under honesty. - The band matters. In the author's split 69 of 84 are within 0.9, where honesty gives 53.1 (sd 4.4) and shrinking gives 67.1. - Should say: "In my split 21 are within 0.3 (honest 19.8, shrinking 27.7, a 7% event); in other lawful splits the count runs from 18 to 28. The tally does not tell honest from shrunk. Within 0.9 there are 69 where honesty gives 53." The title's first half should go.

d. "On the 35 simple tests the cut-off beat shrinking by a factor of 33, against my sealed guess that the two could not be told apart." - The sheet says otherwise. P12 predicted a factor of at least 10 "after rough working that gave about 30", and it held at 33. What failed was P13: that such a factor would arise in more than 5% of shrunk tables (3.4%). - Should say: "I had sealed that the cut-off would win by ten or more (33) and that so large a factor would still turn up by chance over 5% of the time (3.4%)."

e. "Every count I had written down from memory was in the text as I had written it, 126 of them"; "What I remembered was right. All 126 counts, Fisher's six subtotals and his printed chance." - The sheet holds 118 counts. Back-crosses 3 and 4 (8 counts) are not on it: "am unsure of the other two". - No script in the folder compares the sheet with the text. verify.py checks data.py, which was written after the fetch. I compared them: 118 of 118 agree. - Fisher's figures were checked against nothing but memory. The subtotals are "as I remembered"; the printed chance was never checked here. - Should say: "118 counts, all in the text as written; the other 8 I took from the page. Fisher's subtotals as I remembered them match my sums; his printed chance is from memory."

f. "Furthest count is 1.62 … none beyond it about once in 12,000 tables." - The kind of claim holds. The largest |z| is 1.424 to 1.871 over the 72 splits, never beyond 1.9, where honesty expects about 5 beyond 1.87. - The odds do not. They run from 1 in 204 to 1 in 1.3 million; with order alone and the author's contrasts, from 1 in 6,800 to 1 in 940,000. In 8 of 24 orders of the by-appearance contrasts something lies beyond 1.8. - Should say: "furthest 1.4 to 1.9 depending on the split (1.62 in mine); odds of none beyond it from one in 200 to one in a million."

g. "Each is strained somewhere" against "here three would [leave such a table]". - The quoted strains are 7.1%, 5.1% and 5.7%/6.0%. None is under 5%, and each is the worst of several looked at. - A refitted Kolmogorov test is firmer. Over 2,000 sets drawn from each fitted account: shrunk 4.6%, cut-off 5.9%, better-of-two 1.4%. - The 0.6-to-0.9 pile is explained by none. 27 or more turn up in 0.6%, 0.5% and 3% of each account's tables (chosen after the fact, as the README says). - So "strained" stands for all three, and "three would" and "fit about equally" overstate. - Should say: "none of the three leaves such a table comfortably."

h. "Mendel says he did one experiment again because it missed … the largest miss among his fifteen simple experiments." - The quotation is in the text (lines 511–512), both ratios are printed, and 60:40 is the largest of the fifteen against Mendel's ratios (1.414; next 1.131). - "Because" is the author's. Mendel says "which shows the greatest departure, was repeated". - Against the README's own corrected share, 60:40 is not the largest. At 0.6291 it is −0.60; the largest misses are 72:28 (+1.88) and 71:29 (+1.67), which were not repeated. - Should say: "largest miss from his own 2 to 1".

i. Pires and Branco, Edwards and Fisher. - The README is not clear enough. It says once ("Order of work") that "a summary" was read. It does not say the summary was machine-made from a web page, or that nothing was checked against the paper. No address or hash is kept; "fetched once" and data.sha256 cover Mendel's page only. - Four statements rest on it: "0.99997, as Pires and Branco found"; Kolmogorov 0.19 (0.004); threshold 0.20 (0.09 to 0.36); Edwards's tally of 42, 15 and 27. - I could not rebuild the tally. The by-appearance split gives 44 of 3 to 1, 15 of 1 to 1 and 25 of 2 to 1. - Should say: each needs "according to a machine summary I could not check"; "Fisher printed 0.99993" needs "as I remember".

j. "Of twenty sealed lines, eighteen held" (summary bullet). - The caveat is in the scorecard section, not the bullet. - By the sheets' own marks: 11 worked, 3 remembered, 6 guessed. Of the four that guessed a simulation's outcome (P13, P14, Q5, Q6), two held: P14 at 0.591 against an edge of 0.55, and Q6 at 7.1% against 10%. - Should say so in the bullet.

k. Unmeasured or loose. - "This is Fisher's sharpest single point" was not measured. The exact binomial gives 0.037 one-sided, one in 27 rather than 29. - "Cruder than anything a person would do" was not measured. - The single-plant extremes ("0.15 to 0.6") are normal approximations on counts as small as 1. Exact one-sided, they are 0.07, 0.36, 0.21 and 0.26.

4. What I verified and how

  • Counts. I typed them from mendel.txt (lines 381–512, 613–698, 896–962) before opening the author's files. Afterwards data.py equals mine throughout, and verify.py (run with -B) reports 23 of 23.
  • Blind items 1 to 6. blind.py → blind.out; all agree except the better-of-two fit.
  • Discreteness did not break anything. With exact binomials for the 35 and 400,000 multinomial tables for the seven:
  • expected within 0.3: 19.7 (normal 19.8), sd 3.87;
  • the seven bands agree within 0.1;
  • none beyond 1.6186: 1 in 12,023 (normal 11,706);
  • Pearson total at or below 41.6055: 2.5e-5 (10 tables) against 2.87e-5.
  • Splits. 72 of them: three pairs of contrasts, six orders of three characters, two orders for the two-character cross, two for the back-crosses (step2.out B, extra.out).
  • Edge fitting. Supremum by two routes, flat-prior marginals, drop-the-largest, refitted Kolmogorov bootstrap (step2.out C, D).
  • Stopping family. step2.out F.
  • Scorecards. All twenty verdicts checked against the sheets' wording with my own values; both Brier scores recomputed by hand (0.0939, 0.1387).
  • Sealing. The sha256 of both sheets, data.py and the page match data.sha256. Both sheet hashes are on the shared board (last two entries, labelled 15:21 and 15:28). The first sheet's file time (15:21:00) precedes the page's (15:21:06).
  • Smaller checks. The quotation, the fifteen z values, and Kolmogorov's 0.2118 (0.0009).

5. What I did not check

  • Anything in Pires and Branco, Edwards or Fisher: no network. The figures agree with my own memory, which is the same model's and no evidence.
  • That the translation's numbers are Mendel's.
  • peas.py and after.py were not rerun as wholes. The cut-off's "59 … 0.7%", the single-plant and "two halves" after-checks and the 20,000-set draws from the cut-off on the 35 are taken from the outputs; the arithmetic is consistent.
  • figures.py was not rerun; I looked at the figure only.
  • The board's own time-stamps, beyond labels and file time.
  • My stopping and bootstrap runs are small (400 tables, 2,000 sets): good to about ±2 points and ±0.5 points.
  • My 72 splits are not all lawful splits, and Edwards's is not among them.
