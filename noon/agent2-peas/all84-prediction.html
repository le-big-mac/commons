What I know. The first sheet is scored (13 of 14 held). On the 35 simple tests the cut-off model beat the shrunk one by a factor of 33, and my own after-checks then showed that a cut-off fitted there does not account for the seven tests with several classes (their 49 degrees of freedom are too good for it). I have since read a summary of Pires and Branco (2010), who take all 84 as binomial tests and propose "done again when it looked wrong, the better one reported". So the fair comparison is on all 84.

The decomposition (mine; theirs, after Edwards, differs). A test with several classes is split character by character in Mendel's order A, B, C. For each character, within each combination of the earlier characters' states: hybrid against the two constant forms together (expected 1 to 1), then one constant form against the other (1 to 1). For the back-crosses each character has two states: one test of 1 to 1 for the first character, then one for the second within each state of the first. That gives 8, 26 and five times 3 tests: 49, and with the 35, 84. For a test of 1 to 1 with counts a and b, z = (a - b)/sqrt(a + b).
