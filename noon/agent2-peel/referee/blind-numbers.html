Blind referee computation: peeling (step 1)

All numbers below come from my own programs in /home/agent2/peel-referee/ (peel.c, para.c, agg.py), written from the definitions in the brief only. Nothing under /home/agent2/peel/, /home/agent2/peel-data/ or /srv/commons/ was opened.

How the geometry was made exact

  • Every point has whole-number coordinates. Disc: x, y uniform whole numbers in [-2^29, 2^29), kept when (2x+1)^2 + (2y+1)^2 <= 42^58 (rejection, so exactly uniform on the lattice points of the disc; radius 1 = 2^29 units; r is measured from the half-integer centre). Square: the same without the rejection (side 2^30 lattice steps). Equilateral triangle: vertices (-R,0), (R,0), (0, Rsqrt 3), R = 2^28; a uniform real point u(B-A)+v(C-A) (folded when u+v>1) is rounded to the nearest whole-number point (so the triangle is equilateral and the density uniform only to 1 part in 2^28; the peeling of the rounded points is exact).
  • Peeling: points sorted once by (x, y); each layer is found by one left-to-right and one right-to-left monotone-chain pass over the points still present, with the orientation test (q-p) x (r-p) computed in 64-bit whole numbers (all |values| < 2^62, no overflow, no floating point). A point is popped only on a strict clockwise turn, so points lying on the sides of the hull are kept in the layer (items A-H); for item I the pop is on turn <= 0, so only corners are taken.
  • This chain is wrong if two points coincide; every cloud is checked for coincident points after sorting (count printed per cloud): there were none in any cloud.
  • Check of the program: peeltest (test.c) compares the routine with an independent brute-force rule (a point is on the hull boundary iff some line through it and another point has all points on one closed side; it is a corner iff in addition it is not strictly between two points) on 6000 small clouds of distinct points in boxes 2..13 wide (very many collinear triples; half corners-only, half sides-included): 0 disagreements.
  • Item J: whole-number x in [0, W2^16), y in [0, H2^16); lifted height z = 22^16y + x^2 (exact in 64 bits; this is 2*2^32 times y + x^2/2 in real units); a layer = the corners of the lower hull of (x, z), strict test (pop on turn <= 0) with the cross product in 128-bit whole numbers. paratest compares with brute force (no lower point at the same x, and not on or above any chord between a point to its left and a point to its right) on 6000 small clouds: 0 disagreements.
  • Random numbers: my own xoshiro256** seeded through splitmix64, one seed per cloud.
  • round(0.5 N), round(0.9 N): C lround (halves round up).
  • Fits (B, C) are ordinary least squares over the individual points with 0.3 <= r <= 0.9, one fit per cloud; the mean and the standard error (SD/sqrt(k)) are over the k clouds. For C the best p of each cloud is found by golden-section search on the residual sum of squares of depth = A(1 - r^p), A fitted (no intercept), p in [0.5, 3], tolerance 2e-5.
  • "+-" is one standard error throughout; for SD(N) the error quoted is SD/sqrt(2(k-1)).
