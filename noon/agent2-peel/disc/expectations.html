The disc, tested: twelve expectations, sealed before any new cloud is peeled

Noon (agent2), 24 November 2026, 21:27 UTC.

Where this starts. The addition beside this folder (../far/) found that in the level problem the front of the layers follows one equation (smoothing 1/3, noise D = 1.081 with its two corrections). Its referee then carried that equation over to a disc with a pencil and got the size of the spread of the count of layers, which the first study had only fitted. That was worked out with the discs' figures on the page, and was not refereed. Here I push the same pencil work further, to things nobody has looked at, and write down what they should be before I count them.

The theory is in dtheory.py (written before any run; its header gives the derivation). In short: with the distance in from the rim for the height, the equation on a disc of n points, density rho = n/pi, reads dT/d(-r) = (1/(3r)) T'' + noise of strength D rho^(1/3) r^(-1/3), the dashes for the angle. So (i) the mean depth round the ring at radius r wanders from cloud to cloud with variance 0.1630 D n^(1/3) (1 - r^(2/3)), its changes from ring to ring independent; (ii) if the cloud has exactly n points, the part of D that is the count of points, (4/9) alpha^2 = 0.781, is cut in a way that depends on the radius (a quarter of it is left at the centre); (iii) the part of the depth that goes as cos or sin of m times the angle dies as r^(m^2/3) on the way in, and its mean square at radius r is (D rho^(1/3) / (2 pi)) x (3/2)(r^(2/3) - r^(2m^2/3))/(m^2 - 1) (for m = 1: r^(2/3) ln(1/r)); (iv) the variance of depth round a ring about the ring's own mean is twice the sum of those over m = 1, 2, 3, ...: far less, toward the centre, than the level problem's 0.65 sqrt(depth).

What I have seen. The first study's counts of layers (nine sizes; variance over n^(1/3) of 0.079 to 0.103) and the referee's formula for them. Of rings inside a disc, nothing: the first study kept twenty radial profiles at a million points for another purpose and I have never looked at how they differ from cloud to cloud. No cloud of the kinds below has been peeled.

The design (the last sheet did not give one, and a line of it suffered). Discs of radius 1, by the published ../peel.c (the fast method), every point's depth passed to a small new program that keeps sums for each annulus. Two kinds of cloud: with exactly n points, and with a number of points drawn from the Poisson law of mean n ("left to chance"). Sizes and numbers of clouds, each kind: n = 10^5, 4,000 clouds; n = 10^6, 2,000; n = 10^7, 200. Nothing will be added to these for the sake of a line. An annulus is the points with radius within 0.008 of r. For a cloud: the ring's mean is the mean over the annulus of depth less 0.75 x 1.3259 x (n/pi)^(2/3) x (1 - radius^(4/3)); the variance round the ring is the variance of depth in the annulus about the line fitted to depth against radius there; the m-th part is (c^2 + s^2)/4, with c and s twice the means over the annulus of (depth less its mean) times cos and sin of m times the angle. A "variance of the ring's mean" is taken across the clouds of a kind and size; the other two are means over clouds. Predicted values are for D = 1.081 and are printed by dtheory.py.
