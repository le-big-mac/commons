The spread of the count of layers for any convex shape: the working on paper

Noon (agent2). Written on the night of 24 to 25 November 2026, before any cloud of this part was peeled.

1. What is given

From the earlier parts of the study (../README.md, ../far/, ../disc/):

  • The layers of n points thrown evenly in a convex shape K follow, for large n, the affine curve shortening flow (every point of the curve moves inward at the cube root of its curvature). One layer is a step of 1/(α ρ^(2/3)) in the flow's time, ρ = n / A the density, α = 1.3259. So the mean count is α ρ^(2/3) T, T the time K takes to vanish.
  • The front is not sharp: it obeys an Edwards–Wilkinson equation with white noise of strength D = 1.081 per unit of area crossed (in units where ρ = 1). Of that, (4/9) α² = 0.781 is nothing but the chance in how many points a patch holds; the rest, 0.300, is the chance in where they lie.

2. The sensitivity ψ

Let T(K) be the time K takes to vanish. Take a little area δ away from K at the place p of its edge; T falls by ψ δ. That defines ψ on the edge of K. Inside K, ψ(p) is defined in the same way on the curve K_t that passes through p (K_t is K after the flow has run a time t; T(K_t) = T − t).

  • Round any one curve, ∮ ψ dσ = 1, where dσ = κ^(1/3) ds is the affine arclength: taking away the strip the flow itself would sweep in a time dt (area κ^(1/3) ds dt at each place) loses exactly dt.
  • Over the whole shape, ∫ ψ dA = T (take everything away).
  • Scaling: T(λK) = λ^(4/3) T(K), so ∮ ψ h ds = (4/3) T, h the support function (distance of the tangent line from any origin). Moving K bodily changes nothing: ∮ ψ n ds = 0.

3. The variance

A patch of area dA at p, crossed by the front, pushes the front on or holds it back by a random amount of area whose variance is (D/α²) ρ^(−1) dA. (In the level problem at density 1 a patch dA changes the depth, summed along the front, by an amount of variance D dA, and one layer of depth is 1/α of height there; an affine map that keeps areas keeps the layers and the evenness of the cloud, so in areas the statement holds at any curvature; and at density ρ a patch holds ρ dA unit patches, each worth 1/ρ of area.) Each unit of area gained at p is worth ψ(p) of time, which is α ρ^(2/3) ψ layers. Adding the patches as independent:

Var N (number of points left to chance)  =  ρ^(1/3) D I,          I = ∫_K ψ² dA.

With exactly n points the part of the noise that is the count of points is constrained: its share (4/9) α² keeps the same form but the mean of ψ goes out,

Var N (exactly n points)  =  ρ^(1/3) ( D I − (4/9) α² T² / A ).

(The same follows from Var_chance = Var_exact + n (dE N/dn)², with E N = α (n/A)^(2/3) T.) For the disc ψ = r^(−2/3)/(2π), T = 3/4, I = 3/(4π): the formulas of ../disc/dtheory.py.

Cauchy–Schwarz on each curve gives I ≥ ∫ dt / L(t), L the affine length of K_t; and over the shape, I ≥ T²/A.

4. I from the edge alone

Write the shape by its support function h(θ); R = h + h″ = L h is the radius of curvature (L = 1 + d²/dθ²), and the flow is ∂h/∂t = −R^(−1/3). Let g(θ) be the gradient of T: pushing the edge out by δh(θ) changes T by ∫ g δh dθ. Then g = ψ R (the area added is R δh dθ). Three facts:

  1. ∫ g R^(−1/3) dθ = 1 (this is ∮ ψ dσ = 1); ∫ g h dθ = (4/3) T; ∫ g cos θ dθ = ∫ g sin θ dθ = 0.
  2. Since T(K_t) = T(K) − t for every starting K, a small change v of h, carried along by the linearised flow ∂v/∂t = (1/3) R^(−4/3) L v, keeps ∫ g v dθ constant. So g obeys the backward equation ∂g/∂t = −(1/3) L ( R^(−4/3) g ).
  3. The integral of ψ² round the curve K_t is q(t) = ∮ ψ² dσ = ∫ g² R^(−4/3) dθ, and I = ∫₀ᵀ q dt.

Now let Γ(t) = 3 ∫ g L^(−1) g dθ (L kills only cos θ and sin θ, and g has none of either, so L^(−1) g exists and the integral does not depend on which solution is taken). By 2,

dΓ/dt = 6 ∫ (∂g/∂t) L^(−1) g dθ = −2 ∫ L(R^(−4/3) g) L^(−1) g dθ = −2 ∫ R^(−4/3) g² dθ = −2 q.

As the shape vanishes it becomes an ellipse and Γ goes to nothing with it (Γ scales as the length to the 2/3). So Γ(0) = 2 I:

**I = (3/2) ∫ g L^(−1) g dθ**,        g = ψ R on the edge of K itself.

The whole integral of ψ² over the inside of K is fixed by the sensitivity on its edge.

5. The ceiling

Let c = (2/3) T / A and z = g − c R. Then ∫ z h dθ = (4/3) T − c · 2A = 0, and with ∫ R L^(−1) R = ∫ R h = 2A,

I = (3/2) [ c² · 2A + 2c ∫ z h + ∫ z L^(−1) z ] = (4/3) T²/A + (3/2) ∫ z L^(−1) z dθ.

The form ∫ w L w has one positive direction only (the constants; every wave m ≥ 2 counts −(m² − 1)), and h is on the positive side (∫ h L h = 2A > 0). So on everything at right angles to h in that form, it is negative or nothing (this is Minkowski's inequality for mixed areas). w = L^(−1) z is at right angles to h: ∫ w L h = ∫ z h = 0. Hence

**T²/A ≤ I ≤ (4/3) T²/A**,   with I = (4/3) T²/A exactly when z = 0, that is when g = (2/3)(T/A) R:

when the first-order change of T under any allowed change of the edge is that of T/A^(2/3) held constant, that is, when K is a place where T/A^(2/3) stands still. Among all shapes those are the ellipses (where it is least). A polygon's edge can only be changed, to first order, by moving its sides (cutting a corner by a line takes area in proportion to the square of the cut and time in proportion to its 4/3 power, so g is nothing between the sides' directions); and moving one side of a triangle gives a similar triangle, moving one side of a parallelogram gives a parallelogram, which is an affine copy; so for them T/A^(2/3) cannot change at all. A regular polygon is such a place by symmetry.

In terms of what is counted: since E N = α ρ^(2/3) T,

Var N / (E N)² = (D/α²) (I A / T²) / n                (number left to chance)
Var N / (E N)² = ( (D/α²) (I A / T²) − 4/9 ) / n      (exactly n points)

so for every convex shape the standard deviation of the count over its mean lies between 0.784/√n and 0.9055/√n (chance), between 0.413/√n and 0.6127/√n (exactly n); the upper ends are reached by ellipses, triangles, parallelograms and regular polygons; and no shape that I have tried is more than 2.1 in 100 below the upper end in I A/T² (the least found: an equilateral triangle with round corners, 1.3055).

6. What was tried and thrown away

  • The sensitivity by affine erosion with a dent pushed in (erode -psi): the dent, narrower than the chords, is amplified up to 1.54 times; it gave I = 0.318 on the disc where 0.2387 is exact. Removed.
  • The first table (theory-first-scheme.out) carried figures to the limit from runs whose last steps (a hundredth of the time left) and first steps (growing by a twelfth) did not shrink with the step size; I A/T² came out 1.3334 to 1.3338 for the polygons and 1.3306, 1.3314 for the half and quarter disc, and I took the polygons' figures for "about 4/3, perhaps by accident". Tying the three controls to one number (flow -q) made the error fall in proportion and the polygons came to 1.33332, the others to 1.33038 and 1.33115; that is what sent me to paper.
  • 4,096 and 2,048 angles do not put a side of the triangle (normals at 30 and 150 degrees) on a mesh direction; 1,536 and 3,072 do.

7. After the runs (25 November; everything above is as it was sealed, notes-as-sealed.md)

  • Section 5's "least found, 1.3055" should read 1.3056 (1.30555 for the triangle swollen by 0.25 of the unit used there, 1.30562 for the rounded triangle of the runs), and it is the least of 52 shapes (ceiling.out); along the flow from eleven starts the least is 1.309 (along.out).

  • One more bound, from the same two inequalities. Cauchy–Schwarz on each curve gives I ≥ ∫ dt / L(t). Since the area falls at the rate L, dt = −dA / L, so I ≥ ∫ dA / L². The affine isoperimetric inequality says L³ ≤ 8π² A, with equality for ellipses (I have it through a search helper's reading of a paper, see ../sources.md). Hence

    I(K) ≥ ∫₀ᴬ dA / (8π² A)^(2/3) = 3 A^(1/3) / (8π²)^(2/3) = I of the disc of the same area,

with equality only for ellipses. So with the number of points left to chance, no convex shape's count has a smaller variance than an ellipse's of the same area: the ellipse has the fewest layers, the least spread in layers, and (with the triangle and the others at the ceiling) the greatest spread in proportion. With exactly n points the same holds for every shape in theory.out, but I have not proved it: the two bounds on I do not settle the sign there. * Two rules of flow.c varied (stopcheck.out): stopping at a ten-thousandth of the area in place of a thousandth moves I by 7 parts in a million at most; at a hundredth, by 1 in 10,000 (the triangle is not yet an ellipse there); a starting swell a hundred times smaller moves T and I by under 7 in a million. The step-halving rule never acted in the 27 runs the theory's table rests on. * I from the edge and I summed inside share the backward solve, so their agreement tests the identity, not the solve. The solve is tested by erode.c alone: the gradient of T by moving each side of a square with a corner cut off, then the edge formula, gives I to 3 parts in 10,000 of flow.c's (stest.py). * Which polygons are at the ceiling. A polygon is at the ceiling exactly when no way of moving its sides changes T/A^(2/3) to first order. Triangles and parallelograms: nothing can change it. Regular polygons: by symmetry. Kites too: a kite is the one mirror-symmetric member of the one-parameter family of quadrilaterals with its directions of sides, so the derivative along the family vanishes there. The referee's blind step found four polygons with an incircle that it could not tell from 4/3 to 5 parts in 10,000. At 1 part in 100,000 (incircle.out) six such polygons fall short by 0.000007 (a kite, and a quadrilateral that is nearly a parallelogram: nothing, to the reckoning's own floor, which is what a rectangle reads) up to 0.000124 (an irregular hexagon; the same on a finer mesh, at half the step and with a hundredth of the starting swell). So an incircle brings a polygon close to the ceiling and not onto it. * What the referee found thin in sections 4 and 5 (its report, referee/report.md, finding 5; it found no hole). (i) The end of the flow. "It becomes an ellipse and Γ goes to nothing with it" leans on a theorem I have not read. It is not needed, by the referee's argument, which I have followed: g ≥ 0 (a larger shape lasts longer); the kernel of L⁻¹ is bounded, so |∫ g L⁻¹g| is at most a constant times (∫ g dθ)²; the form is a mixed area and area-keeping affine maps leave it alone; map the shape so that the largest ellipse inside it is a disc of radius r; then h ≥ r, so ∫ g dθ ≤ (4/3) T / r, and T is at most the time of the disc of radius 2r that holds the shape; so Γ is at most a constant times r^(2/3) and goes to nothing with the shape. (ii) Polygons. At a sharp corner the identity is a limit: with corners rounded at radius ε the share of g that lies between the sides' directions falls only as about ε^0.37 (the referee's solver), though it does fall. (iii) I A/T² is not smooth at a polygon: rounding a triangle's corners at 1e-4 costs 0.00028 and at 0.02 costs 0.0113, about the 0.7 power; at the 1e-6 of every figure here that is 0.00001, which is what the polygons read below 4/3. * Not proved: section 5 says "among all shapes those are the ellipses". That an ellipse is such a shape is true; that no other smooth shape is, I have not shown. The theorem is: I ≤ (4/3) T²/A, with equality exactly where T/A^(2/3) stands still.
