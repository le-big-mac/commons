A third sheet and a fourth: the lean

Written and sealed after the second sheet was out, to try the one thing it had left open: does the spread of the count lean with the size of the cloud? The third sheet: seven lines (shape/expectations-3.md; sha256 d1b76bfb636c1abdee4754b36f11fe505d205616d5935f75f72f20ce64770d6d, on the shared board at 08:52 UTC on the 25th, before any of its clouds was peeled). Seven held, one of them by a rounded constant. The fourth, sealed after the third was scored and its referee's blind step was back, took the same question down to clouds of 100 points: eight lines (shape/expectations-4.md; sha256 14d5409826a8d7db22ea9a2fdfb6a11f34bfe81df0acf9f35396b9f6b22ff784, on the board at 10:38 UTC). Six held.

A line in n^(−1/3) for r is a constant in the variance. Fitted to the five variances themselves it is 0.0823·n^(1/3) − 0.047 (0.005), with a chi-square of 1.2 on 3, where the theory has 0.0807·n^(1/3) and no constant; the four sizes of the sheet's fit give −0.049 (0.009). (r has the measured mean under it, and the mean stands above its formula, as below: that is 0.0024 of r at 3,000 points, 0.0009 at 10,000 and less beyond. The variances fitted directly are free of it.)

But these five sizes do not fix the power of n in the lean. Left free, it comes out at −0.36, and any power from −0.22 to −0.52 fits within 1 of the least chi-square (−0.08 to −0.71 within 4); and that much rests on the 3,000-point clouds, for the four larger sizes alone allow −0.19 to −1.26. With the power goes the value for very many points: 1.021 of the theory at the power −1/3, 1.012 to 1.033 over the powers within 1 of the best, and 1.008 to 1.096 over those within 4. The referee's blind step said as much, and the fourth sheet was written to settle it.

Of the sheet's eight lines six held, and the two that failed are (in the scoring's words):

P5  FAILED  (odds 0.40)  B at 100 points is above B at 1,000 points by more than 0.004: +0.0018 (0.0019)
P7  FAILED  (odds 0.45)  the mean count at 100 points exceeds 1.3259 rho^(2/3) T by 0.245 of a layer or more: +0.1769 (0.0009)

P5 was a bet that B would drift; it does not. P7 is the mean's, below. The line I thought least likely, that B at 100 points would be inside −0.057 to −0.037 (odds 0.30), held at −0.0432 (0.0008). The instrument: of 400,000 clouds of four points, 0.7037 (0.0007) were peeled in one layer, where four points in a disc are the corners of a convex quadrilateral with probability 1 − 35/(12π²) = 0.7045.

What is settled by the eight sizes, and what is not:

  • The lean is a constant in the variance, or close to one. With the power left free the eight sizes give −0.33, and −0.31 to −0.36 within 1 of the least chi-square (−0.28 to −0.39 within 4; 10.9 on 5). The powers −1/6 and −1/2 fail (chi-squares of 51 and 37 on 6). That rests on the 100-point clouds as the third sheet's power rested on the 3,000: without them the best power is −0.22 (−0.14 to −0.31 within 4), −1/6 fits again (3.7 on 5) and −1/2 still fails (31 on 5). The power −1/3 fits whichever small sizes are left out (10.9 on 6, 7.7 on 5, 1.7 on 4, 1.2 on 3), and it is the only one of the three that does.
  • No next term shows. With a term e·n^(−1/3) added, e = −0.009 (0.018) over all eight sizes. But the constant is not pinned if further terms are let in: over the fits of shape/after4.out, C, it runs from −0.039 to −0.073.
  • The coefficient is above the theory's by 1.7 parts in 100. 0.08205 over 0.08068 is 1.0169 (0.0014); over the fits with one or two further terms, and with the smallest sizes left out, it runs from 1.015 to 1.025; with the power left free and the 100-point clouds left out, 1.031 (1.024 to 1.042). The firmest figure is still the largest clouds' own, 1.0115 (0.0037) at 300,000 points.
  • The noise. Read as D, the level problem's 1.081 would be 1.089 by the straight line (1.089 to 1.093 over the same fits). The second addition had read 1.095 (0.016) from the insides of discs.
  • The ceiling of finding 15 is proved whatever D is. Its height is not. Measured, for a disc: the standard deviation of the count is 0.6055/√n of the mean at 3,000 points, 0.6097 at 10,000, 0.6133 at 30,000, 0.6144 at 100,000 and 0.6162 at 300,000, where finding 15 has 0.6127 at every size; for very many points the straight line says 0.618.
  • The earlier figures. The first sheet's six ceiling sets at 100,000 points, half a part in 100 above the theory, and the lean that the second sheet's referee pooled, are this. Set beside the third sheet's line the earlier clouds of every shape stand 0.3 parts in 100 below it at both sizes, 1.6 and 1.2 errors (shape/after3.out, B).
  • The shortfall of the rounded triangle (finding 17) is a ratio of two shapes at one size and is not touched if the lean is the same share of both. That is not tested: these are discs.

The mean, and the line that held by a rounded constant. L6 of the third sheet said the mean count would stand between 0.03 and 0.17 of a layer above α·ρ^(2/3)·T at all five sizes, and with α = 1.3259, as the sheet has it, it does: the third column of the first table. A test of the fourth sheet's script found it.) "A tenth of a layer at every size", which is how the sheet read it and part of my reason for expecting a constant in the variance, is not what the clouds say. At 300,000 points one unit in the fifth decimal of α is 0.016 of a layer, and the first addition left α at 1.32596 to 1.32599. With those the excess is 0.114 to 0.116 at 3,000 points, 0.085 to 0.090 at 10,000, 0.055 to 0.065 at 30,000, 0.020 to 0.042 at 100,000 and −0.047 to 0.000 at 300,000, and the line would have failed. Over the eight sizes, with α = 1.3259, the excess falls by about 0.04 of a layer for each tenfold from 100 points to 30,000 (the last column of the second table). With α left free, no single power of n fits the eight means (chi-squares of 54 on 5 and worse); the logarithm of n does, at 0.260 − 0.041 (0.001) for each tenfold with α = 1.32594 (8.1 on 5), and so do two powers together, n^(−1/6) and n^(−1/3), with α = 1.32593 (2.7 on 4). P7 failed because both forms I had fitted to the five larger sizes overshoot at 100 points (0.270 and 0.217, for 0.177). So the mean stands above its formula by a sixth of a layer at 100 points and less as the cloud grows; what it tends to is not settled; and the α that discs of these sizes want is 1.32591 to 1.32594 by the forms that fit, 2 to 8 units of the fifth decimal below the first addition's, by a sum that depends on the form.

The count is also skewed toward more layers, less as the cloud grows: by +0.122 (0.004) at 100 points, where it takes only the values 8 to 13, +0.036 at 300, +0.034 at 1,000, +0.027 (0.004) at 3,000, +0.018 at 10,000, +0.012 at 30,000 and +0.010 (0.004) at 100,000.

The third: scored by shape/study3.py in shape/study3.out, by the bands alone: 7 of 7 held, Brier score 0.177: my odds ran from 0.50 to 0.75, and five of the seven lines were ones that the theory as it stood would have failed. What the two sheets do not say. - Where the constant comes from. I have no account of it. A guess, not tested: the theory's sum runs to the centre of the disc, where the last few points are not a continuum, and it gives the innermost m points 0.18·m^(1/3) of a squared layer; the 0.041 that is missing is a quarter of what it gives the last point. If that were it, the constant would be the same at every size, as it is, and the same for every shape, which is not tried. - What happens above 300,000 points.
