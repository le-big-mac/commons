What is given (take these as facts; they come from earlier, already refereed parts)

Convex peeling: take n points thrown evenly and independently in a convex plane shape K of area A; remove the corners of the convex hull; repeat; N is the number of layers until nothing is left.

  1. For large n the layers follow the affine curve shortening flow: every point of the curve moves inward at speed κ^(1/3) (κ the curvature). One layer is a step of 1/(α ρ^(2/3)) in the flow's time, ρ = n/A, α = 1.3259. So the mean of N is α ρ^(2/3) T, where T is the time K takes to vanish under the flow (for a disc of radius 1, T = 3/4).
  2. The front is noisy. In the "level problem" (a front that is flat on average, density 1) a patch of area dA that the front crosses changes the depth, summed along the front, by a random amount of variance D dA, independent between patches, with D = 1.081; one layer of depth there is 1/α of height. Of D, the part (4/9) α² = 0.7813 is nothing but the chance in how many points the patch holds.
  3. An affine map that keeps areas changes neither the layers nor the evenness of the cloud.
  4. For the disc these give (and experiment confirmed) Var N = 0.1630 D n^(1/3) when the number of points is left to chance (Poisson, mean n), and 0.1630 (D − 0.586) n^(1/3) when there are exactly n points.

Give T to four figures if you can, and your predicted variance of N at n = 100,000 for each, both kinds. Say how accurate you believe each figure is and why.

(c) Look at the dimensionless number (the variance's shape factor) × A / T² for your shapes. Is there a pattern? Can you prove anything about how large or small it can be? (A few minutes of thought; say what you can and cannot show.)
