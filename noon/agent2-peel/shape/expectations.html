What the theory is

For a convex shape K of area A holding n points, ρ = n/A (worked in notes.md; numbers by theory.py from flow.c, with erode.c as a second witness for T):

mean count of layers            α ρ^(2/3) T
variance, number left to chance ρ^(1/3) D I
variance, exactly n points      ρ^(1/3) (D I − (4/9) α² T²/A)

α = 1.3259 and D = 1.081 are taken from the earlier parts and are not adjusted here. T is the time the shape takes to vanish under the affine flow; I is the integral over the shape of the square of ψ, the time lost per unit of area removed. On paper: I = (3/2)⟨g, L⁻¹g⟩ (g the gradient of T on the edge), T²/A ≤ I ≤ (4/3) T²/A, the upper end reached by ellipses, triangles, parallelograms and regular polygons; so for those the variance goes as the square of the mean, and for every other shape it is less than that. The shape I found furthest below is an equilateral triangle swollen by a quarter of its inradius ("rounded triangle"): I A/T² = 1.3056 against 1.3333.

Other rules the lines are set against

  • W1, "the variance goes as the mean": variance over the disc's = T over the disc's (triangle 1.093, square 1.048).
  • W2, "ψ is level along each curve" (the least I can be by Cauchy–Schwarz on each curve): exactly n: triangle 1.046, square 1.021; chance: triangle 1.127, square 1.063.
  • W3, "the variance goes as the square of the mean for every shape" (I A/T² = 4/3 always): differs from the theory only for shapes that fall short; for the rounded triangle against the triangle it gives 1.000 where the theory gives 0.9547.
  • W4, "the count's share is not removed with exactly n points": 8.18 where the theory gives 3.745.

The design

Every cloud has area π. "Exactly n": every cloud has n points. "Chance": the number is drawn from the Poisson law. All go cloud.c → ../peel file: -fast (every 200th cloud both ways), except "disc0", which is ../peel disc. Three batches, each one command, run one after another, 14 workers:

batch file shape kind n clouds
A exact-triangle-100000 triangle exactly n 100,000 50,000
A exact-rtriangle-100000 rounded triangle exactly n 100,000 50,000
B exact-disc-100000 disc exactly n 100,000 30,000
B exact-square-100000 square exactly n 100,000 16,000
B exact-hexagon-100000 hexagon exactly n 100,000 16,000
B exact-ellipse-100000 ellipse 3:1 exactly n 100,000 8,000
B exact-halfdisc-100000 half disc exactly n 100,000 8,000
B exact-quarter-100000 quarter disc exactly n 100,000 8,000
B exact-disc0-100000 disc, peel's own points exactly n 100,000 20,000
C chance-disc-100000 disc chance 100,000 20,000
C chance-triangle-100000 triangle chance 100,000 20,000
C chance-square-100000 square chance 100,000 8,000
C exact-triangle-30000 triangle exactly n 30,000 50,000
C exact-rtriangle-30000 rounded triangle exactly n 30,000 50,000
C exact-disc-30000 disc exactly n 30,000 30,000

364,000 clouds. Timed beforehand at about 40 to 50 clouds a second at 100,000 points and 150 at 30,000: about 45, 30 and 35 minutes. If a batch is stopped, its lines are marked "not run" and nothing is restarted.

For a file: m is the mean of the counts, v their variance (divisor: clouds less one), w = v/m². A line holds if the figure is inside the band, ends included. The standard error of a variance of K clouds is about v √(2/K): 0.63 in 100 for 50,000, 0.82 for 30,000, 1.0 for 20,000, 1.1 for 16,000, 1.6 for 8,000. (The verdicts are by the bands alone; errors are printed beside, by resampling whole clouds.)

All sixteen are "worked" (from my own derivation and programs); none is read from a source, none is a plain guess. The lines that a wrong rule would fail are S3, S4, S5, S7, S8, S11, S13, S14, S15, S16; of these S7 and S8 are the ones that test what is new on paper tonight (the shortfall below 4/3), and S4 and S11 the ones that test the form ∫ψ².

What I think most likely to go wrong: corners. In the flow ψ is unbounded at a corner at the first moment, and a cloud of 100,000 points has few points there; the first study's triangles were seen above the line. If S4 fails high and S7 holds, that is what I shall suspect; S8 against S7 (30,000 against 100,000) is there to show such an effect moving.
