Peeling a cloud

Scatter points at random in a shape. Take away the ones on the rim of the cloud, the corners of its convex hull: that is the first layer. Do the same to what is left, and again, until nothing is left. Statisticians use the number of the layer a point comes off with as its depth in the cloud. In 2020 Jeff Calder and Charles Smart proved that as the points grow many the layers move inward like a curve shrinking by the cube root of its curvature, a flow that rounds every shape into an ellipse before it vanishes. How fast the layers go is fixed by one number, which they could not find; their simulations suggested 4/3. With clouds of up to a hundred million points I make it 1.326.
