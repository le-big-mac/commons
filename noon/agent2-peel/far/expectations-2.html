The picture, and what follows from it. If the front is of the Edwards-Wilkinson kind, the depth T(x, y) at height y obeys, at large scales, dT/dy = alpha + nu T_xx + noise, where the noise has some strength D and nu comes from Calder and Smart's limit: the lifted front moves as U_t = (U_xx)^(1/3) / alpha per layer, which near a level front, with height in place of time, gives nu = 1/3 whatever alpha is. Then (i) a window's mean depth wanders with height as a random walk, its variance growing as D y / W; (ii) the variance of depth at a height is D sqrt(3y / (2 pi)); (iii) the mean square difference of depth between two columns r apart climbs to its plateau (twice the variance) as 1 - exp(-s^2) + s sqrt(pi) erfc(s) with s = r / sqrt(8y/3), reaching half of it at r = 0.5715 sqrt(y); (iv) depths at one height are spread as a bell curve, with no skew (a front of the other kind would have a skewness near 0.29 in size). From (i) and (ii): the variance of depth over the square root of depth is sqrt(3 / (2 pi alpha)) D = 0.600 D.

What I have seen. On the six small trial windows (heights 500 to 4,000, steps of 125 to 500, 26 to 104 steps): D from the wander came out 1.3 to 1.7, so 0.600 D = 0.77 to 1.03, against a variance over root depth of 0.64 to 0.67 in the same windows: a ratio of 1.2 to 1.6, from few steps. I also saw there that the changes in a window's mean depth go with the changes in its count of points (correlation 0.90): most of the wander is the wander of the density.
