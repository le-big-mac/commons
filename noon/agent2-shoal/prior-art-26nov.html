0. In short

  • The README's closing contrast ("two coexisting, long-lived states chosen by the starting conditions rather than … clean bands") is the 2002 paper's own headline result, its "collective memory". Tunstrøm et al. (2013) state it in nearly the README's words. The README credits neither.
  • The one result not in print, mills at 35–60% of runs at every width 2–8, is the opposite of what is reported for the 3-D original, where the two-state range is narrow (Couzin 2002 Fig. 4; Chan and Kanso 2025: 100% mills up to radius 2.8, 100% schools from 3.4).
  • The program departs from the 2002 rule in a way the README does not own up to: it normalises the alignment and attraction vectors before averaging. In a five-run check of mine, the printed rule gave a different outcome every time.
  • "Never in between" and "23 never changed" overstate the study's own files: 24 of 1,440 long-run checks were neither state, and 211 of the 280 runs were split schools scored on their largest piece.
  • I used 26 of 30 requests and about 10 of 20 minutes. I did not obtain Strömbom (2011), Costanzo and Hemelrijk (2018), or any paper on a 2-D version of this exact zone model.

1. Claims (written before any search; saved in step1-claims.txt)

  • C1, model. A 2-D version of the Couzin 2002 zone model with N = 100: repel inside radius 1, copy headings in the orientation zone, turn toward fish in the attraction zone, blind wedge behind, turning limit 40°/s. "The only thing varied is the width of the orientation zone." Presented as a known model, "my own 2-D reduction, not a replication". The README is silent on noise (the code has 0.05 rad), speed (3), the 90° blind wedge, and the fixed outer radius 14.
  • C2. Width 0 gives a cohesive disordered swarm, alignment about 0.17. Not marked new or known.
  • C3. Width 1.5 and up: "two outcomes and nothing between". The largest group mills (alignment about 0.1, rotation 0.6–0.9) or travels aligned (about 0.85); which one depends on the seed. Presented as the study's finding.
  • C4. Both states last: in 24 runs of 3,000 time units, checked 60 times each, 23 never changed, one mill became aligned, none went the other way. Presented as the study's finding.
  • C5. Mills do not get rarer with width: 20, 17, 15, 14, 16, 20, 24 of 40 at widths 2–8 (35–60%). The first sweep's decline "was noise". All 280 runs ended as one state or the other. Presented as the study's finding.
  • C6. The start weakly foretells the outcome: AUC 0.63 (3.7 s.e.) for initial circulation, 0.58 (2.3 s.e.) for initial alignment. Presented as the study's finding.
  • C7, closing. "The textbook sequence swarm → mill → aligned school is here, but as two coexisting, long-lived states chosen by the starting conditions rather than as clean bands." It implies the 2002 paper found one state per band, and that coexistence is the departure.
  • C8, caveats. Largest connected group; own thresholds; the original is 3-D.
  • Front page. (a) The rule is "turn toward their neighbours". (b) The control is the strength of "the pull". (c) "Never in between."

2. What is in print, claim by claim

C1: FOUND, with differences (listed in section 3). - Couzin et al. 2002, J. Theor. Biol. 218, 1–11 (dl/02/couzin2002.pdf). Fig. 3 caption, p. 6: "N = 100, rr = 1, α = 270, θ = 40, s = 3, σ = 0.05. Data shown in (E) and (F) are the mean of 30 replicates per parameter combination." - p. 3: the model is "simulated in continuous three-dimensional space", with time step "0.1 s". - The zone model is not theirs alone; p. 2: "Following the approach of Aoki (1982), Reynolds (1987) and Huth & Wissel (1992), we…". - Calovi et al. 2014 (dl/04) call it "the Aoki-Couzin model". - Sure, from the page itself.

C2: FOUND in substance; the figure 0.17 is not in a source. - Couzin 2002 p. 5: "Swarm: an aggregate with cohesion, but a low level of polarization … This occurs when individuals perform attraction and repulsion behaviours, but little or no parallel orientation". - p. 7: "If ro is close to 1 (Δro ≈ 0), the group exhibits swarm behaviour (low pgroup, low mgroup)." - Sure.

C3: FOUND for the 3-D model; the 2-D numbers NOT FOUND in what I read. - Couzin 2002, Discussion p. 9: "our model reveals that two very different collective states can occur for identical individual behaviour, demonstrating the importance of the previous history of the group structure." - Same page, on "nothing between": the model predicts groups "change rapidly between these states (for example, between the torus and dynamic parallel group types) since intermediate group types are relatively (dynamically) unstable." - Tunstrøm et al. 2013, PLoS Comput Biol 9(2) e1002915, Introduction (dl/03): "In the model of Couzin et al. [3], for example, the milling and polarized state co-exist over a region of parameter space (see Paley et al. [10] for a formal analysis of this bistable regime). The question of which collective behavior is adopted therefore depends on the initial configuration of the system, in this case the positions and orientations of individuals". - Their own runs of the 2002 model (500 replicates per speed), Fig. 8 caption: the system "is found either in a highly polarized state or in a milling state." - Paley, Leonard, Sepulchre and Couzin 2007, Proc. 46th IEEE CDC (dl/14), Section II: "For intermediate values of Δρ(2), the collective exhibits either circular motion or parallel motion". - Calovi et al. 2014, abstract (a 2-D model of a different kind, N = 100): "a transition region between milling and schooling, in which the school exhibits multistability and intermittency between schooling and milling for the same combination of individuals parameters." - Chan and Kanso, "Noise-Induced Collective Memory in Schooling Fish", arXiv:2507.16102v1, 21 Jul 2025, a preprint (dl/26), Fig. 4 caption: "Bimodal histograms of P and M at zl = 3 and zl∗ = 3.1 highlight that near the bifurcation, either milling or schooling are attainable." - Sure that the substance is old.

C4: FOUND IN PART (one-way change and escape from milling, in 3-D); the counts NOT FOUND. - Couzin 2002 p. 8, on decreasing r_o: "Group polarization does not decrease, and angular momentum remains low and the group does not adopt the torus conformation." - Tunstrøm 2013: "if groups start in a relatively disordered state they tend to form a milling formation; but tend to remain in the polarized configuration if they begin sufficiently aligned with one another." - Chan and Kanso 2025: "in the backward direction, the group remains locked in the schooling state and is unable to transition back to milling." - The same preprint measures an "Escape rate κ from milling to schooling" fitted by Kramers' formula, and reports "transient milling that gives way to schooling as time progresses." - The field would therefore read the README's single mill-to-aligned event as a one-way escape from a metastable mill. One event in about 40,000 time units of milling gives no usable rate. - Fairly sure.

C5: NOT FOUND, and the 3-D sources say the opposite. - Couzin 2002 p. 5: the torus "occurs when Δro is relatively small and Δra is relatively large". - Fig. 4 text, r_a = 14, the same line the README runs: "As ro increases towards 1.5, the group … enters the torus phase … Beyond 2.5, however, the group enters a polarized configuration". - Chan and Kanso 2025, Fig. 4 (N = 100, z_r = 1, z_a = 10, σ = 0.01, 300 runs per value, 3-D): "For zl ≤ 2.8, 100% of the simulations converge to milling and for zl ≥ 3.4, 100% converge to schooling". Text: "bistability is a phenomenon localized near the bifurcation point". - Heins et al. 2024, arXiv:2307.14804v4 (dl/23): "the original 3-zone Couzin model, where milling was only stable under a relatively limited range of parameters". - Which to believe: both, each for its own model. The README's counts are right for its program; my control reproduced five of five labels in trend.json. They are not a property of the 2002 model. - The README's own data also show the mill share tracks fragmentation: mills were 46 of the 69 unsplit runs and 80 of the 211 split runs (own_numbers.py). - Sure of the contradiction; not sure of its cause (dimension, the combining rule, or fragment size).

C6: FOUND IN PART (direction only); the AUC figures NOT FOUND. - The Tunstrøm sentence under C4 is the known dependence: disordered starts tend to mill, aligned starts stay polarised. - The README tests only random starts, so it never tests that known dependence. - In couzin.py one random stream sets both the start and the noise through the run. "Depends on the seed" is shown; "chosen by the starting conditions" is not. - Chan and Kanso attribute the split near the transition to noise ("noise-induced bistability").

C7: the first half is FOUND (it is the 2002 paper's Fig. 4 and title); the implied contrast is wrong. - Couzin 2002 abstract: "we present the first evidence for collective memory in such animal groups (where the previous history of group structure influences the collective behaviour exhibited as individual interactions change)". - Paley 2007: "This hysteresis phenomenon … is called 'collective memory' [4]". - What is actually different in the README is the width of the coexistence range, which is C5.

Front page. (a) and (b) are wrong against the README's own model: there are three rules, and the knob is the alignment zone, with the attraction zone shrinking from 13 to 5 as it widens. (c) is covered in section 4.

3. Credits and unacknowledged differences

Credits - Lines 27–28: credit Couzin et al. 2002 for the coexistence (hysteresis, "collective memory"), and Tunstrøm et al. 2013 for the plain statement that the initial configuration decides. Paley et al. 2007 is the formal treatment. - Line 3: the zone model descends from Aoki (1982), Reynolds (1987) and Huth and Wissel (1992), which the 2002 paper itself says. I did not read those three. - Lines 15–19: cite the 3-D results that contradict the claim, Couzin 2002 Fig. 4 and Chan and Kanso 2025. - Line 27, "textbook sequence": it is specifically the increasing branch of Couzin 2002 Fig. 4.

Differences from the 2002 model that the README does not state 1. Combining rule. couzin.py uses .5*(nrm(d_o)+nrm(d_a)). The paper's eqns (2)–(3) are plain sums of unit vectors and d = ½[d_o + d_a]; I checked this on the page image (pages/couzin2002-p3-03.png). Chan and Kanso write the normalised form, so the README's rule has a precedent, but it is not the 2002 rule. 2. Line 6, "the only thing varied". The outer radius is fixed at 14, so the attraction width falls from 13 to 5 as the orientation width goes from 0 to 8. This is the paper's Fig. 4 design ("keeping ra fixed"), run from random starts instead of as a ramp. 3. Fragmented schools. The paper: "analysis is only performed on non-fragmented groups", and it marks a region where groups "have a greater than 50% chance of fragmenting". The README scores the largest piece of split schools: 211 of 280 runs were split, the largest group averaged 77.8 fish, and the smallest was 23. The paper links fish by "the presence of other individuals within the field of perception"; the code links by distance under 14 alone. 4. Number of states. The paper has four (swarm, torus, dynamic parallel, highly parallel); the README has three. 5. Noise. The README never mentions it. The code has it, at the paper's value. 6. Start. A square of side 18, against the paper's "random positions within a sphere in which each can detect at least one individual". 7. Replicates. The paper used 30 per setting; Tunstrøm used 500 and Chan and Kanso 300. The README used 6, then 40. 8. Group size. One N only. The paper says "the collective behaviour is dependent on group size". Tunstrøm: "For groups of 300 agents the milling state is dominant and no instances of the polar state are found". Calovi: milling "does not occur for arbitrarily large groups". 9. Noise level. One level only. Chan and Kanso report no milling at σ = 0.10.

4. Sentences that say more than the sources or the data allow

Each fair sentence was checked against the files by own_numbers.py and fair_check.py.

  • Front page.
  • Fair: "A hundred model fish that keep clear of close neighbours, line up with those at middle distance and turn toward those farther off. Once the lining-up zone is at least 1.5 units wide, the largest group in every run ended either circling or swimming one way, and which of the two changed from run to run; that such a model has two states under the same rules has been known since 2002."
  • Checked: 84 sweep runs, 280 forty-seed runs and the last check of all 24 long runs, none neither.
  • Lines 11–12, "nothing between".
  • Fair: "By my thresholds, each of the 84 runs at widths 1.5–8 ended with its largest group either milling (rotation 0.61–0.88, alignment 0.07–0.22) or aligned (0.84–0.88); at widths 0.5 and 1.0, three of twelve runs were in between."
  • The three in-between runs have alignment/rotation 0.54/0.33, 0.65/0.25 and 0.48/0.49.
  • Lines 13–14, "23 never changed".
  • Fair: "20 of 24 runs had the same label at all 60 checks; three more never crossed between mill and aligned but had checks that were neither (19, 4 and 1 of 60); one mill became an aligned school; no aligned school became a mill."
  • Lines 17–18, "that was noise".
  • Fair: "The first sweep had 7 mills in 36 runs at widths 5.5–8.0, the 40-seed runs 60 in 120 at widths 6–8, about three standard errors apart (z = 3.25); I have not explained the gap, though the twelve long runs at width 6.0 ended with 6 mills."
  • Lines 15–16, the heading.
  • Add: "This is unlike the 3-D original, where mills from random starts are reported only in a narrow range; and here mills were 46 of 69 unsplit runs against 80 of 211 split ones."
  • Lines 27–28.
  • Fair: "So the sequence swarm → mill → aligned school of Couzin et al. (2002, Fig. 4) appears here with the mill and the aligned school both occurring, from random starts, at every whole-number width from 2 to 8 (and in the six-seed sweep at every width from 1.5 to 8 except 5.5). That two states can occur under identical rules is that paper's own result; what differs in this 2-D variant is how wide the range is. Whether the start or the noise during the run decides, I did not separate."
  • Title, "one knob". Two zone widths move (item 2 in section 3).
