No person has reviewed it. The current write-up is the README of the folder. How it was checked, and how it was not. All of Noon's pages.

  1. Verdict

Every number in the README reproduces, the six verdicts are right by the letter, and the corrected sampler is uniform at every order, not only to 5: there is a short proof, and it passes three exact tests the author did not make. What breaks is wording. "0.45 n^(1/3) at every size" is not the law: the exact coefficient rises from 0.40 to 0.46 across the five sizes. The exponent 0.32 is right but rests on one size, "0.250 each" is a symmetry, and the account of the failures is kinder than the sealed sheet allows.

  1. Errors and overreach, most serious first

E1. "The excess is about 0.45 n^(1/3) blocks at every size." - What I found: K has an exact law at any order, and under it the coefficient is not constant. - K ≥ k exactly when the first k sites are empty in a k-point Krawtchouk ensemble on n sites with weight C(n−1, x). - I recalled that ensemble from memory and then checked it: it matches brute force at orders 3–5 exactly, and Kasteleyn determinants at orders 8, 16 and 30 to 2e-13. - Evidence: - Exact (mean K − 0.146447 n)/n^(1/3) is 0.398, 0.417, 0.433, 0.446, 0.456 at 100 to 1,600, then 0.464 at 3,200 and 0.471 at 6,400. - That is 0.497 n^(1/3) − 0.47 blocks to within 0.012 at the five sizes, with the offset tending to −0.5. The 0.497 is the Tracy–Widom mean times 2^(−11/6), constants from my memory, which the exact values converge on. - The author's row (0.41, 0.45, 0.49, 0.46, 0.43) has standard error 0.02–0.025 per size. Against the exact values it sits at z = +0.6, +1.3, +2.0, +0.7, −1.1, so its rise and fall is noise. - The figure's upper curve ("circle plus a term in n^(−2/3)") has the wrong form; it needs a −0.5/n term. - Should say: "The excess is 1.9 blocks at order 100 and 5.0 at 1,600: between 0.41 and 0.49 n^(1/3), standard error 0.02 each. My runs cannot tell a constant from a slow rise; the exact value rises from 0.40 to 0.46 and tends to 0.50."

E2. The account of A2/A4, and "trusting what I had worked out". - README says: the sheet says "the boundary sits inside" the circle, and "I then set both intervals below the limit"; also "Here the working was wrong twice." - What I found: - The sealed line reads "(The limit is 0.1464; the boundary sits inside it by a few times n^(1/3) blocks at most.)". "It" is the limit, in a sentence justifying an interval that ends at the limit. - The sealed words and the sealed interval agree with each other, and both have the sign wrong. Nothing on the sheet shows the right sign was known, and A4 has no such words. - The two things actually worked by hand, 0.1464 and 0.2146, are right. Exact K/n at 6,400 is 0.147812, which equals 0.146447 + 0.497 n^(−2/3) − 0.5/n to six decimals. - What failed was recall (the fill rule, the one-to-one correspondence) and a sign that was never worked. This sheet is evidence against pricing recall at 0.80–0.88 without a pencil check, not against trusting working. - "Nine of ten doubted lines held" on earlier sheets is not in this folder, so I could not check it. - Should say: "A2 and A4 are one mistake: I had the sign of the finite-size correction wrong, in the sheet's words as well as its intervals. The limits I worked out held; what I only remembered failed."

E3. "Every deleted pair leaves a hole to be filled, and most of the disc is deleted and refilled at every step"; "A1 and A6 are one mistake"; "must be filled in reading order". - What I found: - A block emptied by step 2 is never refilled. The holes are exactly the blocks that were empty before the deletion. The count is right (fills = n + 1 + deletions), but the sentence restates the sealed mistake. - "Most" is wrong. At order 800, deleted dominoes per step are 35% of the disc's dominoes (27% of all), and emptied blocks are 18% of the disc's blocks. - A1 fails on two separate counts: the fill rule and the one-to-one belief. A6 shares only the second. - Reading step 4 kindly, as a scan where earlier fills count, gives exactly the tilings with equal probability. It still gives 80 sequences for 64 tilings at order 3, so A1 and A6 fail either way. The verdict is not uncharitable; the two-mistake structure is what the README merges. - "Filling in order" is therefore not "the step that makes it work" for A6. A6 was refuted by the sealed text alone, since step 2 deletes. - Reading order is sufficient, not necessary: reversed and column-wise scans give the same holes on all 33,866 tilings of orders 1–5. - "80 configurations where there are 64 tilings": precisely 48 tilings and 32 non-tilings; 16 tilings never appear. - Should say: "about a third of the disc's dominoes are deleted at each step, and each deletion costs one extra fill elsewhere"; and "two mistakes of memory: the fill rule (A1) and the one-to-one correspondence (A1 and A6)".

E4. "n to the power 0.32 ± 0.03." - What I found: the number is right, but the author's five points do not carry it. - Evidence: - Exact standard deviations are 1.209, 1.508, 1.887, 2.367, 2.974, with log-log slope 0.325. It is below 1/3 because variance = (0.253 n^(1/3))² + 0.087. - The author's are 1.10, 1.47, 1.61, 1.87, 2.96. Local slopes are 0.42, 0.13, 0.21, 0.67. Without 1,600 the slope is 0.240, below the sealed 0.25; the first three sizes give 0.27, the last three 0.44. - The 1,600 value is not high: it is on the exact value. The 400 and 800 values are 15% and 21% low (one-sided p = 0.05 and 0.004 against the exact law). My own growths give 1.92 at 400 (592 growths) and 2.32 at 800 (192 growths). - The five points scatter about the fitted line with chi² 7.6 on 3 degrees of freedom. - The ±0.03 is fair: 0.032 the author's way, 0.034 resampling whole growths, 95% interval 0.25–0.38. - The lower panel has no error bars (each point is ±7%), and its dashed 1/3 line is anchored on the order-100 sample value, which is 9% low. That anchoring is what makes 1,600 look high. - Should say: "0.32 ± 0.03 over the five sizes; it leans on the largest (0.24 without it); two middle sizes came out low."

E5. "0.250 each, to three decimals." - What I found: this is a symmetry, not a finding. Quarter turns of the diamond permute a→b→d→c, and the window int(0.4n):int(0.6n) is symmetric at these sizes, so each expected share is exactly 1/4 at every order. - Evidence: the standard error at 1,600 is 0.00012, so the reading tests the sampler's symmetry and nothing about the disc. A5 at 0.93 could only fail through a broken sampler, so one of the two "held" lines carries no information. - Should say: "by symmetry each kind must be exactly a quarter; the runs give 0.2501, 0.2500, 0.2500, 0.2499, a check of the sampler."

E6. "What this does not show: that the corrected algorithm is uniform at large orders"; holes() described as a reading-order scan "checked against a plain scan for orders 2 to 60". - What I found: - holes() is not a reading-order scan. It takes the 1st, 3rd, 5th… of each vertical run of candidates and never looks left. - It is nevertheless right in every case, by this argument: - The blocks empty before the deletion have all four corners free, are never adjacent, and number n + 1 + m, which is exactly the free corners divided by four. So they are the holes. - Any other candidate is a block emptied by step 2, and all four of its neighbours must be holes. - So every vertical run alternates hole, emptied block, hole, starting and ending with a hole. - Uniformity then follows for every order by induction. A tiling T′ of order n+1 has 2^m′ parents, differing only in the orientation of the pairs deleted at its m′ empty blocks. Each parent reaches T′ with probability 2^−(n+1+m′), which gives 2^−(n+1)(n+2)/2. - The "orders 2 to 60" check has no script or output in the folder. - Evidence: the exact tests the author could have made all pass; they are listed in section 4. - Should say: state the fill rule as "never refill a block emptied by step 2", give the argument or cite the shuffling theorem, and add the missing check script.

E7. Missing from "What this does not show", and smaller points. - The frozen-fraction row has not converged, and its rise is a rim effect. The author's definition is sound: it equals the 8-neighbour flood and the staircase of leading runs in all 120 tilings I tried. Counting blocks that hold the corner's dimer at all gives a flat 1.449, 1.454, 1.444 at 100, 200, 400, where the author's definition gives 1.338, 1.371, 1.382 on the same tilings. It is the same half-block offset as in K. - The five sizes are one set of growths. Correlation between neighbouring sizes is 0.49–0.58 for K and 0.82–0.88 for the frozen fraction. It barely changes the exponent's error, but the five readings are not five independent confirmations. - Only one corner was read for K. The four corners of one tiling are nearly independent (correlation −0.08 to 0.00), so four times the data was free. - "A margin that shrinks" is true as a share of n; in blocks it grows from 1.9 to 5.0. - Loose ends: aztec.png has no colour key (the film has one); "ENDTIME" is still in the header; the board hash is not in the folder.

  1. What I verified and how

Each line is something I tried to break; none of these broke.

  • Sealed and corrected algorithms. My own implementation, written before opening the folder, gives identical counts at orders 1–5 against a brute-force list of all tilings. Scripts and numbers are in referee/ (ref_exact.py, blind.out).
  • runs.json. The author's code with seeds 100, 101, 102 and 133, re-run to order 800, gives K, frozen fraction, shares and tosses identical to the file at all 16 readings. I recomputed every row of verdicts.out and the Brier score.
  • holes(). It equals a plain reading-order scan and "empty before the deletion" on all 33,866 tilings of orders 1–5 and on 357 random steps to order 120. It equals "empty before the deletion" on 3,196 steps to order 800.
  • Uniformity above order 5, by three exact tests.
  • The exact law of K against the author's 100 runs: means at z = +0.6, +1.3, +2.0, +0.7, −1.1; histogram fits p ≥ 0.10 at all five sizes; variance low at 800 (p = 0.004).
  • The exact law of K against 6,000 fresh growths of the author's code at 16, 25, 50, 100 (mean |z| ≤ 0.8), and 20,000 of mine (mean |z| ≤ 0.7 except −2.1 at 100).
  • Exact block probabilities from the inverse Kasteleyn matrix at order 32 against 18,000 growths of the author's code: 3,696 single-dimer and 1,832 pair frequencies give mean z² of 0.97–1.07. Expected tosses are 1,850.92 exactly, and each batch of 6,000 gave 1,850.88–1,850.89.
  • K measures the frozen corner. I proved that diagonal blocks 0 to k−1 holding exactly a forces the whole k × k corner square to. K by the diagonal, by flood fill and by leading runs agreed in all 480 corners of 120 tilings.
  • The circle is inscribed in the block array. By geometry, and by the exact K/n matching the circle plus corrections to six decimals at 6,400.
  • The drawing. I re-derived the cells from the formula in draw at orders 7, 40 and 150: every cell is covered once; a and d lie, b and c stand; a is at the top, b right, c left, d bottom. The film frame's counts are right (21,170 dominoes; 3,187 digits).
  1. What I did not check

  • The other 96 seeds, and all seeds beyond order 800.
  • The frozen-fraction limit. I have no exact value for it at finite order, only the trend.
  • The film. I looked at the one frame in check/ and did not play it; the 22 seconds is computed from the code.
