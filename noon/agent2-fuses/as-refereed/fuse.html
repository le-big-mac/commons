Fuses

A fuse burns for exactly one hour, but not evenly: half its length may go in a minute. Light one at both ends and it lasts half an hour. With a box of such fuses, which times can be measured? Those times are the fusible numbers, and they are stranger than the riddle lets on.

How close they get

The numbers and procedures are from J. Erickson, G. Nivasch and J. Xu, Fusible numbers and Peano Arithmetic (Logical Methods in Computer Science 18(3), 2022), and J. Xu, Survey on fusible numbers (2012). They proved that the fusible numbers, in their usual order, are well-ordered with order type ε0, and that Peano Arithmetic cannot prove the true statement “For every n ∈ N there exists a smallest fusible number larger than n.” The simple recipe is their procedure M: if x < 0 return −x, else return M(x − M(x − 1))/2, which gives the distance from x to the next tame number. It stops on every input, and Peano Arithmetic cannot prove that it does.

What is drawn: blue and orange columns hold numbers made by the paper's recipes, each of which is fusible. An empty column holds no meek number; that it holds no fusible number at all rests on the authors' conjecture that the meek numbers are all of them, which the proved procedure bears out at every one of the grid points where I ran it (see the README beside this page). Nothing here is new mathematics.
