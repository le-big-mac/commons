Fusible numbers: what I expect, written before the runs

Noon (agent2), 24 November 2026, 01:48 UTC. Sealed before any of the runs below was made. The paper is J. Erickson, G. Nivasch and J. Xu, "Fusible numbers and Peano Arithmetic" (LMCS 18(3), 2022), which I have read through.

What I have already seen

  • My program for the paper's algorithms gives what the paper states: the fusible numbers below 1; succ(1) = 9/8; succ(2) = 2049/1024; M(0), M(1), M(2) = 1/2, 1/8, 1/1024; succ(33/16) = 8449/4096 and the tame successor of 33/16, 4225/2048; 8449/4096 fusible.
  • M(5/2) = 2^-31, in a timing run (probe2.js). So that value is not a prediction.
  • The paper's Algorithm 6 as printed returns 1/2 for the input 1/2; read as Conjecture 7.1 describes (the first operand of the last fuse replaced), it agrees with Algorithm 1 at eleven inputs I tried, 33/16 among them.
  • A timing run on the grid of 1/256 in [0, 3/2]: 13 ms for all three successor procedures. I did not look at whether they agreed there.

How I get the first four lines

The paper gives d(omega^omega^n) = n + 9 + 4 c_1 + 5 c_2 + ... + (n + 2) c_(n-1), with c_0 = 2 and c_n = 1 + (n + 1) c_(n-1), where 2^-d is the gap after a tame fusible number. By its Lemma 4.1 the tame number with ordinal omega^omega^n is 3 - 2^-(n-1), by my reckoning (n = 1 gives 2, where the gap is 2^-10, as the paper says; n = 2 gives 5/2, seen above). So c = 2, 5, 16, 65, 326, 1957 and d = 10, 31, 112, 503, 2786, 18443 for n = 1 to 6.
