1. Erickson, Nivasch and Xu, 2022: the paper everything here follows

J. Erickson, G. Nivasch and J. Xu, "Fusible numbers and Peano Arithmetic", Logical Methods in Computer Science 18(3), 2022, pp. 6:1–6:26; arXiv 2003.14342 (version 9). I fetched the arXiv PDF and the journal's own PDF; the two files are the same, byte for byte (cmp). Read through, before the sheet was sealed.

The riddle and the definition:

"A popular mathematical riddle goes as follows: We have available two fuses, each of which will burn for one hour when lit. How can we use the two fuses to measure 45 minutes? The fuses do not burn at a uniform rate, so we cannot predict how much of a fuse will burn after any period of time smaller than an hour."

"The solution is to light both ends of one fuse simultaneously, and at the same time light one end of the second fuse. The first fuse will burn after 30 minutes. At that moment we light the second end of the second fuse. The second fuse will burn after an additional 15 minutes."

"If |y − x| < 1, then the number x ∼ y (read “x fuse y”) represents the time at which a fuse burns, if its endpoints are lit at times x and y, respectively. Then F is defined recursively by 0 ∈ F, and x ∼ y ∈ F whenever x, y ∈ F with |y − x| < 1."

"Note that we do not allow lighting a fuse at only one endpoint. This entails no loss of generality, since the same effect can be obtained by lighting a fuse at both endpoints simultaneously, and then lighting a second fuse at both endpoints simultaneously."

"Every z ∈ F is a nonnegative dyadic rational, meaning a nonnegative rational number whose denominator, when in lowest terms, is a power of 2."

What is proved:

"Theorem 1.1. The set F of fusible numbers, when ordered by the usual order “<” on R, is well-ordered, with order type ε0 ."

"(1) PA cannot prove the true statement “For every n ∈ N there exists a smallest fusible number larger than n.”"

"(3) Consider the algorithm “M (x): if x < 0 return −x, else return M (x − M (x − 1))/2.” Then M terminates on real inputs, although PA cannot prove the statement “M terminates on all natural inputs.”"

"Lemma 2.18. Algorithms Succ, WeakSucc, and IsFusible terminate on all inputs and return the correct output."

From that lemma's proof, the range the first procedure's loop has to cover:

"We must have z = x ∼ y for some x, y ∈ F satisfying r − 1 < x ≤ y ≤ r and y > r − 1/2."

The tame numbers, the procedure M, and the number that is not tame:

"The recursive algorithm TameSucc(r) shown in Table 2 returns the smallest tame fusible number larger than r, and M is a concise algorithm that returns the difference TameSucc(r) − r."

"Not all fusible numbers are tame. The smallest “wild” fusible number that we know of is 8449/4096."

The formula my first four sealed lines rest on (section 4.1; the left-hand sides are d(ω^ω^n) and d(ω^ω^ω), which the text conversion flattens, so only the right-hand sides are in quotes.json):

d(ω^ω^n) "= n + 9 + 4c1 + 5c2 + · · · + (n + 2)cn−1 ," ... "where c0 = 2, and cn = 1 + (n + 1)cn−1 for n ≥ 1." ... d(ω^ω^ω) = 1 + d(ω^ω^11) "= 1541023937."

The conjecture, and the sixth procedure as it is printed (page 6:22; I looked at the page image, p22-top.png beside the PDF, and the last line is printed so):

"Recall that by Corollary 2.13 we have s(n) (x) = x + (2 − 21−n )m for every n ∈ N."

"Conjecture 7.1. Every fusible number in Ix,n , n ≥ 1 can be written as s(n) (x) ∼ z for some z ∈ F ∩ [x + 1 − 21−n m, x + 1)."

"We have done extensive computer experiments, and we have not found any counterexample to Conjecture 7.1. Table 3 shows a recursive algorithm for the next meek fusible number, analogous to algorithm TameSucc of Table 2. If Conjecture 7.1 is true, then this algorithm is equivalent to algorithm Succ of Table 1."

"y ←MeekSucc(2r − x − 1 − 1/Denominator(x) + 2dlog2 (x−r+1)e )" / "return x ∼ y"

(the "d ... e" are the ceiling brackets). For the meek numbers at ω^ω^ω the paper says the value "is too large to calculate explicitly."

Sentences in this paper that cut against what I did or thought.

  1. "The third author later on released a manuscript [Xu12] with further results, as well as some corrections to the claims in the slides. The present paper builds upon these two unpublished works, also making some additional minor corrections."

I read this sentence before I sealed, and did not fetch the manuscript until after. It holds two of my sealed lines in print (see source 2).

  1. "In algorithm Succ, the first candidate x ∼ y is not always the best one. Consider for example r = 33/16 ∈ F. Its successor is r + 1/4096 = 8449/4096 = 19/16 ∼ 3969/2048, but the first candidate Succ tries is r + 1/2048 = 9/8 ∼ 2049/1024."

So that the loop of the first procedure matters from 33/16 on is the paper's own remark, not a thing my tests discovered.

  1. "We will prove below that, already for r ∈ N, algorithm Succ(r) has an incredibly large running time in the size of the input. On the other hand, we have not been able to rule out the possibility that algorithms WeakSucc and IsFusible run in polynomial time in the size of the input."

My timings of the first procedure are of my own implementation with a memory the paper does not describe, at a few numbers below 5/2; they say nothing about this question.

  1. "A main open problem is to derive an upper bound for the inverse-largest-gap g(n)−1 . One way to do this would be to prove Conjecture 7.1 and then establish an upper bound for the growth rate of d00 . We are also missing upper bounds for the running time of algorithms IsFusible and WeakSucc."

The conjecture is open. Agreement at grid points is not a proof of anything.

2. Xu, 2012: the manuscript the paper builds on

J. Xu, "Survey on fusible numbers", arXiv 1202.5614 (version 1, 25 February 2012), ten pages. Read through, after the sheet was sealed (fetched at 01:57 UTC; the sheet is stamped 01:48).

"We point out that the recursive formula that appears in Erickson’s presentation Fusible Numbers is incorrect, and pose an alternate conjecture about the structure of fusible numbers."

"Since (1) gives m(31/16) = 2−11 , we have 19/16 ∼ s(31/16) = 33/16 +2−12 ∈ F, so m(33/16) ≤ 2−12 . But (1) gives m(33/16) = 2−11 ." "This counterexample tells us that the process described above doesn’t" "produce all of the fusible numbers." (the sentence runs over a page break)

"Starting from 33/16, the result of Erickson’s formula diverge from the value given by Main Conjecture further and further. The difference soon becomes enormous, as demonstrated in the following table."

"Table 1. Values of − log2 m(3 − 2−n ) according to Erick- son’s formula (1) and Main Conjecture (3)." "(1) 31 112 503 18443 ... 1541023936" / "(3) 51 48804 >2 ↑↑ 9 >2 ↑↑↑ 10"

(the columns are n = 1, 2, 3, 4, ..., 10; I looked at the page image, p5-05.png, and the entry under 4 in row (1) is 18443). Its formula (3), the recursion equivalent to the conjecture, is m(x) = m(x − a − 1/d + 2^⌈log₂ a⌉)/2,

"where a = m(x − 1) and d is the denominator of s(x − 1), a power of 2."

Its other stated values that the tests use: "Example. m(0) = 1/2, m(0.4) = 0.1, m(1/2) = 1/4, m(3/4) = 1/8, m(1) = 1/8, and m(2) = 1/1024."; "In fact, 17/16 is not fusible."; and the list of the last numbers with each gap, "the first few terms of which are 0, 1/2, 1, 5/4, 3/2, 13/8, 7/4, 29/16, 15/8, 2, 129/64, 33/16."

What this manuscript had in print that I sealed as a guess. That the simple recipe first fails at 33/16 (my lines 5 and 6 "guessed" it from the 2022 paper's phrase "that we know of"), and the two values 51 and 48804 that my reading of the sixth procedure reproduces. It also has a procedure of its own for the true gap ("Exploiting well-orderedness, we have the following algorithm that calculates the function m without assuming Main Conjecture, which however is quite inefficient."), in Mathematica, which I did not run.

3. Nivasch and Shiboli: a later paper on procedures like M

G. Nivasch and L. Shiboli, "Ordinals and recursively defined functions on the reals", arXiv 2311.17210 (version 6). Read through (its sections 3 and 4 are proofs about a wider class of procedures and nothing here rests on them). It states M(1) = 1/8, M(3/2) = 1/32, M(2) = 2⁻¹⁰, M(5/2) = 2⁻³¹ and M(3) = 2^−1,541,023,937 (the line is broken up in the text conversion, so it is not in quotes.json), and it prints a graph of M: "Figure 1: The graph of M (x) up to x = 2.1." So a picture of the tame numbers on a line is in print. It also says in one sentence what M gives: "In other words, the set {x + M (x) : x ∈ R} is actually a countable set, which equals F ′ ."

4. A statement of the conjecture in Lean

FormalConjectures/Paper/FusibleNumber.lean in the public repository google-deepmind/formal-conjectures (fetched 24 November 2026; copyright line 2025). It states Conjecture 7.1 with the index moved by one ("This formalization differs from Conjecture 7.1 in the paper in four ways:") and marks it "@[category research open, AMS 5]". Its operand, x + (2 − 1/2ⁿ)m for the interval from y + 1 − m/2ⁿ, is the one my reading of the sixth procedure fuses with.
