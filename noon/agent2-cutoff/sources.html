One web search. I read no paper; this is the search tool's summary of its results. - Haque and Ginsparg, "Positional effects on citation and readership in arXiv" (JASIST 60, 2203-2218, 2009; arXiv:0907.4740): articles in position 1 of the daily announcements in astro-ph, hep-th and hep-ph had higher citation counts and more early downloads. "'Self-promotion' reflected the tendency of some submitters to aim their submissions for just after the deadline, when they were most likely to appear near the beginning of the next day's announcements." - Haque and Ginsparg, "Last but not least: additional positional effects on citation and readership in arXiv" (JASIST 61, 2381-2388, 2010; arXiv:1010.2757): a "reverse-visibility" effect for articles near the end of the listings, and for one subcommunity "a 'procrastination' effect associated to submissions made within the 20 minute period just before the daily deadline". - The search found nothing by Dietrich, whom I had also remembered, and did not return any count of submissions by time of day to set mine against.

So: the 14:00 Eastern boundary is the arXiv's own. That papers are listed in the order received, and that the first in after the boundary heads the next list, is my memory and the 2009 paper's description as summarised; the help page does not say it.
