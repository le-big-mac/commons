# What I looked up, and when

Nothing before the sheet was sealed and scored, apart from one test query of the API (five records) made after sealing to
see what it returns. After scoring, at about 17:16 UTC on 22 November 2026:

**The arXiv's own help page** (`https://info.arxiv.org/help/availability.html`), read through a tool that fetches a page
and has a small model answer questions about it. It reported the page's table of "Submissions received between (all
times Eastern US)" with 14:00 as the boundary on each weekday, for example "Monday 14:00 – Tuesday 14:00 | Tuesday 20:00",
"Thursday 14:00 – Friday 14:00 | Sunday 20:00" and "Friday 14:00 – Monday 14:00 | Monday 20:00"; and that the page does
not state the order in which papers are listed.

**One web search.** I read no paper; this is the search tool's summary of its results.
- Haque and Ginsparg, "Positional effects on citation and readership in arXiv" (JASIST 60, 2203-2218, 2009; arXiv:0907.4740):
  articles in position 1 of the daily announcements in astro-ph, hep-th and hep-ph had higher citation counts and more
  early downloads. "'Self-promotion' reflected the tendency of some submitters to aim their submissions for just after
  the deadline, when they were most likely to appear near the beginning of the next day's announcements."
- Haque and Ginsparg, "Last but not least: additional positional effects on citation and readership in arXiv" (JASIST 61,
  2381-2388, 2010; arXiv:1010.2757): a "reverse-visibility" effect for articles near the end of the listings, and for one
  subcommunity "a 'procrastination' effect associated to submissions made within the 20 minute period just before the
  daily deadline".
- The search found nothing by Dietrich, whom I had also remembered, and did not return any count of submissions by time
  of day to set mine against.

So: the 14:00 Eastern boundary is the arXiv's own. That papers are listed in the order received, and that the first in
after the boundary heads the next list, is my memory and the 2009 paper's description as summarised; the help page does
not say it.
