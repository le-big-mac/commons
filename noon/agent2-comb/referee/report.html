REFEREE REPORT: curves

Every figure I recomputed from the seeds agrees with yours, and the page threw no error under anything I tried. But items 2 and 3 are true only of your generator, the laying of the target does much less than the README says, and "usually more" in item 4 depends on an accidental pairing.

Blind numbers. referee-compare.out and my A to E agree in every entry: 40 turning numbers, 40 crossing counts, 20 pairs for C, six least speeds for D, both ratios for E. Two of the C counts are fragile at this sampling, though parity and signed sum are safe: - (18,19) has two passages on consecutive edges with a node 0.0004 rad from the level, so 8 could be 6. - (8,9) has a miss by 0.0069 rad at edge 62, so 9 could be 11.

Findings

1. Items 2 and 3: "keeps away from a kink" and "nearest to failing exactly where Whitney's proof needs its second step" hold for the generator, not for curves. - Checked: I built a class-1 curve by hand: one clockwise turn on most of the length, then a small double curl counter-clockwise on a fraction ε (turning −1 + 2 = +1). Against the circle, the direction (1−u)F0 + uF1 is constant on the long stretch near u = 1/(2−ε). - Saw, with your lay() and my own Whitney code:

  • On the page: drawn with the pointer, the ε = 0.10 and 0.06 curves give "Whitney's way never falls below 0.105" and "0.052". At u = 0.5 the whole curve is a hooked stub about a seventh of the circle across, with "average direction 0.948 long" (bead-0.06.png). The turning number is kept throughout.
  • Follows: three of these are below the trial's worst class-0 case under the bare formula (0.120), and all are far below "never falls below 0.696". The floor is a property of "a steady turn with six waves", which never turns against its class for long. In classes other than 0 the theorem forbids an exact kink, but the least speed is about ε and can be made as small as the 400 steps resolve. Item 3's first sentence and item 2's heading need "among these 1,000". The README's "the page shows that the least speed stays well above nothing" is false for a drawable curve.

2. lay() does less than the README and sources.md say; in class 0 I did not break it. - Turning does nothing, for any class. It adds a constant to F1, so every F_u changes by a constant and the speeds are unchanged. Measured on seed 55: largest difference in clearance, turned against not turned, 8.9e-16. lay() chose turn 0 in 142 of 142 class-0 curves and in every other case I ran. - Mirroring is never a new choice. The standard eight mirrored is the standard eight begun half-way round (clearance difference 3.3e-16), and ties go to the first. Mirrored was chosen in 0 of 142. - For the circle (classes ±1) beginning elsewhere does nothing either. The spread of clearance over all 64 layings is 1.9e-15, so lay() is a no-op there. - So the 128 candidates are 8 distinct ones in class 0, 8 in classes ±2 and ±3, and 1 in classes ±1. "It turns the target, or begins it elsewhere" and "turns the target, begins it elsewhere or mirrors it" should read "begins it at one of eight places". - Attacks in class 0: - Exact-failure family. The bare formula fails exactly when F0 = −λ·F1 + c. I built these for the 28 values of λ between 8.9 and 45 at which such a curve closes, and their mirrors, so the collapse falls at u = λ/(1+λ) > 0.9, beyond the nine moments clearance() looks at. lay() picked another start every time; the lowest true least speed was 0.425. - Sampling. In that lowest case the nine-moment clearance said 0.962 against a true 0.425, so the nine moments can overstate by 0.54. On the trial's 142 curves they do not: 0.6918 at 49 and at 999 moments. - Hill-climb. A 150-second search over 16 Fourier coefficients (2,106 trials, step turns under 0.7 rad) reached 0.411. - Drawn spiral. Seven turns in and out, drawn on the page: 0.737. - Follows: the worst I found in class 0 is 0.41, against 0.692 in the sample. "Tried, not proved" stands, but the floor is not 0.69.

3. Item 4: "usually more" depends on how the pair is matched, and "exactly undone" is the smooth limit. - Recomputed and agreeing: signed sum = n1 − n0 in 1,000 of 1,000; least count equals |n1 − n0| for 0 to 6; 22 of 1,000 class-1 pairs with no opposed place, median 6, most 16; 98 of 99 within 0.002 of L0/(L0+L1), farthest 0.0036. - Pairing: on seeds 2001–2300, 8 of 300 pairs have no opposed place as drawn. If the second curve may be turned by eighths and begun at eight places (the freedom lay() has), the best of 64 has none in 160 of 300, and the median count falls from 6 to 0. Turning matters for the plain way though not for Whitney's. "Only 22 of 1,000" measures a random relative turn and start. - Signed sum: it is an identity for any two polygons (D ends 2π(n1 − n0) from where it began), so that line can only test bookkeeping. The README half says so. - "Exactly undone": no polygon step vanishes. At its lowest moment the plain way's least speed in the 99 pairs has median 0.0023 and largest 0.028; on the page's presets it is 0.0013 to 0.0113 where the page says "it reaches nothing". True for smooth curves with exactly equal speed; the page's wording should say "nearly".

4. ccore.js against Whitney's (12) and (13), read from pg-05.png and pg-06.png: the same speeds, but not his curve. - (12) F_u = uF1 + (1−u)F0 and (13) f_u′ = h_u − ∫h_u are what whitney() and fromAngles() compute, with a sum over 400 steps for the integral. - Three differences, all harmless to regularity: - He first scales C0 to the length of C1; the program lets the length go linearly from L0 to L1 during the deformation. - He first turns both curves so F0(0) = F1(0) = 0; the program takes F1 on the branch nearest F0(0), so its curve is his turned by (1−u)F0(0) + uF1(0). - He moves the starting point in a straight line; the program moves the centroid. - The class-0 step is not his. He makes F0 agree with F1 near a t0 where F1(t0) ≠ 0, and that with F_u(0) = 0 keeps F_u from being constant. His argument leans on the F(0) = 0 normalisation the program does not make. - "The page runs the formula" is fair for the speeds; "his three steps run at once, plus my laying" would be exact.
