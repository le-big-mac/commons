A gasket with numbers missing

Three circles that touch one another leave a gap, and exactly one circle fits the gap touching all three. Fill every gap for ever and you have an Apollonian gasket. Begin with four circles whose curvatures (one over the radius) are whole numbers, and every curvature in it is a whole number. Which whole numbers? For twenty years the conjecture was: all of them, past some point, that pass a test of remainders on division by 24. In 2023 four mathematicians showed that this is false: in many gaskets a whole family, such as the squares, never appears.

The theorem and the table of families are from S. Haag, C. Kertzer, J. Rickards and K. E. Stange, The local-global conjecture for Apollonian circle packings is false (Annals of Mathematics 200, 2024). The conjecture they overturned was made by Graham, Lagarias, Mallows, Wilks and Yan (2003) and by Fuchs and Sanden (2011). The six lists in the second picture were computed here, and are the same, number for number, as the lists the paper's authors have published. Nothing on this page is new mathematics.

What is certain and what is not: that a family never appears is the paper's theorem. That the blue numbers give out is not known; the paper conjectures it, and the lists only show that nothing else is missing up to a hundred million (the authors looked at least a hundred times further). In the third panel, the kind of gasket and its families are worked out from the four numbers by the paper's rules, and the list of missing numbers by going through the circles.
