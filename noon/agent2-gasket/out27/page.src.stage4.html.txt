    <h2>What is going on</h2>
    <div class="about" id="about">
      <p>Three circles that touch one another leave a gap, and exactly one circle fits the gap touching all three. Fill every gap, then every new gap, and so on for ever: that is an Apollonian gasket. A circle's number here is one over its radius, so small circles have big numbers; mathematicians call it the curvature. If the first four numbers are whole, every number in the gasket is.</p>
      <p>A gasket is named here by its four largest circles, as <b>(&minus;4, 5, 20, 21)</b>. The number with the minus is the circle round all the rest.</p>
      <p>Not every whole number can turn up. Divide a number by 24 and look at what is left over. In <b>(&minus;4, 5, 20, 21)</b> and in <b>(&minus;3, 5, 8, 8)</b>, the two gaskets of the picture's button, only numbers that leave 0, 5, 8, 12, 20 or 21 are ever a circle's number. 36 leaves 12, so it passes that test. In <b>(&minus;4, 5, 20, 21)</b> it is there; in <b>(&minus;3, 5, 8, 8)</b> it never comes.</p>
      <p>For twenty years the guess was that every number that passes the test turns up, once the numbers are large enough. In 2023 four mathematicians (Haag, Kertzer, Rickards and Stange; their paper is named at the foot of the page) showed that the guess is false: in many gaskets a whole family of numbers that pass the test never appears. Below, such a family is called barred. In <b>(&minus;3, 5, 8, 8)</b> it is the squares, and the numbers six times a square; in <b>(&minus;4, 5, 20, 21)</b> it is the numbers twice a square and three times a square.</p>

  <p class="note">The theorem and the table of families are from S. Haag, C. Kertzer, J. Rickards and K. E. Stange, <i>The local-global conjecture for Apollonian circle packings is false</i> (Annals of Mathematics 200, 2024). The conjecture they overturned was made by Graham, Lagarias, Mallows, Wilks and Yan (2003) and by Fuchs and Sanden (2011). The six lists in the second picture were computed here, and are the same, number for number, as the lists the paper's authors have published. Nothing on this page is new mathematics.</p>
  <p class="note">What is certain and what is not: that a family never appears is the paper's theorem. That the blue numbers come to an end is not known; the paper conjectures it, and the lists only show that nothing else is missing up to a hundred million (the authors looked at least a hundred times further). In the third panel, the kind of gasket and its families are worked out from the four numbers by the paper's rules, and the list of missing numbers by going through the circles.</p>
