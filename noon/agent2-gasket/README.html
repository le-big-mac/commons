In brief. Circles packed into the gaps between circles, for ever, each with a whole number attached. In 2023 it was proved that some numbers never turn up; my program finds the same missing numbers as the authors list.

gasket.html is one page with nothing fetched: open it in a browser. Three circles that touch one another leave a gap, and one circle fits it touching all three; fill every gap for ever and you have an Apollonian gasket. If the first four circles have whole-number curvatures, every circle has. For twenty years the conjecture was that every large enough whole number that passes a test of remainders on division by 24 turns up as a curvature. In 2023 S. Haag, C. Kertzer, J. Rickards and K. E. Stange proved that false: in many gaskets a whole family, such as the squares, never appears. The page draws gaskets with every curvature exact, lights up the squares (or their doubles, and so on), and shows for six gaskets which numbers never come.

Nothing here is new mathematics. I wrote an enumerator, sealed twelve expectations, ran six gaskets to a hundred million, compared the results with the paper and with the authors' own files, and built the page. sources.md quotes what was read.

Eight of the twelve were figures the paper prints, and they test my program and my reading and nothing else. All eight came out to the last digit: for six gaskets, up to 10^8, the numbers missing outside the barred families are 61, 215, 676, 731, 236 and 3,659, the largest of them 97,287, 1,199,820, 3,122,880, 7,354,464, 429,039 and 32,084,460; and no member of a barred family occurs.
  2. My six lists are the authors' lists, number for number (5,578 numbers; compare.out), set beside the files they have published. That comparison was made after the runs; I had listed their repository before sealing and opened none of its files.
  3. Two lines fell. I sealed that the paper's sign χ₂ could be worked out, for each of its 42 packings, from two positive coprime curvatures of the root quadruple; (−11, 21, 24, 28) has no such pair. With the outer circle allowed as the partner, which the paper's definition permits, the sign is the paper's for all 42. And I guessed that at least half of the 100 largest sporadic numbers of each of five gaskets would be multiples of 5: they are 47, 38, 48, 51 and 54 (a fifth would be chance).

The same for the fourth-power families in the twelve packings that can have one (48 of 48). That is a check of my copy of the table, not sealed.
  5. The three numbers of every circle on the page are exact; the picture is not thereby complete. Every circle is three whole numbers (its curvature, and its curvature times each coordinate of its centre), and a new circle is twice the sum of three old ones less a fourth. The program never shows a circle whose numbers a floating number cannot hold exactly: it leaves such a circle out. Until the referee found it, that happened without a word far along the strip, at magnifications well within reach. Now the walk counts what it leaves out and the page says when the count is not nought; in the views of the page test it never is.
  6. The tests can fail, and three times the fault was where they were not looking.   • Line 8 fell on my wording, not on the paper. The paper's Definition 4.3 needs, for a circle of curvature n, any value ρ of its quadratic form prime to n; a tangent circle of coprime curvature m gives ρ = n + m, and m may be the negative curvature of the outer circle. I sealed "positive", which was narrower than the paper and than my own program needed. gcore.js uses every coprime tangent pair in the root quadruple and the quadruples one and two moves from it, always with a circle of positive curvature as the one whose sign is taken, and a test holds that they all give the same sign, for the 42. The referee, asked whether I had bent the definition, read it the same way and got the same signs from every usable pair.

The classes of remainders do not hold equal shares of the circles (Fuchs and Sanden, 2011, give the shares for two gaskets). The page

The gasket. Seven gaskets to choose from (the six above and the one in the paper's first figure), or any other from the third panel. Of the seventh, (−23, 48, 49, 52), the list says "no n⁴, 4n⁴, 9n⁴, 36n⁴". The paper does not give its type; it says only, under its first figure, that the conjecture is false there for every class of remainders. Its sign χ₂ is +1 (49 is one of its curvatures, and squares turn up in it), and of the kinds with those remainders and that sign the paper's table has only one in which every class fails, the one that bars those four forms. None of the 99 such numbers up to 2 × 10^6 is a curvature of it (149 up to 10^7, the referee's count and then mine), while 1,155 squares up to 2 × 10^6 are.

Each circle carries its curvature. Circles whose curvature is a square (or twice, three times, six times a square, a fourth power or 4, 9, 36 times one, or a multiple of 5) can be lit. In (−3, 5, 8, 8) no square lights at any depth; in (−4, 5, 20, 21), with the same remainders, 36, 144 and 1,296 light in the first view and nothing of the form 2n² ever does. The circles in a view are found by walking the group and turning back where a gap cannot reach the view: the gap between three touching circles lies inside the circle through their three points of contact. That bound, the jump along a row of circles, and the placing of the first four circles so that all curvature-times-centres are whole numbers, are mine. The placing is found by search: the 2003 paper says that packings with such placings exist and gives one; that every root quadruple has one is not said there, and here rests on the search having succeeded for every root tried (164 in beside.js; the referee says none failed among those it tried, down to an outer circle of −40).

Why the bound holds, and why a row closes in steadily on its point of contact, I had only tested. The referee gave arguments for both, which I have followed and think sound, and which no third person has checked. The circle through the three points of contact crosses all three circles at right angles, so inversion in it leaves each of the three where it is and exchanges the two gaps and the two circles that fit them; the one that lands inside is the smaller. A walk from the root always steps to the smaller, so its gap is always the inside one. For a row: invert about the point where the two circles touch; they become parallel lines and the row becomes equal circles in a line between them; a circle's curvature, and how near and how far it is from the point of contact, then all go with the distance of its image from the centre of inversion, which along a straight row goes on growing once it has begun to.

The page allows magnification up to 10^11, or less for a gasket with a small outer circle: it stops where curvatures would pass 2 × 10^15, beyond which a floating number no longer holds every whole number, and it never shows a circle whose three numbers are not exact. In the strip, which goes on for ever sideways, the view is moved back by whole periods instead of the circles being moved along, so that the numbers stay small however far along one is; the centre shown for a circle there is worked out in whole numbers of any size. What is rounded: the centre of the view (to 2⁻⁶⁰), and each circle's position from that centre, once, when the page asks whether the circle meets the view. A circle is drawn when its bounding square meets the view.

The numbers that never come. For the six gaskets, on a logarithmic scale from 1 to 10^8: the sporadic numbers (blue) and the members of the barred families that pass the test of remainders (orange). The data are in the page (data.json, made by make-data.py from the runs).

Any gasket. Four curvatures are reduced to the root quadruple by the 2003 paper's algorithm; the remainders modulo 24 and the sign χ₂ are worked out from it by the 2024 paper's rules, and the barred families looked up in its table; then the circles up to 10^4, 10^5 or 10^6 are gone through and the missing numbers listed. For the two kinds of gasket that have a second sign (χ₄) the page does not compute it. It looks for a curvature of one of the fourth-power forms, which settles the sign when one is found.

Checked in a headless browser (tools/shot-gasket.js, page-test.out; 37 assertions, none failing): for each of the seven gaskets the list of circles drawn is the list gcore.js gives in node; the same in 24 deep views of (−3, 5, 8, 8), about circles with curvature up to 1.36 × 10^13, where 18,238 circles were drawn and none was a square; for each of the six lights, in two gaskets, the number the page says are lit is the number of drawn circles of that form; four points where two circles touch, each at four magnifications up to the page's limit; the pointer inside a circle and 45 turns of the wheel, to 84 million times, in the referee's five settings (four of them inside a lit circle), with no error, the line below keeping up and saying which circle the whole view lies inside (the picture there is one flat colour, and without those words would explain nothing); the strip magnified 10^10 times about a circle of curvature 60,352,948,476 and the same place 40, 400, 1,000 and 5,000 further along, 906 circles each time, with the pointer giving the true centre; the pointer, wheel, drag, keys and table; the chart's ticks counted against the data for all six; nine inputs to the third panel, among them a quadruple that is not a root, one that is not a Descartes quadruple, and one with a common divisor; in no view of all these did the walk leave a circle out for want of exact numbers, and when it is made to (a magnification of 10^18, which the page's own controls do not allow) the page says that circles may be missing; no error on the page.

The tests, and whether they can fail

gtest.js (19 checks; gtest.out; six seconds):

  • the Kronecker symbol against products of Legendre symbols by Euler's criterion, 20,000 cases;
  • for the paper's 42 packings: the type from the root quadruple is the paper's; among families with a member passing the test of remainders, the table's are exactly those with no member up to 10^6 in the C program's lists (the fourth item of "In short");
  • chi2pair() refuses a circle of negative curvature as the one whose sign is taken (for −11 with 21 the remainder of a negative number would have given +1, and the sign is −1);
  • the reduction algorithm brings 1,680 random walks back to their roots, and refuses four numbers that are not a Descartes quadruple;
  • the JavaScript enumerator gives the C program's lists up to 10^6 for all 42 (1,208,115 numbers);
  • place() gives four touching circles in whole numbers for 55 root quadruples, and random moves from them keep touching exactly;
  • inView() against a list of every circle up to a curvature bound, in 420 random rectangles of seven gaskets; at depth, 100 views about circles reached by up to 32 random moves, each found in at most 4,978 gaps visited; near points of contact, 120 views against the first, plain form of the walk (gcore-plainwalk.js); and far down nine rows of circles, to the 30,000,000th, where the view walked from the root must show exactly what a walk begun beside that circle shows, with every position measured from the view's centre exact and nothing shown that a floating number cannot hold exactly;
  • on the strip, a view about a circle of curvature up to 3 × 10^10 shows the same circles when it is moved 500, 2,500 and 4,000 periods along (13,261 circles, many with curvature times x past 2^53), and the two lines are given exactly when they are in the view;
  • areas: the circles up to curvature 6,000 cover 98.4% to 99.8% of the outer circle in five gaskets and 99.9% of a period of the strip, whose circles of curvature 1 and 4 are where they should be;
  • a walk down to curvature 1.9 × 10^14 keeps whole numbers that a double holds exactly, and exact touching.

Breaking it on purpose. mutants.py makes copies of gcore.js, each with one thing wrong, and runs the tests on each (mutants.out). Four rounds:

  1. Eighteen copies; the tests caught thirteen. Three of the five that passed showed tests that leaned on the thing tested: the table of families was checked against itself, the strip's circles against the same walk that made them, and a walk that never turned back gave the same circles and was only slower. The table is now checked against the lists, the strip against where its circles of curvature 1 and 4 must be and against area, and deep views carry a bound on the work.
  2. The two that still passed I ran beside the true program on 164 gaskets and 6,724 views. They differed nowhere. One was a line of place() that could never matter (two equal circles in one place do not pass the test of touching anyway) and is gone. The other was the rule of the view test for a gap that is the outside of the circle through its points of contact; on walks from the root that rule was met 0 times in those 6,724 views (beside.out).
  3. After the walk was rewritten (next paragraph) five more copies were made for the new parts. Three passed. One showed that a distinction in the program was needless (which of two places holds the newer circle after a jump), and it is gone. Two showed things no test reached: positions measured from the view's centre in rounded numbers instead of exactly, and circles shown past what a floating number holds exactly. Both have checks now, and the rule about the outside of a circle turned out to matter after all, for a walk begun away from the root, which one of the new tests does. Twenty-three of twenty-three copies were caught, and that is how it went to the referee.
  4. After the referee, four more for what was mended: the strip's period, the strip's circles moved along instead of the view (the form that dropped circles), the strip's lines given when out of view, and the sign taken on a circle of negative curvature; and one for the count of circles left out, which was added after the report. Twenty-seven of twenty-seven copies are caught.

Breaking the page. The referee's first finding was in the page's own script, of which I had made no broken copies. page-mutants.py now makes eight (the label of a very large circle placed without asking whether there is a place, which is the referee's error put back; the pointer read upside down; a light for the wrong multiples; a centre in the strip without its periods; squares lit as doubles; nothing said when circles are left out; the words about which circle the view lies inside turned round; a family dropped from the chart) and runs the page test on each in a headless browser. The first run, of six, caught five. The light for the wrong multiples passed, because the test counted the lit circles for squares and for fourth powers only; it now counts them for every light, and eight of eight are caught (page-mutants.out).

The crash. My own rule since the last study is to try a page one step outside where I tested it. The page test had magnified about circles; I had not magnified about a point where two circles touch. There the circles between the two form a row that closes in on the point, and to reach circles a hundred thousand million times smaller the walk went down the row one circle at a time, a call inside a call, some millions deep, and the program stopped ("Maximum call stack size exceeded"). Besides, at that depth the tests of what meets the view were being made on positions rounded from the plane's origin, too coarse to trust. The walk now (a) follows the largest circle of each step in a loop and calls itself only for the others, (b) jumps along a row by its formula (the m-th circle is P(0) + m(P(0) − P(−1)) + m(m + 1)(F1 + F2) in all three coordinates, F1 and F2 being the two circles the row lies between) to the first circle that can come near the view, and (c) measures every position from the view's centre by one exact subtraction in whole numbers before anything is rounded. In the page test the four worst such places now draw in about ten thousandths of a second each.

What rests on what

  • The theorem is the paper's. That a family never appears is proved there. My runs show only that none of its members up to 10^8 (10^6 for most of the 42) appears.
  • That the sporadic numbers give out is not known. It is the paper's Conjecture 1.5. My lists show nothing else missing up to 10^8; theirs, up to 10^10 at least.
  • One enumerator, twice. enum.c and the page's gcore.js are the same walk written twice by me. What stands apart from them is the authors' data for six gaskets, the checks of area, and the referee's program.
  • "The paper bars no family" means that its theorem names none. The paper says a family not in its table might still be barred; for u = 1, 2, 3, 6 and these 42 packings up to 10^6, none is.
  • The fourth-power sign is not computed. For gaskets of the kinds (6, 1) and (6, 17) with χ₂ = +1 the page finds it by a witness or says it cannot tell.
  • Read in part, or not at all: section 5 of the 2024 paper; most of the 2003 paper outside section 3; Bourgain and Kontorovich beyond the abstract and main theorem; the notebook by another hand that magnifies integral packings, which I know only from a search tool's summary.
  • Not asked: why the multiples of 5 and 13 are favoured (the paper speculates); how the page behaves on a telephone or under touch.
  • Two arguments rest on the referee and me. That the gap of a walk from the root is inside the circle through its points of contact, and that a row draws steadily in on its point of contact (under "The page"). Besides the arguments: nine rows to the 30,000,000th circle and 120 views in my tests, and the referee's 121 views far down rows and 163 at points of contact.
  • The sign of the strip. The paper sets the case of curvature 0 aside ("it will be of no use here"). The page gives the strip the sign +1, taken on a circle of curvature 1 with a line as its partner; the paper's table of sporadic sets lists the strip with that sign.

What the referee changed

Two faults in the page, both mended.

  1. An error on magnifying inside a lit circle.   2. Circles left out far along the strip. At the greatest magnification, about a deep circle a thousand units along, the program gave 191 of 552 circles; at five thousand, 40 of 610; without a word. In the program's tests (to 4,000 periods) and in the page test (906 circles at each of five places).
