6. The paper

I made one request, to export.arxiv.org/e-print/2307.02749, and read the whole TeX source and bibliography. The source files are dated 5 Sep 2024. I did not read the Annals PDF or open the two figure PDFs. The source's "Authors' note" says: "The published version of this article differs slightly from this version. A few proofs, minor results, tables, and figures were either shortened or removed for publication."

(a) Type and main theorem.

The type is defined in three steps: - "Let R(A) be the set of residues modulo 24 of the curvatures in A. Then R(A) is one of six possible sets, labelled by a type as follows": (6,1): 0,1,4,9,12,16; (6,5): 0,5,8,12,20,21; (6,13): 0,4,12,13,16,21; (6,17): 0,8,9,12,17,20; (8,7): 3,6,7,10,15,18,19,22; (8,11): 2,3,6,11,14,15,18,23. - "The type (x, k) denotes that R(A) has cardinality x, and the smallest positive residue in R(A) coprime to 24 is k." - "The (extended) type of A is either the triple (x, k, χ2) or (x, k, χ2, χ4), where A has type (x, k) and corresponding values of χ2 (and χ4, if relevant)."

On the two invariants: "in the simplest cases, χ2(A) = (b/a) for any coprime pair of curvatures a and b of tangent circles in A". The full definition has ρ = a+b for a coprime tangent pair and takes (ρ/n) if n ≡ 0,1 mod 4, (−ρ/(n/2)) if n ≡ 2 mod 4, (2ρ/n) if n ≡ 3 mod 4. χ4 exists only for types (6,1) and (6,17), takes values in {1, i, −1, −i}, and satisfies χ4² = χ2.

The main theorem is the fourth numbered statement of Section 2 by my count of the source; I did not compile it. It reads: "The type of A implies the existence of certain quadratic and quartic obstructions, as described by the following table (which also includes the list of residues modulo 24 where Conjecture [1.1] is false, and those where it is still open)".

What surprised me

  1. The list in question 4 does not end below 10^6. Seven more exceptions lie between 1,067,436 and 3,122,880. "Largest 990864" is true only with "up to 10^6" attached.
  2. Mirror symmetry is easy to miscount. In (-4,5,20,21), (-1,2,2,3), (-3,4,12,13) and (-2,3,6,7), swapping the largest root circle gives the same curvature but a different circle. A program that drops "equal" swaps loses half the gasket. That is why 36, 144 and 1296 each have two circles.
  3. A form that is not obstructed is not wholly present. (-4,5,20,21) lacks seven admissible squares and four 6n². (-3,5,8,8) lacks 72, 7200, 300 and 432. Even the strip lacks 48, 300 and 1176.
  4. In (-23,48,49,52) the fourth-power family is a small part of what is missing at this scale. Up to 10^6, 59,251 of 250,001 admissible numbers (24%) are not curvatures, against 83 family members. At 10^7 it is 196,080 (7.8%), at 10^8 352,062 (1.4%). Most squares are curvatures there (768 of 1000), and 49 is in the root.
  5. The paper's appendix writes "18n²" for type (8,7,−1) where the theorem writes 2n². They are the same set: 2n² is admissible there only for n an odd multiple of 3, which gives my 118 values up to 10^6.
  6. The sporadic counts and maxima are in the arXiv version only, by the authors' own labels. They should not be cited to the Annals article without checking it.
  7. Two thresholds in question 5 are fragile: 788.5 and 1685.7 each sit just above a curvature that several circles share.
