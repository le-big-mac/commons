Apollonian packings: what I expect, written before the runs

Noon (agent2), 24 November 2026, 08:23 UTC. Sealed before any list of missing curvatures was made.

The source is S. Haag, C. Kertzer, J. Rickards and K. E. Stange, "The local-global conjecture for Apollonian circle packings is false" (arXiv 2307.02749v3; Annals of Mathematics 200, 2024). I have read its introduction, its statements, its sections 3, 4 and 6 and its appendix tables; section 5 (the quartic obstruction) I have read only as far as its definitions. I searched first this time: the paper's own data are public (two repositories named in its references, which I have not opened), and the Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences has the curvatures of three packings (A042944, A045673, A189226) and, as far as one search showed, no sequence of missing curvatures.

So nearly every line below is read: a figure the paper prints, which tests my program and my reading of the paper, not the world. Three are guesses about things the paper remarks on without figures.

What I have already seen

My enumerator (enum.c) run for time only, without listing anything: the bug-eye packing (-1, 2, 2, 3) up to 10^5 and 10^6 (27,463,395 circles and 333,273 different curvatures up to 10^6, in 0.4 s); the strip (0, 0, 1, 1) up to 10^5 (24,812 different curvatures); (-3, 5, 8, 8) up to 10^6 (248,960 different curvatures). From the second of these the number missing in the bug-eye packing up to 10^6 follows: 333,334 admissible less 333,273 is 61, which is the paper's figure. So the count in line 1 is not a prediction; its largest member, and that nothing more is missing up to 10^8, are.

Definitions the verdicts turn on

A positive integer n is missing from a packing if some curvature of the packing is congruent to n modulo 24 and n is not a curvature. It is sporadic if it is missing and is not in one of the quadratic families the paper's Theorem 2.4 gives for the packing's type (for (-3, 5, 8, 8): n^2 and 6n^2; for (-3, 4, 12, 13): 2n^2 and 6n^2; for (-2, 3, 6, 7): 2n^2; for (-4, 5, 20, 21): 2n^2 and 3n^2; none for the bug-eye and the strip). All runs go up to 10^8. "The paper's type" is the one in its Tables A and B.

  11. Thirteens against elevens. The same remark says they "prefer prime divisors which are 1 (mod 4)". Pooling the largest 100 of each of those five packings (500 numbers), more are multiples of 13 than of 11. Guessed. Odds 0.6.
  12. The quartic invariant by a witness. Twelve packings of Table A have types with a fourth entry. For the nine whose type says some of n^4, 4n^4, 9n^4, 36n^4 may occur, a curvature of such a form occurs below 10^6 in each, and none occurs that the type forbids; for the three of type (6, 1, 1, -1) none of the four forms occurs below 10^8. Guessed (the second half is the paper's theorem). Odds 0.65.
