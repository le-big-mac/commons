## 1. Haag, Kertzer, Rickards and Stange: the paper this follows

S. Haag, C. Kertzer, J. Rickards and K. E. Stange, "The local-global conjecture for Apollonian circle packings is
false", arXiv 2307.02749 (version 3, the longer one); *Annals of Mathematics* 200 (2024), 749–770. Fetched and read
**before** the sheet was sealed: the introduction, the statements (section 2), sections 3 and 4, section 6 and the
appendix tables. Section 5 (the quartic obstruction) I read only as far as its definitions, and the page does not
compute its sign.

> "In a primitive integral Apollonian circle packing, the curvatures that appear must fall into one of six or eight
> residue classes modulo 24. The local-global conjecture states that every sufficiently large integer in one of
> these residue classes will appear as a curvature in the packing. We prove that this conjecture is false" "for
> many packings, by proving that certain quadratic and quartic families are missed."

> "Call a positive curvature c missing in A if curvatures equivalent to c (mod 24) appear in A but c does not."

> "Conjecture 1.1 ([GLM+ 03, FS11]). The number of missing curvatures in A is finite."

> "Definition 1.4. Define the sporadic set SA to be the set of missing curvatures that do not lie in one of the
> quadratic or quartic obstruction classes described in Theorem 2.4."  "Conjecture 1.5. The set SA is finite."

> "Theorem 1.6. The Apollonian circle packing A generated by the quadruple (−3, 5, 8, 8) has no square" curvatures.
> (the last word is on the next line of the conversion)

> "We say that the set Sd,u forms a reciprocity obstruction to A if infinitely many elements of Sd,u are admissible
> in A modulo 24, and yet no element of Sd,u appears as a curvature in A."

The table of Theorem 2.4 (which family is barred from which type) is in `gcore.js` as `TABLE`, typed in by me from
the page; the text conversion breaks its raised figures on to separate lines. A test sets it against the lists
(`gtest.out`: 156 of 156 and 48 of 48). The sign χ₂ is the paper's Definition 4.3, which the conversion loses; I
read it from the page image (`pg-09.png` beside the PDF): for a circle of curvature n, with ρ a value of its
quadratic form prime to n, it is (ρ/n) for n ≡ 0, 1 (mod 4), (−ρ/(n/2)) for n ≡ 2, (2ρ/n) for n ≡ 3, in Kronecker
symbols; and from the proof of Proposition 4.4,

> "we can take ρ(fC1 ) = a + b = ρ(fC2 ) (noting that a + b > 0)."

> "Proposition 3.4. Let C be a circle of curvature n in A. The multiset of curvatures of circles tangent to C in A
> is {fC (x, y) − n : gcd(x, y) = 1}."

What it computed, and how far:

> "In particular, for each of the 14 types listed in Theorem 2.4, we took three small root quadruples, and computed
> the sporadic curvatures up to a bound N in the range [1010 , 1012 ]. The bound N was chosen so that the ratio of N
> to the largest sporadic curvature found exceeds 10, providing good evidence towards Conjecture 1.5."

> "Remark 6.1. There are only five pairs of residue class and packing for which it appears that every single
> positive residue in that class appears."  "(0, 0, 1, 1) and 1 (mod 24), which only misses the curvature 241 up to
> 1010 ;"  "(−1, 2, 2, 3) and 2 (mod 24), which only misses the curvature 13154 up to 1010 ."

> "Remark 6.2. An intrepid observer of the raw sporadic sets may remark that, toward the tail end, the sporadic
> curvatures are disproportionately multiples of 5. In fact, they generally prefer prime divisors which are 1 (mod
> 4)."  "We speculate that this is another local phenomenon: a result of certain symmetries of the distribution of
> curvatures in the orbit of quadruples modulo p ≡ 1 (mod 4)"

The caption of its first figure ("Figure 1. Circles of curvature ≤ 15000 in the Apollonian circle packing
corresponding to" (−23, 48, 49, 52)) goes on to say that the conjecture is false for every residue class in that
packing; the page uses that packing as its seventh.

**Sentences in this paper that cut against what I did or say.**

1. Their bound and mine. They went to between 10^10 and 10^12, "so that the ratio of N to the largest sporadic
   curvature found exceeds 10". I went to 10^8. For (−4, 5, 20, 21) my ratio is 3.1; for (−3, 4, 12, 13) it is 13.6.
   My lists agree with theirs because theirs end below 10^8, not because I looked as far.
2. > "Note that not listing a value of u as a quadratic obstruction in the table does not imply that it cannot be
   > an obstruction, only that this proof method does not rule it out. The completeness of these lists is
   > discussed in Section 6."
   So "the paper bars no family from this gasket", on my page, means only that its theorem names none.
3. > "Corollary 2.6. The local-global conjecture 1.1 is false for at least one residue class in all primitive
   > Apollonian circle packings that are not of type (6, 1, 1, 1) or (8, 11, 1)."  "The exceptions where the
   > local-global conjecture may yet hold include the strip packing (root quadruple (0, 0, 1, 1)), and the bug-eye
   > packing (root quadruple (−1, 2, 2, 3))."
   The conjecture is not refuted for every gasket; the page's first paragraph says "in many gaskets".
4. > "(see GitHub repositories [Ric23a, Ric23b] and Section 6). The data appears to support Conjecture 1.5."
   Everything I computed was already public, as files.

## 3. Graham, Lagarias, Mallows, Wilks and Yan, 2003

"Apollonian circle packings: number theory", J. Number Theory 100 (arXiv math/0009113). Read: section 3 (root
quadruples, the reduction algorithm, Theorem 3.3) and the conjectures and open questions at the end; the rest
looked through.

> "Definition 3.2. A Descartes quadruple v = (a, b, c, d) with L(v) = a + b + c + d > 0 is a root quadruple if a ≤ 0
> ≤ b ≤ c ≤ d and a + b + c ≥ d."

> "(1) Test in order 1 ≤ i ≤ 4 whether some Si decreases the sum a + b + c + d. It so, apply it to produce a new
> quadruple and continue."

> "Then the set of integer curvatures occurring in P, counted with multiplicity, consists of the four elements of v
> plus the largest elements of each vector M v, where M runs over all elements of the Apollonian group A."

That theorem is my enumerator. The conjecture as first made:

> "Strong Density Conjecture. In any primitive integral Apollonian packing, all sufficiently large integers occur,
> provided they are not excluded by congruence conditions."

And what the page's exactness rests on (stated there without proof, with a reference I did not fetch):

> "(6) There exist strongly integral Apollonian packings, in which the circles all have integer curvatures and
> also the curvature×centers of the circles are Gaussian integers, where the circle centers are coordinatized as
> complex numbers. (See [18].)"

## 4. Fuchs and Sanden, 2011

"Some experiments with integral Apollonian circle packings", arXiv 1001.1406. Read: the abstract, the
introduction, and section 3's observations on the two packings they ran; the heuristics for primes looked through.

> "Conjecture 1.1. Local to Global Principle for ACP’s: Let P be an integral ACP and let P24 be the set of residue
> classes mod 24 of curvatures in P. Then there exists XP ∈ Z such that any integer x > XP whose residue mod 24 lies
> in P24 is in fact a curvature of a circle in P."

> "our data suggests that XPB exists and is ≤ 106 , as we find no integers x > 106 which violate the above
> conjecture."

They also give, for each residue class, the share of circles whose curvature falls in it (for the packing (−11,
21, 24, 28): a tenth each for the classes 0 and 12, three tenths for 13), and say of the number of times each
integer occurs: "The frequencies seem normally distributed, and the means of the distributions can be computed, as
we do in (3.6)."

## 5. Bourgain and Kontorovich, 2014

"On the local-global conjecture for integral Apollonian gaskets", arXiv 1205.4416. Read: the abstract and the
statement of the main theorem only.

> "We prove that a set of density one satisfies the local- global conjecture for integral Apollonian gaskets."

> "Theorem 1.2. Almost every admissible number is the curvature of a circle in G . Quantitatively, the number of
> exceptions up to N is bounded by O(N 1−η ), where η > 0 is effectively computable."
