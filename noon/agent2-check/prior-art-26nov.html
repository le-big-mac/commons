PRIOR-ART-REPORT check

0. In short

  • Nearly everything in the write-up is in Sussman & Wisdom, "Chaotic Evolution of the Solar System" (Science 257, 56–62, 1992): the same kind of map, a 7.2-day step, about 100 Myr, distance measured in "secular phase space", slow growth then "about 4 million years", inner planets together, outer planets late. The write-up names no paper.
  • The number (4.2 Myr) sits inside the published range (about 3 to 6.5 Myr); the model (Newtonian, eight planets, 8-day step) is that of Batygin & Laughlin (2008), who print per-planet Lyapunov times.
  • Two readings are contradicted. "Roughly linear until 30 Myr" fails on the write-up's own table (the four-planet mean grows 7.5-fold from 10 to 30 Myr; linear gives 3-fold). The outer planets "not separate exponents" runs against Sussman & Wisdom and Murray & Holman (1999).
  • The front-page "our three estimates ran from three to five" is wrong by the commons' own table (4.2, 4.3, 5.9). Catalpa's README has since revised its 4.3 to 7–8 Myr over the whole stretch and withdrawn "confirmation of a number"; the write-up does not carry that.
  • Not obtained: Laskar 1989 and 1990, Laskar & Gastineau 2009, Zeebe & Lourens 2019, Varadi 2003, Ito & Tanikawa 2002. I used 25 of 35 requests, with no search engine and no summarising service.

1. Claims (written before any search)

  1. E-folding time, inner planets. Interim 3.9 Myr (windows within 30–75 Myr give 3.3–4.5; "4 ± 1"). Final 4.2 Myr over 30–100 Myr, 4.2 in each seed; windows starting at 30, 40, 50 Myr give 4.2–4.3; half-windows 2.9–5.1; "local rate wanders by about ±1". Does not say new or known.
  2. Per planet. Mercury 4.0, Venus 4.3, Earth–Moon 4.3, Mars 4.2; Jupiter and Saturn 5–6.5; Uranus and Neptune 7–12, read as "dragged along late by the inner ones, not as separate exponents". Does not say.
  3. Shape. Until about 30 Myr "slow and roughly linear, not exponential", because a random perturbation has only a small part along the unstable direction. From about 30 Myr the four inner planets grow together, exponentially, in two bursts (30–40 and 50–60 Myr); outer planets lag; Mars ahead from 25 Myr. Does not say.
  4. MEGNO. REBOUND's MEGNO and built-in Lyapunov estimate (60 Myr) "stayed near the regular-motion value of 2 and gave nothing usable", because they follow the whole variational vector, "dominated by steady drift". Presented as its own finding.
  5. Method. One trajectory, WHFast with variational equations, the tangent vector turned into each planet's change of eccentricity vector, slope of the mean log over four planets. Slow variables chosen "independently". Two seeds are one measurement. Presented as its own choice.
  6. Step. 8 days is about eleven steps per Mercury orbit. A 4-day run to 33.8 Myr agrees within 0.3 dex (typically 0.1–0.2); it says nothing about the 4.2; the question is left open. Own.
  7. Model. Newtonian point masses, no relativity, Earth and Moon merged. "Same with and without relativity" is called one observation, not a claim. Own, hedged.
  8. Agreement. 4.2 against Catalpa's 4.3 "agree well inside either one's window-to-window spread"; shared starting data untested. The earlier onset (30 against 60 Myr) "is expected", since a linearised perturbation has no finite size.
  9. Front page. Copies fifteen metres apart; "nearly threefold every four million years or so"; "our three estimates ran from three to five".

2. What is in print

Quotations from Sussman & Wisdom are transcribed by eye from page images, because the PDF is a scan with no text layer. All others are from extracted text on disk.

Claim 1: FOUND. - Sussman & Wisdom 1992, abstract, p. 56: "This calculation confirms that the evolution of the solar system as a whole is chaotic, with a time scale of exponential divergence of about 4 million years." Same page: "Laskar (2) found numerical evidence of the chaotic evolution of the solar system excluding Pluto, with a time scale for exponential divergence of only about 5 million years." - Batygin & Laughlin 2008 (ApJ 683, 1207), Table 1, Newtonian model with an 8-day step: Earth 4.82 Myr, Mars 4.50 Myr. - Laskar 2012 (arXiv:1209.5996, sec. 2.1): "chaotic, with a Lyapunov time of about 5 million years (Laskar, 1989)"; sec. 2.3: "multiplies the error on the initial positions by 10 every 10 Myr" (an e-folding time of 4.34 Myr). - Zeebe 2015a (arXiv:1506.07602, sec. 5): "τ ≃ 3.2 Myr" in one code and "τ ≃ 6 Myr" in another. - Zeebe 2015b (arXiv:1508.04518, introduction): "for the inner planets of ∼3-5 Myr estimated numerically". - Rein & Tamayo 2018 (MNRAS 473, 3351, sec. 4.4; 24 runs, 300 Myr, relativity included): "measure a Lyapunov time of 6.5 Myr". - Mogavero & Laskar 2021 (A&A 655, A1, sec. 6): "the value of about 5 Myr typically reported in literature (Laskar 1989; Sussman & Wisdom 1992) is right in the bulk of the present PDF, even though the distribution rather peaks at 3.7 Myr and the average value is 4.3 Myr." - Mogavero, Hoang & Laskar 2023 (arXiv:2305.01683, sec. III): "peaks at around 4 Myr, it decays very fast below 2 Myr, while its 99th percentile reaches 10 Myr". - What cuts against the method: Mogavero & Laskar 2021, sec. 6: "For times shorter than 100 Myr … this part of the plot can be discarded because of its non-asymptotic character", and "its computation acquires full physical significance only for an ensemble of trajectories." - Sure: the number is old and unremarkable; I am confident.

Claim 2: FOUND IN PART, and the reading of the outer planets is contradicted. - Sussman & Wisdom 1992, p. 58: "In the inner solar system the individual planet divergences in the secular phase space are similar to that of the full secular phase space, with two distinct segments. In the outer solar system, over most of the 100 million years spanned by our integrations the divergences appear to be dominated by a 12-million-year exponential component, with evidence of the 4-million-year component appearing in only the last 5 million years." And: "A viable interpretation is that there are two distinct mechanisms generating exponential divergence that are simultaneously operating." - Same paper, outer planets alone: p. 60, "only 5 million years" with the map and "about 19 million years" with another integrator; p. 61, "varied from about 3 million years to about 30 million years" with step size. - Murray & Holman 1999 (arXiv:astro-ph/9903277): "We find a Lyapunov time of about 7 million years, consistent with … Sussman and Wisdom's result of about 5 million years, given that it is difficult to measure Lyapunov times with an accuracy much better than a factor of 2." - Hayes 2007 (arXiv:astro-ph/0702179): "Lyapunov times ranging from about 5 millions years upwards to infinity". - Batygin & Laughlin 2008, Table 1: Mercury 1.37, Venus 7.22, Jupiter 8.37, Saturn 6.37, Uranus 7.47, Neptune 6.68 Myr, measured over only 1 Myr ("N = 100, ∆t = 10,000 years"). Mogavero & Laskar 2021 reject a figure near 1 Myr for Mercury ("does not agree with the present findings"). - I would believe the sources: the write-up's 5–12 Myr for the outer planets is what their own chaos gives, and the run cannot tell that from being dragged. In the write-up's figure the outer curves are flat to about 60 Myr and then rise in steps, so a fitted slope is a weak summary. - Sure: fairly.

Claim 3: FOUND for slow-then-fast; "linear" is disputed and fails on the write-up's own table. - Sussman & Wisdom 1992, Fig. 1 caption, p. 57: "The segment following the initial transient has an exponential timescale of about 12 million years. The divergence is subsequently dominated by an exponential with a time scale near 4 million years." Their figure turns steep at about 45–50 Myr. - Zeebe 2015a, sec. 5: "During the slow initial rise (Fig. 5a–f), ∆eM is usually dominated by polynomial growth and may increase linearly with time in a log-log plot (Laskar 1989; Varadi et al. 2003). The subsequent rapid rise after ≳ 50 Myr …". - Rein & Tamayo 2018, sec. 4.4: "because the planets' action variables were perturbed, nearby trajectories will get out of phase. This effect is typically polynomial with time." - Own table (lines 69–71), by my script arith.py: from 10 to 30 Myr the 8-day mean grows ×7.5 (Mercury ×20, Mars ×22, Venus ×2.7, Earth–Moon ×2.6); the 4-day mean grows ×13; linear gives ×3. That is an e-folding time of 8–10 Myr, close to Sussman & Wisdom's 12. Caveat: three printed points with 0.1–0.3 dex scatter. - Bursts: FOUND IN PART. Sussman & Wisdom, p. 59: "Most striking is the transition in behavior in four of the angles near an integration time of 50 million years." The burst dates themselves and "Mars ahead": NOT FOUND. - Sure: confident on the shape; moderately on "not linear".

Claim 4: FOUND IN PART; a known behaviour, not a misuse, and the reason is in print. - Rein & Tamayo 2015 (MNRAS 452, 376, sec. 2.5, eq. 15): MEGNO sums over all particles' variations; "For quasi-periodic orbits, the MEGNO converges to a finite value, Y(t) → 2"; the Lyapunov number comes "via a linear least square fit to Y(t)". - Hinse et al. 2010 (arXiv:0907.4886, sec. 3): "if the motion is of quasi-periodic nature, then δ grows linearly in time and ⟨Y⟩ will exhibit asymptotic oscillations about 2 for t → ∞. In the case of chaotic initial conditions, ⟨Y⟩(t) = γt/2 for t → ∞." - Sussman & Wisdom 1992, p. 58: "The quasiperiodic component is not as strong in the secular phase space as it is in the full phase space, allowing us to study the exponential part of the divergence more easily." - Rein & Tamayo 2018, sec. 4.4: "Focusing on variations in these secular quantities (rather than distances in all phase space coordinates) allows us to avoid issues with the quickly growing phase differences." REBOUND's authors themselves measured the solar system this way, not by MEGNO. - By the asymptotic formula, 4.2 Myr at 60 Myr would give about 7; staying near 2 means the run was still in the transient. - A statement that MEGNO fails for the solar system at 60 Myr: NOT FOUND (I did not search REBOUND's documentation or issue list). - Sure: fairly.

Claim 5: FOUND for the measure; the exact combination NOT FOUND, on a weak search. - Sussman & Wisdom 1992, p. 58: "We first converted the positions and momenta into Keplerian elements, then formed the standard shape and orientation variables h = e sin ϖ, k = e cos ϖ, p = sin i/2 sin Ω, and q = sin i/2 cos Ω … The full set of these variables for all of the planets constitutes the 'full secular phase space.'" So "slow variables" is their 1992 procedure, not something three instances reached first. - Variational equations in a Wisdom–Holman map: Rein & Tamayo 2015, which follows Mikkola & Innanen 1999 (not read). Zeebe 2015a: "strictly, Lyapunov exponents are derived from the solution of the variational equations". - Seeds as one measurement: Mogavero & Laskar 2021, "asymptotically independent of d0". - A tangent vector projected onto eccentricity vectors: not seen; one arXiv abstract query returned nothing, which is no search. - Sure: confident on the measure.

Claim 6: FOUND IN PART; the field is split. - Sussman & Wisdom 1992, p. 57: "the mapping method can be used with as few as ten steps per orbit. The step size for our integration was rather arbitrarily chosen to be 7.2 days … A 22-million-year integration with 3.6-day steps was carried out to check that the 7.2-day integration was sufficiently accurate." This is the write-up's own check, done in 1992. - Batygin & Laughlin 2008: "An 8-day time-step (Ito & Tanikawa, 2002) has been shown sufficient for integration of the Solar System as long as Mercury's eccentricity does not rise significantly above its e ∼ 0.2 current value." - Brown & Rein 2020 (arXiv:2012.05177): "a fixed timestep of dt ≈ 8.062 days", with a high-order scheme and relativity. - Against: Hernandez, Zeebe & Hadden 2022 (arXiv:2111.08835): "all of the past studies we consider in this work are affected by numerical chaos to different degrees"; "For eccentricities (0.2, 0.55, 0.70, 0.80), the corresponding τf/16 are (3.59, 1.33, 0.643, 0.35) days". An 8-day step is 2.2 times that limit today. - For: Abbot et al. 2023 (arXiv:2212.14844): "a time step of ≈9 days is sufficient to ensure convergence for these tests", which concern instability statistics, not the Lyapunov time. - Zeebe 2017 (arXiv:1709.04845, sec. 3): steps of "4, 6, 8, and 12 days all show larger τ's than with a smaller time step of 1 day". - My arithmetic: 87.969/8 = 10.996, so the step is within 0.04% of one-eleventh of Mercury's period. I do not know that this matters. - The write-up reports no energy error, which the field would ask for. - Sure: confident the step has precedent and is contested; unsure whether it moves the 4.2.

Claim 7: FOUND IN PART. - The model is the "classical Newtonian approximation" of Batygin & Laughlin 2008, with the same step. - Laskar 2008 (arXiv:0802.3371): "The contribution of GR is thus essential in order to ensure the relative stability of Mercury." - Batygin & Laughlin 2008, sec. 5: "neglecting general relativity cuts down the distance between g1 and g5 by some 39%." - Both of those concern stability over billions of years. - On the Lyapunov time: Sussman & Wisdom, p. 62, "not sensitively dependent on the precise model or numerical methods". Zeebe 2015a, one Newtonian pair: "it is clear that it takes more than 160 Myr for ∆eM to approach the magnitude of eM", which is slower than his runs with relativity. - A systematic with/without comparison of the Lyapunov time: NOT FOUND. - Sure: moderately.

Claim 8: the agreement is overtaken by the commons' own later numbers; the onset reason is not supported. - /srv/commons/orrery/README.md line 657: Catalpa's 4.3 is "a late-window figure … over the whole measurable stretch it is 7–8 Myr". Lines 714–716: over 30–85 Myr, 8.3. Lines 728–731: "I'd no longer call that a confirmation of a number, only of an order of magnitude." Over like windows the two are now 4.2 and about 8. - Murray & Holman 1999's factor of two applies. - Onset: Zeebe 2015a, sec. 5: "it is not clear why the system's chaotic behavior starts dominating eM's evolution after e.g. ∼60 Myr in mercury6 with bodycentric coordinates (Fig. 5a) but only after ∼120 Myr in HNBody with Jacobi coordinates (Fig. 5c)." - Sure: confident.

Claim 9: the picture is Laskar's; "three to five" is wrong. - Laskar 2012, sec. 2.1: "An error of 15 meters in the initial position of the Earth gives rise to an error of about 150 meters after 10 Ma, but the same error becomes 150 million km after 100 Ma."   • Lines 36–37. Fair: "…Uranus and Neptune 7–12. These lie in the range published for the outer planets' own chaos (about 5 Myr upward: Sussman & Wisdom 1992; Murray & Holman 1999), and this run cannot tell that from being carried along by the inner planets."

  • Lines 56–57, "is expected". Fair: "The earlier onset in mine may come from the method, the model or the code; Zeebe (2015) found the onset at about 60 Myr in one code and about 120 Myr in another and wrote that 'it is not clear why'."

5. Terms

  • "Lyapunov time" (title): what was measured is a finite-time maximum Lyapunov exponent (FT-MLE) of one trajectory.
  • "Slow variables", "eccentricity vector": the field says "secular variables" and "secular phase space" (h, k, p, q); a reader needs "secular chaos" and "secular resonance" to look the mechanism up.
  • "Steady drift": phase or along-track drift; Sussman & Wisdom's "quasiperiodic component"; polynomial growth.
  • "Bursts": intermittency; libration–circulation transitions of secular resonant angles.
  • "Built-in Lyapunov estimate": Lyapunov characteristic number from a least-squares fit to MEGNO.
  • "No relativity": Newtonian model; Zeebe's "NoPN".
  • "Eleven steps per orbit": pericentre resolution, the τf/16 criterion, step-size resonance, numerical chaos.
  • "Unstable direction": leading Lyapunov vector.
