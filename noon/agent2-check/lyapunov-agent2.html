Written at 14:20 UTC on 19 Nov 2026, before reading agent1's result. Interim: the runs are at 75 of 100 Myr.

Interim number: e-folding time about 3.9 Myr for the inner planets, with fits over different windows between 30 and 75 Myr ranging from 3.3 to 4.5 Myr. I would quote it as 4 ± 1 Myr.

How: one trajectory of the Sun and eight planets (Earth and Moon merged), Newtonian point masses, REBOUND's WHFast at an 8-day step, with the variational equations integrated alongside (lyap2.py). Every 0.1 Myr I convert the variational vector into the change it implies in each planet's eccentricity vector and log its size. The slope of the logarithm against time, averaged over the four inner planets, gives the number (fit.py).

What the runs show: - Until about 30 Myr the growth is slow and roughly linear, not exponential. A random starting perturbation has only a small part along the unstable direction. - From about 30 Myr the four inner planets grow together, exponentially; the outer planets lag well behind. - REBOUND's MEGNO indicator and its built-in Lyapunov estimate, which I tried first (lyap.py, 60 Myr), stayed near the regular-motion value of 2 and gave nothing usable. They follow the size of the whole variational vector, which is dominated by steady drift.

What this shares with agent1's computation: the J2000 state vectors and masses. What differs: the integrator, the method (variational equations against displaced copies), and the model — mine has no relativity and no Moon.

Limits: - The two seeds give almost identical numbers. That is not two confirmations: both variational vectors converge on the same unstable direction of the same trajectory. - An 8-day step is about eleven steps per Mercury orbit. I have no 4-day run with this diagnostic to compare. - The window-to-window spread (3.3 to 4.5) is the honest size of the uncertainty; the local rate wanders.

Final (16:06 UTC, runs at 98–100 Myr; still written before reading agent1's result)

e-folding time 4.2 Myr for the inner planets, fitted over 30–100 Myr (4.2 in each seed; windows starting at 30, 40 and 50 Myr give 4.2–4.3; half-windows range from 2.9 to 5.1, so the local rate wanders by about ±1). Per planet over the same span: Mercury 4.0, Venus 4.3, Earth–Moon 4.3, Mars 4.2; Jupiter and Saturn 5–6.5 and Uranus and Neptune 7–12, which I read as the outer planets being dragged along late by the inner ones, not as separate exponents. The interim figure above (3.9, "4 ± 1") stands inside that.

A 4-day-step run with the same diagnostic was started at 14:35 UTC to test the step size; it is not in this number.

The two numbers agree well inside either one's window-to-window spread. The agreement does not test the starting data, which is common to both, and both of us chose slow variables as the measure (independently — I came to it after the whole-vector indicator showed nothing). That the figure is the same with and without relativity is an observation from one pair of runs, not a claim that relativity doesn't matter to the chaos. The earlier onset in mine is expected: a linearised perturbation has no finite size to grow from, while a 15-metre displacement first has to outgrow the slow drift between copies.

The 4-day-step comparison run (stopped at 33.8 Myr, 20 November 2026, 02:25 UTC)

The run started at 14:35 UTC on 19 November to test the 8-day step was starved of CPU by my own later jobs and by the load on the machine (it advanced 0.45 Myr in its last hour), so I stopped it at 33.8 Myr instead of 70. What it shows over the span both runs cover: the size of each inner planet's eccentricity-vector perturbation agrees between the 4-day and 8-day runs to within 0.3 in log₁₀ at every time I compared (5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 33 Myr), typically 0.1–0.2, and Mars is ahead of the other three from 25 Myr on in both.

It says nothing about the burst itself (30–40 Myr in the 8-day runs) or about the fitted 4.2 Myr, which comes almost entirely from later times: the step-size question for that number is still open, and I am not pursuing it — Catalpa's and Yonder's runs (4-day and finer steps, with correctors, with and without relativity) cover it better than mine would.
