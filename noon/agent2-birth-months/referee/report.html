  1. Verdict

Every number in the README reproduced from my blind code, all eleven verdicts are right by the letter, and no data artefact I tried changes a verdict. Five "In short" sentences say more than their numbers: Germany (it is men only that move on a late-1990s timetable), Spain's and the United Kingdom's single dividing dates, baseball's "year groups from 1 August", and "politicians show no such thing". The Brier lead is arithmetically right but rests on one expectation that failed by 16 births.

  1. Errors and overreach, most serious first

a. Germany: "Germany moved its date, and the surplus moved with it … changed its dividing date in 1997." - Found: the move holds for men; women move about ten birth-years later, which a single 1997 change does not explain. - Evidence:

1990–94 22.0 / 36.4 24.8 / 24.1
1995–2005 18.4 / 36.9 23.1 / 29.3
  • For 1985–89 the sexes differ by 3.9 and 4.2 standard errors, so the README's "transition" row (25.6 / 27.3) is an average of two opposite patterns.
  • The old band is 9% women and the new band 73%; the README mentions the imbalance only under "German women".
  • The 1997 date is unverified (I recall the same date from the literature, but that is memory and I had no network), and the data cannot date a rule.
  • Should say: "Among German men the peak is August to October for those born 1940–79 (31.8%) and January to March for those born 1985 or later (35.4%); women follow only from births of about 1995. Not registered; the 1997 date is from a summary of a paper I have not read."

b. Spain: "year groups from 1 January" (also the figure). - Found: older Spanish players behave like a milder Germany. - Evidence: those born 1960–79 have Aug–Oct 28.0%, equal to their Jan–Mar 28.0% (per-day index Jul 80, Aug 110, Sep 120). For 1940–79 the fortnight before 1 August has 94 births and the fortnight after 136 (2.8 SE; one of three such looks, Brazil 123:107 and Canada 147:162). For 1980 and later Aug–Oct is 20.4%. - Either Spain also had an August date, in which case "1 January" is wrong for the 42% born before 1980, or August peaks arise without a rule, which would weaken Germany. No source for Spain's or Brazil's dates over the century was consulted. - Should say: "Spain: 1 January holds for those born 1980 or later (32.2%); older players show a second peak after 1 August that I have not explained."

c. United Kingdom: "British footballers (1 September)". - Found: recent cohorts have a second step at 1 January. - Evidence: births 18–31 Dec against 2–15 Jan are 226:319 for those born 1990–2005 (4.0 SE), 152:193 for 1980–89, and 597:615 before 1980; politicians 247:271. Sep–Nov falls from 33.2% (1940–79) to 29.6% (1990 on) while Jan–Mar rises from 24.7% to 27.5%. The files cannot separate the home nations. - Should say: "1 September is the English and Welsh school year; the list includes Scots and Northern Irish, and those born 1990 or later also show a step at 1 January."

d. Baseball: "peak in August and September (year groups from 1 August)". - Found: the step at 1 August exists only for births 1950–89, and September's excess does not survive the House control. - Evidence: 18–31 Jul against 1–14 Aug is 238:342 for 1950–89 (4.3 SE). Players born before 1950 (43% of the list) have Aug–Oct 27.7% with no step (286:317, 1.3 SE). - Top month in Noon's own four bands: Sep, Oct, Aug, Sep. The README quotes only the band where August wins. - Baseball over House (same years) by month: Aug 1.16 ± 0.08, Sep 1.04 ± 0.07, Oct 1.10 ± 0.08. The House "113" is ± 6. - Should say: "A step at 1 August among players born 1950–89 only; allowing for House members the excess is August's, not September's; older players have a late-summer hump without a step."

e. Politicians: "show no such thing … between 22.4% and 26.1%". - Found: the range is right and the control survives matching on years of birth, but the politicians' months are not even. - Evidence it survives: restricted to those born 1960 or later, controls are 25.0, 23.2, 25.5, 23.0, 25.2, 24.0, 25.7 (Spain, Brazil, Germany, Canada, UK, Japan, House) against athletes 31.2, 31.1, 28.1, 32.1, 32.0, 34.7, 29.4. Politicians have no step at the date (fortnight ratios 0.87–1.10, against 1.24–3.2 for athletes). - Evidence it overreaches: - Germany is +1.6 points from even (5.3 SE) and Japan −2.6 (5.6 SE). - Germany's registered quantity separates footballers from politicians by only 1.2 points (27.3 against 26.1). - The US control born 1960 or later is 591 people. - "Japanese born January to March are over-represented among them" turns a count into a cause. The Jan–Mar share falls with year of birth (34.5%, 30.1%, 27.1%, 23.6%), and 1.05% of them are dated 1 January, which points to how dates were recorded. - "Removes 0.2% to 0.5% of each group" is false for that file (1.05%) and for UK politicians (0.57%). - Should say: "Politicians show no step at the dividing date and no comparable surplus; their months are not even (Germany, and Japan before 1940)."

f. Brier: "0.140 against 0.152". - Found: the arithmetic is right, but the order is decided by M10 alone. - Evidence: without M10 it is 0.118 against 0.098, forecasters ahead. Had August beaten September (they are 0.3 SE apart), it would be 0.122 against 0.092. Noon was closer on 6 of 11. - Should say: add "the order is decided by one expectation that failed by 0.3 standard errors".

g. "In every group." - Found: it holds for the pooled, mostly male lists (85–98% men outside Germany), not for British women. - Evidence: UK women 25.6% (n=2,021; even 25.0). Elsewhere women are 26.9–29.7% against men's 29.7–35.2%. - Should say: "in every pooled group; the surplus is smaller among women and absent among British women".

h. Smaller points. - The figure legend says of politicians "nobody sorted them by age"; schools did. - "Should not be ranked" sits beside "Japan is the steepest". - "So it reaches much further down the leagues" for German women is untested. - Japan's step is at 2 April, as its school law has it, not 1 April: 11 births on 1 April, 55 on 2 April.

  1. What I verified and how
  • Blind recomputation. I used my own loader and rules, written before opening anything except expectations.md; results are in /home/agent2/birth/referee/blind.out.
  • Definitions. I tried first, earliest and latest date, dropping everyone with two dates, keeping 1 January, and dropping every first of the month. The largest shift is 0.4 points and all eleven verdicts are unchanged, including M7 and M10.
  • Duplicates. There are 107,159 distinct people in 107,254 athlete rows; 95 appear under two countries, and removing them changes nothing.
  • Heaping. Athletes have 2.96–3.31% of dates on a first of the month (even births: 3.02%), and every timestamp is midnight. Only Japanese politicians heap (4.1% on the 1st, 4.0% on the 15th).
  • Files. All fourteen match data.sha256 and end in a complete row with no error text.
  • Step at the dividing date. Fortnight after against fortnight before: Spain 1.72, Brazil 1.63, Canada 1.76, UK 1.53, Japan 3.19 (at 2 April), baseball 1.24. Germany 1940–79 is 1.40 at 1 August and 0.99 at 1 January; Germany 1990 on is 2.14 at 1 January.
  • Germany. Seven placements of the cuts give Aug–Oct 30.9–31.8 for the old band and Jan–Mar 29.4–31.0 for the new. It holds in every five-year band from 1940 to 1979 and among men alone. German politicians have Aug–Oct 24.0–24.3 in every band. There is no step at 1 July (189:190), a school date I supplied from memory, not from the README.
  • Controls. I restricted both sides to four bands of birth year and reweighted the control to the athletes' years (attack.out section E).
  • Brier. Recomputed from expectations.md, f1.json to f3.json and the verdicts, with leave-one-out.
  • My own error. My first January fortnight compared 12 days with 13 and overstated the ratios by 8%. It is flagged in attack.out and corrected in attack2.out; the figures above are the corrected ones.

Scripts and outputs are in /home/agent2/birth/referee/: reflib.py, blind.py, attack.py, attack2.py, attack3.py and their .out files.
