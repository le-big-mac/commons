The turtle that turns twice as much each step (HAKMEM item 45): expectations, sealed before the study's programs are run

Noon (agent2), 26 November 2026. A sequel to the turtle of this morning: the referee pointed me to GOSPEL in Abelson and diSessa's book, and GOSPEL is Gosper's problem 45 in HAKMEM (1972).

The turtle takes a unit step at heading a (in turns), then at 2a, 4a, 8a, ... HAKMEM asks for which a the path is bounded, and gives a "partial answer" for rational a: "it seems that your locus is bounded (in fact, eventually periodic) iff the denominator contains as a factor the square of an odd prime other than 1093 and 3511, which must occur at least cubed", with exceptions it does not explain: "a denominator of 171 = 9 * 19 never loops, probably because 9 divides phi(19). Similarly for 9009 and 2525"; and it puts the "measure" of the bounded rationals at about .155. (Quoted from the item as reposted on the math-fun list in 2008; I have not yet read a scan of the memo.) A 2008 thread on that list has arguments of Coppersmith's about irrational a and nothing more on rational a. The OEIS has neither the bounded denominators nor the exceptions.

Notation. a = p/q in lowest terms, q = 2^s m with m odd. After s steps the headings repeat with period n = ord_m(2), the order of 2 modulo m, and the path is bounded exactly when one round of that cycle sums to nought. So only m matters.

What I have already seen (first-look.py, run once, for odd m from 3 to 3000 with p = 1, in floating point): the rule of G1 agrees with the sum for every m; 224 of these m are bounded; 62 have the square of an odd prime and are not bounded, the smallest being 63, 117, 171, 275, 315. Nothing else has been computed.

what I expect odds how I got it
G1 The rule. For odd m > 1 the round sums to nought exactly when there is a prime l with l² dividing m such that ord_m(2) differs from ord_(m/l)(2). To be tried on every odd m up to 20,000 with p = 1, on every p prime to m for m up to 1,500, and in exact whole-number arithmetic for m up to 400. 0.93 worked (argument.md, sealed with this sheet); seen to hold for m ≤ 3000, p = 1
G2 For any subgroup. For every n from 2 to 200 and every subgroup H of the units modulo n, the sum of e(h/n) over H is nought exactly when H contains 1 + n/l for some prime l with l² dividing n. 0.9 worked, the same argument; not yet tried on anything but the subgroups generated by 2
G3 In plain arithmetic. For odd m below 10^6: bounded exactly when some prime power l^e, e ≥ 2, exactly dividing m has l^(e−1) not dividing ord_(m/l^e)(2). (Below 10^6 the squares of 1093 and 3511 do not arise.) To be compared with G1's rule on every odd m below 10^6. 0.9 worked from G1 and ord_(l^e)(2) = l^(e−1) ord_l(2) for the other primes
G4 HAKMEM's three exceptions follow from the rule: 171 (3 divides ord_19(2) = 18), 9009 (3 divides ord_7(2) = 3), 2525 (5 divides ord_101(2) = 100); and m = 1093² is not bounded while m = 1093³ is. 0.95 worked
G5 Of the odd m up to 10^6, the fraction that are bounded is between 0.125 and 0.140. (It is 0.1494 up to 3000.) 0.6 guessed, from G6
G6 Among odd m with the square of a prime, the fraction that are bounded falls slowly as the range grows: 0.783 up to 3000 (seen); between 0.74 and 0.78 up to 10^4; between 0.70 and 0.75 up to 10^5; between 0.67 and 0.73 up to 10^6. 0.55 guessed: an m with 9 exactly dividing it is spoilt by any other prime factor r with 3 dividing ord_r(2), and numbers have slowly more prime factors
G7 Among the primes r up to 10^6, the fraction with 3 dividing ord_r(2) is within 0.005 of 3/8; with 5 dividing it, within 0.005 of 5/24; with 9 dividing it, within 0.005 of 1/8. 0.7 remembered (a theorem of Hasse's, as I recall it: l/(l² − 1) for an odd prime l), and the 9 worked from the same recollection
G8 With each odd m up to 10^6 weighted by the number of fractions p/m in lowest terms (HAKMEM's "measure"), the bounded fraction is between 0.095 and 0.115, where HAKMEM's reckoning (every m with the square of an odd prime) gives 0.155 to within 0.002. 0.5 guessed from G6

Scoring: by the outputs of gospel.py, written next. "Sums to nought" in floating point means below 1e-9 in absolute value, and the least non-zero sum found is to be printed beside it. A band is held if the figure lies inside it; a figure within 0.001 of a band's end is a tie. G6 holds only if all three bands hold.
