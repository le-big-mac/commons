Faults, the most serious first

  1. The recipe gives the wrong answer for 1/441 if the reader crosses out the 3. Moderate; 1/441 is one of the clock's buttons. "The same number (1/63 and 1/21 both take 6): it marches away. If two primes appear twice, either may do it." Taken to the letter: 441 = 3 × 3 × 7 × 7; cross out a 3 to get 147; the counts are 42 for 1/441 and 42 for 1/147 (extra.out). "The same number: it marches away." It comes home: with a 7 out the count is 6, and 42 is seven times that. "Either may do it" is meant to cover this and is too short to be understood before the reader has stopped. Replacement: "If two primes appear twice, try each. It comes home if either gives the longer count, and marches away only if every one gives the same number."

  2. The recipe's count is wrong for an even bottom number unless its last sentence is done first. Moderate. "Otherwise cross one of the repeated primes out (45 becomes 15) and count the steps before each turtle's turns repeat: 12 for 1/45, 4 for 1/15." and, last, "An even bottom number never brings the turtle home: take its 2s out first, and where the rest would have brought it home it circles one loop for ever." For 1/18 a reader who counts as told gets 7 turns before one comes again (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 14, 10, then 2), and 3 for 1/6: neither three times as many nor the same (extra.out). The instruction that mends this says "first" and stands last. Replacement: open the recipe with it. "First take any 2s out of the bottom number. (An even bottom number never brings the turtle home: where the recipe below says 'comes home', it circles one loop for ever.) Then split what is left into primes…" Smaller, same place: the recipe never says the fraction must be in lowest terms (3/45 is 1/15).

  3. The caption's multiplication fails in a printed digit for one turtle of 1,663. Low. For 50/7319 the page prints: "They do not balance: the cross is their balance point, 0.012686388 of the way from the centre to the rim. Each round of 3,372 steps leaves the turtle 3,372 times that, 42.778 steps, further on." 3,372 × 0.012686388 = 42.7785003, which rounds to 42.779. The distance itself is right (my walk gives 42.778). The balance point is already printed to nine places and still does not carry it. Replacement: print the distance first and the balance point as a rounded figure. "Each round of 3,372 steps leaves the turtle 42.778 steps further on; the cross, their balance point, is about 0.0127 of the way from the centre to the rim (the distance divided by 3,372)."

  4. "Dots that all lie in less than half the clock, as for 1/7, are lopsided, and each round shoves the turtle the same way." Low; true, and it describes very few turtles. Among odd bottom numbers to 2001 with first turn 1, 854 rounds do not close. In 9 of them the hours all lie within less than half the clock; in 845 they do not (extra.out). 1/21, another of the buttons, is one of the 845. A reader will take the sentence as the picture of a marching turtle and then not find it. Replacement: add "Others are lopsided less plainly, as for 1/21: the cross on the clock shows which way."

  5. "Dots evenly spread, as for 1/9, balance, and the shoves cancel." Low. The dots of 1/9 are at 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8: gaps of 1, 2, 1, 2, 1, 2. They are two triangles, not evenly spread. Replacement: "Dots that fall into triangles, as for 1/9, balance, and the shoves cancel."

  6. "1/63 breaks that; it is the smallest that does, and is not among the exceptions the memo itself named (1/171 is one)." Low; true, and "smallest" of what is left to the reader. It is true of bottom numbers, for every first turn: over every fraction with bottom number from 2 to 130, the ones that break the memo's rule have bottom numbers 63, 117 and 126, and all break it the same way (they march away though an odd prime comes in twice). Replacement: "1/63 breaks that; 63 is the smallest bottom number that does, and it is not among the exceptions the memo itself named (171 is one)."

  7. The wait before an example walks was longer than a second and a half on the first tap. Low. The first example button I pressed still said "About to set off." at 1.9 s with no step drawn; the next two had started by then (wait.js). The sentence itself is true while it shows.
