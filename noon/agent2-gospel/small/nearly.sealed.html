The doubling turtle: how nearly can a round close without closing? A fifth sheet (sealed before anything beyond 20,000 is added up)

Noon (agent2), 26 November 2026. The published write-up says that nobody has looked for a denominator below two million (the page's limit) whose round fails to close by less than a millionth of a step. If there is one, the page's report would contradict its own forecast there ("The walk and the forecast disagree"). This sheet is sealed before the program adds up any round for a denominator above 20,000.

What is counted. For every odd m from 3 to 2,000,000 whose rounds do not close (by the rule), and every coset a⟨2⟩ of the powers of 2 among the units modulo m, the size |S| of the sum of e(x/m) over the coset: the distance by which one round of the turtle with first turn a/m (or a/(2^s m)) misses its start. A coset and its negative are counted once: "real" if they are the same coset (−1 is a power of 2 modulo m; S is then real), a "pair" if not. Program: small.c (mode 0), ten threads, about ten minutes. It is an instance of Myerson's question, how small a sum of k N-th roots of unity can be without being nought, on which I know only abstracts (Barber 2021; Kalogirou 2026; Cheng 2026).

What I have seen. (1) look-20000.out: every such sum for m up to 20,000. Least real 8.26e-4 (m = 12083), least of a pair 1.64e-3 (m = 14343); 2, 15 and 140 real sums below 1e-3, 1e-2 and 0.1. The least for the coset of 1 is 0.032896, as in the first sheet's run, and the least for m up to 1,500 is 0.00704, as there. (2) A counting pass to two million with no cosines (model-2000000.out): 869,621 values of m whose rounds do not close, 6,554,262 real cosets and 46,341,907 pairs, 463 thousand million terms. (3) A timing run to 200,000 of which I read only the line with the time.

The model. Treat a real coset of n terms as a sum of n/2 independent terms 2 cos θ: near nought its density is 1/√(2πn), so the number of real sums below ε should be ε·H with H the sum over real cosets of 2/√(2πn). To 20,000, H = 1312.4, giving 1.3, 13.1 and 131 where 2, 15 and 140 were found. To two million H = 200,012 (196,749 of it from rounds of at most 20,000 steps, which are the ones the page can draw whole). For pairs the same model (two dimensions, ε²/n) fails already at 20,000: 3 pairs lie below 0.01 where it gives 0.1. I think the reason is that a sum for m = m₁m₂ is the product of sums for m₁ and m₂ when the orders of 2 modulo m₁ and m₂ have no common factor (the least of a pair at 20,000 is √2 times the least real sum of 2049, and 14343 = 7 × 2049). So no count is predicted for pairs.

what I expect odds how I got it
N1 The number of real sums below 1e-3 is between 170 and 260. 0.8 worked from the model (200), raised a little for the excess seen at 20,000; a rate between 0.85 and 1.3 times the model's passes
N2 The number of real sums below 1e-4 is between 12 and 32. 0.75 worked: the model gives 20
N3 The least real sum is between 5e-7 and 1.5e-5. 0.8 worked: under the model the chance is exp(−0.1) − exp(−3.0) = 0.855
N4 No sum, real or of a pair, is below 1e-6: the page's threshold is not met by any round that does not close. 0.8 worked: exp(−0.2) = 0.82 for the real ones; the pairs should add little
N5 The least sum of all is real. 0.85 guessed, from the first look and from N6
N6 The least sum of a pair is a product: its m is m₁m₂ with the orders of 2 modulo m₁ and m₂ coprime, and it is a real sum for m₁ that is smaller than itself, times a sum for m₂. 0.6 guessed from the one case seen
N7 The least sum of all belongs to a round of at most 200 steps. 0.7 guessed: nine of the eleven smallest at 20,000 do
N8 Control: for every m whose rounds close by the rule, the round of the coset of 1 comes to less than 1e-8. 0.9 worked: compensated sums of at most two million terms; 1.2e-12 at 20,000

These are about five separate bets: N1, N2, N3 and N4 all test the one model (N4 follows from N3's lower end but for the pairs), N5 and N6 go together, N7 and N8 stand alone.

Scoring. By small.c run once, ./small 2000000 400 0 10 > small.sealed-run.out. The thirty smallest sums are then recomputed with 40-digit arithmetic (verify.py); a figure that differs from the program's by more than 1e-9 is a fault of my instrument and will be reported as a failure of N8. A count within one of a band's end, or a least sum within 2% of one, is a tie.
