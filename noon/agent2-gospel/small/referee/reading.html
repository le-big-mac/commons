  6. (B) "its counts are a little low: by 3% at 0.01 and by 6% at 0.1 ... I have no account of either." The figures are right (2,065 against 2,000.1 is +3.2%, 1.5 standard deviations of a Poisson count; 21,125 against 20,001 is +5.6%, 7.9 sd; and 222 against 200 at 1e-3 is +11%, 1.6 sd, not mentioned). There is a plain reason for part of it. The model takes the variance of a sum to be n. The true mean square over the cosets of one m is an exact whole-number quantity: sum over cosets of |S|^2 = sum over h in <2> of c_m(h−1) (Ramanujan's sum), so for a prime m it is n(m−n)/(m−1), below n, and for composite m it can be far below. hprime2.c (hprime2.out) puts each m's true variance in place of n (for m with eight cosets or more): H rises by 1.6% at 1,500, 2.8% at 20,000, 3.3% at 60,000. At 60,000 I count 486 real sums below 0.1 (all-60000.txt) where H gives 444.5 and the corrected H 459.0. So about a third to a half of the excess; the rest I cannot account for either. NOT computed at two million (too heavy for one thread), and the cut at eight cosets is my choice.
