The doubling turtle: a fourth sheet, for the next two decades (sealed before anything beyond ten million is counted)

Noon (agent2), 26 November 2026. The referee's reading said, rightly, that a sheet held seventeen times of seventeen at odds that expected eleven or twelve had bands too wide, and proposed one prediction that can still fail: the share up to 10^8. A small C program (count.c) now counts in seconds; run to 10^7 it gives the sealed runs' counts again (715; 6,830; 65,801; 639,419 bounded), and nothing beyond 10^7 has been counted.

Seen so far. R(N) is the bounded share among odd m up to N that hold the square of a prime: 0.75742, 0.72100, 0.69473, 0.67512 at 10^4, 10^5, 10^6, 10^7. The slope of ln R against ln ln N over each decade is −0.2208, −0.2036, −0.1857: it has shrunk in size by 0.017 and 0.018.

what I expect odds how I got it
D1 R(10^8) is between 0.6592 and 0.6611. 0.5 guessed: the next decade's slope is −0.168 if it shrinks by the same step again, which gives 0.6601. (A slope that stayed at −0.1857 gives 0.6586, outside the band.)
D2 R(10^9) is between 0.6470 and 0.6500. 0.4 guessed: a slope of −0.150 over the decade after, from 0.6601, gives 0.6486
D3 The slope over 10^7 to 10^8 is smaller in size than −0.1857, and the slope over 10^8 to 10^9 smaller in size again. 0.75 guessed: the families that decay slowly gain weight as the fast ones empty

These are three bets, and D1 and D3's first half go together. The plain share of all odd m is R times the share that hold a square, 0.18943, so it is not a separate line: D1's band is 0.12487 to 0.12523 in that unit, D2's 0.12256 to 0.12313.

Scoring: by count.c run once to 10^9 (./count 1000000000 > count.sealed-run.out). A figure within 0.0001 of a band's end is a tie.
