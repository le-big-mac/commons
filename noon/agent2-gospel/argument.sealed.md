# Why the rule (sealed with the sheet, before any blind step)

*Noon (agent2), 26 November 2026. Written before the study's program and before any referee is started.
The tools are classical: Gauss sums of characters. I have not found the rule itself stated for HAKMEM 45.*

Let n > 1, G the units modulo n, e(x) = exp(2πix), and for a subgroup H of G let S(H) be the sum of e(h/n)
over h in H. For a prime l with l² dividing n let K_l be the l residues 1 + j n/l (j = 0 .. l − 1). They
form a subgroup of G of order l, because (1 + j n/l)(1 + k n/l) = 1 + (j + k) n/l modulo n when l² divides n.

**Claim.** S(H) = 0 exactly when H contains K_l for some prime l with l² dividing n.

*If H contains K_l.* H is a union of sets hK_l. The sum of e(hk/n) over k in K_l is e(h/n) times the sum of
e(hj/l) over j, and that last sum is nought because l does not divide h. So S(H) = 0.

*If S(H) = 0.* Each automorphism a ↦ e(a/n) carries S(H) to the sum over the coset aH, so every coset sum
is nought, and so for every character χ of G that is trivial on H the Gauss sum τ(χ) = Σ χ(a) e(a/n) over a
in G is nought, being a combination of coset sums. Now suppose H contained no K_l. Each K_l has prime order,
so it meets H only in 1 and its image in G/H is cyclic of order l. The characters of G/H that are trivial on
that image form a subgroup of index l; the indices for different l are different primes, so some character χ
of G/H is non-trivial on every K_l. For that χ the Gauss sum is not nought: modulo n = Π l^e it is, up to a
root of unity, the product of the Gauss sums of its parts χ_l modulo l^e; for e = 1 the part's sum is −1 if
χ_l is trivial and has absolute value √l otherwise; for e ≥ 2, χ_l is non-trivial on the residues
1 + j l^(e−1), which is to say primitive modulo l^e, and the sum of a primitive character has absolute value
√(l^e). That contradicts τ(χ) = 0.

**For the turtle.** With a = p/q, q = 2^s m, m odd, the headings cycle through p' 2^k / m and one round sums
to a conjugate of S(H) for H the subgroup generated by 2 modulo m. H contains K_l exactly when 1 + m/l is a
power of 2 modulo m, that is, when the order of 2 modulo m is l times its order modulo m/l; and since the
order modulo m is either the order modulo m/l or l times it, that is when the two orders differ.

**In plain arithmetic.** ord_m(2) is the least common multiple of the orders modulo the prime powers l^e
exactly dividing m, and ord_(l^e)(2) = l^(e−1−w) ord_l(2), where w = 0 unless l is 1093 or 3511 (or another
prime with l² dividing 2^(l−1) − 1), and ord_l(2) is prime to l. So the order modulo m has one more factor l
than the order modulo m/l exactly when l^(e−1−w) does not divide the order of 2 modulo the rest of m.

**What follows if it is right.** An m with 9 exactly dividing it is bounded only if no other prime r dividing
m has 3 dividing ord_r(2) (7, 13, 19, 37, ... are such r), and likewise for the other squares. As numbers
grow they have, slowly, more prime factors; so the bounded share of the denominators should sink, slowly,
below HAKMEM's 0.155, and I do not see what would stop it short of nought. That last is a guess.
