Braids

Water let down a tilted bed of sand. No channel is drawn anywhere: the threads, the bars and the islands make themselves, and do not stay.

One line across the river, through time

Each upright strip is the river along the line marked by the two small ticks on the picture above, three quarters of the way down; a new strip is added every steps, the newest at the right. A thread that wanders draws a slanting band here; a thread that is abandoned ends; a new one begins from nothing.

A bed of sand {{NY}} cells long and {{NX}} across, tilted from left to right between two walls, with the same water poured into every cell at the left-hand edge. Each cell hands all its water to the three cells on its right, more of it where the way down is steeper. The water carries sand, and strong water carries far more than its share: the sand moved goes as the 2.5th power of the stream's strength. A little sand also slides into a stream from a higher cell beside it. Where no way is down, the water goes over the lowest lip all the same: there are no lakes. That is all of it. It is the model of A. B. Murray and C. Paola (1994), as I have it from two descriptions; I could not read their papers.

The river above was grown from rough sand for {{grown_steps}} steps before the page was made; of ten rivers grown the same way it was the one with most of its bed under water. It is not at rest and will not stay as it is. Left to itself for {{soak_minutes}} minutes on the machine it was made on (a million steps), it went from {{grown_streams}} threads on {{grown_wet}}% of the bed to {{soak_last_streams}} on {{soak_last_wet}}%. A longer run from almost the same bed had cut two deep valleys, with a few threads in each, by 4 million steps; other rivers of the same kind gathered sooner, and one had not at 2 million. Yours may do either. Begin again whenever you like.

Three things to try. Begin from smooth sand, and the water stays one sheet, as it did through {{smooth_last}} steps when I ran it: in this program the threads keep themselves going once they are started, but do not start from nothing. Stop the banks crumbling, and in time the threads cut down into a few deep channels (in two runs from this bed, about half a million steps and about nine hundred thousand). Let strong water move only its share of sand, and the river is gone within a few thousand steps.
