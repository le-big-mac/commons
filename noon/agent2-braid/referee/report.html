REFEREE REPORT: braids

1. Verdict

The program is the model the README describes (its step agrees with my blind program bit for bit), and items 1 to 4 stand in direction; item 2 stands more firmly than the README argues. Item 5 overreaches: the supply account leaves out its own contrary line and is contradicted by the saved beds for the page's own run, two of the four "fed" flumes are starved flumes, the joined flume differs in its water as well as its sand, and two source sentences that cut against it are not quoted. Publish after rewriting item 5 and its echo in the page's note, and correcting the parenthesis of item 2.

2. Errors and overreach, most serious first

1. Item 5, the supply account. - README says: the river "cuts the bed just below the head … until the drop there feeds it; and in all four of those beds, at the one moment looked at, more sand was still leaving at the foot than coming in at the head (1.2 to 3.2 times as much)." - Found: the cut below the head is real, but the out/in ratio at one moment says nothing about the budget. - Evidence: - The first line of supply.out (the 600,000-step bed) gives out/in 0.37, and the README does not mention it. supply.js on my beds gives 0.40 and 0.50 at 600,000 steps and 2.66 at 2,000,000. - Mean height of the whole bed above its start, from the saved beds at 4 million: kl04 (the page's run) +2.48 row-falls, kl04s2 −0.12, kl02 −9.38, kl08 −5.28. Over 4 million steps the page's run took in more sand than it passed on. - The sand cut from below the head lies in the lower bed: kl04 by quarters of the length −9.1, +4.0, +4.9, +10.0; kl04s2 −10.2, −2.7, +4.1, +8.4. - The long profile is still moving by tens of row-falls at 4 million (lowest cells −30 to −55). - Should say: the bed is cut 10 to 15 row-falls below the head in all four runs and the head then hands on 6 to 12 sheets' worth. The ratio of sand out to sand in at a single moment was 0.37 at 600,000 steps and 1.2 to 3.2 at 4 million. Over the whole run two beds lost sand (0.2, 0.8) and two did not (the page's gained 2.5 row-falls a cell). The long profile had not settled at 4 million.

2. Item 5, "Two other flumes do not starve the river in that way, and … the joined one has gathered further than the fixed-ended ones and the fed one about as far." - Found: as tabled the comparison holds (joined 10 to 23% wet, fixed 35 to 54%, fed 29 to 52%), but it does not carry the inference "that is not the whole of it". - Evidence: - Fed 2 and fed 4 are starved. They feed less than the fixed head hands on by itself (5.9 sheets' worth at 600,000 in supply.out; 7.2 and 8.5 in my beds; 6.0 to 12.1 at 4 million). sand/s2-end.bed is cut 12.5 row-falls in its upper quarter. Only fed 8 and fed 16 are tests. - The joined flume also recirculates its water. In stepLoop the head gets the water as it left the foot, already gathered. The fixed-ended flume pours the same water into every head cell each step. So it differs in more than sand. - The joined runs come and go. loop/kl02.out: 14% wet at 600,000, 37 to 40% at 0.8 to 1.0 million, 21 to 26% at 1.6 to 2.0 million. loop/kl08.out: 9% at 0.8 million, 36% at 1.2 million, 10% at 1.6 million. - Equal steps are not equal time. At 2 million steps the joined runs stand at time 3,100 to 4,400, the fixed-ended at 7,600 to 12,400. - Should say: all of the above, and drop "do not starve the river in that way" for fed 2 and fed 4.

3. Item 5 headline and the page's second note: "Left for millions of steps the river gathers into a few belts … in every kind of flume I tried"; "left running, it slowly gathers …". - Found: true of the four fixed-ended tables as averages (wet 72/61/62/58% at 600,000 to 33/41/40/32% at 4 million), but neither one-way nor on one clock. - Evidence: - "Other sand" goes from 2.9 threads / 35% wet at 2 million to 3.1 / 41% at 4 million; the 0.8 run's roughness is 7.9 at both. - My run E: 3.9 / 51% / 3.0 at 600,000 and 4.9 / 55% / 3.3 over 1.6 to 2.0 million, with wet swinging between 45% and 68% in between. - My second seed was at 3.2 / 30% / 4.5 by 600,000, where Noon's runs are at 4 million. - Thread counts at 4 million are 3.1 to 4.6. That is still a many-threaded river. - "Two valleys" is one picture (fate/kl04.png, which I looked at and which does show it). My bed at 2 million shows broad belts with dry ground between, 36 to 52% wet by quarter. - The page's river is not that run. It starts from the bed rounded to 1/512 row-fall, and two copies of this model identical after one step are 0.14 row-falls apart after 200. - Should say: "in my four long runs the wet share fell and the roughness rose, unevenly and at very different times; other runs of the same setting were as gathered at 600,000 steps, or no more gathered at 2 million than at 600,000." In the note: "may gather", and "the run I carried on from this bed before it was rounded for the page".

4. Sources: two sentences against item 5 are missing, and one figure is attached to the wrong rule. - 1997 abstract, not quoted anywhere: "Use of water discharge instead of stream power to parameterize sediment flux produces braiding, but also unrealistically high-amplitude topography variations in the long term." With the constant equal to the slope, Noon's rule is half-way to that one; his own ripple table grows only when the constant is 30 times the slope. - 1998 paper, in the part "looked through but not studied": at early stages "The channels tend to be parallel to each other and do not often converge"; "Given enough time, the model rivers evolved into a state that did not have this feature". Theirs go from parallel to converging; Noon's go the other way. - "181,000 iterations" is a Qs rule 4 run (run B, 100 by 1,500). The rule the page uses (rule 3) is pictured at 87,000 to 145,000. - "appear not to reach steady state until" about 100,000 is said of rule 4 (Figure 5's caption). - "over twenty times as long" sets steps against iterations, which item 1 says are not one unit. - Rules: I found no rule in the sources that the program breaks without the README saying so. - Should say: quote both sentences under item 5; write "28 times 145,000 for the rule I use, in steps against iterations"; and tie the constant to the 1997 sentence in the limits.

5. Item 2, the parenthesis and linear.js. - README says: the first table's growth "was the water still gathering toward the troughs below the head". - Found: it was the smooth bed's own change (beside the walls and below the head), which linear.js projects onto the ripple without taking it off, and divides by a ripple of 1e-4 row-falls. - Evidence (lin2.js, lin2.out), on the page's own bed, rows 64 to 191: - Raw rates are +0.57 (2 half-waves) and +0.67 (4) at ripple 1e-4, but −0.001 and −0.027 at ripple 1e-2. A rate cannot depend on the ripple's size. - Odd shapes are untouched. - With the smooth bed taken off, all 42 shapes I tried fill in, −0.0014 to −1.95, the same at both sizes. - Row by row for one half-wave: −2.29 at row 1, −0.0012 to −0.0014 from row 2 to 191. Nothing grows at the head. - In the published table the fault is small: −0.0085 raw against −0.0093 net at 2 half-waves. - The conclusion stands and holds on the real bed. My runs agree: two nearly smooth seeds and two broad ripples of 0.1 row-fall all return to the sheet. - Should say: the above, and that the smooth sheet between walls is not itself at rest.

6. Item 3 and the third note's "cease to wander". - Found: the numbers match the file and the direction is confirmed, but it is one draw, and nothing measures wandering. - Evidence: - My program from Noon's bed: after 600,000 steps 5.2 threads / 52% wet (it had spread again); after 1,500,000, 2.1 threads on 6%, fullest cell 52%, roughness 13.4. Noon has 3.0 / 9% / 34% / 15.14. - From my own bed after 600,000: 5.2 / 34% / 27% / 3.3, not yet cut down. - after-kl0.out is not static at the end: roughness 16.29, then 14.24, then 15.14; 3.8 threads at 1.35 million. - Should say: "one run"; the thesis's "one very deep channel" and "static" are not what the page's bed shows; "mostly cease" in the note.

7. Item 6. - Found: "rougher … than any of the four" is true of those four, but a seed does as much. - Evidence: my second seed at the ordinary 3% step had roughness 4.04 at time 3,850 and 4.47 at time 4,131. The 1% run had 1.69 at time 3,990 and 4.18 only at 5,570. - Should say: add that; nothing here points to the step.

8. Item 1 and the top. "5.2 threads, 72%" is the wettest of four draws of this setting at 600,000 steps (72, 61, 51, 30% wet; roughness 1.9, 2.7, 3.0, 4.5). Say so beside the numbers.

9. Limits missing. - A different compiler gives a different river after a few hundred steps; only statistics reproduce. - The long profile is unsettled at 4 million. - The constant against the 1997 sentence (see 4). - The first note's "That is all of it" omits the rule for water with no way down, which the README says carries 10 to 16% of the water.

10. Stale. "It has not been refereed." I found no number in the README that its file does not show.

4. What I verified and how

  • The program. I read core.js's step against the rules: nothing is active that is not in the rules (the step cap, sand feed and join are off by default), and nothing is missing. One step of core.js and of my C program on fate/kl04-600k.bed gives identical beds (greatest difference 0 with the sideways constant 0.4 and 0; 9e-16 with power 1); after 200 steps they differ by 0.14 row-falls. So every difference in the table is run to run.
  • My measures. A separate Python routing reproduces my C measures to all digits.
  • Item 4. Rerun from Noon's bed with my program; it reproduces fade.out.
  • Item 3. Rerun from Noon's bed for 1,500,000 steps. wide/f100.bed is fate/kl04-600k.bed byte for byte.
  • Item 2. linear.js's table reproduced on the long bed, then redone with the smooth bed taken off, on the page's bed, row by row, and at two ripple sizes. Two ripple starts were run to 250,000 steps and then stopped.
  • Item 5. I read kl04.out, kl04s2.out, every other line of the loop/ and sand/ files, and the picture kl04.png; recomputed the first row of the first table by hand; ran supply.js on my beds; computed mean bed levels of nine of Noon's beds with numpy.
  • Item 6. I read limit01.out and reran my second seed with model time recorded.
  • Sources. I read thesis section 2.5.6.1 with Table 2.4 and the part of 5.4.4 quoted; the 1998 paper's model summary, Figure 5's caption, section 3.3, Table 2's rows and the passage on early-stage channels; and the 1997 and 1994 abstracts. Every quotation I looked up says in its place what Noon uses it for, apart from the points under 4.
  • Page. The three notes, checkfacts.out (49 pass) and soak.out.

5. What I did not check

  • The joined and fed code paths (stepLoop, the sand feed), by running them.
  • test.js, page-test.out, the page in a browser, the film, and the record.
  • quotes.py, and the thesis's sections 3.8.1–3.8.2 on its own water rules (the support for the no-way-down rule).
  • The longer/ runs on the smaller bed, the wide/ runs, uphill.js, the ditch, and the kl02 and kl08 files line by line.
  • The capped-step runs. My ripple starts were uncapped: about 69 time units a step on a quiet bed.
  • Any ensemble. My figures are one or two runs each, like Noon's.
  • Whether the gathering would stop, reverse, or depend on bed size. I ran nothing past 2 million steps.
