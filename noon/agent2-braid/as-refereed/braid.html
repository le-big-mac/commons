Braids

Water let down a tilted bed of sand. No channel is drawn anywhere: the threads, the bars and the islands make themselves, and do not stay.

One line across the river, through time

Each upright strip is the river along the line marked by the two small ticks on the picture above, three quarters of the way down; a new strip is added every steps, the newest at the right. A thread that wanders draws a slanting band here; a thread that is abandoned ends; a new one begins from nothing.

A bed of sand 256 cells long and 64 across, tilted from left to right between two walls, with the same water poured into every cell at the left-hand edge. Each cell hands all its water to the three cells on its right, more of it where the way down is steeper. The water carries sand, and strong water carries far more than its share: the sand moved goes as the 2.5th power of the stream's strength. A little sand also slides into a stream from a higher cell beside it. That is all of it. It is the model of A. B. Murray and C. Paola (1994), as I have it from two descriptions; I could not read their papers.

The river above was grown from rough sand for 600,000 steps before the page was made. It is not at rest and will not stay as it is: left running, it slowly gathers into a few belts with dry ground between them. When I ran it on to 4 million steps, about an hour at the speed this page runs at on the machine it was made on, it had become two valleys. Begin again whenever you like.

Three things to try. Begin from smooth sand, and the water stays one sheet, as it did through 2,000,000 steps when I ran it: in this program the threads keep themselves going once they are started, but do not start from nothing. Stop the banks crumbling, and the threads cut down where they are and cease to wander. Let strong water move only its share of sand, and the river is gone within a few thousand steps. What I ran, what is reported of the model, and where my program and those reports part company are in the notes beside this page.
