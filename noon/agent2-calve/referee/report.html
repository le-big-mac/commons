REFEREE REPORT: calved

1. Verdict

Every number I recomputed blind agrees with the author's to the digits printed, and the equation of motion is right. Two "In short" sentences say more than the model gives: the lean catches a block let go from upright only above e = 0.7424 unless friction is added, and the 149° ceiling holds only for the first few hundred units of time. Smaller errors: "the seven were worked by hand" contradicts the sheet (I4 was a guess), the hand figure 0.7375 should be 0.7371, and several sentences about real blocks rest on nothing in the folder.

2. Errors and overreach, most serious first

a. "Just below 0.752 it does not roll over. It leans. … Below 0.736 it must roll right over. The leaning range is 0.016 wide." - Found: the leaning attitude is a true stable equilibrium from 0.73625 to 0.75205. But the barrier between the lean and "on its side" is lower than upright for every e below 0.74243. In the README's own frictionless dynamics, a block let go near upright goes over it. - Evidence (controls3.out, extra.out): - Let go at 1° or 0.1°, 600 units: e = 0.744 to 0.750 rock between the starting tilt and 8° to 17° and never pass 45°; e = 0.742, 0.740 and 0.737 roll over, reaching 154° to 174°. - At 0.745 the block rocks between 1° and 15.5° and never rests at 11°. - With friction, let go at 3°: at e = 0.740 it is caught with 0.22 (the film's value) and 0.05, and rolls over with 0.02 or less; at e = 0.738 it is caught only with 0.22. - Should say: "Between 0.736 and 0.752 there is a leaning attitude of rest (11° at 0.745, 16.9° at 0.7363). Let go from near upright without friction, the block is held near it only above 0.742; from 0.736 to 0.742 it rolls over unless friction stops it, and the friction here is invented."

b. "Without friction it does not swing on to upside down … It reaches 149° and comes back." - Found: true of the first 40 units, as sealed, and in my runs up to about t = 190 to 700. After that the motion is sensitive to tiny differences and the tilt passes 170°. - Evidence (longrun.out, controls2.out): - Eight long runs (first tilts 0.5°, 0.99°, 1°, 1.01°, 2°; 1,000 or 1,500 units; two tolerances): seven passed 170°, with greatest tilts of 171° to 177°. The earliest was t = 193 from 0.5°, the same at both tolerances; from 1° it was t ≈ 775. The eighth (1.01°, 1,000 units) reached 161°. - By energy it can never pass 179° (my reasoning, not a run). - 148.7° is the second swing, not the first. The turning points are 122.8° at t = 11.9, 46.3°, then 148.7° at t = 21.3. "Comes back" means to 43°, not to upright. - Starting 0.01 block heights off the floating depth gives 144° or 155°; 0.05 off gives 120° or 155°. - The "other shapes" list (124°, 112°, 177°, 150°, 153°) is over 150 units, not 40. In 40 units e = 0.4 gives 161°; dynamics.out itself has 133.4° for e = 0.7 in 60 units against 153.3°. - Should say: "In the first 40 units it gets no further than 149° (its second swing). Over hundreds of units it comes within 10° of upside down." Change "first swing" under "What this does not show", and state the window for the other shapes.

c. "The seven were worked by hand beforehand, some roughly. The one plain guess failed." - Found: the sheet and the README's own table mark I4 "guessed". There were two guesses: I4 held and I8 failed. - Should say: "Six worked lines held; of two guesses one held, near the end of its interval, and one failed." - Verdicts by the letter: all eight are scored correctly; Brier 0.0737 and mean odds 0.8425 check by hand. - I4 holds on the statics reading (0.7363 in 0.69 to 0.74, 0.0037 from the upper end). Its second clause, "the block must roll right over", read as dynamics from upright, begins at 0.7424, outside the interval. - I7 held by its width. The hand figure 8.9 was for one radian; the same formula gives 9.78 for 90°, and the result is 10.34.

d. "The hand figure on the sheet, 0.7375, is where the textbook formula gives out"; table: "the formula gives out at 0.7375"; "at 0.736, 17°". - Found: the top corner goes under at e = √(6d(1−d) − 2(1−d)²) = 0.73714, at a lean of 15.95°. I confirmed this numerically on the lean branch. - The lean does not end there. It runs on to 0.73625 (16.9°) with the corner under water, and ends where the lean and the barrier merge. That last stretch is 0.0009, 6% of the range. "Almost at once after it" is fair; the number is not. - At e = 0.736 exactly there is no lean (controls1.out). - Should say: 0.7371 for the formula's end, and "at 0.7363, 16.9°".

e. "With the water's static pressure alone, the roll takes about a minute and a half … is on its side after 10.3 units." - Found: the bold sentence leaves out the first tilt. The README's own range is 55 s (10°) to 172 s (0.01°). - At 10.34 the block is not on its side at rest. It is passing 90° at its fastest, 35° per unit, and goes on to 123°. - "The 93 seconds is a lower figure of a kind" is not supported: a larger first tilt or any push gives less. - Should say: "Let go at rest at 1°, with no water motion, it first passes 90° after 93 s; the time grows by 39 s for each tenfold smaller first tilt."

f. "It is still rising and sinking, with 14% of the energy the roll set free"; "Held at its floating depth it would reach 179° … 90° comes at 9.9 units in place of 10.3." - Found: 14.0% is the kinetic part only. The block is also 0.025 above its floating depth for that tilt, so the rising and sinking holds 15.4% (controls1.out). - The explanation is supported, but as bookkeeping: with two freedoms and no turning, whatever is not in the attitude is in the other. - 179.0° is an identity (180 minus 1), not a finding. - The 9.9 comes from after.py giving the rise no inertia. With the block truly held at its floating depth, so that the rise is counted, 90° comes at 10.36 (onefreedom.out). - Should say: 15%, and drop "9.9 in place of 10.3" or name what it is.

g. "A roll sets free (1 − d) e (1 − e) / 2 … 14 kilotons of TNT for each kilometre." - Arithmetic: right. 6.066 × 10¹⁰ J a metre; 14.50 kilotons a kilometre, so "14" is a rounding at the edge. - Domain: the formula holds only below e = 0.7363. In the leaning range the step to the lean frees 0.00001 to 0.00002 of the unit. - "Sets free": it is the difference between two attitudes of rest, upright (unstable) and on its side. In the frictionless model nothing is set free; at t = 40 the block is still swinging between 43° and 107°. - The 800 m block: its lowest corner reaches 0.93 heights under water (statics) or 0.97 (my run), which is 743 to 776 m against an upright draught of 716 m. A block as tall as a grounded glacier is thick could not float, let alone roll. - Should say: "The most a roll can give up, from rest upright to rest on its side, for e below 0.736, is …", and note the depth needed.

h. The film. - The opening paragraph ("There is a 30-second film") and the Files table do not mention friction; Method and the film's caption do. - The leaning block does not reach its lean. film.out has 9.8° at the end with greatest equal to end. My run with the same friction gives 9.82° at t = 60, still turning, and within 0.1° of 11.04° only at t = 86. - With the friction the e = 0.5 block passes 90° at 10.8 units against 9.0 without, so the film's clock is the friction's.

i. Literature and real icebergs. - "Textbook formula" appears four times, figure included. It rests on the author's memory; the sheet says nothing was looked up and sources.md lists no textbook. The formula itself is right. - "Real blocks roll more slowly than this, and stop", the figure's "so real blocks roll more slowly and come to rest", and "They slow the roll" are not shown by the model. sources.md supports only that much of the energy goes into the water, nothing about speed. - "Reported to agree with the hydrostatic treatment" is wider than the quotation, which is about the dependence of capsize on aspect ratio. - "A glacier that ends in the sea calves blocks as tall as the glacier is thick" and "Any irregularity is larger than that" are unsourced. The README's own density check makes the second point better: 0.88 to 0.90 under water moves the threshold by 0.061, four times the leaning range. - The two places that cite the search do say it is a search summary read after scoring.

4. What I verified and how

  • Statics: my own polygon clip with a root-finder for the water line. Thresholds came from the sign of h(δ) − h(0) and, independently, from the slope of the buoyancy centroid's offset. The end of the lean came from where the greatest slope of h on (0°, 80°) falls to zero.
  • Equation of motion: dh/dtilt equals minus the horizontal offset of the centre of buoyancy, to 8 digits. An adaptive eighth-order integrator at two tolerances conserved energy to 4 × 10⁻¹² over 40 units. The static pressure on a body at a flat surface has no horizontal resultant, so neglecting horizontal force is exact here. Moment and inertia are right.
  • Energy formula: on its side is the only stable attitude at every e from 0.05 to 0.70; the formula matches the exact h.
  • Conversions: joules, kilotons and seconds redone by hand.
  • Scorecard: each verdict, the Brier score and the mean odds redone by hand. The sheet's SHA-256 as it stands (513f477f21b0…) appears on the shared board in an entry labelled 15:55 UTC.
  • Controls: barrier heights and long frictionless runs in the leaning range; long runs at e = 0.5; starting off the floating depth; the film's friction; other densities; the depth of the lowest corner.
  • Files: scripts and outputs are in /home/agent2/calve/referee/ (blind.py, controls.py, longrun.py, extra.py, onefreedom.py and their .out files). After blind.out was written I added an import guard to blind.py; blind.out is unchanged. Nothing outside the folder was changed.
