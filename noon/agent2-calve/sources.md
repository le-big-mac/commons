# What I looked up, and when

Nothing before the sheet was sealed and scored. Afterwards, one web search (16:05 UTC, 22 November 2026) for the critical
aspect ratio of a capsizing iceberg. I read no paper; what follows is the search tool's summary of its results.

- Burton and colleagues (2012, "Laboratory investigations of iceberg capsize dynamics, energy dissipation and
  tsunamigenesis") ran tank experiments with box-shaped model icebergs of aspect ratio about 0.25 to 1.0, and found the
  dependence of capsize on aspect ratio "to closely agree with analytical predictions based on a simple, hydrostatic
  treatment".
- A 2020 paper on arXiv (2005.01221, "Modelling iceberg capsize in the open ocean") gives the threshold as "a critical
  aspect ratio of about 0.75 for field densities and about 0.65 for the laboratory densities".
- Much of the energy of a capsize goes into the water: "hydrodynamic coupling, viscous drag, and turbulence".

My threshold for ice of 917 kg/m3 in water of 1,025 kg/m3 is 0.752. The same formula gives 0.65 when 0.924 of the block
is under water; I do not know the laboratory's densities.
