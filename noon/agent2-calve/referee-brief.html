The model. A long block of ice with a rectangular cross-section, height H = 1 when upright and width e (so e = W/H), floating in water with the share d = 917/1025 of its area under water. Two dimensions. "Tilt" is the rotation from upright. Statics: for a given tilt, the water line is horizontal and placed so that the area below it is d times the rectangle's area; h(tilt) is the height of the rectangle's centre above the centroid of the submerged part. An attitude is stable if it is a local minimum of h over the tilt. Compute, your own way: 1. the e above which upright (tilt 0) is stable, and the e below which lying on its side (tilt 90 degrees) is stable; 2. the stable tilt strictly between 0 and 90 degrees at e = 0.745, if there is one; 3. the smallest e for which a stable tilt strictly between 0 and 80 degrees exists, and that tilt; 4. the "energy set free" at e = 0.5: e times (h at tilt 0 minus h at the stable attitude the block ends in); and the e, among 0.05, 0.06, ..., 0.70, at which it is greatest. Dynamics (units H = 1, g = 1, ice density 1): a rigid rectangle of mass M = e and moment of inertia M (e^2 + 1)/12 about its centre, with two freedoms: the height y of its centre above a flat water surface, and its tilt. Forces: weight M downward; buoyancy A/d upward, where A is the area of the rectangle below the surface, acting at the centroid of that area, so that its moment about the centre is (horizontal offset of that centroid from the centre) times A/d. No drag, no added mass. Start at rest, tilted 1 degree, at the height where buoyancy equals weight. Compute: 5. the time at which the tilt first reaches 90 degrees, for e = 0.5; 6. the greatest tilt reached in the first 40 units of time, for e = 0.5.
