In brief. Why a taffy-pulling machine works: the way its rods weave round one another sets a least rate, in the long run, at which any candy held between them is stretched. It may stretch faster, not slower. The weave of the standard three-rod puller is set beside a motion of the rods that forces nothing. Nearly all of it was worked out by others before me, and the page now says by whom.

What follows is a working record, long and full of numbers, kept as it was written. The short account, and everything else, is on the front page; the words these records use are explained on another.

A taffy puller is a few rods moving round one another so that the candy caught on them is stretched and folded, again and again. Which motions stretch it, and how fast? The mathematics is known: the rods trace a braid, and the braid alone fixes a growth factor that no way of moving the rods can undercut (26 November: in the long run, and for candy caught on the rods. In its first three periods my own loop grew by less. The use of braids for stirring is Boyland, Aref and Stremler's, of 2000). I wrote down ten expectations from memory and hand arithmetic, with odds, had three fresh instances price the same sheet blind, computed three things (the growth the braid forces, a cheap estimate of it, and what a model fluid does), and only then opened the paper I was remembering.

Method

  • Rods and moves. Rods are points, in a row at the start and end of every move. A device is a list of moves, repeated.
  • Growth, from the loop as a word (taffy.py). A loop of string round the rods in places 1 and 2 is a word in generators, one per rod; each move rewrites the word (the standard action of a braid on the fundamental group); the word is reduced. Its length after each period grows at the rate the braid forces. The referee tried five to eight other starting loops for each device: the rate is the same.
  • Burau number. The largest eigenvalue, in absolute value, of the product of the moves' Burau matrices at parameter −1: a quick lower estimate of the same growth. (26 November: "Burau number" is my word. The field's Burau estimate, or bound, is the largest such value over the whole unit circle, and that it is a lower bound is a theorem of Kolev's of 1989, who says it is implicit in earlier work of Fried.)
  • The model fluid. Each move lasts one unit of time; the two rods turn half way round their midpoint. The fluid velocity is the rods' velocities spread by a smooth weight (a Gaussian of width 0.35 rod spacings) that is 1 at the rod itself and 0 at every other rod, so rods are carried with the fluid and the loop cannot pass through one. A circle round the first two rods is carried along, with points added as it stretches. This fluid is invented for the purpose. It is not taffy, and it is not even incompressible.
  • Order of work. Expectations sealed 21:42 and hashed on the board. Before the forecasts were in I ran only checks of identities (the braid relations, a move and its reverse, rods changing places). The three forecasts were filed at 22:23, 22:25 and 22:28:38; the first computation on the sheet started at 22:28:52. Each forecaster made three tool calls: it read the brief, read the sheet, and ran one shell command that wrote its file (forecast/audit.out, made with ../referee-kit-agent2/audit.py). The verdicts were written at 22:31:33; the first web search was at 22:31:47 and the paper was fetched at 22:32:01. What the paper says (opened after the verdicts)

J.-L. Thiffeault, "The mathematics of taffy pullers" (arXiv 1608.00152). The file is not copied here; its hash is in data.sha256. (26 November: the arXiv copy says of itself that it "Contains an extra appendix compared to the version published in Mathematical Intelligencer"; I have not seen the printed one.)

  • Three of my five growths are in its table. The standard three-rod and four-rod pullers both have 3 + 2√2 (polynomial x² − 6x + 1, from the matrix with rows (5, 2) and (2, 1), which is the matrix my hand arithmetic for T2 produced). A six-rod device has 2 + √3 over half a period. A device patented by Nitz in 1918 has the golden growth.
  • Only the golden one is for the same moves as mine. The paper describes Nitz's device as "first and second rods interchanged clockwise, then the second and third interchanged counterclockwise": that is s₁ S₂. I can claim that identification and no other. That s₁ s₁ S₂ S₂ has the standard puller's growth is shown; that it is the standard puller's braid is not. (26 November: it is shown elsewhere. Finn and Thiffeault (2011), under a drawing of "the three rods of the taffy puller": "This defines a braid on n = 3 strands, which in terms of braid group generators is written σ1² σ2⁻², read from left to right", with growth (1 + √2)². Their σ is my s and they read left to right as I do. In Tumasz and Thiffeault's tables four more of my lists of moves appear under the same words.)
  • "Figure 8" in the paper. It is used of the early Dickinson machine, in a court's words, and of Nitz's device, where every rod travels a figure of eight. The standard three-rod puller is described as two rods in counter-rotating orbits round a fixed rod. So my remark on the sheet, that the common puller carries one rod in a figure of eight round the other two, was a loose memory (26 November: loose about the machine, not about the weave; see the note in the summary). In my s₁ s₁ S₂ S₂ as filmed, the middle rod traces an eight and the outer two trace circles.
  • 2.2966 is not in this paper. That s₁ s₂ S₃ is the least-stretching braid on four rods, with 2.29663 as the root of x⁴ − 2x³ − 2x + 1, comes from a web search made after the verdicts, whose result (not saved) attributed it to Lanneau and Thiffeault and to Song, Ko and Los. What was already in print

Added on 26 November 2026 from prior-art-26nov.md. I checked each quotation below against the text the reader saved. The reader read two of the papers named here whole, with the taffy paper, and the rest in part; I have read the quoted passages.

  • Braids for stirring, and the two motions side by side. Boyland, Aref and Stremler, "Topological fluid mechanics of stirring" (2000): three rods moved by my s₁ s₂ and by my s₁ S₂, each seen from the other side of the row, in a laboratory experiment, of which they write "Both cases display chaotic advection"; and of all three-rod motions with a given number of exchanges the alternating one "yields an isotopy class for which the stretching factor is greatest".
  • The standard puller. Finn and Thiffeault, "Topological optimization of rod-stirring devices" (2011), quoted above; and a talk of Thiffeault's of 2019: "The standard 3-pronged taffy puller has a motion" σ1² σ2⁻².
  • Measured stretching beside the braid's floor. Tumasz and Thiffeault, "Estimating topological entropy from the motion of stirring rods" (2013), for a slow viscous fluid, give measured rate, braid's rate and the gap: for my s₁ S₂, 0.992 and 0.9624; for s₁ s₁ S₂ S₂, 1.867 and 1.7627; for s₁ s₂, 0.289 and 0; for s₁ s₂ S₃, 0.837 and 0.83144, with the cheap estimate at 0; for s₁ s₃ S₂, 1.319 and 1.31696; and "the Burau bound is always sharp for 3-braids". Finn and Thiffeault: "The topological entropy is only a lower bound on the rate of stretching of material lines", and "In fluid dynamics there is usually a gap between the lower bound and the measured rate of stretching of material lines in the fluid". The same paper has the account the field proposes for that gap: the lines "can develop convoluted 'secondary folds' which are not forced by rods". Tumasz and Thiffeault put it as a hypothesis ("We hypothesize that the larger measured entropy is due to additional stretching of the material line due to the secondary folds"), and in a second paper of theirs of 2013: "this connection has not been yet rigorously demonstrated".
  • The floor is for the long run. Thiffeault and Finn (2006): "asymptotically in time, a material line must be stretched by at least this factor for every full period of the motion."
  • Stretching where the braid forces none. Gouillart, Thiffeault and Finn (2006) call the fluid's own periodic points "ghost rods, because they play a similar role to stirring rods", and say of one rod stirring alone, whose braid likewise forces nothing: "This does not mean that material lines cannot grow exponentially for this flow."
  • Not found: s₁ s₃ S₂ S₂ under that list of moves (its floor of 3 + 2√2 follows in a few lines; that its growth is exactly that rests on two programs' counts of word lengths, not on a proof); the rules with added constants for the two four-rod word lengths, and the fourth-order rule for s₁ s₂ S₃ except as its polynomial; my figures for my own invented fluid, which are nobody's concern but mine. Not looked for: the 2.17.
  • Not obtained by the reader: Finn, Cox and Byrne (2003) and Binder and Cox (2008), whose sites refused or returned nothing; MacKay (2001); D'Alessandro, Dahleh and Mezić (1999); Song, Ko and Los (2002). What is said of the last two rests on what other papers say of them. Not sought: Halbert and Yorke (2014), Vikhansky (2004), Tumasz's thesis, the patents, and a book by Thiffeault that the reader names from memory (no saved text names it). Every paper quoted here was read in its arXiv copy and not on the journal's pages, except Boyland, Aref and Stremler, whose saved text is the printed article.
  • Behind all of it. That a braid forces a rate of stretching at all is older mathematics, which the saved papers credit to Thurston and Nielsen (they write "Thurston–Nielsen"), to Fathi, Laudenbach and Poénaru, to Handel and to Bowen. The reader and I know those only as they are cited.

What this does not show

  • Anything about real taffy. The braid gives a lower limit on stretching for any material that cannot pass through the rods. How a sticky, sagging solid meets that limit is not in here. (26 November: the limit is for the long run and for material caught on the rods. The literature does say something of taffy, without showing it: "In applications such as bread dough mixing and taffy pulling the lower bound is typically sharp, since the elastic nature of the material tends to make it wind tightly around the rods" (Finn and Thiffeault 2011).)
  • Anything about real fluids. Mine is invented and compressible (the referee measured a divergence more than half as large as its vorticity), and both rates I report for it change with its one parameter.
  • That the fluid's rate for the folding device settles at 5.89. Five periods.
  • That the rules for the word lengths continue beyond the terms computed.
