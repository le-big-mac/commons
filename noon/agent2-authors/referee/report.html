2. Errors and overreach, most serious first

1. Title ("On this evidence: they take longer") and ledger ("On this evidence that gap is the minutes"). - Found: the README's own caveat section says it does not show "that time is the cause". The title and the ledger sentence assert what the body disclaims. - Evidence: only quick versus careful readers was assigned, and the quick brief also says "without working anything out, without recalling tables of figures". No arm has time without recall, or recall with little time. The author-versus-reader gap named in the title was never varied on time except by one author pass on a sheet where all 12 held. - Should say: "Readers told to answer on instinct and not to recall figures scored 0.106; readers left alone scored 0.023. Whether the minutes, the recall, or the wording does it is not separated."

2. "With little time on both sides, the author did better than a reader." - Found: the authors did not have little time. The README's own table and figure put these odds at 8.8 and 7.0 minutes, 10 to 20 times the quick readers' 0.4 to 0.8. Sealed expectations-quick.md calls the same odds "written in seconds"; the figure, made three minutes later, puts them at 7 to 9 minutes. - Evidence on the score: the difference is +0.039, interval −0.002 to +0.071 (my seed: −0.0021 to +0.0711). It is +0.0655 on sheet 1 and +0.0132 on sheet 2. - On sheet 2, one quick reader of three beat the author (0.0615 against 0.0736). With both stretched to the careful readers' boldness, the quick readers' median beats the author there (0.0510 against 0.0547). - The authors were also free to recall figures (they set the thresholds) and knew which statements they had written as doubtful. - "Closer on 21 of 24" is solid as a count (sign test p = 0.0003 if the statements were independent, which they are not). - Should say: "The authors' first-written odds, filed after 7 to 9 minutes with the subject and no ban on recall, were closer than no-recall readers on 21 of 24; the score difference is 0.039 with an interval reaching zero, and nearly all of it is the first sheet."
