2. Steps needing an extra line (no conclusion affected)

  1. Lemma 2, "Take two distinct elements of B and let ℓ be the level of their lowest differing digit": it should say distinct elements have distinct u, because w = d_{n−a} is a function of u. Also implicit: t ≤ p makes the digits legal, t ≤ q makes the w distinct.
  2. Lemma 2: the hypothesis "0 ∈ F" is never used.
  3. Lemma 3: A ≠ ∅ is implicit in Φ_q ∤ A; worth a word since (I1) is stated for nonempty sets.
  4. Lemma 3 (β), "≥ p^{e+1}·q ≥ p^e·(p + q)": uses pq ≥ p + q.
  5. Lemma 3, Case a = 1, "p^e divides q·α_ρ, hence α_ρ": uses p ≠ q.
  6. Lemma 0: A^ρ is defined in product coordinates and called "a subset of Z_{p^{n−1}q}"; E and F of it must be read through (I3) in those coordinates. This is consistent (I tested with the CRT identification).
  7. Lemma 4, branch 1 ∉ E: the three-way split of the nonempty A^ρ is there but compressed; spelling it out would help.

4. Stated more strongly than the evidence supports

  1. Remark 2, "exactly constrained by": the two conditions are necessary, not a characterisation. 1 of 18 allowed patterns is never realised at M = 12 and 18, and 7 of 54 at M = 24 (for example E = ∅, F = {0, 2} at M = 12). "Satisfy" would be accurate.
  2. Where the weight lies, which the note does not say: for patterns with 0 ∈ F and E ⊄ F that Lemma 2 does not cover, D⁺ = p^e q does occur, and only Lemma 4 excludes a counterexample there. - There is 1 such pattern at M = 18, 24, 50, 75, 80, 98; 9 at M = 54 and 250; 11 at M = 48; 55 at M = 162; 77 at M = 96. - At M = 18 and 24 (the only such M brute force reaches) no set of any size has such a pattern, so brute force confirms Lemma 4's role only by absence. - The recursion shows them occurring at M = 54, 250, 162, 96 (3, 3, 30, 21 patterns), with smallest sets 3 to 5 times p^e q, never p^e q. - For 0 ∉ F the margin is wide: the smallest set is at least 2.25 times D⁺ everywhere.
  3. Section 6, "(10 groups, 1,064 patterns)": the author's certs_pnq.out has 11 such lines totalling 1,072 patterns. Trivial.
  4. Remark 3, "sharp in most cases": by exact pattern it is 15 of 18 at n = 3, 76 of 110 at n = 4, 325 of 570 at n = 5. True, but the fraction falls with n.
  5. Section 6, "Lemma 0, except through everything that rests on it": now checked directly for n ≤ 3.
