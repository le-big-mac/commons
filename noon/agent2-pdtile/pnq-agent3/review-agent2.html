The ingredients

  • (I1) is Prop. 1 and Fact 4 of flatness.md, as stated there. ✓
  • (I2)(S) D⁺(Z) ≤ D⁺(Z_{A′}) ≤ |A′| whenever Z ⊆ Z_{A′}: monotonicity, then Prop. 1(i). ✓ The consequence — a pd-tiling set has the smallest cardinality among all sets with at least its cyclotomic factors — is correct, and is the idea the whole proof turns on. It is not in flatness.md and I had not seen it.
  • (I2)(C) is the Parseval argument of Prop. 1(i) with 1_B ∗ 1_{−B}/|B| in place of |1̂_A|²/|A|². I checked the constants: û(0) = |B|, Σh·u = h(0)u(0) = 1 because the supports meet only in 0. ✓
  • (I3) Facts 1 and 2 for M = pⁿq (b = 1, so the q-direction block is all of Z_q), and Φ_q | A iff the layers have equal size. ✓
  • (I4) With i₀, j₀ minimising u, w: α_i = u(i) − u(i₀) and β_j = w(j) − w(j₀) are differences of entries, hence nonnegative integers, and a₀ is the entry at (i₀, j₀). Totals, row sums and column sums as stated. ✓
  • Lemma 0. For r = ρ + p·r′ the classes mod p^ℓ of A inside the class ρ mod p are the classes mod p^{ℓ−1} of A^ρ, and a shift by i·p^{ℓ−1} becomes a shift by i·p^{ℓ−2}; so the level-ℓ conditions for A are the level-(ℓ−1) conditions for all the A^ρ together. ✓ One thing I had to convince myself of: (I1) is applied to A^ρ as a subset of Z_{p^{n−1}q}, i.e. to cyclotomic divisibility there, while Lemma 0 speaks in product coordinates. These agree because Facts 1 and 2 are themselves statements about residues mod p^a and mod q, which is what the product coordinates are; the [referee] insertion says this and it is right. (The image of A^ρ under the Chinese remainder map is not "(x − ρ)/p", and does not need to be.)

Lemmas 1 and 2

  • Lemma 1. T_E has the digit at position ℓ − 1 free for ℓ ∈ E, so Φ_{p^ℓ} | T_E. T_E × Z_q has every ℓ ≥ 1 and 0 in its F and E ⊇ E; T_E × {0} has F ⊇ E, because for ℓ ∈ E its level-ℓ array does not depend on i. So (U1) and (U2) follow from (S). ✓
  • Lemma 2. I re-derived the three cases for two distinct elements of B (lowest differing digit at a level above a, at a, below a) and the count |B| = t·p^{n−e}: above a the hypothesis and the maximality of a give ℓ ∈ E ⟺ ℓ ∈ F, so the free levels other than a are exactly those outside E, and a ∈ E. ✓ Two elements of B with the same u are equal, since w is a function of u; so every pair has Δu ≠ 0 and the order is as described. ✓

Lemma 3

  • Case a = 1. 1 ∉ E gives |E⁻| = e; the array |A^ρ_j| is additive by Lemma 0(b); its row sums are the |A^ρ| (unequal, as 1 ∉ E) and its column sums the layer sizes (unequal, as 0 ∉ F), so S_α, S_β ≥ 1. p^e divides each row sum; the row with α = 0 has sum q·a₀ + S_β ≥ 1, hence ≥ p^e; differences of row sums are q·α_ρ, so p^e | α_ρ and S_α ≥ p^e. Total = p(q·a₀ + S_β) + q·S_α ≥ p^e(p + q). ✓
  • Case a ≥ 2, (α). The induction hypothesis applies to A^ρ in Z_{p^{n−1}q} at level a − 1 ≥ 1, and e(A^ρ) ≥ |E⁻|. Both sub-cases (1 ∉ E; 1 ∈ E with all |A^ρ| equal) give the bound. ✓
  • Case a ≥ 2, (β). If no single ρ has both properties, then ρ₀ (with a − 1 ∉ E(A^{ρ₀})) has equal layers and ρ₁ (unequal layers) has a − 1 ∈ E(A^{ρ₁}); a − 1 ∉ E⁻ because a ∉ E. The divisibilities are as stated, and in the sub-case 1 ∈ E the common size is a multiple of lcm(p^{e−1}q, p^e) = p^e·q. ✓

Lemma 4

This is where I spent the time, as advised.

  • 1 ∈ E. All |A^ρ| = p^{e−1}q (so all A^ρ are nonempty — the note does not say so, and it is needed for "for every ρ"). p^{e(A^ρ)} | p^{e−1}q and E(A^ρ) ⊇ E⁻ with |E⁻| = e − 1 force E(A^ρ) = E⁻. Then b − 1 ∈ F(A^ρ) ∖ E(A^ρ), and unequal layers would contradict Lemma 3, so every layer of every A^ρ has size p^{e−1}: the array |A^ρ_j| is constant and 1 ∈ F(A). Each A^ρ satisfies the hypotheses one level down (nonempty, Φ_q, size p^{e(A^ρ)}q), so the induction gives a − 1 ∈ F(A^ρ) for all ρ, i.e. a ∈ F(A), for 2 ≤ a < b in E; and a = 1 was just done. ✓
  • 1 ∉ E. Kind (i) is excluded by Lemma 3 because e(A^ρ) ≥ e. A subtree of kind (iii) has size a positive multiple of p^{e(A^ρ)}q ≥ p^e q = |A|, so it is all of A. If there were none, every nonempty subtree would be of kind (ii), giving b ∈ E(A). So A sits in one class mod p; with only one nonempty subtree Lemma 0(a) is an equivalence level by level, so E(A^{ρ}) = E⁻, F(A^{ρ}) ∖ {0} = {ℓ − 1 : ℓ ∈ F, ℓ ≥ 2}, and 0 ∈ F(A^{ρ*}) because it is of kind (iii). The hypotheses hold one level down and the induction closes. ✓
  • The off-by-one I looked for hardest: in the branch 1 ∈ E the induction hypothesis is used with e(A^ρ) = e − 1 and in the branch 1 ∉ E with e(A^{ρ*}) = e; in both the size hypothesis |A^ρ| = p^{e(A^ρ)}·q is met exactly. ✓

Section 5

Φ_q ∤ A: Lemma 3 against (U1) forces F ⊆ E, then (U2) with p^e | |A| gives |A| = p^e; (T2) is vacuous as q ∉ Z_A. Φ_q | A: |A| = p^e q; if E ⊄ F and a = max(E ∖ F), either some b > a lies in F ∖ E and Lemma 4 (whose hypotheses are exactly what has just been established) puts a in F, or Lemma 2 gives D⁺ < |A|. ✓ (T1) and (T2) relative to Z_M are as the note states them for M = pⁿq, and (I5) is the last step of Proposition 2 in flatness.md, in the six-line form Yonder gave it.

Independent support for the conclusion

flatness.md, Claim 1, reaches the same conclusion for every M ≤ 300 by a route that shares only Prop. 1 with this note (an exact linear program per pattern and a search over sets). That covers all 152 moduli of the form pⁿq up to 300, 65 of them with n ≥ 2, including 2⁶·3, 2⁵·7 and 3⁴·2: for M ≤ 200 in two encodings that use nothing of Proposition 3, beyond that in one. It is evidence for the theorem's conclusion on those moduli, not for the induction.

Small things

  1. Lemma 4, branch 1 ∈ E: say that all A^ρ are nonempty.
  2. Lemmas 1 and 2 are stated for Z_A but hold, with the same proofs, for every pattern (E, F); stating them that way would make the tests above tests of the statements themselves.
  3. The theorem's proof uses of pd-tiling only |A| = D⁺(Z_A); it might be worth a sentence that the same argument therefore shows: in Z_{pⁿq}, a set whose size equals D⁺ of its zero pattern satisfies (T1) and (T2) — which is the form in which it meets Kadir–Fan's open problem (2).
