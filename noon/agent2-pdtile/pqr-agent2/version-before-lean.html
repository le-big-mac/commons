1. Statement

Theorem. Let p₁, p₂, p₃ be distinct primes and M = p₁p₂p₃. If A ⊂ Z_M pd-tiles Z_M, then A satisfies (T1) and (T2) relative to Z_M (as in flat/flatness.md, Section 2), and therefore tiles Z_M.

2. Notation and ingredients

Coordinates: Z_M ≅ Z_{p₁} × Z_{p₂} × Z_{p₃}. The order of an element is the product of the p_x over the coordinates x in which it is nonzero. Throughout, {x, y, z} = {1, 2, 3} in some order.

For a set A write a(i₁, i₂, i₃) ∈ {0, 1} for its indicator; N_x(i) for the number of elements of A whose x-coordinate is i; N_xy for the p_x × p_y matrix whose (i, j) entry is the number of elements with x-coordinate i and y-coordinate j; and, for c ∈ Z_{p_z}, the slice A_c for the p_x × p_y matrix of zeros and ones (i, j) ↦ a(…) with z-coordinate c. So N_xy = Σ_c A_c. A matrix is additive if it has the form u(i) + w(j).

E = E(A) = { x : Φ_{p_x} | A },   P = P(A) = { {x,y} : Φ_{p_x p_y} | A },   t = t(A) = [ Φ_M | A ].

So Z_A = {p_x : x ∈ E} ∪ {p_x p_y : {x,y} ∈ P} ∪ {M if t = 1}. For M = p₁p₂p₃, (T1) is |A| = Π_{x∈E} p_x, and (T2) is: if x, y ∈ E then {x, y} ∈ P, and if E = {1, 2, 3} then also t = 1.

(I1) [flat/flatness.md, Prop. 1.] For every nonempty A: D⁺(Z_A) ≤ |A|; A pd-tiles iff D⁺(Z_A) = |A|; and Π_{x∈E} p_x divides |A|. D⁺ is monotone in the pattern.

(I2) [pnq-agent3.md, (I2).] (S) If A′ is a set with Z_{A′} ⊇ Z, then D⁺(Z) ≤ |A′|. (C) If no two distinct elements of B ⊂ Z_M differ by an element whose order lies in Z, then D⁺(Z) ≤ M/|B|.

(I3) Divisibility in coordinates. (F1) x ∈ E iff N_x is constant. (F2) {x, y} ∈ P iff N_xy is additive. (F3) t = 1 iff a = f₁ + f₂ + f₃ with f_w a function not depending on the w-th coordinate; equivalently, iff for every c, c′ ∈ Z_{p_z} the matrix A_c − A_{c′} is additive. Proof. (F1), (F2): [referee] Facts 1 and 2 of flat/flatness.md are stated for a modulus with two prime factors; what is used here is the same argument in the product of three groups. Φ_{p_x} | A (respectively Φ_{p_xp_y} | A) says that the transform of a vanishes at the characters nontrivial in the x-coordinate only (respectively in the x- and y-coordinates only); these are the characters of Z_{p_x} (of Z_{p_x} × Z_{p_y}) of full order composed with the projection, so the condition is the one of Fact 1 (Fact 2) for the count function N_x (N_xy). (F3): Φ_M | A says that the transform of a vanishes at every character of order M, i.e. at every character nontrivial in all three coordinates. The characters trivial in the w-th coordinate span the functions not depending on that coordinate, so the condition is a ∈ V₁ + V₂ + V₃, V_w being that space. If a = f₁ + f₂ + f₃ then, with z = 3 say, A_c − A_{c′} = (f₁(j, c) − f₁(j, c′)) + (f₂(i, c) − f₂(i, c′)), which is additive; conversely if every A_c − A_0 = u_c(i) + w_c(j), then a(i, j, c) = a(i, j, 0) + u_c(i) + w_c(j) has the required form. ∎

(I4) Normal form [pnq-agent3.md, (I4)]. An additive p_x × p_y matrix of nonnegative integers can be written a₀ + α_i + β_j with integers a₀, α_i, β_j ≥ 0 and min α = min β = 0. With S_α = Σα_i, S_β = Σβ_j: its total is p_xp_y·a₀ + p_y·S_α + p_x·S_β; its row sums are p_y·a₀ + p_y·α_i + S_β; its column sums are p_x·a₀ + S_α + p_x·β_j. The row sums are all equal iff α ≡ 0; the column sums are all equal iff β ≡ 0.

(I5) Double centring. For a real p_x × p_y matrix X let X°(i, j) = X(i, j) − (1/p_y)Σ_{j′}X(i, j′) − (1/p_x)Σ_{i′}X(i′, j) + (1/(p_xp_y))Σ_{i′,j′}X(i′, j′). The map X ↦ X° is linear; X° has all row sums and all column sums equal to zero; and X° = 0 iff X is additive (if X° = 0 the formula exhibits X as additive; if X = u(i) + w(j) a direct computation gives X° = 0).

(I6) An additive matrix of zeros and ones is a union of full rows or a union of full columns (possibly empty or everything). Proof. Let X = u(i) + w(j) with entries in {0, 1}. If u is not constant and w is not constant, pick i, i′ with u(i) > u(i′) and j, j′ with w(j) > w(j′); the differences u(i) − u(i′) and w(j) − w(j′) are differences of entries, hence equal to 1, and X(i, j) − X(i′, j′) = 2, which is impossible. So u or w is constant, and X depends on one index only. ∎

(I7) (T1) and (T2) imply that A tiles Z_M (flat/flatness.md, end of the proof of Proposition 2).

3. Two upper bounds

Lemma 1 (subgroups). Let U ⊆ {1, 2, 3} be nonempty. If every d ∈ Z_A is divisible by p_x for some x ∈ U, then D⁺(Z_A) ≤ Π_{x∈U} p_x.

Proof. Let H_U be the subgroup of elements whose coordinates outside U are zero; it has Π_{x∈U} p_x elements. The transform of its indicator vanishes at a character unless the character is trivial on H_U, that is, unless the character is trivial in every coordinate of U. A character of order d is trivial in every coordinate of U iff d is divisible by no p_x with x ∈ U. So Z_{H_U} = {d | M, d > 1 : p_x | d for some x ∈ U} ⊇ Z_A, and (S) applies. ∎

Lemma 2 (a clique). If none of p_xp_y, p_z, M lies in Z_A, then D⁺(Z_A) ≤ max(p_x, p_y).

Proof. Let m = min(p_x, p_y) and B = {b_{i,c} : 0 ≤ i < m, c ∈ Z_{p_z}}, where b_{i,c} has x-coordinate i, y-coordinate i and z-coordinate c. These are m·p_z distinct elements. Two of them with the same i differ only in the z-coordinate: order p_z. Two with different i and the same c differ in the x- and y-coordinates (as 0 < |i − i′| < m) and not in z: order p_xp_y. Two with different i and different c differ in all three: order M. None of these orders is in Z_A, so (C) gives D⁺(Z_A) ≤ M/(m·p_z) = max(p_x, p_y). ∎

4. Counting lemmas

Lemma 3 (two coordinates). If {x, y} ∈ P, x ∉ E and y ∉ E, then in the normal form N_xy = a₀ + α_i + β_j we have S_α ≥ 1 and S_β ≥ 1; so |A| = p_xp_y·a₀ + p_y·S_α + p_x·S_β ≥ p_x + p_y, and |A| ≥ p_xp_y + p_x + p_y if a₀ ≥ 1. Also N_x(i) = p_y·a₀ + p_y·α_i + S_β and N_y(j) = p_x·a₀ + S_α + p_x·β_j.

Proof. N_xy is additive by (F2); its row sums are the N_x(i), not all equal by (F1), so α ≢ 0 by (I4); likewise β ≢ 0. ∎

Lemma 4 (slices). Suppose t = 1. (a) The matrices (A_c)°, c ∈ Z_{p_z}, are all equal; call the common value T. Then (N_xy)° = p_z·T. (b) If moreover {x, y} ∈ P, or if some slice A_c is empty, then T = 0 and every slice is a union of full lines in the x-direction or a union of full lines in the y-direction (which of the two may depend on the slice). (c) [referee] If some slice is empty then {x, y} ∈ P: T = 0 gives (N_xy)° = 0, so N_xy is additive.

Proof. (a) By (F3) each A_c − A_{c′} is additive, so (A_c)° − (A_{c′})° = (A_c − A_{c′})° = 0 by (I5); and N_xy = Σ_c A_c. (b) If {x, y} ∈ P then N_xy is additive, so p_z·T = (N_xy)° = 0. If a slice is empty, T is the double-centred part of the zero matrix. In either case every slice has (A_c)° = 0, so it is additive, and (I6) applies. ∎

Lemma 5. If |A| = p_xp_y, x ∈ E, y ∈ E and t = 1, then {x, y} ∈ P. ([referee] For sets the hypothesis |A| = p_xp_y is not needed: this is G1 in Section 5. It is the form stated here that the theorem uses.)

Proof. By (F1) every row sum of N_xy is |A|/p_x = p_y and every column sum is p_x, so (N_xy)° = N_xy − 1 (the row means, the column means and the overall mean are all 1). By Lemma 4(a), N_xy − 1 = p_z·T. Fix a slice A_c; from the formula for (A_c)°, p_xp_y·T(i, j) is an integer. Also p_z·T(i, j) = N_xy(i, j) − 1 is an integer. As p_z is prime to p_xp_y, T(i, j) is an integer. It is at least −1/p_z, because N_xy ≥ 0; so T(i, j) ≥ 0. But the row sums of T are zero. Hence T = 0, N_xy is the all-ones matrix, which is additive, and {x, y} ∈ P by (F2). ∎

Lemma 6 (patterns that no set has). (Both parts were found independently by the referee pass and by Yonder — G2–G4 and Facts V, W in their reports — after reading a first version of Section 5 that did not have them; the proofs below are theirs in substance and I have checked them.) (a) If {x, y} ∈ P, {y, z} ∈ P and y ∉ E, then x ∈ E and z ∈ E. (b) If x ∈ E, {y, z} ∈ P and t = 1, then y ∈ E or z ∈ E.

Proof. (a) Write N_xy = a₀ + α_i + β_j (rows indexed by the x-coordinate, columns by the y-coordinate) and N_yz = b₀ + α′_j + β′_k (rows by y, columns by z) in normal form. The column sums of the first and the row sums of the second are both N_y(j): p_x·a₀ + S_α + p_x·β_j = p_z·b₀ + p_z·α′_j + S_β′ for every j. So p_x·β_j − p_z·α′_j does not depend on j; at a j with β_j = 0 it is ≤ 0 and at a j with α′_j = 0 it is ≥ 0; hence it is 0, and p_x·β_j = p_z·α′_j for all j. So p_z divides every β_j and p_x divides every α′_j. As y ∉ E, the N_y(j) are not all equal, so β ≢ 0 and some β_j ≥ p_z, and for that j also α′_j = p_xβ_j/p_z ≥ p_x. Now A is a set, so at most p_z of its elements share an x- and a y-coordinate: N_xy(i, j) ≤ p_z. With β_j ≥ p_z this forces a₀ = 0 and α_i = 0 for every i; so the row sums of N_xy are equal and x ∈ E. Likewise N_yz(j, k) ≤ p_x with α′_j ≥ p_x forces β′ ≡ 0, so the column sums of N_yz are equal and z ∈ E. (b) Slice along the x-coordinate. All slices have the same number s = |A|/p_x of elements, because x ∈ E, and by Lemma 4(b) each is a union of full lines in the y-direction or a union of full lines in the z-direction. If some slice is a union of y-lines and some slice a union of z-lines, then s is a multiple of p_y and of p_z; as 0 < s ≤ p_yp_z, s = p_yp_z, A = Z_M and y, z ∈ E. Otherwise all slices are unions of lines in one direction, say the y-direction: then in every slice each value of the y-coordinate occurs equally often, so N_y is constant and y ∈ E. ∎

5. Proof of the theorem

Let A pd-tile Z_M, n = |A|, and E, P, t as above. By (I1), n = D⁺(Z_A) and Π_{x∈E} p_x divides n.

|E| = 3. M divides n ≤ M, so A = Z_M, which satisfies (T1) and (T2).

E = {x, y}. Every divisor of M other than 1 and p_z is divisible by p_x or p_y, and p_z ∉ Z_A. By Lemma 1 with U = {x, y}, n ≤ p_xp_y; as p_xp_y divides n, n = p_xp_y, which is (T1). (T2) is {x, y} ∈ P. Suppose {x, y} ∉ P. If t = 0, none of p_xp_y, p_z, M is in Z_A, and Lemma 2 gives n ≤ max(p_x, p_y) < p_xp_y. If t = 1, Lemma 5 gives {x, y} ∈ P. Contradiction either way.

E = {x}. (T2) is empty and (T1) is n = p_x. If {y, z} ∉ P, every element of Z_A is divisible by p_x, so n ≤ p_x by Lemma 1 with U = {x}, and p_x | n: n = p_x. If {y, z} ∈ P: then {x, y} ∉ P, since otherwise Lemma 6(a), applied to the pairs {x, y}, {y, z} with y ∉ E, would give z ∈ E; likewise {x, z} ∉ P; and t = 0 by Lemma 6(b). So Z_A = {p_x, p_yp_z}. Lemma 2 applies to the pair {x, y} (none of p_xp_y, p_z, M is in Z_A) and, with y and z exchanged, to the pair {x, z}: n ≤ max(p_x, p_y) and n ≤ max(p_x, p_z). If p_x > p_y or p_x > p_z this gives n ≤ p_x, so n = p_x. Otherwise n ≤ min(p_y, p_z), while Lemma 3 for the pair {y, z} gives n ≥ p_y + p_z: contradiction.

E = ∅. (T2) is empty and (T1) is n = 1. For the pd-tiling set A this is the same as Z_A = ∅: a one-element set has no cyclotomic factor, and if Z_A = ∅ then n = D⁺(∅) = 1. Suppose Z_A ≠ ∅. - P has at least two pairs. Two of them share an index y, and y ∉ E, so Lemma 6(a) puts the other two indices in E: contradiction. - P = {{x, y}}. Every element of Z_A (that is p_xp_y, and M if t = 1) is divisible by p_x, so n ≤ p_x by Lemma 1. Lemma 3 gives n ≥ p_x + p_y: contradiction. - P = ∅. Then Z_A = {M} and t = 1. Let p_x be the smallest of the three primes and p_z the largest. By Lemma 1 with U = {x}, n ≤ p_x < p_z, so some slice along the z-coordinate is empty, and by Lemma 4(c) {x, y} ∈ P: contradiction. So Z_A = ∅.

In every case (T1) and (T2) hold, and by (I7) A tiles Z_M. ∎

How this section came to be this short. The first version of Section 5 (pqr-agent2/first-version.md; with the referee's corrections, pqr-agent2/first-version-with-referee-notes.md) did not have Lemma 6. It split E = {x} into five sub-cases and E = ∅ into four and reached a contradiction in each by an inequality between the bounds of Lemmas 1–3. Both readers found those arguments correct, and both observed, independently, that four of the sub-cases contain no set of any size, so that the inequalities there were true statements about nothing. Lemma 6 says so directly. A further fact of the same kind, due to the referee pass (its G1): for sets, Lemma 5 holds without the hypothesis |A| = p_xp_y — the mean of N_xy is the integer k = |A|/(p_xp_y), N_xy − k = p_z·T with T integer-valued, and 0 ≤ N_xy ≤ p_z with 0 < k < p_z (unless A = Z_M) puts T strictly between −1 and 1. The theorem does not need it.

Remarks.

  • As in pnq-agent3.md, the proof uses of pd-tiling only n = D⁺(Z_A), and of D⁺ only the bounds (S) and (C); of the set A it uses only its counts and, in Lemma 6(a), that no point is counted twice. So it shows: in Z_{pqr}, a set whose size equals D⁺ of its zero pattern satisfies (T1) and (T2).
  • Lemmas 3, 4 and 5 use nothing about D⁺. Lemma 5 in particular is a statement about sets, and with the hypothesis |A| = p_xp_y also about multisets: |A| = p_xp_y with Φ_{p_x}, Φ_{p_y} and Φ_M forces Φ_{p_xp_y}.
  • The argument does not extend as it stands to four primes or to repeated primes: Lemma 4 depends on the slices being two-dimensional, where "additive" has the simple description (I6).

6. What was checked, and by whom it has been read

By me. - Solver tests of the counting lemmas (pqr-agent2/lemma_tests.py; CP-SAT on the plain counts model of flat/sat.py, which uses nothing of this note). Seven triples — (2,3,5), (2,3,7), (2,5,7), (3,5,7), (2,3,11), (2,7,11), (3,5,11) — and every assignment of the roles x, y, z; 210 questions. 140 are statements of impossibility taken from the first version of the note (Lemma 5; the bound of Lemma 3; the sub-cases of E = {x} with t = 1 and with a second pair; Z_A = {M}; three pairs) and all came back infeasible. 70 are controls and all came back with a set: Lemma 5 without Φ_M; Lemma 3 at size exactly p_x + p_y; the last sub-case of E = {x} at size exactly p_x·min(p_y, p_z); Z_A ∋ M at size exactly the smallest prime. The second-pair and three-pair questions have no controls of their own (by G3 and G4 none of the relaxed-size kind could exist). A solver's "infeasible" is not a certificate, and seven triples are not all triples. - The conclusion on small moduli. flat/flatness.md, Claim 1, has the theorem's conclusion, by a route that shares only Proposition 1 with this note and in an encoding that does not use Proposition 3, for all 32 moduli of the form pqr up to 300.

By the referee pass (a fresh instance, its own code, none of mine read; its report, unedited, and its scripts and outputs are in pqr-agent2/referee/): - every step of Sections 2–5 re-derived; the case division tabulated over all 128 patterns (E, P, t), each falling in exactly one bullet; every application of Lemmas 1–5 checked for the right primes in the right places and for every ordering of the three primes; - M = 30, all 536,870,912 subsets containing 0 (a C program): (F1)–(F3) against exact division; the normal form; Lemma 3 (10,288,290 sets meet its premise); Lemma 4(a) (220,281) and its converse; Lemma 4(b) (70,374); (I6); Lemma 5 (131); G1, G2; no failure. The theorem itself: exactly 33 (pattern, size) classes have |A| = D⁺(Z_A), comprising 40,605 sets, all satisfying (T1) and (T2), and a brute-force search for complements finds exactly those 40,605 sets as the tiles of Z_30; - M = 42, all 2⁴² − 1 nonempty subsets (meet in the middle with exact keys, validated against the M = 30 program): 33 classes with |A| = D⁺, comprising 7,280,934 sets, all satisfying (T1) and (T2); brute-force tiling on 154,922,882 sets agrees; Lemmas 3, 4, 5 and (I6) on all sets meeting their premises, no failure; - Lemmas 1 and 2 against exact D⁺ (its own rational simplex) for all 165 triples of primes up to 31: 60,720 and 7,920 instances, no failure; and, mechanically for those 165 triples, that the bound each bullet of Section 5 invokes closes for all 128 patterns; - its own CP-SAT model on further triples up to (5,7,11): every decided question as expected, a handful undecided at the time limit, none returning an unexpected set.

Not checked: the hand proofs for general primes by anyone who is not an instance of the model that wrote them; the literature. The exhaustive checks cover two moduli; the theorem is about all of them.
