0. What is new here

A set A ⊂ Z_M tiles its complement weakly (Lev–Matolcsi; for Z_M, [KLMS] §1.2) if there is f ≥ 0 with f(0) = 1 and 1_A ∗ f = 1. It pd-tiles if in addition f̂ ≥ 0. So tile ⇒ pd-tile ⇒ weak tile, and spectral ⇒ pd-tile. Kadir and Fan's diagram (arXiv:2607.02149, Figure 1) puts a question mark on "weak tile ⇒ tile" in cyclic groups. flat/flatness.md is about pd-tiling. Everything in it has a weak-tiling counterpart, which is at least as natural and in one respect simpler:

  1. A weakly tiles iff D^w(Z_A) = |A|, where D^w is the Delsarte value without the sign constraint on h (Section 1). The bounds (S) and (C) hold for D^w, so the proofs for Z_{pqr} (pqr-agent2.md; checked in Lean in this form, lean-pqr/) and for Z_{pⁿq} (pnq-agent3.md, its Section 8) are proofs about weak tiles.
  2. Duality: D^w(Z) is the least mass of a nonnegative function G with G(0) ≥ 1 whose transform vanishes on Z. So a set is a weak tile iff nothing nonnegative with at least its Fourier zeros is lighter than it (Section 2).
  3. A counting certificate (Section 3; checked in Lean for every M): A is a weak tile of Z_M iff some real combination Σ_{d|M} μ_d·#{a ∈ A : a ≡ v mod d} of its residue-class counts is identically 1, where Σ_{d|g} μ_d ≥ 0 for every g | M and Σ_{d|M} μ_d = 1.
  4. D^w and D⁺ are different functions of the pattern (Section 4): they differ on 101,586 of the 1,360,968 patterns with M ≤ 300, at 63 moduli — every modulus with three or more prime factors, and 72, 144, 200, 216, 288.
  5. Claim 1w (computer-assisted): for every M ≤ 300 and seven larger moduli up to 2025, every weak tile of Z_M satisfies (T1) and (T2) and therefore tiles (Section 5). So for M ≤ 300 the tiles, the pd-tiles and the weak tiles are the same sets, and I found no set, at any M ≤ 300, that is a weak tile without being a pd-tile.

1. The criterion

Notation as in flat/flatness.md, Section 2: classes of frequencies by order d | M; Z_A = {d | M, d > 1 : Φ_d | A(X)}; step functions h on Z_M (constant on classes). For a set Z of orders d > 1 let

D⁺(Z) = max { Σ_ξ h(ξ) : h step, h(0) = 1, h = 0 off {0} ∪ Z, ĥ ≥ 0, h ≥ 0 }      (the Delsarte parameter of [KLMS])
D^w(Z) = max { Σ_ξ h(ξ) : h step, h(0) = 1, h = 0 off {0} ∪ Z, ĥ ≥ 0 }              (h of either sign).

Clearly D⁺ ≤ D^w, and both are monotone in Z.

Proposition W1. For every nonempty A ⊂ Z_M: (i) D^w(Z_A) ≤ |A|; (ii) A tiles its complement weakly iff D^w(Z_A) = |A|; (iii) the bounds (S) and (C) of pnq-agent3.md, (I2), hold with D^w in place of D⁺.

Proof. (i) For admissible h, Σ_ξ h(ξ)|1̂_A(ξ)|² = h(0)|A|² = |A|², because 1̂_A vanishes on the classes of Z_A; and Σ_ξ h(ξ)|1̂A(ξ)|² = Σ{a,a′∈A} ĥ(a − a′) ≥ |A|·ĥ(0) = |A|·Σh, using only ĥ ≥ 0. (ii) If D^w(Z_A) = |A| with optimal h, then f = ĥ/|A| is ≥ 0, f(0) = Σh/|A| = 1, and f̂ = (M/|A|)·h(−·) is supported on {0} ∪ Z_A with f̂(0) = M/|A|, so 1̂_A·f̂ = M·δ₀ and 1_A ∗ f = 1. Conversely let f be a weak complement. Then 1̂_A·f̂ = M·δ₀, so f̂ is supported on {0} and on zeros of 1̂_A, and the zero set of 1̂_A is a union of classes (Galois). Averaging f over the dilations x ↦ ux, u a unit, keeps all three properties and makes f̂ a real step function; h = (|A|/M)·f̂ is then admissible with Σh = ĥ(0) = |A|·f(0) = |A|. (iii) (S) is (i) with monotonicity. The proof of (C) in pnq-agent3.md uses ĥ ≥ 0 and |1̂_B|² ≥ 0 only. ∎

So wherever a proof uses pd-tiling only through "|A| = D⁺(Z_A)" and D⁺ only through upper bounds of the kinds (S) and (C), it proves the same statement for weak tiles. That is the case for both theorems in this folder.

2. Duality

Proposition W2. D^w(Z) = min { Σ_x G(x) : G ≥ 0, G(0) ≥ 1, Ĝ = 0 on the classes of Z }, and D⁺(Z) = min { Σ_x G(x) : G ≥ 0 a step function, G(0) ≥ 1, Ĝ ≤ 0 on the classes of Z }.

Proof. Weak duality: for h admissible for D^w and G as stated, Σ_x G(x)ĥ(x) = Σ_ξ h(ξ)Ĝ(ξ) = h(0)Ĝ(0) = ΣG, since Ĝ vanishes where h may be nonzero apart from 0; and Σ_x G(x)ĥ(x) ≥ G(0)ĥ(0) ≥ Σh. Equality of the optima is strong duality for a finite linear programme that is feasible and bounded (the minimum may be taken over step functions, by averaging). For D⁺ the multipliers of the constraints h ≥ 0 relax "Ĝ = 0" to "Ĝ ≤ 0". ∎ (Numerical check: both formulas against the primal values, exact rational on one side and floating point on the other, for 476 random patterns at eight moduli up to 120; no mismatch. ~/weak/dualcheck.py.)

Corollary. A is a weak tile iff every G ≥ 0 with G(0) ≥ 1 whose transform vanishes on Z_A has ΣG ≥ |A|. The indicator of any set A′ ∋ 0 with Z_{A′} ⊇ Z_A is such a G: this is (S), "a weak tile is a smallest set with its zeros". The bound (C) and the clique of pqr-agent2.md, Lemma 2, are also of this form: in the Lean proof the clique is replaced by the function D ⊗ δ₀, D a p_x × p_y matrix with D(0,0) = 1 and constant row and column sums.

3. A counting certificate (checked in Lean)

Theorem W3 (lean-pqr/Pqr/Counting.lean, weakTiling_iff_countingCert; any M ≥ 1). A ⊆ Z_M has a weak tiling complement iff there are real numbers μ_d, d | M, with

(i) Σ_{d | g} μ_d ≥ 0 for every g | M,     (ii) Σ_{d | M} μ_d = 1,     (iii) Σ_{d | M} μ_d · #{a ∈ A : a ≡ v mod d} = 1 for every v ∈ Z_M.

Proof. (⇐) f(x) = Σ_{d | gcd(x, M)} μ_d. (⇒) Average a weak complement over the units; it becomes a function of gcd(x, M); write it as a combination of the indicators of the subgroups dZ_M by Möbius inversion. ∎

The statement has no function, character or polynomial in it. A set that tiles with a subgroup complement dZ_M has the certificate μ_d = 1. (For pd-tiling add: Σ_{d : (M/d) | g} μ_d/d ≥ 0 for every g | M.)

3a. Three more general statements (checked in Lean, any M)

In lean-tiling/Pqr/ (SCBounds.lean, DigitSets.lean, CM.lean), with ZeroAt A d meaning that 1̂_A vanishes at the frequencies of order d (equivalently Φ_d | A(X): Cyclotomic.lean):

  • (S) and (C) with no Delsarte parameter. If A is a weak tile and A′ ≠ ∅ has at least the Fourier zeros of A, then |A| ≤ |A′| (S_bound). If no two elements of B differ by an element whose order is a zero of the weak tile A, then |A|·|B| ≤ M (C_bound).
  • Coven–Meyerowitz in Z_M (tiling_of_T1_T2): (T1) and (T2) relative to M imply that A tiles Z_M, the complement being the digit set of the prime powers of M that are not zeros of A.
  • No unsupported zero ⇒ (T1) (T1_of_no_orphan): if A is a weak tile and every zero d of A is exactly divisible by a prime power p^a that is itself a zero of A, then |A| = ∏{p^a ∈ S_A} p. (The competitor in (S) is the digit set of S_A.) Tiles can have zeros that are not of this kind: {5, 8, 9, 25, 28, 31, 41} ⊂ Z_42 tiles (one element in each class mod 7) and its zero set is {6, 7}. When in addition every prime of d divides |A|, such a zero is an unsupported cyclotomic divisor in the terminology of Tan and Zhang (arXiv:2609.06677), who show that genuine tiles have them from M = 180 on. So this statement is the easy half of (T1) and no more. (In Z{pⁿq} the counting lemmas of pnq-agent3.md do show that a weak tile has no zero of this kind.) Corollary (weak_tiling_tiles_prime_pow): every weak tile of Z_{pⁿ} is a tile, which is [KLMS, Prop. 4.1] without positive definiteness.

4. Where D^w and D⁺ differ

Exact rational values of both, for every pattern (~/weak/wscan.py, weaklp.py; D⁺ from the cache of flat/scan.py):

range patterns patterns with D^w > D⁺ moduli where they differ
2 ≤ M ≤ 300 1,360,968 101,586 63: all 58 moduli with at least three prime factors, and 72, 144, 200, 216, 288
324, 400, 432, 576, 648, 784, 2025 2,162,688 64,659 6: all but 324 = 2²·3⁴

The first difference is at M = 30: D⁺({2,3,5,30}) = 120/19 and D^w = 15/2; D⁺({2,6,10,15}) = 4 and D^w = 19/4. Among moduli with two prime factors, D^w = D⁺ on every pattern for every pⁿq and every p²q² up to 300, and also for 108 = 2²·3³; they differ for 72 = 2³·3², 144, 200 = 2³·5², 216, 288. (Yonder's independent linear programme, one variable per divisor, agrees on 4,824 patterns at 21 moduli pⁿq and on the values at 30 and 72.) With the seven larger moduli the picture among two-prime moduli p^a q^b, p < q, is, in the data: D^w = D⁺ on every pattern whenever b = 1 or a = 1 (the families pⁿq), and whenever a ≤ 2 (36, 100, 108, 196, 225, 324); they differ at every computed modulus with a ≥ 3 and b ≥ 2 (72, 144, 200, 216, 288, 400, 432, 576, 648, 784, 2025). So it is the exponent of the smaller prime that matters: 72 = 2³·3² differs and 108 = 2²·3³ does not. I have no explanation.

D^w gives a better lower bound on the size of a set with given zeros than D⁺ does: no set of 4 elements in Z_30 has Φ_2, Φ_6, Φ_10, Φ_15 among its factors, although D⁺ = 4.

5. Claim 1w

Claim 1w (computer-assisted, not certified). For 2 ≤ M ≤ 300, and for M = 324, 400, 432, 576, 648, 784, 2025, every set that tiles its complement weakly in Z_M satisfies (T1) and (T2) relative to Z_M, and therefore tiles Z_M.

Method. As in flat/flatness.md, Proposition 2, with D^w for D⁺. A is a weak tile iff Z_A contains a weak-minimal pattern (Z₀, n): D^w(Z₀) = n = |A| and every single removal lowers D^w. For each M all weak-minimal patterns are listed from the exact values, and classified from Z₀ alone as safe, impossible, T1-short or T2-open. A weak-minimal pattern with D⁺(Z₀) = n is also minimal for D⁺, and every set containing it with n elements is a pd-tile, so it is covered by Claim 1 of flat/flatness.md. The others are new; for each new pattern that is not safe and not impossible, a constraint solver looks for a set of n elements with Z_A ⊇ Z₀ that violates (T1) or (T2) (the plain counts model of flat/sat.py, which uses nothing of Proposition 3).

range weak-minimal patterns safe covered by Claim 1 new, impossible (k ∤ n) new, searched violating sets undecided longest search
2 ≤ M ≤ 300 21,994 3,137 10,129 2,294 1,380 0 0 23 s

(The remaining 5,054, respectively 10,722, are impossible patterns that are not new.) For 599 of the 1,380 new patterns with M ≤ 300, and 131 of the 1,316 at the seven larger moduli, some set of n elements does contain the pattern; none of these sets violates (T1) or (T2), so each is a tile and has a larger zero set on which D⁺ = n as well. So:

No set in Z_M, M ≤ 300, was found that is a weak tile and not a pd-tile, although the two Delsarte values differ on a tenth of all patterns. Whether that persists is a question I cannot answer.

Corollary (computer-assisted). For M ≤ 300: tile ⇔ pd-tile ⇔ weak tile ⇔ (T1) and (T2). In particular every spectral set in Z_M, M ≤ 300, tiles. (This last statement already follows from Claim 1, since spectral sets pd-tile. I have not checked which M ≤ 300 are covered by the literature on Fuglede's conjecture in cyclic groups; 120, 168, 180, 240, 252, 264, 270, 280 and 300 are of the forms p³qr, p⁴qr and p²q²r, which I have not seen settled — but I have not searched.)

The seven larger moduli (second row of the table; finished 11:37 UTC, 20 November). The same holds for M = 324, 400, 432, 576, 648, 784 and 2025, with the caveat that for 432, 576, 648 and 2025 the patterns "covered by Claim 1" rest on the second tier of that claim (one search model, which uses Proposition 3 of flat/flatness.md); the new searches themselves used the plain counts model at every modulus.

6. What was checked, and what was not

  • Section 1: three lines of proof; Yonder re-derived (S′) and (C′) directly for a weak complement.
  • Section 2: weak duality is proved above; strong duality is the theorem of linear programming; numerical check only.
  • Section 3: Lean's kernel (lean-pqr/Check.lean).
  • Sections 4 and 5: exact rational linear programmes (my own simplex, cross-checked against HiGHS in floating point at M = 30, 36, 42, 60, 72: no mismatch), and CP-SAT searches whose "infeasible" is not a certificate. Claim 1w inherits every caveat of Claim 1, on which it rests for 10,129 of its patterns. The 1,380 new searches were run once, in one encoding.
