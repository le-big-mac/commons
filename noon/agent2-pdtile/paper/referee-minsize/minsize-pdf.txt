         Abstract.     Let p ̸= q beP  primes, n ≥ 0 and M = pn q . For a multiset A on ZM with
         mask polynomial A(X) = x A(x)X x , call the set of divisors d > 1 of M with Φd | A(X)
         the pattern of A. We determine, for every one of the 22n+1 patterns, the least number of
         elements of a nonempty multiset with exactly that pattern. If Φq ∤ A it is pe or pe (pk + q),
         where e counts the prime powers pa in the pattern and k the levels a with pa q in the pattern
                                             ′
         and pa not. If Φq | A it is q pe (pk + x), where k′ counts such levels beyond the rst level
         missing from the pattern's multiples of q , and x ∈ {0, 1} is decided by four clauses, one
         of which separates p = 2 from p ≥ 3. Both the lower bounds and the constructions are
         elementary inductions on a tree of residue classes; the lower bounds come from following the
         set of sizes that a class can have, not only its least size. The whole statement, for multisets
         on Zpn q and in terms of cyclotomic polynomials dividing the mask polynomial, has been
         formalised in Lean 4 with Mathlib and is accepted by Lean's kernel.

1.3.   Sets. Theorem 1 is about multisets. For sets (multiplicities 0 and 1) a pattern can fail
to occur at all, and the least size can be larger. No unbalanced set has F = [1, n] ̸= E : all its
classes are unbalanced down to the leaves, two leaves of equal shape that are 0/1 vectors and
not constant are equal, and so, going up the tree, all leaves are equal and every level is in E .
In the ten groups with n ≤ 4 in which the author computed sets, 736 of the 1,088 patterns
have a set, and each of those has a set of the least multiset's size.In Z96 (p = 2, q = 3,
n = 5) a fresh instance refereeing the working note found, by a recursion over the tree and
again by an integer program, that two patterns have a set but none of the least multiset's
size: balanced with E = {1, 4}, F         = {5}, and balanced with E = {2, 4}, F = {1, 5}; each
has least multisets of 24 elements and least sets of 36. We know no rule for sets.

1.4. Method. Write ZM = Zpn × Zq and hang A on the tree of residue classes modulo
p, p2 , . . . , pn ; a class carries the vector in Nq of its elements' second coordinates. Divisibility
by Φpa says that the p children of every class of depth a − 1 have equal totals; by Φpa q ,
that they have equal shapes (vectors diering by constants); by Φq , that the root's vector is
constant (Section 2). The lower bounds are inductions on this tree. The one idea beyond
bookkeeping is to prove, for a class that is not balanced, a statement about the set of its
possible sizes,
