       Abstract. Let p ̸= q be P     primes, n ≥ 0 and M = pn q. For a multiset A on ZM with
       mask polynomial A(X) = x A(x)X x , call the set of divisors d > 1 of M for which the
       cyclotomic polynomial Φd divides A(X) the pattern of A. We determine, for every one of
       the 22n+1 patterns, the least number of elements of a nonempty multiset with exactly that
       pattern. If Φq ∤ A it is pe or pe (pk + q), where e counts the prime powers pa in the pattern
                                                                                        ′
       and k the levels a with pa q in the pattern and pa not. If Φq | A it is q pe (pk + x), where
         ′
       k counts such levels beyond the first level missing from the pattern’s multiples of q, and
       x ∈ {0, 1} is decided by four clauses, one of which separates p = 2 from p ≥ 3. The lower

       set of sizes that a class can have, not only its least size; the constructions are explicit sets

                                           1. Introduction
1.1. The question. Let  PM be a positive integer and A : ZM → Z≥0 a multiset
                                                                          P on the cyclic
group ZM , with |A| = x A(x) elements and mask polynomial A(X) = 0≤x<M A(x)X x .
Which cyclotomic polynomials Φd , d | M , divide A(X) is the same as at which characters
of ZM the Fourier transform of A vanishes, and it governs whether a set tiles or is spectral:
the conditions of Coven and Meyerowitz [1], and most of what is known about Fuglede’s
conjecture in cyclic groups, are stated in these terms. A recurring step in that work is a
lower bound for |A| from a list of cyclotomic divisors of A(X), often together with a list of
Φd that do not divide it [4, 7, 3, 6, 5, 2].

  This note answers the sharpest form of that question in the simplest family of groups
where it is not trivial. Let p ̸= q be primes, n ≥ 0, M = pn q. The divisors d > 1 of M are
pa and pa q for 1 ≤ a ≤ n, and q. For a multiset A on ZM put
          E(A) = {a ∈ [1, n] : Φpa | A(X)},         F (A) = {a ∈ [1, n] : Φpa q | A(X)},
and call A balanced if Φq | A(X) and unbalanced otherwise. The pattern of A is the triple
(E(A), F (A), balanced or not); there are 22n+1 patterns. We ask: given a pattern, how
few elements can a nonempty multiset with exactly that pattern have? Exactness matters:
adding divisors can make the least size smaller as well as larger (compare E = ∅, F = {1}
with E = F = {1} below).
Theorem 1. Let p ̸= q be primes, n ≥ 0, and E, F ⊆ [1, n]; put e = |E|.
    (a) The least size of an unbalanced multiset A on Zpn q with E(A) = E and F (A) = F is

                 pe if F ⊆ E,        pe (pk + q) with k = |F \ E| otherwise.
    (b) The least size of a nonempty balanced multiset with E(A) = E and F (A) = [1, n] is
        q pe .
    (c) Let F ̸= [1, n], let a0 be the least level in [1, n] that is not in F , and let G = {a ∈
        F \ E : a > a0 }, k ′ = |G|. The least size of a balanced multiset with E(A) = E and
        F (A) = F is

        where x = 0 if at least one of the following holds, and x = 1 otherwise:
         (1) k ′ = 0;
         (2) some level a < a0 is in E;

         (4) k ′ ≥ 1, a0 ∈ E, p = 2, and some level of E \ F lies strictly between a0 and

In each case a multiset with exactly that pattern and that many elements exists, so every
pattern occurs.
   The empty multiset has every Φd as a divisor; it is excluded by “nonempty” in (b), and in
(a) and (c) by the pattern itself. For n = 1 the theorem gives the eight values 1, p, p + q, p
(unbalanced, for (E, F ) = (∅, ∅), ({1}, ∅), (∅, {1}), ({1}, {1})) and q, pq, q, pq (balanced, in
the same order); the value p + q for “Φpq and nothing else” is the classical one [4].

1.3. Sets. Theorem 1 is about multisets. For sets (multiplicities 0 and 1) a pattern can fail
to occur at all, and the least size can be larger. No unbalanced set has F = [1, n] ̸= E: all
its classes are unbalanced down to the leaves, two leaves of equal shape that are 0/1 vectors
and not constant are equal (in Zpq this is [3, Proposition 3.8(a)]), and so, going up the tree,
all leaves are equal and every level is in E. In the ten groups with n ≤ 4 in which the author
computed sets, 736 of the 1,088 patterns have a set, and each of those has a set of the least
multiset’s size. In Z96 (p = 2, q = 3, n = 5) a fresh instance refereeing the working note
found, by a recursion over the tree and again by a constraint-programming model, that two
patterns have a set but none of the least multiset’s size: balanced with E = {1, 4}, F = {5},
and balanced with E = {2, 4}, F = {1, 5}; each has least multisets of 24 elements and least
sets of 36. The author knows no rule for sets.

1.4. Method. Write ZM = Zpn × Zq and hang A on the tree of residue classes modulo
p, p2 , . . . , pn ; a class carries the vector in Nq of its elements’ second coordinates. Divisibility
by Φpa says that the p children of every class of depth a − 1 have equal totals; by Φpa q ,
that they have equal shapes (vectors differing by constants); by Φq , that the root’s vector is
constant (Section 2). The lower bounds are inductions on this tree. The one idea beyond
bookkeeping is to prove, for a class that is not balanced, a statement about the set of its
possible sizes,
    |N | ∈ {pu t + qm : t ≥ 1, m ≥ 0},          u = the number of levels of E ∪ F below N ,
with refinements (m ≥ 1, or q pu | |N |) when some level of F is not in E (Lemmas 6, 7,
8). Such statements pass through sums of children, where a bound on the least size alone
does not, and clauses (2), (3), (4) of (c) are exactly the configurations in which a nonempty
balanced class is there already and supplies the extra q pe elements for nothing (under clause
(1) nothing extra is needed).. The constructions (Section 4) are digit sets with one layer
switched according to the digits.


                               2. The tree of residue classes
   Throughout, p ̸= q are primes, n ≥ 0, M = pn q, and multisets are functions to Z≥0 ; sums
of multisets are sums of functions.

Classes.PnIdentify  ZM with Zpn × Zq by x 7→ (x mod pn , x mod q). Write u ∈ Zpn in base
p, u = a=1 ua pa−1 with digits ua ∈ {0, . . . , p − 1}; ua is the digit of level a. An element is
a pair (u, w), and w ∈ Zq is its layer. For 0 ≤ d ≤ n, a class of depth d is the set of all (u, w)
with prescribed digits u1 , . . . , ud (a residue class of u modulo pd ); the class of depth 0 is the
root. A class N of depth d < n has p children N0 , . . . , Np−1 , the classes of depth d + 1 inside
it, Nj being the one with ud+1 = j. A class of depth d has n − d levels below it, and its own
level l (1 ≤ l ≤ n − d) is the level d + l of the group.

Vectors. For a multiset A and a class N , g(N ) ∈ Nq is the vector whose entry at w is the
number of elements of A in N in layer w, and |N | is the sum of its entries, the total of N .
1 = (1, . . . , 1). N is empty if g(N ) = 0, balanced if g(N ) is a multiple of 1 (an empty class

   Elements of Zpn are written as digit strings. For a set T of levels let D(T ) be the set of

                                  5. The formal proof
   Theorem 1 is formalised in Lean 4 (toolchain v4.37.0-rc1) with Mathlib, in the library
Pnq of the development that also holds the tiling theorems of the companion papers. At the
time of writing the eleven new files are in minsize-agent3/lean-min/, beside the working
note, with a log of what was checked, and build in a private copy of the development.
The statements. A multiset on Zpn q is a function w : ZMod (p ^ n * q) → N, its mul-
tiplicities. Its mask polynomial, with exponents the representatives in [0, M ), is
noncomputable
  P           def maskW (w : ZMod M → N) : Polynomial Z :=
     a : ZMod M, Polynomial.C (w a : Z) * Polynomial.X ^ a.val
   and levelsEW w, levelsFW w are the sets of ℓ ∈ [1, n] with Polynomial.cyclotomic (p
^ ℓ) Z | maskW w, respectively Polynomial.cyclotomic (p ^ ℓ * q) Z | maskW w. The
formulas of the theorem are four definitions:
def gOf (Es Fs : Finset N) (a0 : N) : Finset N := (Fs \ Es).filter (fun ℓ => a0 < ℓ)

def xOf (p : N) (Es Fs : Finset N) (a0 : N) : N :=
  if gOf Es Fs a0 = ∅ ∨ (∃ ℓ ∈ Es, ℓ < a0) ∨ (a0 ∈ Es ∧ 3 ≤ p) ∨
      (a0 ∈ Es ∧ p = 2 ∧ ∃ b ∈ Es \ Fs, a0 < b ∧ ∀ g ∈ gOf Es Fs a0, b < g)
  then 0 else 1

def minU (p q : N) (Es Fs : Finset N) : N :=
  if Fs ⊆ Es then p ^ Es.card else p ^ Es.card * (p ^ (Fs \ Es).card + q)

def minB (p q : N) (Es Fs : Finset N) (a0 : N) : N :=
  q * p ^ Es.card * (p ^ (gOf Es Fs a0).card + xOf p Es Fs a0)

How the formal proof differs from this paper. The formal lower bound for part (a) is
not Proposition 9: it is the direct induction of the working note (two claims and four cases),
written before the argument by sets of sizes was found. The formal lower bounds for part (c)
follow Lemmas 4 to 8, Lemma 10 and Proposition 11. The formal constructions are recursive
builders of trees that follow Section 4, with changes of convenience that the instances that
wrote them reported: a string counts as having all its digits at the levels of H (or of E \ F )
nonzero also when there are no such levels, which renames layers; in modes Q, W the vector
for digit 0 is 1 + e0 − e2 for q ≥ 3 and 1 + e1 − e0 for q = 2; and in case (III) any level of E
before a0 serves as host. The remark on host levels at the end of Section 4 is not formalised.
How it was made, and what was checked. The author fixed every definition and every
theorem statement first, in files that compiled with the proofs left open, and before any proof
was written had Lean evaluate minU, minB and q pe on the 1,265 patterns whose least size the
solvers had decided (Section 6): all agree. Two fresh instances of the model had written the
proofs of the lower bounds on the tree in this way earlier (Sharp.lean, Balanced.lean); four
more then each wrote the proofs of one of the remaining parts in a copy of its own, forbidden
to change a statement. On the merged copy the author checked that every fixed declaration
stands character for character; that Lean prints each fixed definition and statement exactly
as it printed them before the proofs existed; that the new files contain no sorry, no added
axiom, and no instance, notation, macro or attribute that could change what a statement
means; that the whole development builds; and that #print axioms gives, for each of the
theorems named above, only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. Two readings
of the statements followed. Noon read Rule.lean and MinGroup.lean whole, and the defi-
nitions of Weighted.lean, against the rule of the working note, and found the four clauses
to be the note’s word for word; it did not read the constructions; it also ran a trial of its own,
by integer programming on multisets, which is Noon’s to report. A fresh instance was asked
to audit the three statements and the seven definitions and to try to show that they say
something other than intended: it read them in Lean’s printout with every instance shown
and found no difference from the claim; proved in Lean that the hypotheses on a0 hold for
exactly one number when F ̸= [1, n] and for none otherwise, so that the three statements
cover every pattern once; compared Lean’s values with the rule as worded in the working note
on 103,752 patterns; and, by enumerating multisets with exact polynomial division, found
Lean’s value to be the least size on every pattern of Z2 , Z3 , Z5 , Z6 , Z10 , Z15 , Z12 , Z18 , Z20 , Z24
and on every pattern it could settle in nine further groups: 936 patterns, no mismatch. It
did not read the proofs.
What the formal proof does not show. That the theorem is new; that the prose proofs
of this paper are right (they were read by the author and, in their working-note form, refereed
by fresh instances, which is a weaker thing); anything about sets; and nothing has been read
by a person.
