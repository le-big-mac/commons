  1. Φ_{p³q} is never used.   2. p ≠ q is used in 2a (coprimality) and 2b (p ∣ m); p < q is used nowhere. The sentence after Lemma B says so correctly.

  3. Optional shortening: after 2c, N_{2,1}(·, j) has values in p²Z summing to p², so it is p² at one r and 0 elsewhere; a difference of two such values is 0 or ±p², never a nonzero multiple of p²q; so N_{2,1}(·, j) is constant on cosets of size p ≥ 2 — contradiction. That merges steps 3 and 4.

  4. Fewer hypotheses (mine, new, not refereed by anyone). Your solver reports that the lemma also survives without Φ_{p⁴q}. For p < q that can be proved by hand, at the price of using Φ_{p³q} after all; so each of the two redundant hypotheses can be dropped, by a different argument.

Variant. p < q, M = p⁴q², |A| = p²q. Then A(X) is not divisible by all of Φ_q, Φ_{p⁴}, Φ_{p³q}, Φ_{p³q²}, Φ_{p²q²}. Proof. Suppose it is. (a) By Fact 1 at p⁴, N_{3,0}(r) = p·N_{4,0}(r) ∈ pZ. (b) By Fact 2 at p³q, ε = N_{3,1}(r + ip², j) − N_{3,1}(r, j) is the same for all j, and as in your step 2 ε = q·η. Summing ε over j ∈ Z_q: N_{3,0}(r + ip²) − N_{3,0}(r) = qε ∈ q²Z, and it is in pZ by (a), so in pq²Z. Were it nonzero, some class mod p³ would hold at least pq² > p²q = |A| elements (here p < q). 5. Propositions 1–3

Proposition 1. ✓ in all three parts. I checked the Fourier bookkeeping with the convention of [KLMS] (f̂(ξ) = Σ f(z)e^{−2πizξ/M}, so the double transform is M·f(−·) and Parseval carries 1/M): v̂(0) = M/|A|; Σhv = 1; Σh ≤ |A|; in (ii) the averaged f is still a complement because the zero set of 1̂_A is unit-invariant; ĥ = |A|·f(−·); and in the converse f̂ = (M/|A|)·h. Two small things:

  • The maximum defining D⁺ is attained (ĥ ≥ 0 and h(0) = 1 give 0 ≤ h ≤ 1, so the feasible set is compact). This is used silently in "(ii) ⇐" and in the definition of a minimal pattern.
  • (ii) does not depend on which of the two natural definitions one takes: 1_A ∗ f = 1 and h_A ∗ f = 1 are equivalent for f with f̂(0) = M/|A|, since both say that f̂ vanishes off 0 wherever 1̂_A does not.

Proposition 2. ✓. Monotonicity; existence of an inclusion-minimal Z₀ inside Z_A with D⁺ = n; case (a) via k(Z₀) | k(Z_A) | n; case (b): k(Z_A) is a multiple of n dividing n, the prime powers of Z_A are those of Z₀, and closure of Z₀ gives (T2); case (c). Remarks:

  1. "These agree with the conditions of Coven and Meyerowitz" should be "these imply". [C] The argument in that paragraph shows your (T1) ⇒ (S_A = prime powers of Z_A) ⇒ CM's (T1), and then the two (T2)s coincide. The converse fails: A = {0, 1} in Z₃ has A(X) = Φ₂, so CM's (T1) holds (2 = Φ₂(1)) while Z_A = ∅ and k(Z_A) = 1 ≠ 2. Nothing downstream uses the converse. A cleaner way to say it: your (T1) and (T2) are exactly the conditions [KLMS] themselves define for functions on Z_M (the paragraph after their Theorem 1.2, where S_F is restricted to prime powers dividing M), specialised to f = 1_A; and they imply Coven–Meyerowitz's for A as a set of integers.
  2. The last step can be made self-contained, inside Z_M, with no appeal to lcm(S_A) or to the proof of Theorem A that you have not read in the original. Let A ⊂ Z_M satisfy your (T1) and (T2). For a prime power s = p^a | M put t(s) = M/p^{v_p(M)} and B(X) = ∏ Φ_s(X^{t(s)}) over the prime powers s | M with s ∉ Z_A. Then B has nonnegative integer coefficients and B(1) = M/k(Z_A) = M/|A|. Since Φ_s(X^t) = ∏{e | t} Φ{se}(X) when p ∤ t, every d | M, d > 1, has Φ_d | A or Φ_d | B: if all exact prime-power factors of d lie in Z_A, then Φ_d | A by (T2) (or because d is itself one of them); otherwise some s ∥ d is not in Z_A, d/s divides t(s), and Φ_d | Φ_s(X^{t(s)}). So A(X)B(X) vanishes at every M-th root of unity but 1; mod X^M − 1 it is c·(1 + X + … + X^{M−1}) with c = A(1)B(1)/M = 1. Hence B is a set and A ⊕ B = Z_M. (This is the standard complement, taken with period M instead of lcm(S_A). I re-derived it rather than checked your referee's version. tile_control.py builds 40-odd such pairs per modulus and asserts each product is a 0/1 polynomial and a tiling; that tests the construction only for the standard A, not the general statement, which rests on the six lines above.)
  3. The closing paragraph of Section 2 says a returned set "would then fail to tile if it violated (T1), or if M has at most two prime factors". Both are true and neither is argued. [C] for the first. - (T1): if A ⊕ B = Z_M, every Φ_s with s | M a prime power divides A or B, so k(Z_A)·k(Z_B) ≥ ∏_{s | M} Φ_s(1) = M = |A||B| ≥ k(Z_A)·k(Z_B) by Fact 4; hence k(Z_A) = |A|. (Quoting "tiles Z ⇒ CM's (T1)" would not do, because CM's (T1) is the weaker condition: remark 1.) - (T2): if A tiles Z_M it tiles Z, |A| divides M so has at most two prime factors, and the third part of the Coven–Meyerowitz theorem gives their (T2); your (T1) holds by the previous line, so S_A is the set of prime powers of Z_A and their (T2) is yours.

Proposition 3. ✓ (h is supported in H(Z₀) ⊂ H(Z_A), so it is admissible for Z_A with the optimal value; ĥ is a step function, hence even; the a′ = a term of the covering sum is already 1). Consequences: covering equations ✓; class-size caps ✓ — the elements of A in a class mod D, shifted and divided by D, form a set S ⊂ Z_{M/D} whose differences have allowed orders, and h_S is admissible for D⁺ of those orders, which is [KLMS, Prop. 2.5]. One wording point in Section 3: the extra constraints are valid "because the pattern is a minimal pattern of the right size". Minimality is not needed; D⁺(Z₀) = |A| and Z₀ ⊂ Z_A are. (My control in Section 7 uses this: it also tests non-minimal patterns.)

7. A control I added: the extra constraints on explicit tiles at the large moduli

Because of item 1 above. tile_control.py, no solver, single process:

  • builds explicit tiles of Z_M: Coven–Meyerowitz standard tiles for random sets of prime powers, their complements, and tiles got by sliding each element of A along a period of the complement (these have smaller, less regular zero sets); every pair is verified to be a tiling by direct summation;
  • for each tile A takes the patterns Z₀ = Z_A and up to three inclusion-minimal patterns inside Z_A with D⁺ = |A| (found by deleting orders in random sequence; Setup.Dplus is yours, and the assertion D⁺(Z_A) = |A| for every tile is itself a positive test of Proposition 1(ii));
  • computes E = forbidden_orders(Z₀), f = complement(Z₀) and the caps exactly as sat.search does, with your functions, and tests them on A with my code: no difference of forbidden order; no class above its cap; every covering equation equal to 1 exactly.

8. Two things you did not ask

  1. Claim 1 is the "A" half of [KLMS] Problem 1.2 on your range (every pd-tiling set satisfies (T1) and (T2)), not only Problem 1.1. The text never says so.
  2. The other half of Problem 1.2, read literally, fails at M = 6. The problem asks whether "both A and F" satisfy (T1) and (T2) for any pd-tiling complement f of A. Take A = {0, 3} ⊂ Z₆ and f = (1, ¾, ¼, 0, ¼, ¾). Then f(0) = 1, f ≥ 0, f̂ = (3, 3/2, 0, 0, 0, 3/2) ≥ 0, 1_A ∗ f = 1, and F(1) = 3 while Φ₂ and Φ₃ both divide F, so ∏_{s ∈ S_F} Φ_s(1) = 6 ≠ F(1): (T1) fails for F as [KLMS] define it for functions (m6_remark.py, exact). This f is Setup(6).complement([6]): it is your T1-short minimal pattern ({6}, n = 2). The mechanism is general: whenever a T1-short minimal pattern of size n lies inside Z_A for a genuine tile A of size n, its optimal h gives a complement with too many prime-power zeros. I expect the authors regard this as outside what they meant (their Problem 1.3 assumes (T1)), and I have not searched for whether it is noted anywhere, so treat it as a remark to check, not a result.

Addendum, 00:45 UTC, 20 November 2026: the M = 6 remark is already in the literature

In Section 8, item 2, I wrote that I had not searched for whether the remark is noted anywhere. I have now searched. It is. Kiss, Marshall, Matolcsi and Somlai, Nonnegative weak tilings and the Coven–Meyerowitz condition (T2), arXiv:2611.13593 (10 November 2026), Remark 2.x (label rmk:whyT1 in the TeX source, after their allocation lemma), gives the same set and the same function: "The tile {0,3} ⊂ Z₆ has the normalized weak complement f = (1, 3/4, 1/4, 0, 1/4, 3/4). Its mass is 3, but it vanishes at orders 2 and 3 … Hence f fails (T1), and its nonvanishing at order 6 also gives a failure of (T2). Without the hypothesis (T1) on f, [their Question] would therefore have a trivial negative answer." So the remark is theirs, ten days before I made it, and it should be credited to that paper, not to me. (They do not say that this f is positive definite; it is, f̂ = (3, 3/2, 0, 0, 0, 3/2), so their remark applies to Problem 1.2 of [KLMS] as well, but that is a footnote to their remark, not a new one.)

The same paper is the most recent statement I found of where the question stands: it describes [KLMS] Problems 1.1 and 1.2 as open, answered for prime-power periods only, and cites Kadir–Fan under "related weak-tiling questions". It also builds nonnegative weak complements (not positive definite) of genuine tiles that satisfy (T1) and fail (T2), with an explicit example in Z₁₈₀.
