The question

p ≠ q primes, n ≥ 1, M = pⁿq. A is a multiset on Z_M, not empty. E = {1 ≤ a ≤ n : Φ_{p^a} | A}, F = {0 ≤ a ≤ n : Φ_{p^a q} | A}. For each pair (E, F): m(E, F) = the least |A| over multisets with exactly that pattern; s(E, F) = the same over sets.

What I have seen before sealing

  • minsize.py run for n = 1 only (Z_6, both solvers, multisets and sets): eight patterns, all as worked by hand (1, 3, 5, 3, 2, 6, 2, 6; for sets the patterns E = ∅, F = {1} and E = {1}, F = {0} have none).
  • The second reader's example of 26 November: p = 3, q = 2, E = ∅, F = {1, 2}, a set of 11 in Z_54.
  • Lemma 3 (Lean-checked): 0 ∉ F and F∖E ≠ ∅ give |A| ≥ p^|E| (p + q). Łaba and Marshall: q ∤ |A| gives |A| ≥ p^|E ∪ F|.

The picture the expectations come from

Write Z_M as Z_{pⁿ} × Z_q and hang A on the tree of classes mod p^a (depth a = 0..n): each node carries the vector in N^q of its elements' residues mod q; children add up to the parent. Call a vector's class modulo the all-ones vector its shape and its sum its total. Then for a ≥ 1: a ∈ E iff at every node of depth a−1 the p children have equal totals; a ∈ F iff at every such node the p children have equal shapes; and 0 ∈ F iff the root's shape is zero. A shape that is not zero cannot be shared by an empty child, so under a node of nonzero shape every level of F branches p ways, and every level of E branches p ways under any node that is not empty. Two siblings of equal shape differ in total by a multiple of q. A node's total is divisible by p^c, c the number of levels of E below it.

Expectations (multisets unless said; each with its reason and a chance)

Let e = |E|, k = |F∖E| (levels in F and not in E; level 0 is never counted in k).

M1 (85%). When 0 ∉ F: m(E, F) = p^e (p^k + q) if k ≥ 1, and p^e if k = 0, for every pattern computed (n = 2 and 3 for (p, q) = (2,3), (3,2), (2,5), (5,2), (3,5), (5,3); n = 4 for (2,3) and (3,2) where the solver finishes). Reason: p^(e+k) single elements are forced on the leaves under the root; each level of F∖E needs two siblings of equal shape and unequal total, which costs one more fibre of q elements, and every level of E multiplies that fibre by p; one fibre on one path serves every level of F∖E at once. The lower bound I can argue when the two siblings sit under a node of nonzero shape; under a node of zero shape the nonzero siblings seem to cost a factor q instead of a summand, which is dearer, but I have not closed that case. For k = 1 it is Lemma 3 and its known examples.

M2 (95%). When 0 ∉ F: m(E, F) ≥ p^|E ∪ F| in every pattern computed, also where q divides the minimum. Reason: the branching argument needs only a root of nonzero shape, which is Φ_q ∤ A; that is weaker than Łaba and Marshall's q ∤ |A|. (M1 implies M2; M2 is listed because it could hold where M1 fails.)

M3 (90%). When F = {0, 1, ..., n}: m = q p^e. Reason: every node then has shape zero, A is a union of fibres of q elements, and the fibres form a multiset on Z_{pⁿ} with exactly the divisors E.

M4 (55%). When 0 ∈ F and F ≠ {0, ..., n}: let a₀ be the least level ≥ 1 not in F and k′ the number of levels of F∖E below a₀ (deeper than a₀). Then m = q p^e (p^k′ + x), with x = 0 if k′ = 0; x = 0 if k′ ≥ 1, a₀ ∈ E and p ≥ 3; x = 1 otherwise. Reason: above a₀ all shapes are zero and levels of F cost nothing; at a₀ some children must have nonzero shapes adding to zero, which takes q elements' worth of shape, and below a₀ those children branch as in M1. The summand x is the extra fibre for unequal totals; when a₀ ∈ E and p ≥ 3 a third child of zero shape has room to supply it free, because equal totals at a₀ force every child's total to be a multiple of q anyway. This case I worked through only for n = 2, so the chance is low. Particular cases worked by hand, each counted separately: - M4a (75%). n = 2, E = ∅, F = {0, 2}: m = q(p + 1). - M4b (60%). n = 2, E = {1}, F = {0, 2}: m = 6q for p = 2 and q p² for p ≥ 3.

M5 (75%). Levels in neither E nor F do not matter: m depends only on the order of the types (E only, F only, both) of the other levels, and on whether 0 ∈ F. Tested by comparing n = 2 patterns with the n = 3 patterns that insert an empty level above, between or below. Reason: a level in neither can be passed through with one nonempty child, and I see nothing that several children could save; but a free level below everything gives room that sets need (S2), and I have not proved it gives multisets nothing.

M6 (80%). For the patterns with 0 ∉ F the minimum is the same polynomial in p and q for all six pairs of primes (no dependence on which is larger or on residues). For 0 ∈ F I expect p = 2 to differ from p ≥ 3 in at least one pattern (M4's x), 65%.

S1 (certain, a check of the code). s(E, F) ≥ m(E, F) wherever a set exists.

S2 (85%). If level n is in neither E nor F then s = m. Reason: the free bottom level lets a class mod p^(n−1) spread a single element and a whole fibre over different leaves.

S3 (95%). No set has 0 ∉ F, F ⊇ {1, ..., n} and E ≠ {1, ..., n}. Reason: then all leaves have one nonzero shape; two 0/1 vectors of equal nonzero shape are equal; so every level has equal totals.

S4 (60%). Among patterns with 0 ∉ F where a set exists and level n is in E or F, at least one has s > m.

The two solvers. CP-SAT (class counts) and HiGHS (polynomial remainders) agree on every pattern both finish (97%); every witness passes the third check, by complex values (99%).

How it will be scored

One line per expectation: held if true of every pattern computed that it speaks of; M1 and M4 are also reported as counts (patterns right of patterns computed). Patterns where a solver does not finish in its time limit are listed and left out. Brier score over the lines with a stated chance.
