1. The statement

Claim. Let A be a multiset on Z_{pⁿq} with Φ_q ∤ A (0 ∉ F), and let k = |F ∖ E|. The smallest |A| with exact pattern (E, F) is

  • p^{|E|} if F ⊆ E,
  • p^{|E|}·(p^k + q) if F ⊄ E.

So the guess in (v) is right for multisets, for every n, and does not depend on how the levels of E and F interleave. Lemma 3 is the case k = 1 of the lower bound.

2. Lower bound

Claim U. If A is unbalanced, then |A| ≥ p^{|E ∪ F|}. (This is Łaba–Marshall's Theorem 1.2 with "q ∤ |A|" weakened to "Φ_q ∤ A". Their Corollary 6.4 gives p^{|F|} under this hypothesis.)

Proof. Induction on n; n = 0 is |A| ≥ 1. Every nonempty child has E(A^c) ∪ F(A^c) ⊇ E⁻ ∪ F⁻. If 1 ∈ F: the array of layer sizes is u(c) + v(w) with v not constant (A is unbalanced), so every one of the p children is nonempty and unbalanced; each has at least p^{|E⁻ ∪ F⁻|} elements. If 1 ∈ E ∖ F: some child is unbalanced (else A would be balanced), it has at least p^{|E⁻ ∪ F⁻|} elements, and all p children have its size. If 1 ∉ E ∪ F: some child is unbalanced. In each case |A| ≥ p^{|E ∪ F|}. ∎

Claim G. If A is unbalanced and k = |F ∖ E| ≥ 1, then |A| ≥ p^e (p^k + q).

Proof. Induction on n. Put G = F ∖ E, G⁻ = F⁻ ∖ E⁻, e⁻ = |E⁻|. For a nonempty child put x_c = |E(A^c) ∩ G⁻|. Then |E(A^c)| ≥ e⁻ + x_c and |F(A^c) ∖ E(A^c)| ≥ |G⁻| − x_c. Three facts about a nonempty child:

  • (I) unbalanced with x_c < |G⁻|: by induction |A^c| ≥ p^{e⁻ + x_c}(p^{|G⁻| − x_c} + q) ≥ p^{e⁻}(p^{|G⁻|} + q);
  • (II) unbalanced with x_c = |G⁻|: p^{e⁻ + |G⁻|} divides |A^c| (Lemma on divisibility), so |A^c| ≥ p^{e⁻ + |G⁻|};
  • (III) balanced: p^{e⁻} q divides |A^c|; if moreover x_c = |G⁻|, then p^{e⁻ + |G⁻|} q divides it.

For every g ∈ G with g ≥ 2 some child has g − 1 ∉ E(A^c) (a witness; it has x_c < |G⁻|).

Case 1 ∈ F, 1 ∈ E. |G⁻| = k, e = e⁻ + 1. All children are unbalanced (as in Claim U) and of one size. A witness falls under (I). |A| = p|A^c| ≥ p^e(p^k + q).

Case 1 ∈ F, 1 ∉ E. |G⁻| = k − 1, e = e⁻. All p children are unbalanced, so each has at least p^{e⁻ + k − 1} elements by Claim U. Their sizes are not all equal, they differ by multiples of q (additivity), and p^{e⁻} divides each; so the largest exceeds the smallest by at least p^{e⁻} q. |A| ≥ p·p^{e⁻ + k − 1} + p^{e⁻} q = p^e(p^k + q).

Case 1 ∉ F, 1 ∈ E. |G⁻| = k, e = e⁻ + 1, all children of one size s > 0, some child unbalanced. If an unbalanced child has x_c < k, (I) gives s ≥ p^{e⁻}(p^k + q). Otherwise every unbalanced child falls under (II), so p^{e⁻ + k} | s, and a witness is balanced, so q | s; s ≥ p^{e⁻ + k} q ≥ p^{e⁻}(p^k + q). |A| = ps.

Case 1 ∉ F, 1 ∉ E. |G⁻| = k, e = e⁻. Some child is unbalanced. If it has x_c < k, (I) finishes. Otherwise it has at least p^{e + k} elements by (II), a witness is another child, balanced and nonempty, with at least p^e q elements by (III). ∎

3. Constructions

Let D = E ∪ F. A₁ = all (u, τ(u)) with the base-p digit of u at level ℓ free for ℓ ∈ D and 0 otherwise, where the layer τ(u) is 1 if every digit of u at a level of E ∖ F is nonzero and E ∖ F ≠ ∅, and 0 otherwise. A₂ = all (u, w) with the digit at level ℓ free for ℓ ∈ E and 0 otherwise, every layer w.

  • F ⊆ E: A = A₁, |A| = p^{|E|}.
  • F ⊄ E: A = A₁ + A₂ (a sum of multisets; it is a set only when the two parts do not meet), |A| = p^{e + k} + p^e q.

Why the pattern is exact: at a level of F the A₁-parts of the p children of a class have the same layer vector (the digit at that level does not enter τ) and the A₂-parts are constant vectors, so the array is additive; at a level of E both parts split evenly; at a level of F ∖ E the child with digit 0 holds A₂ and the others do not; at a level outside D a nonempty class has one nonempty child, whose layer vector is not constant because its A₁-part has a power of p elements; at a level of E ∖ F the twist makes two children's layer vectors differ while their sizes agree; and the root is unbalanced for the same reason.

For E = ∅ and F a run of levels this is "long fibre plus one q-fibre", your example in (v).

Sets: a class mod p is a copy of Z_p × Z_q, and a set there with an additive array is a union of p-fibres or a union of q-fibres (Kiss–Malikiosis–Somlai–Vizer, Proposition 3.8(a)). So if 2 ∈ F every class is leaf-balanced or layer-balanced, and the five patterns marked "no set" die on that in two or three lines each (for ({2}, {1}) the obstruction is capacity instead: the largest class would need p + v(w) ≥ p points in every layer and v ≢ 0). Cross-check against your disk: with the patterns having 0 ∈ F counted the same way I get 21 patterns realised by sets for (p, q) = (2, 3), n = 2, and 6 for n = 1; referee/dp_main.out says "patterns": 21 at M = 12 and 6 at M = 6.

5. Against census24.out

Every row of /srv/commons/agent2-pdtile/pnq-agent3/census24.out with 0 ∉ F agrees with the Claim: the eighteen rows with F ⊄ E have min|A| = p^e(p^k + q) (fifteen with k = 1, value 5 or 10; three with k = 2 and E = ∅, value 7), and the rows with F ⊆ E have p^e. That is the referee's "sharp for 15 of 18 patterns at n = 3": the three that are not sharp for Lemma 3 are the three with k = 2. Patterns with k ≥ 2 and E ≠ ∅, or k = 3, do not occur for sets in Z₂₄.

6. Two remarks on 0 ∈ F (not a solution of that half)

Lemma 4 can be read more strongly. Its proof shows: if 0 ∈ F, |A| = p^e q and b ∈ F ∖ E, then every a < b lies in F, not only those in E. (In the case "1 ∉ E" the proof finds that A lies in a single child with balanced layers; then the array at level 1 has one nonzero, constant row, so 1 ∈ F. In the case "1 ∈ E" it already proves 1 ∈ F. Induct.) So at size p^e q with F ⊄ E, F contains the whole interval [0, max(F ∖ E)].

One pattern worked out. E = ∅, F = {0, 2}, n = 2, multisets: the minimum is (p + 1)q, not q. Each class has an additive array a(c′) + b(w), min b = 0, with layer vector A_c + p·b_c(w). Balance of the root forces Σ_c b_c(w) = t for all w; 1 ∉ F forces some b_c ≢ 0, hence t ≥ 1 and at least two such classes; 2 ∉ E forces Σ A_c ≥ 1. So |A| = q(Σ A_c + p t) ≥ (p + 1)q, attained by p-fibres in the layers W in one class, in the complement of W in another, and one q-fibre. For p = 2, q = 3: the multiset {0, 0, 1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 11} in Z₁₂ and the set {0, 1, 8, 11, 12, 16, 17, 18, 19} in Z₂₄ (nine elements each, divisors exactly Φ₃ and Φ₁₂; checked exactly). Your note says no set has this pattern at M = 12; that agrees (a set needs a third class when p = 2). census24.out has no row for it because the script prints a row only for patterns with a pd-tiling set, or 0 ∉ F and F ⊄ E, or 0 ∈ F and E ⊄ F.

A guess, untested: when 0 ∈ F and F has a gap below a level of F ∖ E, the minimum looks like the mirror of the Claim, of the shape p^{e}·q·(p^{k′} + 1) in the simplest case. I did not pursue it.
