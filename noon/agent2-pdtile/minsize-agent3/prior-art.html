0. In short

  1. As posed, the question is not in print that I can find: no paper asks for, or tabulates, the smallest set or multiset whose divisors among the Φ_d, d | M, are exactly a prescribed list.
  2. But little of it is untouched. - With non-divisors not prescribed it is solved on pⁿq: MIN(S) = FIB(S) for every S (Kiss–Łaba–Marshall– Somlai, Corollary 7.3). - Your example in (v) is a minimiser, and your guess is proved by print for a whole family (0 ∉ F, F a run of consecutive levels, E a proper subset of F): two lines from their Proposition 6.3. Shown in Section 3. - For sets, the table is in good part on your own disk already, from 19–20 November: census24.out prints min|A| by exact pattern for Z₂₄, and the referee's recursion ref_dp.py computes it for five prime pairs, n up to 5 (its saved outputs print counts, not the minima). Section 5. The brief does not mention it.
  3. For multisets with Φ_q ∤ A I believe the whole answer is a half-page induction of the same kind as your Lemma 3. It is in the sealed file, unread by anyone. What may be new is the half with Φ_q | A, and the set version as n grows.
  4. Credits your items do not give. The bound |A| ≥ p^{|E|}·q when some a lies in F ∖ E is inside Malikiosis–Kolountzakis 2017 (proof of Proposition 5.2), by the counting of your Lemma 3's first case. The bound in (iii) is implicit in Malikiosis, arXiv May 2020 (proof of Theorem 6.1), by another method. Łaba–Marshall's Corollaries 6.4 and 6.5 are lower bounds that use a non-divisor.
  5. Items (i) to (vii) are all stated correctly. Remarks in Section 6.
  6. I made 35 requests: 27 fetches and 8 general web searches. Nothing was refused.

1. The question in the field's words

The field would call this lower bounds for mask polynomials with prescribed cyclotomic divisors, the problem MIN(S) of Kiss, Łaba, Marshall and Somlai, with the non-divisors prescribed as well; or the smallest multiset in Z_{pⁿq} with a given root pattern.

yours theirs
pattern (E, F) "root patterns" S_A^N(p), R_A^N(p) (Malikiosis 2022, Definition 10.1, p. 21: "The sets thus defined will be called root patterns of A"); the "zero set" Z(A) or Z_A in the Fuglede papers; S_A and the exponent sets EXP_i(S) in KLMS
class of depth d a grid Λ(x, p^d); "p-adic digits"
additive array Φ_{pq} divides the restriction to a D(N)-grid; "a nonnegative combination of p-fibers and q-fibers"
the comparison sets T_E × Z_q, T_E × {0} "standard" sets A♭; "long fibers"
a ∈ F ∖ E with 0 ∉ F close to an "unsupported divisor" (KLMS Definition 1.5, which also asks p, q to divide the size)

Do not say "divisor set": Div(A) = {(a − a′, M)} is Sands's and Łaba–Londner's object (KLMS p. 41). In KLMS and Łaba–Marshall a "set" is A ⊂ ℕ₀, which is a multiset on Z_M (KLMS (2.2), p. 7). Your "sets A ⊂ Z_M" with M fixed is the Fuglede literature's object.

The quotations.

  • Lam–Leung, p. 2: "Main Theorem. For any m = p₁^{a₁} · · · p_r^{a_r} as above, the weight set W(m) is exactly given by ℕp₁ + · · · + ℕp_r." p. 7, Theorem 3.3: "(2) If r = 2, ℕG ∩ ker(ϕ) = ℕP₁ · σ(P₂) + ℕP₂ · σ(P₁)."
  • Malikiosis–Kolountzakis, p. 6: "Proposition 2.6. Let N have only two prime divisors, say p and q, and A(ω^n) = 0, for some n | N, so that A(X^n) ≡ P_n(X^n)Φ_p(X^{N/p}) + Q_n(X^n)Φ_q(X^{N/q}) mod X^N − 1. If A(ω^{np^a}) ≠ 0 for some a > 0, then P_n ≢ 0, and if A(ω^{nq^b}) ≠ 0 for some b > 0, then Q_n ≢ 0." p. 10: "Claim 1. ‖A(X^u)‖∞ ≥ p^i q." p. 11: "Claim 2. ‖A(X^{n{j+1}})‖∞ ≥ p‖A(X^{n_j})‖∞" and "so we would get #A = A(1) ≥ p^m q". Their ω has order N = pⁿq, so A(ω^{p^a q}) = 0 means n − a ∈ E and A(ω^{p^a}) = 0 means n − a ∈ F; m is |E| (Definition 5.1, p. 9); u = p^a q for a level n − a in F ∖ E. The proof of Claim 1 is the computation of your Lemma 3, case a = 1: class sizes on a p-cycle differ by multiples of q and are divisible by p^i, so a difference is at least p^i q.
  • Kiss–Malikiosis–Somlai–Vizer, p. 10: "Proposition 3.8. (a) Every non-Pompeiu set in Z_pq with respect to a faithful representation is either the union of cosets of Z_p or those of Z_q."
  • Malikiosis, v3 p. 12, with F(X) = A(X)·∏{r ∉ R} Φ{p^r}(X) and R the set of exponents r ≥ 1 of p at which A vanishes: "F(1) = R(1) = cp^m + qQ(1) ≥ p^m". That is A(1) ≥ p^{|R|}. It uses q ∤ A(1) and nonnegative coefficients only (the spectrum is used for the opposite inequality). The argument reduces mod q and is not Łaba–Marshall's. Theorem 6.1 and this proof are in v1.
  • Łaba–Marshall, p. 3: "Theorem 1.2. Let A(X) be a polynomial with non-negative coefficients and distinct cyclotomic factors Φ_{m₁}(X), ..., Φ_{m_k}(X). Assume that there exist distinct prime numbers p, q, and exponents α_j, β_j ∈ ℕ ∪ {0} such that m_j = p^{α_j} q^{β_j} for each 1 ≤ j ≤ k. Assume further that q ∤ A(1). Then we have the lower bound A(1) ≥ p^{E_p}, where E_p = |{α₁, ..., α_k}|." p. 22: "Corollary 6.4. [...] Suppose that the assumptions of Proposition 6.3 are satisfied, and, additionally, there exists α₀ with 0 ≤ α₀ < α₁ such that Φ_{m} ∤ A, where m = m₀p_i^{α₀}. Then |A| ≥ p_i^ℓ." (Proposition 6.3: Φ_{m_j} | A for m_j = m₀p_i^{α_j}, 1 ≤ α₁ < · · · < α_ℓ, m₀ prime to p_i. Take p_i = p, m₀ = q.) "Corollary 6.5. [...] Assume further that Φ_{p_i m} | A for i = 1, ..., I, but Φ_m ∤ A. Then A(1) ≥ p₁ ... p_I." p. 18: "Translates of such fibers in both directions can be added to construct more complicated examples."
  • Kiss–Londner–Matolcsi–Somlai, p. 4: "given a collection H = {m₁, m₂, ..., m_s} of divisors of M, the main difficulty is to decide whether there exists a set A such that the cyclotomic divisors of A(X) are exactly Φ_{m_j} (1 ≤ j ≤ s), and A satisfies condition (T1). It is not hard to see that this question is equivalent to solving an integer programming (IP) problem".
  • KLMS, p. 1: "MIN(S) := min{|A| : A ≠ ∅ and Φ_s(X) | A(X) for all s ∈ S}". p. 2: "To force an increase in size, we need additional assumptions on A, S, or both." p. 20: "Corollary 7.3. Let M = p^{n₁}q^{n₂} and A ∈ M⁺(Z_M). Let S ⊂ D(M) be nonempty, and assume that Φ_s | A for all s ∈ S. Assume further that |EXP_i(S)| ≤ 2 for some i ∈ {1, 2}. Then |A| ≥ FIB(S)." p. 18: "Proposition 6.3. (Long fiber decomposition) Let M = ∏ p_i^{n_i}, and let N | M satisfy N = ∏ p_i^{n_i − α_i + 1} with 1 ≤ α_i ≤ n_i. Let A ∈ M(Z_M), and assume that Φ_L(X) | A(X) for each N | L | M. Then, there exist polynomials P_i(X) ∈ ℤ[X] such that A(X) = P₁(X)F_{1,α₁}(X) + · · · + P_K(X)F_{K,α_K}(X) mod X^M − 1. Moreover, if A ∈ M⁺(Z_M) and K = 2, then we may assume that the polynomials P₁(X) and P₂(X) each have non-negative coefficients." "Lemma 6.2. [...] Then Φ_L(X) | F_{i,α}(X) if and only if p_i^{n_i−α+1} | L | M." p. 34: "Theorem 9.4. Let M = p^m q^n and suppose that A ∈ M⁺(Z_M) satisfies (T2). If there exists some N = p^γ q^η such that Φ_N(X) | A(X) and Φ_{p^γ}(X), Φ_{q^η}(X) ∤ A(X), then A(1) > ∏_{s∈S*_A} Φ_s(1)."

Is there a structure theorem that gives every minimum quickly? Not one that can be cited. The nearest are Proposition 6.3 (next section), and the pair "localise to grids" (KLMS Lemma 2.4, p. 10; Łaba–Marshall Lemma 4.4) with de Bruijn's positivity, which together are your recursion (2). KLMS's theorems that sets need not be "fibered on each scale" (Theorem 1.3, Section 8) need both exponents large and say nothing on pⁿq, where Corollary 7.3 always applies.

3. What follows in two lines from Proposition 6.3 (your example, and a family)

Let 0 ∉ F, let F be a run of consecutive levels with top b, and let F ⊄ E. Read A modulo p^b q and apply Proposition 6.3 with N = p^{b−|F|+1}q: the Φ_L with N | L | p^b q are exactly the Φ_{p^a q}, a ∈ F. Then A is a nonnegative combination of long p-fibres of length p^{|F|} and of q-fibres, so

|A| = p^{|F|}·x + q·y with integers x, y ≥ 0.

By Lemma 6.2 a q-fibre is divisible by Φ_q, so x ≥ 1 (else Φ_q | A); a long fibre is divisible by Φ_{p^a} for every a ∈ F, so y ≥ 1 (else F ⊆ E). By (i), p^{|E|} divides |A|; if E ⊆ F then |E| < |F|, so p^{|E|} divides qy, hence y. Therefore

|A| ≥ p^{|F|} + p^{|E|}·q = p^{|E|}(p^k + q), k = |F ∖ E|, whenever 0 ∉ F, F is a run and E ⊊ F.

A long fibre in one layer plus p^{|E|} q-fibres attains it (the recipe of Łaba–Marshall §6.2). I checked seven such sums exactly; they have the wanted patterns.

  • Your example in (v) is the case p = 3, q = 2, E = ∅, F = {1, 2}: |A| = 9x + 2y with x, y ≥ 1, so nothing smaller than 11 exists. Read modulo 18; Φ₅₄ plays no part.
  • Your guess is therefore proved by print on this family, and I found nothing in print that proves or refutes it elsewhere (F with a gap; E not inside F). There Proposition 6.3 does not apply as it stands, and the truncation of KLMS Proposition 4.1 loses the non-divisors.
  • When F is a run but E ⊄ F, the same lines give only |A| ≥ p^{|F|} + p^{min(|E|,|F|)}·q, which is less than (ii) in general.

This derivation is mine, made in this sitting. It is short enough to check by eye.

7. One sentence, and how sure

The question as posed is not in print as far as I can find, but it is mostly not new either: one half (multisets, Φ_q ∤ A) looks like an easy and unrecorded exercise, part of it two lines from a 2025 theorem; the set version you have in good part computed already; what may be new is the half with Φ_q | A.

  • That no paper states the exact-pattern minimum problem on pⁿq: about four chances in five. The Fuglede papers hide lemmas of this kind inside proofs (the one I found was inside a proof), and I read five of them, not all.
  • That no paper prints the table: about nine in ten.
  • That the sealed half-page argument is right: about five in six. It agrees with the eighteen relevant rows of your census and with seventeen constructions checked exactly, but nobody has read the proof.
  • 35 requests found none; that is not a survey.
