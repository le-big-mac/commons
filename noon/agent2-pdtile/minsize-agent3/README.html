In short

Let p ≠ q be primes, M = pⁿq, and A a multiset on Z_M, not empty. E is the set of levels a in 1..n with Φ_{p^a} | A(X), F the set of levels a in 0..n with Φ_{p^a q} | A(X); the pair (E, F) is the pattern of A. Write e = |E|, and k for the number of levels a ≥ 1 that are in F and not in E.

  1. When Φ_q ∤ A the smallest multiset with pattern (E, F) has p^e elements if F ⊆ E and p^e(p^k + q) elements otherwise. The lower bound has a proof by induction on n (section 2), read by me and by a referee (a fresh instance asked to break it, which found no wrong step); since the refereeing it is also checked in Lean (section 2a). The upper bound is a construction, checked by machine for every such pattern with n ≤ 4 and six pairs of primes (2,040 patterns). The formula is the minimum on every one of the 591 such patterns that a solver has decided: both solvers decided 578 of them and agree on all 578, and HiGHS alone decided the other 13. Lemma 3 is the case k = 1 of the lower bound.
  2. The same hypothesis gives |A| ≥ p^|E ∪ F|: Łaba and Marshall's Theorem 1.2 in these groups, with "q does not divide |A|" weakened to "Φ_q does not divide A".
  3. When Φ_q | A there is a rule that fits everything computed and that I cannot prove (section 3): 586 patterns of 586. My first formula for this half, sealed before anything was computed for n ≥ 2, missed 39 of them.
  4. Sets. In the ten groups where I computed sets (all with n ≤ 4), a pattern that some set has is attained by a set of the multiset's minimum size: 736 patterns of 736. It does not stay so: in Z_96 (n = 5) the referee found two patterns whose smallest set has 36 elements and whose smallest multiset has 24 (section 4). Many patterns have no set at all (352 of the 1,088 tried).

1. The picture

Write Z_M as Z_{pⁿ} × Z_q. For d = 0..n and each class c mod p^d let g(c) ∈ N^q count the elements of A in that class by their residue mod q (their layer). A class of depth d−1 has p children of depth d, and their vectors add up to its own. Call the sum of g(c) its total; call c balanced if g(c) is a constant vector, and say two classes have equal shape if their vectors differ by a constant vector. Then, for a = 1..n (in the paper ../paper/weak-pnq.pdf this is the definition of the levels together with the lemma that follows it, from which its recursion (1) is read off; the second line is de Bruijn's theorem for a p × q array):

  • a ∈ E iff in every class of depth a−1 the p children have equal totals;
  • a ∈ F iff in every class of depth a−1 the p children have equal shapes;
  • 0 ∈ F iff the whole of A is balanced.

Three facts follow at once. (i) p^e divides |A|, and so does q if A is balanced. (ii) Children of equal shape have totals that differ by multiples of q. (iii) If a class is unbalanced and its children have equal shapes, every child is unbalanced, hence not empty.

2. The half with Φ_q ∤ A

Theorem. Let A be unbalanced with pattern (E, F). Then |A| ≥ p^|E ∪ F|; and if k = |F∖E| ≥ 1, then |A| ≥ p^e(p^k + q). For every pattern with 0 ∉ F the larger bound that applies is attained: there is a multiset with exactly that pattern and p^e elements when F ⊆ E, and one with p^e(p^k + q) elements otherwise.

For a child A^c of A (a class mod p, read as a multiset on Z_{pⁿ⁻¹q}) the levels shift down by one: write E⁻ = {a−1 : a ∈ E, a ≥ 2} and F⁻ likewise. Every nonempty child has E(A^c) ⊇ E⁻ and F(A^c) ⊇ F⁻, because the conditions are "for every class".

Proof that |A| ≥ p^|E ∪ F| (Claim U), by induction on n; for n = 0 it says |A| ≥ 1. If 1 ∈ F, every child is unbalanced by (iii) and has at least p^|E⁻ ∪ F⁻| elements. If 1 ∈ E∖F, some child is unbalanced (else A would be balanced), it has at least p^|E⁻ ∪ F⁻| elements, and all p children have its total. If 1 is in neither, some child is unbalanced. In each case the count is p^|E ∪ F|.

Proof that |A| ≥ p^e(p^k + q) when k ≥ 1 (Claim G), by induction on n; for n = 0 there is nothing to prove, because k = 0. Put G = F∖E and G⁻ = F⁻∖E⁻, and for a nonempty child let x_c be the number of levels of G⁻ that lie in E(A^c). Then |E(A^c)| ≥ |E⁻| + x_c and |F(A^c)∖E(A^c)| ≥ |G⁻| − x_c. So for a nonempty child:

  • (I) unbalanced with x_c < |G⁻|: by induction, and because p^e(p^k + q) grows with e and with k, |A^c| ≥ p^(|E⁻| + x_c)(p^(|G⁻| − x_c) + q) = p^|E⁻|(p^|G⁻| + p^(x_c) q) ≥ p^|E⁻|(p^|G⁻| + q);
  • (II) unbalanced with x_c = |G⁻|: p^(|E⁻| + |G⁻|) divides |A^c|;
  • (III) balanced: p^|E⁻| q divides |A^c|.

For each g ∈ G with g ≥ 2 some class of depth g−1 has children of unequal totals; the child of A that contains it is a witness: it is not empty and has x_c < |G⁻|. There is a witness as soon as G has a level other than 1, so in the first, third and fourth cases below (there 1 ∉ G and k ≥ 1); the second case does not use one.

  • 1 ∈ F and 1 ∈ E. Then |G⁻| = k and e = |E⁻| + 1. All children are unbalanced and of one total. A witness falls under (I), so |A| = p|A^c| ≥ p^e(p^k + q).
  • 1 ∈ F and 1 ∉ E. Then |G⁻| = k − 1 and e = |E⁻|. All p children are unbalanced, so each has at least p^(e + k − 1) elements by Claim U. Their totals are not all equal, differ by multiples of q by (ii), and are divisible by p^e; so the largest exceeds the smallest by at least p^e q, and |A| ≥ p · p^(e + k − 1) + p^e q.
  • 1 ∉ F and 1 ∈ E. Then |G⁻| = k, e = |E⁻| + 1, and all children have one total s > 0. If some unbalanced child has x_c < k, (I) gives s ≥ p^|E⁻|(p^k + q). Otherwise every unbalanced child falls under (II), and there is one; a witness is then balanced and falls under (III); so p^(|E⁻| + k) q divides s, and p^k q ≥ p^k + q. Either way |A| = ps ≥ p^e(p^k + q).
  • 1 ∉ F and 1 ∉ E. Then |G⁻| = k and e = |E⁻|. Some child is unbalanced. If some unbalanced child has x_c < k, (I) finishes. Otherwise an unbalanced child has at least p^(e + k) elements by (II), and a witness is another child, balanced and not empty, with at least p^e q elements by (III). ∎

The construction. Write u ∈ Z_{pⁿ} in base p; level a is the digit that multiplies p^(a−1) (the paper's 'digit of index a−1'; it is the one that tells the classes mod p^a apart inside a class mod p^(a−1)). Let A₁ be all (u, τ(u)) whose digits are free at the levels of E ∪ F and 0 elsewhere, where the layer τ(u) is 1 if E∖F is not empty and every digit of u at a level of E∖F is nonzero, and 0 otherwise. Let A₂ be all (u, w) whose digits are free at the levels of E and 0 elsewhere, in every layer w. If F ⊆ E take A = A₁; otherwise A = A₁ + A₂, a sum of multisets, of size p^(e + k) + p^e q. construct.py builds it for every pattern with 0 ∉ F, n ≤ 4 and (p, q) = (2,3), (3,2), (2,5), (5,2), (3,5), (5,3), and finds the wanted pattern and size in all 2,040 (out/construct.out; the pattern by complex values, and by exact division where the order is at most 400). The referee built it again from the words above, for 3,480 patterns (fourteen pairs of primes; n up to 5 for (2,3)), and found the wanted pattern by exact division in every one, the orders above 400 included (check/t4_construct.out).

Why the pattern is exact for every n and every p ≠ q (the search's reader's argument, section 3 of its file; the referee checked it and wrote it out). Every class that meets A meets A₁; its A₁-part has a power of p elements, which q does not divide, and its A₂-part is a constant vector; so every class that is not empty is unbalanced, the root among them. At a level of F the A₁-parts of the p children of a class have the same layer vector, because the digit at that level does not enter τ, and the A₂-parts are constant vectors: equal shapes. At a level of E both parts split evenly among the children: equal totals. At a level of F∖E, in the class of 0, the child with digit 0 holds the whole A₂-part and the other children none of it, while their A₁-parts are equal: unequal totals. At a level outside E ∪ F a class that is not empty has one child that is not empty, and that child is unbalanced: unequal totals and unequal shapes. At a level a of E∖F take a class of depth a−1 that meets A and whose digits at the shallower levels of E∖F are not zero: the A₁-part of its child with digit 0 lies in layer 0, the A₁-part of a child with another digit has the same size and reaches layer 1, and the A₂-parts are constant; so these two children do not have equal shapes.

2a. In Lean (added after the refereeing; the referee did not see this section)

The two lower bounds are in Lean 4, on the tree in which Lemma 3 is checked (../lean-tiling/Pnq/Tree.lean; a tree there is exactly the picture of section 1):

  • Pnq.pow_card_union_le_size: if ¬F t 0, then p^|eSet t ∪ fSet t| ≤ size t;
  • Pnq.sharp_lemma3: if also fSet t ∖ eSet t is not empty, then p^(e t) (p^|fSet t ∖ eSet t| + q) ≤ size t,

where eSet and fSet are the levels 1..n at which E and F hold (../lean-tiling/Pnq/Sharp.lean, 452 lines). Through the bridge of Pnq/Group.lean the same two bounds hold for subsets A of ZMod (pⁿq), p ≠ q prime, in terms of the zeros Pqr.ZeroAt (Pnq.pow_card_levels_le_card and Pnq.sharp_lemma3_zmod, in Pnq/SharpGroup.lean, 67 lines). The three statements in Sharp.lean are mine and were fixed before any proof; the proofs there were written by a fresh instance, following the proof above; SharpGroup.lean is mine. Lean accepts all four with no axiom beyond the standard three. I built the whole folder with them (../lean-sharp-26nov-agent3.log). Noon then read the four statements against the definitions, rebuilt the folder from clean with the same result, and tested both bounds on every subset of Z_12, Z_18 and Z_24, where neither is ever broken (../lean-sharp-rebuild-26nov-agent2.log, ../sharp-brute-agent2.py). Not in Lean: the construction; multisets on the group itself (the tree has them, the bridge is for sets); section 3.

3. The half with Φ_q | A

Here A is balanced. Let a₀ be the first level in 1..n that is not in F, and k′ the number of levels of F∖E deeper than a₀.

  • If F is all of 0..n the minimum is q p^e (every class is balanced, and A is a union of fibres of q elements over a multiset on Z_{pⁿ} with divisors E). Computed: 68 patterns of 68.
  • Otherwise the minimum, in everything computed, is q p^e(p^k′ + x), where x = 0 if one of these holds and x = 1 if none does: (1) k′ = 0; (2) some level shallower than a₀ (one of 1..a₀−1) is in E; (3) a₀ ∈ E and p ≥ 3; (4) a₀ ∈ E, p = 2, and strictly between a₀ and the first level of F∖E deeper than a₀ there is a level in E and not in F. Computed: 518 patterns of 518, among them all 240 at order 48, whose table I did not open until the rule was sealed. (That run began at 03:28:36 and the sheet was last written at 03:29:46; by the rows' own timings about 60 rows were on disk by then, 29 of them among these 240 and four of them rows on which the rule and my first formula differ. The file times cannot show that I had not looked.)

I have no proof of this rule. The picture behind it: above a₀ every class is balanced; at a₀ some children must have shapes that are not zero and add up to zero, which takes q elements' worth of shape, and below a₀ those children branch as in section 2. The summand x is one more fibre, needed for two children of equal shape and unequal total at a level of F∖E; a balanced class that is not empty supplies that for nothing, and clauses (2) to (4) are the three ways I found such a class to be there already. A fourth way would make the rule wrong for larger n. The referee looked for one in larger groups, with a recursion over the tree of classes that uses neither of my solvers (check/dp.c; each minimum it reports has a witness checked by exact division), and found none. The rule gives the minimum on all 992 such patterns of Z_96 (n = 5), on all 240 of Z_162 (p = 3, q = 2, n = 4) and on all 56 of Z_250; on every such pattern it could settle in Z_192, Z_486, Z_1458, Z_686 and Z_1250 (those with a minimum up to 144 in Z_192 and up to 255 in the other four: 3,668, 871, 2,578, 43 and 182 patterns); and, by a CP-SAT model of the referee's own, on the 12 such patterns each of Z_28, Z_63 and Z_44 and on 53 of the 56 of Z_56, the other 3 undecided (check/dp-summary.out, check/cpsat-table-*.out). That is q = 2 and 3 for n ≥ 4; for larger q nothing beyond n = 3.

4. Sets

A set is a multiset with every weight 0 or 1. In the ten groups where sets were computed (n = 2 for six pairs of primes, n = 3 for orders 24, 54 and 40, n = 4 for order 48), 736 patterns have a set and each has a set of the multiset's minimum size; the other patterns tried have none (352 of 1,088). It is not so in general. The referee's recursion over the tree of classes (check/dp.c) finds that in Z_96 (p = 2, q = 3, n = 5) 1,502 patterns have a set; 1,500 of them have a set of the multiset's minimum size, and two do not: E = {1, 4}, F = {0, 5} and E = {2, 4}, F = {0, 1, 5} have a smallest multiset of 24 elements and a smallest set of 36 (a CP-SAT model of the referee's own agrees: no set of 12 or of 24, one of 36; check/cpsat-order96-sets.out). In Z_48 the same two patterns with one empty level fewer have no set at all. Both have 0 ∈ F; among the patterns with 0 ∉ F every one that has a set in Z_96 has a set of the minimum size (813 patterns), and so has every pattern with a set in Z_162 (382) and in Z_250 (90). For Z_24 this repeats my census of 20 November over all its subsets that contain 0 (../pnq-agent3/census24.out, written at 00:39 UTC that day): the same number of patterns, 83, and the same minimum in each of the 79 rows that file prints. One reason for a pattern to have no set rests on a fact in print: a set in Z_pq divisible by Φ_pq is a union of fibres of one kind (Kiss, Malikiosis, Somlai and Vizer, Proposition 3.8(a)). Let 0 ∉ F and let every level 1..n be in F. By (iii) every class is unbalanced, down to the leaves. Two leaves in one class of depth n−1 are 0/1 vectors of equal shape, neither of them constant, so they are equal (this is the fact in print). The classes of depth n−1 inside one class of depth n−2 are then p times such vectors and of equal shape, so they are equal too; and so on up the tree. All leaves are equal, and E must be every level (54 patterns of 54; this accounts for 54 of the 352 patterns that have no set).

5. What was computed

minsize.py finds, for a pattern, the least size at which a multiset (or set) with exactly that pattern exists, by trying sizes upward. Two solvers with separately written encodings: CP-SAT on the class counts of section 1, HiGHS on the remainders of A(X) modulo each Φ_d; every witness is checked a third way, by the complex values A(ζ_d). A pattern is decided when a solver has found a witness and reported every smaller admissible size impossible (admissible: a multiple of p^e, and of q as well when 0 ∈ F, by fact (i)). CP-SAT searches in exact integers. HiGHS works in floating point, so where it alone decided a pattern (25 of the 1,177 in the table below) 'impossible' is that solver's verdict and not a proof.

7. What was already in print, and what is whose

From the search (prior-art.md; 35 requests; the question as posed was not found):

  • Divisors only. With no non-divisor prescribed the minimum on pⁿq is known: Kiss, Łaba, Marshall and Somlai, arXiv:2507.11672, Corollary 7.3.
  • Part of the theorem follows from print. When F is a run of consecutive levels and E a proper subset of it, the bound p^e(p^k + q) follows in two lines from their Proposition 6.3 and Lemma 6.2 (the search's reader's derivation, prior-art.md section 3).
  • |A| ≥ p^|F| from Φ_q ∤ A alone is Łaba and Marshall, arXiv:2202.07555, Corollary 6.4; |A| ≥ p^|E ∪ F| under q ∤ |A| is their Theorem 1.2, and is implicit in Malikiosis, arXiv:2005.05800 (proof of Theorem 6.1).
  • |A| ≥ p^e q for a level in F∖E, with no hypothesis on Φ_q, is inside the proof of Proposition 5.2 of Malikiosis and Kolountzakis, arXiv:1612.01328 (Claims 1 and 2), by the counting of the first case of my Lemma 3. I read those two pages on 26 November. Neither ../paper/weak-pnq.tex nor the joint paper says so yet.
  • The proof in section 2 is the search's reader's, in my words; fact (iii) and the witness are its ideas. My own route to the formula (in expectations.md) argued the bound only when the two children of unequal total sit under an unbalanced class.
  • The rule of section 3 and the statement about sets I have not found anywhere; the reader's sealed file has the first case of the rule (n = 2, E = ∅, F = {0, 2}) worked by hand, and an untested guess that when F has a gap below a level of F∖E the minimum has "the shape p^e·q·(p^k′ + 1) in the simplest case", which is the shape of my first formula; I had sealed that formula before I opened the file. It also remarks that the proof of my Lemma 4 (Lemma 11 of the paper) shows more than the lemma states; I have not checked that.

8. What this is and is not
