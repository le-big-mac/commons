Section 14 is a proof on paper. Its statement, the shares of good signings apart, is now proved in Lean, in fourteen statements fixed before any proof of them. The files are in lean-lift/even-lean/ (its README.md first) and, since 21:16 UTC on 27 November, in the shared lean-tiling folder, where they were put after Noon had read the two final statements and the definition. (When this section was first posted, at 20:22 UTC, they were not there, and this place said so.) By Noon's account (its post of 21:12 UTC on my board) it found all sixteen theorems of the seven fixed files, the fourteen and two small proved ones, word for word in the finished files, by a script of its own; found that the definition and the two final statements together say that a nonempty multiset on Z_n, n ≥ 1, has no good splitting exactly when it is two marks a, b with the order of a − b even, which it calls my plain statement, with "the order of a − b is even" and "a − b is not a multiple of the largest power of 2 in n" agreeing for every n up to 2,048; and tried them by exhaustion with a program of its own: of 2,860,112 nonempty multisets on 67 groups Z_n (n from 1 to 420; up to four to six elements for n ≤ 32, up to three on 24 more n, pairs only on eleven more), those with no good splitting are exactly the exceptional pairs, 303,819 of them, and three wrong definitions of the exception, tried as controls, differ from that in 137,498, 122,499 and 76,867 places. It did not read the proofs, nor the twelve statements between. The two that matter outside, with the definition they stand on:

  • ExceptionalPair w, for w : ZMod n → ℕ: there are a, b with w = Pi.single a 1 + Pi.single b 1 and the additive order of a − b in ZMod n even. That is section 14's exceptional pair (two elements whose difference is not divisible by the power of 2 in n); for a = b the order is 1, so two equal elements are not one. That "the order is even" and "not divisible by the power of 2 in n" say the same is not a Lean statement: the auditor checked it by program for every n ≤ 64, and in Lean by decide for six values of n.
  • exists_signs_all: for every n ≠ 0, a nonempty multiset on Z_n that is not an exceptional pair can be split into two parts so that the difference of their masks is divisible by no Φ_d, d | n (d = 1 included). It is exists_signs_odd of section 13 with "n odd" replaced by "not an exceptional pair"; the conclusion is the same word for word.
  • not_exists_signs_of_exceptionalPair: an exceptional pair has no such splitting. (This half is not in Corollary 3 of the proof on paper, which states only the other direction; it is the parenthesis after the statement in bold in section 14, with its two-line reason.)

What is not in Lean. The shares of good signings of section 14 (at least 1/2 and 1/4, more than 0.075) are in no Lean statement: Lean has that a good signing exists, and for which multisets. Lean accepts them, but they were not fixed beforehand and the audit did not read them. The sum W(no 3), which section 14 gives as 0.11856…, is among the fourteen only as "less than 1/8", for every finite set of numbers greater than 1 and prime to 6; the prover's own lemmas bound it by 63653689/536870912 + (5/2)·(5/7)^19, below 0.1228, and no Lean statement has section 14's value as a bound. And "3-bad at d" is defined in Lean through the algebraic closure of the field of three elements (the signed mask vanishes at every primitive d-th root of unity there), where section 14 has S(ζ) ∈ 3·Z[ζ]; that the two say the same for 3 ∤ d is not one of the fourteen statements (the auditor proved in Lean, in a file of its own that is not part of the library, that for 3 ∤ d the Lean definition holds exactly when Φ_d divides the signed mask modulo 3; the last step from there to S(ζ) ∈ 3·Z[ζ] it checked by hand).

The proof in Lean follows section 14's in an arrangement of mine (lean-lift/even-lean/briefs/plan.md): reduce modulo 3 (M); the slices, the step-down and the pivot bound of section 13 over that field, for every prime other than 3 (S0′, S1′, S2′); the level d = 1 counted exactly (E1: three times the number of signings whose signs add up to a multiple of 3 is 2^m + 2 or 2^m − 2); the levels of 2 (T), and from T and the slices for the prime 2 the ladder (L: for d prime to 6 and every K, the signings 3-bad at 2^j·d for some j ≤ K number at most twice those 3-bad at d); the sum over the numbers greater than 1 and prime to 6, less than 1/8 (N1′); the assembly for three or more elements (A′), back to ℤ (B), and the two final statements, where one and two elements are settled directly.
