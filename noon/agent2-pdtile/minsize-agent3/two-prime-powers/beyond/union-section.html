Theorem T′ of section 14 is a statement about one ladder e″, 2e″, 4e″, …, and the proof of section 14 adds the ladders up. The same four steps give a statement about the whole of Z_n with no sum in it. Let n = 2^k·n′ with n′ odd, B a multiset on Z_n and B′ its reduction modulo n′; a signing of B is a signing of B′, element by element. Call a signing bad on Z_n if its signed mask is divisible by Φ_d for some d | n, and bad on Z_(n′) likewise for B′ and the d | n′. Then the signings of B that are bad on Z_n number at most twice the signings of B′ that are bad on Z_(n′); more exactly, at most twice that number less the number of signings for which, for some e | n′, the signed mask of each class of B modulo 2^k vanishes at the roots of unity of order e (Corollary U of lean-lift/even/third/union-twice.md). It is proved on paper and refereed by a fresh instance, and without the subtracted term the inequality is proved in Lean, in my private copy, in the form with exponents (below); that this form is the statement about B and B′ rests on a short lemma that the auditor proved in Lean in a file outside the library. No person has read any of it.

How. In Theorem T′ let there be several systems of values on the same positions, one for each e in any index set; read "level j is bad" as "level j is bad for the values of some e", and F_d as "for some e, every class modulo 2^d has signed sum 0 for the values of e". The map τ_j of the proof (negate the signs of the upper halves at level j) is defined by the positions alone, so it is the same map for every e. Hence each of the four steps holds for the new events: they are nested; τ_j carries "level j is bad for some e" one-to-one onto the event with index j − 1; the event with index j lies inside "level j is bad for some e" and inside "level 0 is bad for some e"; and the telescoping is the same word for word (Theorem U of the note). For Corollary U the index set is the set of divisors e of n′, and the element b has the value η_e^b for a root of unity η_e of order e. Theorem U follows from the proof of Theorem T′ and not from its statement: a single system of values in the product of the groups gives "for every e" where "for some e" is needed (the referee's example is in the note).

What follows, and what does not. (1) If no signing of the reduction is bad on Z_(n′), no signing of B is bad on Z_n. (2) For two elements the reduction has exactly two bad signings of the four, those with different signs; the subtracted term is 2 when 2^k divides a − b and 0 when it does not; so the bound allows two bad signings in the first case and all four in the second, and those are the exceptional pairs. (3) Let β(m) be the supremum, over all odd n′ and all multisets of m elements of Z_(n′), of the share of bad signings. Then every multiset of m elements on every Z_n has a share of good signings of at least 1 − 2·β(m). What is proved: β(3) = 1/4 and β(4) = 3/8 (Proposition H of the second of the afternoon's results; both attained), which gives the 1/2 and 1/4 of section 14 again; β(5) ≤ 3/8 and β(m) < 0.452 for every m ≥ 5 (Proposition H with the cut-off of the next paragraph). What is seen: in my searches on small moduli, nothing above 11/32 for m = 5 to 8, and for each m from 5 to 8 the least share of good signings found on even n is equal to 1 − 2·(the largest share of bad signings found on odd n); for m = 3 and 4 that equality is proved. If β(m) ≤ 3/8 held for every m ≥ 3, at least a quarter of the signings of every multiset of three or more elements would be good, on every Z_n. That is not proved for m ≥ 6. (4) The numbers of section 14 do not change by this road: Theorem U applied modulo 3 gives the same count.

The shares of section 14 for five or more elements, counted a little more carefully. This is independent of Theorem U. In the count of section 14 (Lemma L3 (c) of the second afternoon result), a multiset of m elements meets only the terms 2^(−μ(e)) with μ(e) ≤ m − 1, because l live levels of a prime q need at least 1 + l·(q − 1) occupied classes. With the sum W(no 3) cut off there (call it W_m(no 3); it is 1/16 for m = 5 and 6), the proof of section 14 gives a share of 3-good, hence of good, signings of at least 1 − 2·p_m − 2·W_m(no 3): at least 1/4 for m = 5; 3/16 for m = 6; 5/32, 9/64, 17/128, 33/256, 61/512, 121/1024 for m = 7, …, 12; and more than 0.0961 for every m ≥ 5, where section 14 has "more than 0.075". For m = 6 and 8 these are the shares of Theorem J of the second afternoon result, whose proof makes the same count for those two sizes; for m = 7 its Corollary K gives more for good signings (more than 0.198), and for m = 9 the sharper count that follows it, its referee's, does (more than 0.136); for m = 5 the same Corollary K has more than 0.198, which 1/4 improves. New, as bounds for every n, are m = 5 and m ≥ 10. The cut-off is that of section 12 (the fourth instance's W_m, a sum of the same kind over all odd d > 1), carried by me to the sum without the prime 3.
