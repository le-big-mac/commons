In short

On Z_M with M = p²q² there are 256 patterns: sets of divisors d > 1 at which Φ_d is to divide the mask A(X) = Σ A(x)·X^x of the multiset, and at no others. An up-set is a pattern that holds, with a divisor, every multiple of it that divides M; its corner sum is the sum of M/c over its corners, the divisors c of M outside it that divide no other divisor outside it. Theorems give the least size for the 19 up-sets (Theorem S of ../README.md). For 55 more, which use only the divisors of p²q or only those of pq², the theorem for Z_{pⁿq} (../../../paper/minsize.pdf) gives a lower bound by reduction, and in Z_36, Z_100 and Z_225 a multiset of that size is on file for every one of them, so that there it is the least size. By a lemma of section 2 that size is reached whenever the prime in which one lifts is odd, so for all 55 when p and q are both odd; when that prime is 2 it is not proved here. [Added 26 November: since proved, on paper and then in Lean; section 7.] For the other 182 there were two tables, Z_36 and Z_100, and no rule. This note adds six things.

  1. A lower bound LB and an upper bound U, neither of which needs a solver. LB puts the two theorems together with a divisibility. U is the smallest size of a multiset built out of cosets by six moves (three when the second sheet below was sealed; three were added afterwards) and checked to have exactly the pattern. For each of the 256 patterns of Z_36, of Z_100 and of Z_225 such a multiset is on file.
  2. For odd p and q the first three moves are proved (two lemmas in section 2). So there the bound from those moves, U₃, is an upper bound on every Z_{p²q²}; the 55 patterns above have the theorem's value as their least size; and on Z_225 the 144 patterns with LB = U₃ are settled with no solver at all.
  3. A third table, Z_225 = 3²·5², the first with both primes odd: 203 of its 256 patterns are decided by the integer programme written by the referee of ../README.md, a fresh instance (HiGHS) at 150 seconds a pattern.
  4. In all three groups the least size, where it is known, is LB or U for all but a few patterns: for 244 of 256 on Z_36, 235 of 242 on Z_100 and 201 of 206 on Z_225. The two bounds coincide for 149, 149 and 148 patterns. The open patterns are not a fair sample: of the 12 that lie strictly between the bounds on Z_36, 11 are among the 50 left open on Z_225 and 5 among the 14 on Z_100, and for 12 of those 50 and 5 of those 14 a multiset smaller than U is already on file.
  5. A sealed prediction held: formulas fitted to the values on Z_36 and Z_100, where one of the two primes is always 2, gave the value on Z_225 for every one of the 116 patterns the run settled, out of 138 predicted. I had put the chance of that at 0.12. Of the thirteen other sealed lines that the run settled, nine held and four did not; and the two sheets are not independent, since the prediction equals U₃ for 134 of the 138 patterns.
  6. Not done. For p = 2, and for stacking and the balanced lift at any primes, U rests on the multisets built, group by group, and not on a proof. There is no rule for the patterns whose least size lies strictly between the bounds, or sits at LB by a construction that the six moves do not make.

1. The lower bound

For a pattern D on Z_M, M = p²q², LB(D) is the least multiple of p^a·q^b that is at least r₁, r₂ and r₃, where:

  • a and b are the numbers of pure powers of p and of q in D. If Φ_{p^u} divides the mask A(X) for a values of u, their product divides A(X), and at X = 1 the product is p^a and A is the size. So p^a divides the size, and likewise q^b.
  • r₁ is the least size that the theorem for Z_{pⁿq} gives, on Z_{p²q}, for the pattern D cut down to the divisors of p²q; r₂ is the same for pq². A multiset on Z_M reduced modulo a divisor N of M is a multiset of the same size on Z_N, and for d dividing N, Φ_d divides the one mask exactly when it divides the other; so its pattern is D cut down to the divisors of N, and its size is at least the least size for that pattern.
  • r₃ is the bound of Corollary P of ../README.md: the corner sum of the largest up-set inside D.

Each of the three is a theorem. (Checked in Lean: the theorem for Z_{pⁿq}, and the bound of Corollary U with the non-divisibility asked at the corners only, lower_bound. On paper only: the reduction modulo N and the divisibility by p^a·q^b, both above, and the two lines of Corollary P. LB itself is not in Lean.) Program: lowerbound.py.

2. The upper bound

Atoms. For c dividing N dividing M: a coset of cZ_N, with each of its N/c elements lifted to Z_M. If the lifts are in general position, its pattern is the set of divisors of N that do not divide c.

The first three moves. - Convolution, A₁ * A₂ (all sums a₁ + a₂): the masks multiply and Φ_d is irreducible, so the pattern is D₁ ∪ D₂ exactly. Sizes multiply. - Sum, A₁ together with a translate of A₂: the pattern contains D₁ ∩ D₂, and is D₁ ∩ D₂ unless a divisor outside D₁ ∪ D₂ comes in by cancellation. Sizes add. - Reduce and lift: reduce modulo a divisor N of M and lift each element again. If the lifts are in general position the pattern becomes D cut down to the divisors of N. Same size.

U₃(D) is the least cost of an expression in the atoms with value exactly D by these three moves; algebra.py finds it by closing the 256 patterns under the moves. For instance {pq, p²q²} costs 2pq: on Z_36 the expression found is (coset of 2Z_6 * coset of 18Z_36) + (coset of 3Z_6 * coset of 12Z_36).

General position, proved for odd primes. Two things are needed: that a multiset can be lifted without a new divisor, and that a sum can be translated without one.

Lemma (lifts; the reader's). (In this lemma M and N are any numbers as stated, not the M = p²q² of the note.) Let r be an odd prime, N = r^k·N′ with r not dividing N′ (k ≥ 0), M = N·r, and A a nonempty multiset on Z_N. Write an element of Z_M as (a, b), a modulo N′ and b modulo r^(k+1). Lift every element of A to (a, b) with 0 ≤ b < r^k, except one, which goes to (a₀, b₀ + r^k). The lifted multiset has exactly the pattern of A.

Proof. For d dividing N nothing changes. A divisor of M that does not divide N is d = d′·r^(k+1) with d′ dividing N′, and a primitive d-th root of unity is ω·η with ω of order d′ and η of order r^(k+1). The mask there is Σ g_b(ω)·η^b over 0 ≤ b < 2r^k, and the coefficient of η^(b₀ + r^k) is ω^(a₀) ≠ 0. The degree of η over Q(ω) is r^k·(r − 1) ≥ 2r^k, since d′ is prime to r and r ≥ 3. So the powers of η up to 2r^k − 1 are linearly independent over Q(ω), and the value is not 0. ∎

I have read this proof and it holds; I also tried it with a program of my own (lift_lemma_own.py): the pattern was kept in 556 of 556 random multisets with r odd, where the plain lift, with no element moved up, kept it in 478 of the same 556; with r = 2 the lemma's lift kept it in 197 of 200, so the lemma does fail there, as its proof allows. The reader's own test is reader/lift_lemma.py. Applying the lemma once for each prime factor of M/N lifts a multiset from Z_N to Z_M with exactly its pattern, when those primes are odd.

Lemma (translates; mine). Let M be odd with two prime factors. For multisets A₁, A₂ on Z_M with patterns D₁, D₂ there is a translate of A₂ whose sum with A₁ has pattern exactly D₁ ∩ D₂.

Proof. A divisor in D₁ ∩ D₂ divides every such sum, and one that is in exactly one of D₁, D₂ divides none. Let d be outside D₁ ∪ D₂. If two translates t and t′ of A₂ both let Φ_d divide the sum, then Φ_d divides (X^t − X^t′)·A₂(X), hence X^(t − t′) − 1, since it is irreducible and does not divide A₂; so d divides t − t′. The translates that let d in therefore lie in one residue class modulo d, and are at most M/d in number. The sum of M/d over the divisors d > 1 of M is σ(M) − M, which is less than M because σ(M)/M < (3/2)·(5/4) for odd M with two prime factors. So some translate lets nothing in. ∎

So for p and q both odd, atoms, convolutions, sums and reduce-and-lift can always be made, and U₃ is an upper bound on Z_{p²q²} (by induction on the expression: each step asks of its inputs only their exact patterns, and each lemma holds for every multiset with those patterns). The same two lemmas serve for any odd pⁿq^m; the reader built every pattern of Z_225 and of Z_441 from its three-move expression in this way, with no random choice (reader/second/odd_end_to_end.out). For p = 2 the count in the second proof is not enough and the first lemma fails, and I have no argument. Apart from any proof, in the three groups realise.py builds, for every pattern, a multiset from an expression of cost U₃ with random lifts and translates and checks its pattern by exact division of polynomials: all 768 were realised with exactly U₃ elements, most at the first attempt (169 of 256 on Z_36, 222 of 256 on Z_100, 248 of 256 on Z_225; never more than 11 attempts). The multisets are in realised-36.jsonl, realised-100.jsonl, realised-225.jsonl.

Three more moves, added after the sheets of section 4 were scored. Looking at the patterns the three moves miss (section 5) gave three more. The first two use the quotient group Z_K, K = N/c, for a divisor c of N: - Dilation: for a multiset B on Z_K, the multiset cB on Z_N has the same size, and its pattern is the set of d with d/gcd(d, c) in the pattern of B. - Stacking: put a multiset B_r on Z_K into each class r + cZ_N, all of the same size w. Every divisor d > 1 of c then divides, because the classes carry equal numbers; a divisor d that does not divide c divides if d/gcd(d, c) is in the pattern of every B_r, and otherwise not, in general position. The size is c·w. The B_r may have different patterns, so what the move needs from Z_K is not the least size of a pattern but which sizes it can have. - Balanced lift: for a prime r with r^j the exact power of r in N, j ≥ 2, and K = N/r: a multiset B on Z_K whose pattern holds r, …, r^(j−1) and whose size is divisible by r^j is lifted to Z_N so that each class modulo r^(j−1) is spread evenly over its r lifts modulo r^j. Same size. The pattern keeps what it had on the divisors of K and gains r^j. For the other prime s, Φ_{r^j·s} comes in exactly when every cell (one class modulo r^(j−1) within one class modulo s) is spread evenly too, which cannot be avoided when each class modulo r^(j−1) lies in one class modulo s. The programs suppose that nothing else comes in. (The criterion for r^j·s is the reader's, with a proof by cuboids in reader-report-2.md; I have followed the proof and it holds.)

algebra2.py (five moves) and algebra3.py (six) keep, for every pattern of every proper quotient group, the set of sizes it reaches (up to twice the group's order), and compute the least cost on Z_M. A multiset built on a quotient group is lifted to the larger group in general position, which keeps its pattern and size. Dilation changes no value in these three groups: without it the five-move bound and the six-move cost are the same for all 768 patterns, and the gain of the five-move bound over U₃ comes only from stacks whose classes carry different patterns. (This is the reader's finding: reader/variants.out for five moves, reader/second/variants-six.out for six.) I have checked it with a program of my own (dilation_check.py). With five moves the bound is below U₃ for the same six patterns in each group ({10 20 22}, {01 02 22}, {01 10 20 22}, {01 02 10 22}, {01 10 11 20 22}, {01 02 10 11 22}, written as exponent pairs: uv stands for the divisor p^u·q^v, so that {01 02 22} is {q, q², p²q²}); realise2.py built all 768 multisets of the five-move bound and checked them.

The balanced lift needs a caution. I had first written that the pattern gains r^j "and, in general position, nothing else", and that is false for some inputs: on Z_225 the nine elements that the derivation builds on Z_{pq²} with pattern {p, pq, pq²} are q times a multiset of Z_{pq}, so all in one class modulo q, and their lift gains Φ_{p²q} as well as Φ_{p²}, every time. Nine elements with the same pattern that meet two classes modulo q lift with Φ_{p²} alone (reader/second/balanced.out). With the six moves realise3.py could not build 8, 7 and 2 of the 256 multisets of Z_36, Z_100 and Z_225 from the derivations algebra3.py had chosen. All 17 are of one kind: somewhere in the derivation a balanced lift is applied to a multiset each of whose classes modulo r^(j−1) lies in one class modulo the other prime s, and then Φ_{r^j·s} comes in whatever the lift. In none of those cases is the six-move cost below the five-move cost, so no bound was lost; but it means that a derivation is not a multiset. From here on U is the smallest size of a multiset that has been built and checked: the five-move bound, except for the four patterns in each group where the balanced lift gives less and its multiset was built ({10 12 20}, {01 02 21}, {01 10 12 20}, {01 02 10 21}; realised3-36.jsonl, realised3-100.jsonl, realised3-225.jsonl); status.py stops if a six-move cost below the five-move cost has no multiset on file. The sizes that the programs derive on the quotient groups rest on the same unproved clauses and are not built one by one; only the multisets on Z_M are.

5. Two patterns outside the first three moves, by hand

(a) D = {q, q², p²q}: in all six readings the least size is LB = q²·⌈(p + 1)/q⌉. Reduced modulo p²q the pattern is {q, p²q}, for which the theorem for Z_{pⁿq} gives q(p + 1); and q² divides the size. So LB is the least multiple of q² that is at least q(p + 1). In the six readings (2,3), (3,2), (2,5), (5,2), (3,5), (5,3) the least size is 9, 8, 25, 12, 25, 18, and that is LB each time, while U₃ and the five-move bound are both 27, 16, 75, 24, 100, 54. The balanced lift makes it: for the pattern {q, p²q} on Z_{p²q} the programs reach every size q·t with t ≥ p + 1, up to their cap (cosets of the subgroup of order q, summed with a stack over the classes modulo q of cosets of the subgroup of order p), and when q divides t the q classes modulo q, which are equal in number, can each be spread evenly over the residues modulo q² above them. The value is not a polynomial in p and q.

(b) D = {q, q², p²q²}: the least size is at least pq² for all p and q, and is pq² in all six readings. Here LB is 9, 8, 25, 8, 25, 9, U₃ is 27, 16, 75, 24, 100, 54 and U is 18, 12, 50, 20, 75, 45 in the six readings. A programme decided five of the six (18, 12, 50, 20, 75); the rest follow from the argument below and the multisets of realised2-*.jsonl, so that the six values are 18, 12, 50, 20, 75, 45.

Why at least pq². Φ_M divides A, so A is a sum of cosets of the subgroup of order p and cosets of the subgroup of order q (Theorem S for the up-set {M}, whose corners are M/p and M/q; this case is Lam and Leung's). Neither Φ_{M/p} nor Φ_{M/q} divides A, so there is at least one coset of each kind (as in the proof of Corollary U). Now reduce modulo q². A coset of the subgroup of order p is x + j·pq², j = 0, …, p − 1: it lands on one residue, p times. A coset of the subgroup of order q is x + j·p²q, j = 0, …, q − 1: it lands once on each of the q residues that lie above x modulo q. Φ_q and Φ_{q²} divide A exactly when every residue modulo q² carries the same number w of elements, and then the size is q²·w. Take a residue that carries α ≥ 1 cosets of the first kind, and let β be the number of cosets of the second kind above its class modulo q: w = pα + β ≥ p. So the size is at least pq². ∎

What the example shows: one level down, a coset from the top is either a weighted point or a set spread evenly, and a divisor at the lower level is an equality between counts. The first three moves of section 2 place their pieces in general position and never use such equalities; the theorem for Z_{pⁿq} is full of them. Stacking is the simplest move that does: it gives pq² here (q classes modulo q, each with pq elements, either q cosets of the subgroup of order p or p cosets of the subgroup of order q, with at least one class of each kind), so with the argument above pq² is the least size wherever a multiset of that size with exactly the pattern has been built, which is in all six readings (in the files the two kinds are arranged at random). (In bounds-225.md the sixth reading still shows as open between LB and U, because the tables use LB and not this argument.)

6. What is open

  • A proof that the moves can be made in general position when p = 2, and that stacking can at any primes (the pattern of a dilation is exact); and for the balanced lift a full statement of what it lets in: the criterion of section 2 covers r^j·s and not r^j·s². With these, wherever LB and U agree as formulas the least size would be a theorem for every Z_{p²q²}.
  • More moves that place pieces so that a lower divisor comes in; and with them, perhaps, a rule. One is plainly missing: a move that equalises counts in two directions at once. From lifts to signs (mine). Write N = 2^k·N′ with N′ odd. An element of Z_N has two lifts to Z_{2N}, a low one and a high one (their residues modulo 2^(k+1) differ by 2^k). For d′ dividing N′, the polynomial Φ_{d′·2^(k+1)} divides the mask of the lift exactly when, for every class modulo 2^k, the signed mask of the elements in that class, taken modulo N′, is divisible by Φ_{d′}; this is the count of roots again, with 2^k roots and a polynomial of degree below 2^k. 8. Added 00:53 UTC, 27 November: on suitable larger groups the least multisets can be taken to be sets

This section was added after section 7 had been read. A fresh instance refereed it, with the paper's subsection and the plain page's paragraphs: it read the proof line by line, tested the proposition and the corollary with programs of its own, and checked the numbers below against the outputs; it then read the changes made after its report, the paragraph on the prime 2 among them (its reports are with the files of this section, lean-lift/sets/referee/).

The theorem for Z_{pⁿq} is about multisets, and on Z_{pⁿq} itself a pattern may have no set at all. Lifting changes that. When a multiset on Z_N is lifted to Z_{N·r}, r an odd prime, the copies of one element need not go to the same lift: they can be spread over its r lifts. The count of roots of the lemma on lifts still gives a criterion. Write N = r^k·N′, and call the slot of a lift y the number ⌊(y mod r^(k+1)) / r^k⌋, from 0 to r − 1; the r lifts of an element lie in the r slots. For d = e·r^(k+1) with e dividing N′, and ω of order e: Φ_d divides the mask of the lift exactly when, in every class modulo r^k, the sum of ω^y over the elements of the lift in that class and in the slot t is the same for all t. So a lift has no new divisor as soon as, in one class, the slots 0 and r − 1 differ by one or two copies of a single element and agree in every other element. If some multiplicity is not divisible by r, that can be arranged while the copies of each element are spread as evenly as possible (r ≥ 3 leaves a middle slot for the odd copies), and the step divides the multiplicities by r, rounded up. If every multiplicity is divisible by r, one element with r·a copies gets a + 1, a, …, a, a − 1 instead; the new multiplicities then stay at most r^(i−1) only if r·a is less than the bound r^i, which is why the multiplicities must not all be equal to the bound.

Proposition (mine). Let r be an odd prime and A a nonempty multiset on Z_N whose elements have multiplicity at most r^j, not all of them r^j. Then A is the reduction of a set on Z_{N·r^j} with the same number of elements and exactly the same cyclotomic divisors. (If all its elements have multiplicity r^j, the only set that reduces to A is the whole preimage of its support, which has every new divisor.)

Corollary. Let m be the least size that the theorem gives for a pattern D of Z_{pⁿq}. If s is divisible by r^j, r an odd prime, with r^j > m, then the least size of a set on Z_{pⁿq·s} with pattern exactly D, among the divisors of pⁿq·s, is m too. The bound r^j > m is crude: what is needed is a least multiset with multiplicities at most r^j.

The proofs are written out in the paper, ../../../paper/minsize.pdf, section "Larger groups", under "Sets on larger groups" (of the criterion, the direction that is used; the converse is not written there). The proposition and the corollary are also proved in Lean (the paragraph "In Lean" at the end of this section); the criterion, the remark that multiplicities at most r^j are enough, and what is said of the prime 2 are not among the statements proved there. Lifting a multiset on Z_N to a set, keeping the divisors Φ_s with s dividing N, is in Kiss, Łaba, Marshall and Somlai: in their Section 2.1 (after (2.2)) the copies of a go to a, a + N, a + 2N, …, a set of integers and so a set on every large enough group, with no attention to other divisors (it can gain one: {0, 0, 1} on Z_2 goes to {0, 1, 2} on Z_6, whose mask is Φ_3); in their Section 8 the lift is made to gain a divisor. What is added here is a lift that gains none, and the condition for it. I made two more searches for it (lean-lift/search-in-print.md, at the end) and did not find it; that is weak evidence. The passage of their Section 2.1 I had not seen; the referee of this section pointed to it.

For r = 2 the proposition is false as it stands: on Z_2 the multiset with 0 four times and 1 twice has no cyclotomic divisor, and each of its six lifts to a set on Z_8 gains Φ_4 or Φ_8 (two_fails.py). 9. Added 00:53 UTC, 27 November: the lemma on signs for squarefree n; the stronger form is false

This section reports work of another instance. It was added after sections 7 and 8 had been read, and was read by a fresh instance, which refereed the proof itself line by line and tested it with programs of its own, and then read what was changed after its report (its two reports, with its programs, are in lean-lift/sign-general/referee-of-the-author/).

Section 7 left the lemma on signs open from n = 1155 on, and said of the stronger form (all signs + or one sign turned is always enough) that it was neither proved nor refuted. I put both to a further fresh instance, with what was known (lean-lift/brief-sign-general.md). Its text is lean-lift/sign-general-result.md, its programs lean-lift/sign-general/. None of the mathematics that follows is mine, and none of it is in Lean.

Proved there. The lemma on signs holds for every squarefree odd n; for every odd n in which at most one prime is repeated; and for further n given by a recursion (its Theorem 6). With the two cases of section 7 that is every odd n below 118125 = 3³·5⁴·7; among the odd n below 200,000 only 118125 and 165375 = 3³·5³·7² are covered by nothing (its coverage.py). The general case is still open.

How. Write n = m·q^b with q a prime that does not divide m (or take m = n, and no q). Z_m is read as a box with one coordinate for every base-p digit, and the signs are of product form: one function with values ±1 on each coordinate, multiplied. A function on the box is good if at every set of coordinates some alternating sum over a cube is not 0; by the two lemmas of the first instance (descent modulo q; functions that sum to 0 over cosets) a signing whose fold to Z_m is good modulo q (for m = n: good over the integers) has no cyclotomic divisor. The new step (its Lemma 3 and Theorem P) is a count: splitting off one coordinate of size p at a time, at least the fraction 2^−k of all product patterns on a box with k coordinates makes a given nonzero function good, provided the sides are odd, the j-th smallest side is at least 2j, and q divides no side. The count is what carries the induction: the hypothesis "a good pattern exists" alone does not. Distinct odd primes satisfy the condition on the sides, which gives the squarefree case and, with q the repeated prime, the case of one repeated prime; many equal small sides (3, 3, 3) do not, and that is where it stops.

The stronger form is false. With the nine primes 3, 5, …, 29 (the first odd primes whose reciprocals add up to more than 1) and one further prime r, a factor of the number of elements, the instance builds a multiset on Z_n, n = 3·5·7·11·13·17·19·23·29·r, in which, for each of the nine primes p, the class 0 modulo p holds exactly two elements more than every other class, every element is 0 modulo one of the nine, and the classes modulo r are equally full. Then all signs + is divisible by Φ_r, and turning the sign of any one element makes the classes modulo one of the nine primes equal, so that Φ_p divides. With r = 103 this is a multiset on Z_333189201345, of about 3.3·10^18 elements. It is a set only if r is at least the largest number of elements that agree modulo all nine primes, and no prime factor of the number of elements is. With every multiplicity tripled, except that of the two elements that are 0 modulo all nine primes, r = 7735396963941761 will do: that is a set, on Z_n with n = 25022822684488282627989015. In everything I had searched, and in the instance's own searches on Z_105, Z_1155, Z_1575, Z_3465 and Z_5005, all signs + or one turned sign was enough. A counterexample of this kind, in which every single turn fails at a prime, needs at least nine primes besides r: the classes 0 must hold every element, and for that the reciprocals of the primes must add up to 1, nearly. Whether a smaller counterexample of another kind exists, with turns that fail at a composite d, the instance did not settle.

11. Added 07:33 UTC, 27 November: the lemma on signs for every multiset of at most 36 elements

This section reports a note of mine. It was added after sections 7 to 10 had been read. A fresh instance refereed the note twice, as told below, and then read this section against the note and its own two reports; it required seven changes of wording, which are made in its words (its third report is with the other two).

Sections 9 and 10 carry the lemma on signs to every odd n below 6587407633447265625 and to some larger n; in general it is open. This section bounds the multiset and not n. For every odd n and every nonempty multiset on Z_n with at most 36 elements the lemma on signs holds; more generally, whenever at most 36 elements of Z_n have odd multiplicity in the multiset (equal elements are signed in cancelling pairs, a remark of the first instance). The note is lean-lift/small/small-multisets.md (its Theorem S).

How. Write the residues modulo p^a (the power of p in n) in base p. The residues that agree modulo p^(i−1), that is in their i − 1 lowest digits, form a group at level i, with p subclasses by the next digit, the classes modulo p^i (the tree of section 10). If at some level every group that holds an element of the multiset has a subclass that holds none, that digit can be deleted from every element (its residue modulo n/p^a is kept): the multiset moves to Z_{n/p}, and every signing that is good there is good on Z_n (the note's Lemma 2, the contraction lemma; the converse is false in general and is not needed). Contracting as long as possible leaves a multiset in which every level of every tree has a group with all its p subclasses occupied. With m elements that allows at most ⌊(m − 1)/(p − 1)⌋ levels for the prime p, and no prime above m. So a multiset of m elements ends on a group whose order divides n′_m = ∏ p^⌊(m−1)/(p−1)⌋, the product over the odd primes p ≤ m, and n′_m divides n′_36 when m ≤ 36. Theorem 7 of section 10 covers n′_36 (with the prime 3 taken out as q, Γ > 0, about 0.001), and the lemma on signs passes from n to every divisor of n (the note's Lemma 4: multiply the elements by the quotient). Theorem 7 does not cover n′_37. A multiset with more elements still has a good signing as soon as the lemma on signs holds on the group it contracts to, for instance when at most two primes keep a level.

Whose it is. The reduction is mine: deleting the level from the group, the group of order dividing n′_m on which a multiset of m elements ends (the note's Proposition 3), and the use for few elements. Its two ingredients are in the third instance's proof of its Lemma T: the levels that cannot be contracted are the levels it calls possible, the step that uses the empty subclass is its Fact 2 with Fact 3, and the count of the depth is the count of that proof. Proposition 4 of the first result (lean-lift/sign-lemma-result.md) is a part of the case in which every level can be contracted. The theorem that finishes the proof, Theorem 7, is the third instance's. That a prime larger than the number of elements can be dropped also follows from a theorem in print (H. B. Mann, 1965, as B. Poonen and M. Rubinstein quote it); the note says what I read of it and what I searched (five searches; I did not find the lemma on signs for small multisets, or the contraction, in print).

How it was checked. A fresh instance refereed the note twice (lean-lift/small/referee/report.md, report-2.md). It found no error in the mathematics. It recomputed Γ for the shapes n′_m with a program written from the definition: positive for 7 ≤ m ≤ 36, not positive for 37 ≤ m ≤ 60. It tested the contraction lemma by exhaustion on 16 small groups (32,258,961 signings, no violation), and it searched a good signing for 903 multisets of 5 to 36 elements on nine groups of order up to 2.9·10^33, 670 of them on four groups that none of the earlier theorems covers: it found one each time and confirmed it exactly (847 by long division; the 56 on the largest group by an exact product in its place). 12. Added 11:21 UTC, 27 November: the lemma on signs for every odd n and every multiset

The instance that refereed the result, as told below, then read this section against the result as revised, its own two reports, the author's two replies, my brief, the note of section 11, Lemma T of the third result, the two reports of the search, the lines of Meckes's paper and of the preprint that I say below I read, my sealed sheet with its scoring, and section 7 for what is in Lean; it ran my program for the constants and counted its own figures again. It did not repeat the search or read the other papers that the search names, and it did not check the time of posting, the paths or anything in Lean.

Section 11 bounds the multiset. This section needs no bound. For every odd n and every nonempty multiset on Z_n the lemma on signs holds: each element can be given a sign so that the signed mask is divisible by no Φ_d, d | n. It is Theorem 4 of a fourth fresh instance (lean-lift/four/sign-four-result.md), which I asked for with the three earlier results, their referee's reports and the note of section 11 in hand. More exactly: of the 2^m ways of signing m elements, counted with multiplicity, the share of good ones is more than 0.1549 if m is odd, more than 0.039 if m is even and at least 14, and at least 1/2, 3/8, 3/16, 9/128, 11/256, 23/1024 for m = 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12.

How. Contract the multiset as in section 11 until no level can be contracted: then every level of every tree has a group with all its p subclasses occupied, and a signing that is good for the contracted multiset is good for the first. Now let each element take its sign by a fair coin, independently of the others. Let d > 1 divide the order of the contracted group, and let p^i be the power of a prime p in d. By the slice criterion (Lemma 1 of the note of section 11), Φ_d divides the signed mask only if in every group at level i of the p-tree the p subclasses have slices that are congruent modulo Φ_{d/p^i}. For a group with k occupied subclasses that happens for at most the share 2^(−min(k, p − 1)) of the signs of its own elements, and different groups have no element in common. Because the levels below i have full groups, at least 1 + (i − 1)(p − 1) groups at level i hold an element, and one of them is full; so the share of signings for which Φ_d divides the mask is at most 2^(−i(p−1)). With the best prime of d it is at most 2^(−μ(d)), where μ(d) is the largest v_p(d)·(p − 1) over the primes p of d. The number of odd d with μ(d) ≤ t is ∏ (1 + ⌊t/(p − 1)⌋), the product over the odd primes p, far smaller than 2^t, and the sum of 2^(−μ(d)) over all odd d > 1 is a constant, W = 0.84447…, less than 1 whatever n and the multiset are. With an odd number of elements Φ_1 never divides the mask (the sum of the signs is odd), so some signing is good. With an even number m, Φ_1 divides the mask for the share C(m, m/2)/2^m of the signings. For m ≤ 12 only the d with μ(d) ≤ m − 1 can occur, and the six sums are below 1 as they stand (the largest, at m = 12, is 1001/1024). From m = 14 on the level d = 3 is counted more exactly (at most 5/32 in place of 1/4), and the sum is below 0.961.

Whose it is. The count is the fourth instance's: one prime charged for each level, the constant W, the case of an even number of elements, and the theorem. It stands on the contraction of section 11, which is mine, and on the slice criterion of the same note; and its bound for one prime, 2^(−i(p−1)), is the bound of the third instance's Lemma T, carried over from sign vectors on a tree to independent signs on the elements. Two leads of my brief to it, given there as unchecked thoughts, are in it: to give each element its own independent sign, a level then costing a power of 1/2 that grows with the number of occupied classes; and that a sum over the levels, weighted by how many elements they need, might converge. The head of its text says what it took from each, in words that its referee asked for and its author agreed to (lean-lift/four/sign-four-revision-1.md has the author's own account). Theorem 7, the recursion Γ and the descent modulo q of sections 9 and 10 are not used, so the result does not depend on which n those cover.
