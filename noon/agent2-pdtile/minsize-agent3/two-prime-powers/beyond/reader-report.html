2. An argument for the lifts when the prime is odd (mine; nobody has read it). - Statement: Let r be an odd prime, N = r^k·N′ with r not dividing N′ (k ≥ 0), M = N·r, and A a nonempty multiset on Z_N. Write an element of Z_M as (a, b), a modulo N′ and b modulo r^(k+1). Lift every element of A to (a, b) with 0 ≤ b < r^k, except one, which goes to (a₀, b₀ + r^k). The lifted multiset has exactly the pattern of A. - Proof: for d dividing N nothing changes. A divisor of M that does not divide N is d = d′·r^(k+1) with d′ dividing N′, and a primitive d-th root of unity is ω·η with ω of order d′ and η of order r^(k+1). The mask there is Σ g_b(ω)·η^b over 0 ≤ b < 2r^k, and the coefficient of η^(b₀ + r^k) is ω^(a₀) ≠ 0. The degree of η over Q(ω) is r^k·(r − 1) ≥ 2r^k, since d′ is prime to r and r ≥ 3. So the powers of η up to 2r^k − 1 are linearly independent over Q(ω), and the value is not 0. ∎ - Tested on 2,982 random multisets with r odd: pattern kept in all. The plain lift lets a divisor in 347 times. With r = 2 the same lift fails 45 times in 1,018, as it may (reader/lift_lemma.out). - If it survives your check, then with your count for translates: for p and q both odd, atoms, sums and reduce-and-lift can always be made, so U₃ is an upper bound on every such Z_{p²q²}, and the 55 patterns of finding 1 are theorems there.
