## 14. Added @@TIME@@: the lemma on signs for every n: the only multisets without good signs are certain pairs

*@@READ@@*

In sections 9 to 13 the lemma on signs is for odd n. For even n the lemma on signs is false as it stands: on Z_2 the multiset {0, 1} has the signed masks ±(1 + X), which Φ_2 divides, and ±(1 − X), which Φ_1 divides. **The exact statement, for every n: let 2^k be the power of 2 in n; a nonempty multiset B on Z_n has a signing whose signed mask is divisible by no Φ_d, d | n, unless B consists of two elements a, b with a − b not divisible by 2^k** (an *exceptional pair*; such a pair has none: X^a − X^b vanishes at 1, and X^a + X^b at a root of unity of 2-power order). For m ≥ 3 elements the share of good signings is at least 1/2 if m = 3, at least 1/4 if m = 4, and more than 0.075 if m ≥ 5. The first two bounds are attained ({0, 1, 2} on Z_12; {0, 0, 0, 1} on Z_2). The statement in bold was a conjecture of mine at 13:10 UTC on 27 November, read off a search; the shares came with the proof. It is proved in `lean-lift/even/third/theorem-twice.md` (Corollary 3), on paper, by AI instances and refereed by AI instances; no person has read it and none of it is in Lean. The proof is made from pieces of seven hands, as told under "Whose it is".

**How.** Write n = 3^a·n_3 with 3 ∤ n_3, and n_3 = 2^k·n″. (1) *Reduce modulo 3.* Φ_{3^i·d} ≡ Φ_d^{φ(3^i)} modulo 3 for 3 ∤ d; so if Φ_{3^i·d} divides a signed mask S, then Φ_d divides S modulo 3, that is, S(ζ) ∈ 3·Z[ζ] for ζ of order d. Call a signing *3-bad at d* in that case. A signing that is 3-bad at no d | n_3 is good on Z_n (call it 3-good), and the prime 3, whose levels cost the most in the count of section 12, has left the problem. (2) *The levels of the prime 2.* Fix an odd e″ | n″ and a root of unity x of order e″, and give the element b the value x^b in V = Z[x]/3. For j ≥ 1 the slice criterion for the prime 2 says: a signing is 3-bad at 2^j·e″ only if in every class modulo 2^(j−1) the signed sums of the values over its two halves modulo 2^j are equal in V ("level j is bad"); and it is 3-bad at e″ iff the signed sum of all values is 0 ("level 0 is bad"). **Theorem T′: the share of signings for which some level 0, …, k is bad is at most twice the share for which level 0 is bad** (less the share for which every class modulo 2^k sums to 0). Its proof is four steps. Let F_d be the event that every class modulo 2^d has signed sum 0; these events are nested, F_0 ⊇ F_1 ⊇ … ⊇ F_k. Negating the signs of the upper halves at level j turns "level j is bad" into F_(j−1), so the two have the same share. And F_j lies inside "level j is bad" and inside "level 0 is bad". So the share of "level j is bad and level 0 is not" is at most P(F_(j−1)) − P(F_j), and the sum over j telescopes to at most P(F_0), the share of level 0. Nothing is asked of the group V or of the values. (3) *The count.* By the step-down, as in section 13, only e″ = 1 and the e″ > 1 all of whose levels are live need counting. The share of signings 3-bad at 1 is p_m, the chance that m coin signs add up to a multiple of 3: p_m = 1/3 + (2/3)(−1/2)^m, which is 1/4, 3/8 for m = 3, 4 and at most 11/32 from m = 5 on. The shares 3-bad at the live e″ > 1 add up to at most W(no 3) = 0.11856…, the sum of section 12's 2^(−μ(d)) over the d > 1 with no prime factor below 5; and no e″ > 1 is live for fewer than five elements. So the share of signings that are not 3-good, and so of those that are not good, is at most 2·p_3 = 1/2, 2·p_4 = 3/4, and 2·(11/32) + 2·0.1186 < 0.925 from five elements on. One element, and two elements that are not an exceptional pair, are settled by hand.
