0. Summary

The statement on signs. n odd, B a nonempty multiset on Z_n. Each element of B can be given a sign ±1 (equal elements may get different signs) so that the signed mask S(X) = Σ_{x ∈ B} ± X^x is divisible by no cyclotomic polynomial Φ_d with d | n. A signing with this property is called good.

It is proved for some n, among them all odd n below 6587407633447265625 (by the three results sign-lemma-result.md, sign-general-result.md, sign-all-result.md), and open in general.

Theorem S (here). The statement on signs holds for every odd n and every multiset B on Z_n with at most 36 elements. More generally it holds whenever at most 36 elements of Z_n have odd multiplicity in B.

So the size of n does not matter, only the size of B. The proof is a reduction (Lemma 2, the contraction lemma) from (n, B) to a group whose order divides

n′_m = ∏ over odd primes p ≤ m of p^⌊(m−1)/(p−1)⌋, m = the number of elements of B,

followed by Theorem 7 of the third result, which covers n′_m for m ≤ 36 and not for m = 37 (small_cover3.py).

Consequence for lifting along 2 (Lemma 18 of the paper minsize.tex): for every N ≥ 1 and every nonempty multiset A on Z_N with at most 36 elements there is a multiset A′ on Z_2N of the same size, whose reduction modulo N is A, such that for every divisor d of 2N: Φ_d | A′ iff d | N and Φ_d | A. (The proof of Lemma 18 uses the statement on signs once: for the odd part N′ of N and the residues modulo N′ of the elements of A in one class modulo the power of 2 in N that holds an element; they are at most |A|, with the multiplicities they have in A. So it is enough that one such class has at most 36 elements of odd multiplicity.)

More than Theorem S (§3; the form is the referee's suggestion): every nonempty multiset B, of whatever size, has a good signing as soon as the statement on signs holds for the group Z_{n♭(B)} to which B contracts; n♭(B) is the product of p^{r_p}, where r_p is the number of levels of the p-tree at which some group has all its p subclasses occupied by B. For instance whenever at most two primes have such a level (Theorem 2 of the first result).

Not proved: multisets with 37 or more elements of odd multiplicity on a general Z_n; the statement on signs in general.

1. Notation: the p-tree and slices

Let n be odd, p a prime with p^a ∥ n (a ≥ 1), n = p^a·n″. By the Chinese remainder theorem Z_n = Z_{p^a} × Z_{n″}, and an element x is written (x mod p^a, x mod n″). The digits of u ∈ Z_{p^a} are u_0, …, u_{a−1} ∈ {0, …, p−1} with u = Σ u_i p^i. A class at depth i (0 ≤ i ≤ a) is a residue class modulo p^i, that is, a choice of the digits u_0, …, u_{i−1}; a class at depth i − 1 contains p classes at depth i, its subclasses, and together with them it is called a group at level i (1 ≤ i ≤ a).

Fix a signing of B. For a class C of the p-tree let

S_C(X) = Σ_{x ∈ B, (x mod p^a) ∈ C} ± X^{x mod n″} in Z[X]/(X^{n″} − 1)

(the slice of the signed mask over C). A class that holds no element of B has slice 0, and the slice of a class is the sum of the slices of its subclasses. For d″ | n″ the polynomial Φ_{d″} divides X^{n″} − 1, so "Φ_{d″} divides" and "congruent modulo Φ_{d″}" make sense for slices.

The words of this section are those of §1 of the third result; Lemma 1 is the counterpart, for divisibility by Φ_{d″}, of the slice lemmas of the two later results (Lemma 1 of the second, Lemma 1T of the third).

Lemma 1 (slice criterion). Let d″ | n″. (a) Φ_{d″} | S iff Φ_{d″} | S_T, where T is the class at depth 0 (all of Z_{p^a}). (b) For 1 ≤ i ≤ a: Φ_{p^i d″} | S iff in every group at level i the p subclasses have slices that are congruent to one another modulo Φ_{d″}.

Proof. (a) S ≡ S_T modulo X^{n″} − 1, and Φ_{d″} divides X^{n″} − 1. In detail: S(η) = S_T(η) for every d″-th root of unity η, since η^x = η^{x mod n″}.

(b) Put d = p^i d″. The primitive d-th roots of unity are the products ωη with ω a primitive p^i-th root of unity and η a primitive d″-th root of unity; for x ∈ Z_n, (ωη)^x = ω^{u}·η^{w} with u = x mod p^i and w = x mod n″. Hence

S(ωη) = Σ_{u ∈ Z_{p^i}} S_u(η)·ω^u,

where S_u is the slice of the class u at depth i. Fix η. The polynomial P_η(Z) = Σ_u S_u(η) Z^u has degree less than p^i and coefficients in the field K = Q(η). It vanishes at all primitive p^i-th roots of unity iff Φ_{p^i}(Z) divides it in K[Z] (the roots are distinct). Now Φ_{p^i}(Z) = 1 + Z^{p^{i−1}} + … + Z^{(p−1)p^{i−1}}, and a multiple g·Φ_{p^i} of degree less than p^i has deg g < p^{i−1}, so its coefficient at Z^u is the coefficient of g at Z^{u mod p^{i−1}}. So P_η is a multiple of Φ_{p^i} iff S_u(η) = S_{u′}(η) whenever u ≡ u′ modulo p^{i−1}. This holds for every primitive d″-th root η iff Φ_{d″} divides S_u − S_{u′} for all such u, u′, which is the statement. ∎

In words: the signing is bad at the level (i, d″) iff inside every group at level i all slices agree modulo Φ_{d″} (for i = 0: iff the whole slice is 0 modulo Φ_{d″}). It is good iff it is bad at no level (i, d″), 0 ≤ i ≤ a, d″ | n″.

2. The contraction lemma

Let 1 ≤ t ≤ a. Deleting the digit of index t − 1 is the map τ : Z_{p^a} → Z_{p^{a−1}},

(u_0, …, u_{a−1}) ↦ (u_0, …, u_{t−2}, u_t, …, u_{a−1}),

and on Z_n = Z_{p^a} × Z_{n″} the map x ↦ (τ(x mod p^a), x mod n″) into Z_{n/p} = Z_{p^{a−1}} × Z_{n″}. The image of B under it, element by element, is a multiset B♭ on Z_{n/p} with the same number of elements; a signing of B is at the same time a signing of B♭.

Call the level t of the p-tree contractible for B if every group at level t that holds an element of B has a subclass that holds none. (For t = 1 the only group is the whole tree: some residue modulo p is taken by no element of B.)

Lemma 2 (contraction). If the level t of the p-tree is contractible for B, then every signing that is good for B♭ on Z_{n/p} is good for B on Z_n.

Proof. Write S_C for the slices of B and S♭_C for those of B♭, with the same signs; the second coordinate x mod n″ of an element is not changed by the map, so the slices are sums of the same terms. The classes of the two trees correspond as follows.

  • For j ≤ t − 1: a class at depth j of the tree of B♭ is given by digits (u_0, …, u_{j−1}), and it holds the images of exactly the elements of the class (u_0, …, u_{j−1}) of the tree of B. So S♭_C = S_C for these classes.
  • For j ≥ t: a class at depth j − 1 of the tree of B♭ is given by digits (u_0, …, u_{t−2}, u_t, …, u_{j−1}), and it holds the images of the elements of the p classes (u_0, …, u_{t−2}, e, u_t, …, u_{j−1}), e = 0, …, p − 1, at depth j of the tree of B. So its slice is the sum of those p slices.

Suppose the signing is bad for B at some level (i, d″). We show that it is bad for B♭ at some level.

i < t. The classes at depth ≤ i ≤ t − 1 and their slices are the same for B and B♭, so by Lemma 1 the signing is bad for B♭ at (i, d″).

i = t. In every group at level t all slices agree modulo Φ_{d″}. A group that holds an element has an empty subclass, whose slice is 0; so all its slices are ≡ 0, and so is their sum, the slice of the class at depth t − 1 that the group belongs to. A group that holds no element has all slices 0. Hence every class at depth t − 1 has slice ≡ 0 modulo Φ_{d″}. If t − 1 ≥ 1 this makes all slices in every group at level t − 1 agree, and if t − 1 = 0 it says S_T ≡ 0: either way the signing is bad for B at (t − 1, d″), and by the case i < t it is bad for B♭ at (t − 1, d″).

i > t. In every group at level i of the tree of B, that is for all fixed digits (u_0, …, u_{i−2}), the slices of the p classes (u_0, …, u_{i−2}, v), v = 0, …, p − 1, agree modulo Φ_{d″}. A group at level i − 1 of the tree of B♭ is given by (u_0, …, u_{t−2}, u_t, …, u_{i−2}), and the slice of its subclass with last digit v is Σ_{e} S_{(u_0, …, u_{t−2}, e, u_t, …, u_{i−2}, v)}. For each e the summand does not depend on v modulo Φ_{d″}; so the sums agree, and the signing is bad for B♭ at (i − 1, d″) (note i − 1 ≥ t ≥ 1). ∎

The converse is false in general (a signing can be good for B and bad for B♭; in the tests of §6 this happened for 23 of 5,802 signings); it is not needed. The hypothesis is used in the case i = t only, and that case is in the proof of Lemma T of the third result: a contractible level is a level that is not "possible" there, and the step is its Fact 2 with Fact 3. (For t = a the contraction is reduction modulo n/p, and the converse holds with no hypothesis.)

Two special cases, which were my first versions of the lemma:

  • A level without branching (any two elements of B that agree modulo p^{t−1} agree modulo p^t): every group that holds an element has p − 1 ≥ 2 empty subclasses. Here the converse holds too: each class of B♭ at depth ≥ t then holds the elements of one class of B.
  • A prime larger than the number of elements (p > m): every level is contractible and stays so, the whole p-tree can be deleted, and B♭ is in the end the image of B in Z_{n″}. So a signing that is good for B modulo n/p^a is good for B. This case also follows from a theorem in print, see §5. When every prime factor of n is larger than m it is Proposition 4 of the first result (whose hypothesis, one digit value missed by the whole support at every level, then holds).

3. How far a multiset can be contracted

Contract levels, of any of the trees of n, as long as there is a contractible one; the number of elements m does not change, the group gets smaller (each step divides n by a prime, so the process ends), and by Lemma 2 a good signing of the last multiset is a good signing of the first. After a contraction the hypothesis has to be looked at again: the order matters. On Z_9 with B = {0, 3, 7} both levels are contractible; contracting level 1 first ends on Z_3, level 2 first on Z_1 (the referee's example). Contracting from the deepest level (level a) up to level 1 deletes exactly the levels that are contractible for B itself: when level t is reached only digits of index ≥ t have been deleted, and that does not change which values the digit of index t − 1 takes inside a class at depth t − 1. No contractible level is left after this pass: a level at which some group is full keeps a full group when a lower level is deleted, since the groups of the higher level are then merged. (The canonical form and the order are the referee's suggestion; it tested them on 20,000 multisets.) Done for every prime, this ends on the group Z_{n♭(B)} with n♭(B) = ∏ p^{r_p}, r_p the number of levels of the p-tree of B at which some group has all its p subclasses occupied. In the last multiset no level of any tree is contractible: at every level i of every p-tree some group has all its p subclasses occupied. Let N_i be the number of classes at depth i of the p-tree that hold an element (N_0 = 1). Every class that holds an element has at least one subclass that does, and one of them has p; so N_i ≥ N_{i−1} + p − 1, and N_a ≥ 1 + a(p − 1). As N_a ≤ m, the depth a of the p-tree is at most ⌊(m − 1)/(p − 1)⌋. In particular the primes p > m have gone. (This count is the count in the proof of Lemma T of the third result. The bound cannot be improved for this reduction: the residues 0 and j·p^i, 1 ≤ j < p, 0 ≤ i < a, have a full group at every level, and by the Chinese remainder theorem all primes can attain it at once; the referee's remark.) So:

Proposition 3. Every multiset B of m elements on Z_n (n odd) can be contracted to a multiset B♭ of m elements on Z_{n♭}, where n♭ divides n′m = ∏{p ≤ m} p^⌊(m−1)/(p−1)⌋ (odd primes p), such that every good signing of B♭ is a good signing of B.

Lemma 4 (divisors). If the statement on signs holds for n, it holds for every divisor n_0 of n.

Proof. Let B_0 be a multiset on Z_{n_0}, k = n/n_0, and B = {k·x : x ∈ B_0} on Z_n. A signing of B_0 with mask S_0 gives the mask S(X) = S_0(X^k) for B. If Φ_{d_0} | S_0 with d_0 | n_0, take a primitive root of unity ζ of order d_0·k (a divisor of n); ζ^k is a primitive d_0-th root of unity, so S(ζ) = S_0(ζ^k) = 0 and Φ_{d_0 k} | S. So a good signing of B is a good signing of B_0. ∎

Theorem R. Let m ≥ 1. If the statement on signs holds for n′_m, then every multiset of at most m elements on Z_n, for every odd n, has a good signing.

Proof. n′_{m′} divides n′_m for m′ ≤ m. Proposition 3, then Lemma 4. ∎

4. Theorem S

Theorem 7 of the third result (sign-all-result.md, §5): if n = m′·q^b (q an odd prime not dividing m′, b ≥ 0) or n = m′, and Γ(m′) > 0, then every nonempty multiset on Z_n has a good signing; Γ is defined there by a recursion over the prime powers of m′.

small_cover3.py evaluates Γ, with that result's own program coverage2.py, on n′_m with one prime taken out as q, or none. For 3 ≤ m ≤ 6, n′_m has at most two primes (Theorem 2 of the first result). For 7 ≤ m ≤ 36, Γ(n′_m / 3^e) > 0, where 3^e is the power of 3 in n′_m; the values go down from 1/4 (m = 7) to about 0.00099 (m = 36). For m = 37,

n′_37 = 3^18·5^9·7^6·11^3·13^3·17^2·19^2·23·29·31·37,

and Γ ≤ 0 for every choice of the prime taken out and for none; likewise for every m from 37 to 60 (the range computed). The referee recomputed all these values with a program written from the definition, and gives a certificate for m = 36 that can be followed by hand (the steps are in referee/second/second_gamma.out): with q = 3 and the trees taken in increasing order of the primes, 5^8, 7^5, 11^3, 13^2, 17^2, 19, 23, 29, 31, Lemma 3T alone gives Γ ≥ 613754253/1099511627776 ≈ 0.00056. The full recursion, which may also use Lemma 3′, gives 544323375/549755813888 ≈ 0.00099 (order 5^8, 7^5, 11^3, 13^2, 17^2, 19, 31, 23, 29; Lemma 3′ gives the value at 19 and at 23).

Proof of Theorem S. For multisets of at most 36 elements: Theorem R with m = 36 and Theorem 7 (Theorem 2 for the smallest cases; m ≤ 2 leaves n′_m = 1, where a good signing is one whose signs do not sum to 0). For the general form: let B_1 be the set of elements of odd multiplicity. If B_1 is empty, give two copies of one element the sign + and pair off all other copies with opposite signs: S = 2X^x, which no Φ_d divides. Otherwise sign one copy of each element of B_1 by a good signing of the set B_1 (at most 36 elements) and pair off the other copies with opposite signs: S is the good mask of B_1. (This reduction to the elements of odd multiplicity is the remark in §1 of the first result.) ∎

5. What is in print

  • Primes larger than the number of terms. H. B. Mann, On linear relations between roots of unity, Mathematika 12 (1965), 107–117. In the form in which B. Poonen and M. Rubinstein quote it (The number of intersection points made by the diagonals of a regular polygon, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 11 (1998), 135–156; Lemma 1 of the arXiv version, "a corollary of Theorem 1 in" Mann's paper): if a relation Σ a_i η_i = 0 between k distinct roots of unity with positive integer coefficients is minimal, then after a rotation every η_i is a p_1 p_2 ⋯ p_s-th root of unity (its order divides p_1 ⋯ p_s) with distinct primes p_1 < … < p_s ≤ k. From this the special case "a prime larger than the number of elements can be deleted" follows: a vanishing signed sum splits into minimal ones, and in a minimal one with at most m terms no prime above m occurs in the orders of the quotients of its terms. (The referee wrote the deduction out and found it right: the quotient of two terms of one minimal relation has order prime to p, so each relation lies in one class modulo p^i and every slice vanishes; only "primes ≤ k" is used, not "squarefree". Here the special case is proved by Lemma 2.) I have read Lemma 1 and the definitions before it in a copy of the arXiv version of Poonen and Rubinstein's paper (math/9508209, as the site ar5iv renders it), and the reference to Mann's paper there and in a second paper (Christie, Dykema and Klep, arXiv:2008.11268, which says that Mann "classifies all minimal vanishing sums of roots of unity of weight ≤ 7"); I have not read Mann's paper. Lam and Leung's paper On vanishing sums of roots of unity (arXiv math/9511209) was read in full, in its arXiv version, by another instance, in a prior-art reading it made for me on 26 November (prior-art.md, §4, has its account); a search result of today says that it uses the same fact in the form "after a suitable rotation … m_0-th roots of unity where m_0 is square-free", which I have not checked. The text copies are in print/; the lemma's words are in print/poonen-rubinstein.txt.
  • Lemma 1 for i = 1 and squarefree order is in both papers: in the proof of Lemma 3 of Poonen and Rubinstein (the relation is written Σ f_i ζ_{p_s}^i = 0 and "forces the values of the f_i to be equal") and as Proposition 2.3 of Christie, Dykema and Klep ("h vanishes if and only if val(f0) = val(f1) = · · · = val(fp−1)"). (The referee's finding; I have checked both quotations in the copies.)
  • The contraction of a level in which some subclass of every group is empty (Lemma 2 in general) I have not found in print. Searched on 27 November: five searches and two pages fetched (search-in-print.md has the queries word for word): Mann's theorem and its statement (two searches, the two papers above); vanishing sums of roots of unity with coefficients ±1 and a choice of signs that is nonzero at every character (two searches); lifting a multiset from Z_N to Z_2N without new cyclotomic divisors, with the papers on integer tilings (one search). None returned the statement on signs, a lift along 2, or a contraction of this kind. That is little, and the lemma is elementary; it may well be known in another language (it is a statement about the fibres of the map Z_{p^a} → Z_{p^{a−1}} that forgets a digit).
  • The statement on signs for multisets of bounded size I did not find. The first result has Proposition 4: if at every level of every tree one digit value is missed by the whole support (in particular if the support has fewer elements than the smallest prime factor of n), every signing with nonzero sum is good. That is Lemma 2 applied at every level, down to Z_1, when the missed value is the same for the whole support. Lemma 2 gives the same conclusion whenever no level of any tree has a full group; on Z_9, B = {0, 3, 7} is such a multiset to which Proposition 4 does not apply.

6. Tests (exact; whole-number division of the signed mask by every Φ_d)

  • test_reductions.py 1 150 (test_reductions.out): the two special cases as equivalences. A prime q > m deleted: 150 multisets on Z_{n′q^b} (n′ ∈ {3, 5, 9, 15, 21, 25, 27, 45}, q ∈ {5, 7, 11}), all 1,840 signings: good on Z_n iff good on Z_{n′}. A level without branching contracted: 150 multisets, 1,748 signings: good iff good after contraction.
  • test_contract.py 2 300 (test_contract.out): Lemma 2 in general. 300 multisets of 2 to 7 elements on Z_{p^a n″} with a contractible level (in 204 of them the contracted level branches), all 5,802 signings: never good after contraction and bad before; good before and bad after 23 times.
  • small_cover.py, small_cover2.py, small_cover3.py and their outputs: the shapes n′_m and the values of Γ. With my first, weaker reduction (levels without branching only; shape ∏ p^{m−1}) Theorem 7 reaches m = 11; with Lemma 2, m = 36.

Not tested by me: Theorem S itself. The tests above are of Lemma 2 and its special cases, on groups of order at most 315. The referee searched a good signing for 903 multisets of 5 to 36 elements on nine groups up to 2.9·10^33, 670 of them on four groups that none of Theorems 2, 3, 6, 7 covers, found one within four tries every time, and confirmed it exactly. It also tested Lemma 1 (8,000 signed multisets) and Lemma 2 by exhaustion (every multiset of at most k elements, k from 2 to 6 according to the group, on 16 groups from Z_3 to Z_225: 4,447,918 pairs of a multiset and a contractible level, 32,258,961 signings, no violation; without the hypothesis the implication failed 247,924 times) and by sampling (80,000 multisets of 2 to 10 elements), with programs of its own in referee/.

7. What this rests on, and what I am least sure of

  • Theorem 7 of the third result and the value of Γ: not mine. A fresh instance proved it. Another refereed the third result line by line and recomputed its bound (every odd n below 6587407633447265625 is covered by Theorem 2, 3, 6 or 7, that number by none); its report is referee-signs/third/report-3.md beside the paper. That report takes as given Lemma A, Lemma B and the Corollary of the first result, by which a signing good in the tree box has no cyclotomic divisor. The referee of this note read those three, and Lemmas 1T, G′, T, S, 3T, Proposition 5 and Theorem 7 of the third result, and found no error. It did not read Lemma 3′, nor what the third result takes from the second (Theorem P, for Lemma S at a = 1; Lemma 3, for Lemma 3′). By its reading and computation these are not needed for the shape used here: with q = 3 every tree has p ≥ 5, where the value 1/2 of Lemma S at a = 1 follows from that lemma's own argument (5/8 − 2^{1−p} ≥ 9/16), and Lemma 3T alone still gives Γ > 0 for n′_36/3^17 (§4). The values of Γ for the shapes n′_m were recomputed by that referee with a program written from §5 of the third result: the same fractions for 7 ≤ m ≤ 36, and Γ ≤ 0 for 37 ≤ m ≤ 60 for every prime taken out and for none.
  • Lemma 1 and Lemma 2 are stated and proved here by me and are elementary; they lean on the three results. The words of §1 are those of §1 of the third result, and Lemma 1 is the counterpart, for divisibility by Φ_{d″}, of the slice lemmas (Lemma 1 of the second result, Lemma 1T of the third). A contractible level is a level that is not "possible" in the proof of Lemma T of the third result; the case i = t of Lemma 2 is its Fact 2 with Fact 3; the count of §3 is the count of that proof. The reduction to elements of odd multiplicity is the remark in §1 of the first result. The place to look for an error is the bookkeeping of digits in the case i > t of Lemma 2, and the step "every class that holds an element has a subclass that does" in §3; the referee found none.
  • The bound 36 is the reach of the present Theorem 7, not a natural limit: any improvement of the constants there moves it.
