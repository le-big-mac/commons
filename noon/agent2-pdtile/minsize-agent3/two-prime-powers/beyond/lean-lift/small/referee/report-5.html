Must change

  1. "The same is done for every prime that divides the number of hours, place by place and group by group, as often as it can be." Two faults: - The letter p is no longer introduced. The next sentence says "all p digits" and "at least p marks", and the sentence that named p is gone. - The example's recipe fits the clock of 9 hours only. "Its remainder on division by 3, then the number of threes in it" fails on other clocks: on 45 hours, hour 20 has six threes in it, which is not a digit. So the page no longer says what the digits are on any other clock.

You asked for the shortest clause. Replace the sentence by:

The same is done for every prime p that divides the number of hours (on a larger clock the digits are those of the hour's remainder on division by the largest power of p in the number of hours), place by place and group by group, as often as it can be.

  1. "…and that takes at least p marks; so a prime larger than the number of marks goes altogether, and a smaller prime keeps only a few places." The first half follows; the second does not. - Needing p marks at one place does not limit the number of places. The note's count does: each further place needs p − 1 marks more (its §3). - "A few" is also loose: with 36 marks the prime 3 can keep 17 places.

Replace the sentence by:

A place can stay only if some group uses all p digits there. One such place takes at least p marks, and each further one at least p − 1 marks more; so a prime larger than the number of marks goes altogether, and a smaller prime keeps only as many places as the marks allow (with 36 marks at most 17 for the prime 3, 8 for 5 and 5 for 7).

Suggestion

  • "so the second digit could be struck out as well". Striking both works only in the order second, then first: after the first digit is struck, the marks 0, 1, 2 use all three digits. The sentence itself says what is left, so it is not false, but "instead" for "as well" removes the stumble.
