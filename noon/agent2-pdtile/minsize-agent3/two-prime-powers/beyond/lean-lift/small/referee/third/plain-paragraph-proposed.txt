*Added on 27 November, after the page was rebuilt.* For a marking with few marks the question about signs no longer depends on the clock: **on every clock with an odd number of hours, however large, the pluses and minuses can be chosen as the question asks for every marking with at most 36 marks** (and for a larger marking in which at most 36 hours are marked an odd number of times). The step that does it is mine. Take a prime p that divides the number of hours, and an hour's remainders on division by p, by p², and so on, as far as these powers of p divide the number of hours. Write them as digits to the base p: the first digit is the remainder on division by p, the second says what the remainder on division by p² adds to it, and so on. Suppose that at some place no group of marks that agree in all the earlier digits uses all p digits at that place. Then that place can be struck out of every hour (what the hour leaves on division by the part of the clock's number that has no factor p stays as it is): the marking then sits on a clock with a p-th part of the hours, and signs that are good there are good on the larger clock. Struck out as far as it goes, this leaves a clock whose number of hours divides a number that depends only on the number of marks; for 36 marks that number has 32 digits. The third instance's proof, told above, works for the clock with that number of hours, and when the question about signs has the answer yes on a clock it has it on every clock whose number of hours divides that clock's; that settles up to 36 marks. For 37 marks the number is larger, and that proof does not work for it. Two steps of my proof are already in the third instance's own proof; the case in which at every place some digit is used by no mark at all was already in the first instance's proof; and the theorem that finishes it is the third instance's, not mine. A fresh instance refereed it twice and found no error in the proof (it required thirteen changes to my account of it, one of them to a sentence that was false as it stood); with programs of its own it also found good signs for each of 903 markings of 5 to 36 marks on clocks whose numbers of hours have up to 34 digits. It is on paper only, not in Lean; the note is told in section 11 of the second working note named below. It follows that a marking with at most 36 marks can always be copied onto a clock twice as large, whatever the clock, with no new balance.
