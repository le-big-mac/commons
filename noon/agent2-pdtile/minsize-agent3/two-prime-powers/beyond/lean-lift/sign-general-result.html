0. Summary

Proved (complete proofs below; every step tested by programs of my own, §7):

  • Theorem P (product patterns, with a count). Let X = X_1 × … × X_k be a box whose sides n_j = |X_j| are odd, n_1 ≥ 3 and n_j ≥ 2j for 2 ≤ j ≤ k. Let F be Q, or F_q for an odd prime q dividing no n_j. For every function g : X → F that is not identically 0, at least the fraction 2^−k of the sign patterns σ = (σ_1, …, σ_k), σ_j : X_j → {±1}, make the function (σg)(x) = σ_1(x_1)⋯σ_k(x_k)·g(x) good: at every level P ⊆ {1..k} some cube sum of σg is not 0 in F. (Definitions in §2. A version for smaller families of levels, Theorem P′, is in §3.)
  • Theorem 6 (the statement on signs for new n). The statement on signs holds for (a) every squarefree odd n (so 1155, 1365, 5005, 15015, …); (b) every odd n in which at most one prime occurs with exponent ≥ 2 (n = m·q^b, m squarefree); (c) every n = m·q^b (q an odd prime not dividing m, b ≥ 0) for which the number c(m) defined by the recursion of §4 is positive; this includes 1575 = 5²·7·3², 11025 = 3²·7²·5², 7875 = 3²·7·5³. Together with Theorems 2 and 3 of the earlier result: the statement on signs holds for every odd n < 118125 = 3³·5⁴·7 (coverage.py). In case (a), for a set B, the signing can be taken of product form, sign(x) = ∏_p σ_p(x mod p), and at least 2^−ω(n) of all such patterns work.
  • The strong form is false (§5). There is a multiset on Z_n, n = 3·5·7·11·13·17·19·23·29·103 = 333 189 201 345, and a set on Z_n for n = 3·5·…·29·7735396963941761, for which "all signs +" and every signing with exactly one sign turned are divisible by some Φ_d. Two turned signs are enough for the set. The mechanism needs Σ 1/p ≥ 1 over the primes used, hence nine primes. On boxes that are not cyclic groups the strong form already fails for five points in Z_3 × Z_3 × Z_5.

Not proved: the statement on signs for all odd n. The smallest n covered by nothing here is 118125 = 3³·5⁴·7 (next 165375 = 3³·5³·7²). What is missing is exactly Theorem P for boxes with many equal small sides (§6); it holds in every case I computed, with the same count 2^−k, including 3 × 3 × 3 (not exhaustively).

No counterexample to the statement on signs in any test (§7).

1. What is used from the earlier result

n odd; a signing S of a multiset B on Z_n; S is good if S(ζ) ≠ 0 for every n-th root of unity ζ. For m | n the fold S̄ of S to Z_m. A function h : Z_d → Z is primitive if for every prime p | d it sums to 0 over every coset of the subgroup of order p of Z_d; ⟨h, T⟩ = Σ_y h(y)·T̃(y), T̃ the fold of T to Z_d.

  • Lemma B(a): if Φ_d | T in Z[X] and h is primitive on Z_d, then ⟨h, T⟩ = 0.
  • Corollary (criterion): let n = m·q^b, q prime, q ∤ m, and S ∈ Z[X] with fold S̄ to Z_m. If for every d′ | m there is a primitive h on Z_{d′} with ⟨h, S̄⟩ ≢ 0 (mod q), then S(ζ) ≠ 0 for every n-th root of unity ζ. (For n = m no prime q is needed: if for every d′ | m there is a primitive h on Z_{d′} with ⟨h, S⟩ ≠ 0 in Z, then S is good, directly by Lemma B(a).)

2. Boxes, levels, cube sums

A box is X = X_1 × … × X_k, finite sets, n_j = |X_j|. F is a field of characteristic ≠ 2. For g : X → F and P ⊆ {1..k} (a level), a cube at level P is a choice of two values a_i ≠ a′_i in X_i for each i ∈ P; its cube sum is

κ(g) = Σ_{x ∈ X} g(x)·∏_{i ∈ P} ([x_i = a_i] − [x_i = a′_i])

(for P = ∅: the total Σ g). g is good at P if some cube sum at level P is ≠ 0, and degenerate at P otherwise. Degenerate functions at P form a linear subspace. (Equivalently: the marginal of g on the coordinates in P is a sum of functions each not depending on one coordinate of P; this is not needed below.)

Lemma 1 (slices). Let X = X_1 × Y and g_v = g(v, ·) : Y → F the slices, v ∈ X_1. Let P′ be a level of Y. (a) g is degenerate at P′ (as a level of X) iff Σ_v g_v is degenerate at P′ on Y. (b) g is degenerate at {1} ∪ P′ iff g_v − g_{v′} is degenerate at P′ on Y for all v, v′ ∈ X_1.

Proof. A cube sum of g at level P′ equals the same cube sum of Σ_v g_v. A cube sum of g at level {1} ∪ P′ with the values v, v′ in the first coordinate equals the cube sum (same cube of Y) of g_v − g_{v′}. ∎

A pattern is σ = (σ_1, …, σ_k) with σ_j : X_j → {+1, −1}; σ(x) = ∏ σ_j(x_j); (σg)(x) = σ(x)g(x). There are 2^{n_1+…+n_k} patterns.

The digit box of m. Let m = ∏ p^{a_p} be odd. For a prime p | m and 1 ≤ e ≤ a_p let dig_{p,e}(x) = ⌊(x mod p^e)/p^{e−1}⌋. The map x ↦ (dig_{p,e}(x))_{p,e} is a bijection from Z_m to the box with one coordinate of size p for every pair (p, e) (Chinese remainder theorem and base-p digits). A level is a set of pairs (p, e); a tree level is a level containing at most one pair for each prime.

Lemma G (from the box to roots of unity). Let d′ | m, d′ = ∏{p ∈ Q} p^{i_p} with i_p ≥ 1, and P = {(p, i_p) : p ∈ Q} (a tree level). For any cube (a_p ≠ a′p){p ∈ Q} at level P the function h(y) = ∏{p ∈ Q} ([dig_{p,i_p}(y) = a_p] − [dig_{p,i_p}(y) = a′p]) is well defined and primitive on Z{d′}, and for T : Z_m → Z the number ⟨h, T⟩ is the cube sum of T (as a function on the digit box of m) for this cube.

Proof. dig_{p,i_p}(y) depends only on y mod p^{i_p}, so h is a function on Z_{d′} and ⟨h, T⟩ is the cube sum. Primitive: fix p_0 ∈ Q and write i_0 = i_{p_0}. Adding t·d′/p_0 (t = 0..p_0 − 1) to y does not change y mod p^{i_p} for p ≠ p_0, does not change y mod p_0^{i_0 − 1}, and makes dig_{p_0,i_0} run once through all p_0 values (d′/p_0^{i_0} is prime to p_0); so the factor of p_0 sums to 1 − 1 = 0 over the coset and the other factors are constant on it. (For d′ = 1: Q = ∅, P = ∅, h = 1 and ⟨h, T⟩ is the total.) ∎

The tree levels correspond one to one to the divisors d′ of m in this way.

Hence, by the Corollary: if n = m·q^b and the fold S̄ of a signing S, read on the digit box of m and reduced modulo q, is good at every tree level, then S is good. (For n = m: if S itself is good at every tree level over Q, then S is good, by Lemma B(a).)

3. Theorem P: sign patterns of product form

Throughout, F is Q or F_q with q an odd prime; "sign vector" means an element of {±1}^p ⊂ F^p; ⟨s, t⟩ = Σ s_v t_v. "char F ∤ 2p" is no condition for F = Q and means q ∤ p for F = F_q.

Lemma 2 (sign vectors in subspaces). Let p ≥ 3 be odd and char F ∤ 2p. (a) A subspace of F^p of dimension d contains at most 2^d sign vectors. (b) A proper subspace of F^p contains at most 2^{p−1} sign vectors. (c) If s_0 is a sign vector, at most 2^{p−1} − 2 sign vectors s have ⟨s, s_0⟩ = 0. (d) If K is a subspace with {0} ≠ K ≠ F^p and K^⊥ = {t : ⟨t, κ⟩ = 0 for all κ ∈ K}, then at most 2^{p−1} sign vectors lie in K ∪ K^⊥. (e) If s, s′ are sign vectors and s^⊥ = s′^⊥ then s′ = ±s.

Proof. (a) Some d coordinates project the subspace injectively to F^d (a d × p matrix of rank d has an invertible d × d minor); the images of sign vectors are sign vectors of F^d. (b) The subspace lies in ν^⊥ for some ν ≠ 0; take a with ν_a ≠ 0; for a sign vector s and the vector s′ obtained by changing the sign of s_a, ⟨s, ν⟩ − ⟨s′, ν⟩ = 2 s_a ν_a ≠ 0, so at most one of the pair lies in ν^⊥. (c) If s differs from s_0 in j places, ⟨s, s_0⟩ = p − 2j. Let J = {0 ≤ j ≤ p : p − 2j = 0 in F}. Over Q, J = ∅ (p odd). Over F_q: 0, p ∉ J (q ∤ p), (p ± 1)/2 ∉ J (p − 2j = ∓1), J is symmetric under j ↦ p − j without fixed point, and j, j + 1 are never both in J (q ∤ 2). Map j ∈ J to j + 1 if j < p/2 and to j − 1 if j > p/2. Because (p ± 1)/2 ∉ J, an element j < p/2 of J is ≤ (p − 3)/2 and its image is ≤ (p − 1)/2; likewise on the other side; so the map is injective (images of the two sides stay on their sides) and C(p, ·) does not decrease under it. Its image is disjoint from J (no two neighbours in J) and from {0, p}. So 2·Σ_{j ∈ J} C(p, j) ≤ 2^p − 2. By the symmetry j ↦ p − j the sum Σ_{j∈J} C(p, j) is even, and 2^{p−1} − 1 is odd, so the sum is ≤ 2^{p−1} − 2. (d) dim K = d, dim K^⊥ = p − d and K^⊥⊥ = K (the form is nondegenerate; K ∩ K^⊥ may be nonzero over F_q, which does not matter for a union bound). If 2 ≤ d ≤ p − 2 then p ≥ 4 and by (a) the count is ≤ 2^d + 2^{p−d} ≤ 4 + 2^{p−2} ≤ 2^{p−1}. Otherwise one of K, K^⊥ is a line Fκ and the other is κ^⊥. If κ is not proportional to a sign vector the line contains no sign vector and (b) gives ≤ 2^{p−1}. If κ = λs_0, the line contains exactly ±s_0 (entries ±1 force the factor ±1) and κ^⊥ contains ≤ 2^{p−1} − 2 by (c). (e) s′ = λs, and entries ±1 force λ = ±1. ∎

Let 𝓛 be a family of levels of X that is closed under taking subsets. For a coordinate, say the first, put 𝓛_0 = {P ∈ 𝓛 : 1 ∉ P} and 𝓛_1 = {P ∖ {1} : 1 ∈ P ∈ 𝓛}; both are subset-closed families of levels of Y = X_2 × … × X_k, and 𝓛_1 ⊆ 𝓛_0. "σg is 𝓛-good" means: good at every level of 𝓛. (Lemma 3 is written for the first coordinate; by symmetry it holds for any coordinate in its place, and it is used that way below.)

Lemma 3 (one step). Let X = X_1 × Y with p = |X_1| odd, p ≥ 3, char F ∤ 2p, and 𝓛, 𝓛_0, 𝓛_1 as above. Suppose γ ≥ 0 is such that for every u : Y → F, u ≠ 0, at least γ·|Σ_Y| of the patterns σ_Y of Y make σ_Y u 𝓛_0-good (Σ_Y = set of patterns of Y; if Y is a single point, Σ_Y has one element, the only level is ∅, and γ = 1). Then for every g : X → F, g ≠ 0, at least

min( γ/2 , γ − |𝓛_1|·2^{1−p} ) · |Σ_X|

of the patterns of X make σg 𝓛-good.

Proof. Write a pattern of X as (s, σ_Y), s ∈ {±1}^{X_1} a sign vector, and g_v for the slices. For t ∈ F^{X_1} put u_t = Σ_v t_v g_v : Y → F, and K = {t : u_t = 0}, a subspace, K ≠ F^p because g ≠ 0. The slices of (s, σ_Y)g are s_v·σ_Y g_v, and their sum is σ_Y u_s. By Lemma 1, (s, σ_Y)g is 𝓛-good iff (i) σ_Y u_s is good at every P′ ∈ 𝓛_0, and (ii) for every P′ ∈ 𝓛_1 the functions s_v σ_Y g_v (v ∈ X_1) are not all congruent modulo the functions degenerate at P′. Let 𝒜 be the set of pairs (s, σ_Y) with (i), and Lost ⊆ 𝒜 the set of those for which (ii) fails. By hypothesis, for each sign vector s ∉ K (so u_s ≠ 0) at least γ|Σ_Y| patterns σ_Y give a pair in 𝒜.

Claim. If (s, σ_Y) ∈ Lost, with (ii) failing at P′ ∈ 𝓛_1, then there is a class b ≠ 0 of functions on Y modulo the functions degenerate at P′ such that the class of σ_Y u_t is ⟨s, t⟩·b for every t ∈ F^p. Indeed all s_v σ_Y g_v have one class b, so σ_Y g_v has class s_v b and σ_Y u_t has class Σ_v t_v s_v b = ⟨s, t⟩ b; and p·b = class of σ_Y u_s ≠ 0 by (i) (P′ ∈ 𝓛_1 ⊆ 𝓛_0), so b ≠ 0.

Case K ≠ {0}. If (s, σ_Y) ∈ Lost then, by the Claim, for κ ∈ K: 0 = class of σ_Y u_κ = ⟨s, κ⟩ b, so ⟨s, κ⟩ = 0: s ∈ K^⊥. Hence for every sign vector s ∉ K ∪ K^⊥ all pairs (s, σ_Y) ∈ 𝒜 are 𝓛-good, and there are at least γ|Σ_Y| of them. By Lemma 2(d) at least 2^{p−1} sign vectors are outside K ∪ K^⊥. Total: ≥ 2^{p−1}·γ|Σ_Y| = (γ/2)|Σ_X|.

Case K = {0}. Then |𝒜| ≥ 2^p·γ|Σ_Y|. For fixed σ_Y and P′ ∈ 𝓛_1, if both (s, σ_Y) and (s′, σ_Y) are in Lost with (ii) failing at P′, the Claim gives ⟨s, t⟩ b = ⟨s′, t⟩ b′ for all t with b, b′ ≠ 0, so s^⊥ = s′^⊥ and s′ = ±s (Lemma 2(e)). So |Lost| ≤ 2·|𝓛_1|·|Σ_Y| (this bound does not use K = {0}; only the bound on |𝒜| does), and the number of 𝓛-good pairs is ≥ (2^p γ − 2|𝓛_1|)|Σ_Y| = (γ − |𝓛_1| 2^{1−p})|Σ_X|. ∎

Theorem P. Let the sides n_1, …, n_k of X be odd with n_1 ≥ 3 and n_j ≥ 2j for 2 ≤ j ≤ k, and let F be Q or F_q, q an odd prime dividing no n_j. For every g : X → F, g ≠ 0, at least 2^−k·2^{n_1+…+n_k} patterns σ make σg good at every level.

Proof. Induction on k with 𝓛 = all levels, splitting off the last coordinate (size p = n_k) in Lemma 3. For k = 1: γ = 1, |𝓛_1| = 1, and min(1/2, 1 − 2^{1−p}) = 1/2. For k ≥ 2: γ = 2^{−(k−1)} by induction (the first k − 1 sides satisfy the hypothesis), |𝓛_1| = 2^{k−1}, and γ − |𝓛_1| 2^{1−p} = 2^{−(k−1)} − 2^{k−p} ≥ 2^{−k} because p ≥ 2k. ∎

Theorem P′ (other families of levels). For a box with odd sides ≥ 3, a subset-closed family 𝓛 and F as above, define c(𝓛) = 1 for the box with no coordinate, and otherwise c(𝓛) = max over the coordinates j with c(𝓛_0^j) > 0 of min( c(𝓛_0^j)/2 , c(𝓛_0^j) − |𝓛_1^j|·2^{1−n_j} ), where 𝓛_0^j, 𝓛_1^j are formed with the coordinate j and c(𝓛_0^j) refers to the box without the coordinate j (c(𝓛) = −1 if no such j; c depends on the box as well as on 𝓛, and only its being positive is used: values ≤ 0 carry no meaning). If c(𝓛) > 0, every g ≠ 0 has at least c(𝓛)·|Σ_X| patterns that are 𝓛-good. Proof. Lemma 3, by induction on k. ∎

Remarks. (1) The bound 2^−k is attained: g = g_1 ⊗ … ⊗ g_k with g_j = (1, 1, 0, …, 0). (2) The statement is for every nonzero function, not only for 0/1 functions; so every given signing of a set can be made good by turning whole slices {x_j = a}. (3) For sides (3, 3) the proof does not apply (it needs n_2 ≥ 4), although the conclusion holds there (all 19682 nonzero functions with values in {0, ±1}, and Lemma E of the earlier result).

4. Theorem 6: the statement on signs for new n

For odd m = ∏ p^{a_p} let c(m) be the number c(𝓛) of Theorem P′ for the digit box of m and 𝓛 = the tree levels. Because the tree levels are symmetric in the digits of one prime, c depends only on how many digits of each prime have been used, and the recursion reads: c(1) = 1, and c(m) = max over primes p | m with c(m/p) > 0 of min( c(m/p)/2 , c(m/p) − τ(m′)·2^{1−p} ), where m′ is the part of m/p prime to p and τ is the number of divisors (the tree levels of the digit box of m/p without a digit of p correspond to the divisors of m′). coverage.py computes it with exact fractions.

Theorem 6. Let m be odd with c(m) > 0, q an odd prime not dividing m, b ≥ 0, and n = m·q^b (or n = m). Then every nonempty multiset B on Z_n has a good signing. In particular this holds if (a) n is squarefree and odd; or (b) n = m·q^b with m squarefree and odd, q ∤ m; or, more generally, if the digits of m in increasing order n_1 ≤ n_2 ≤ … satisfy n_j ≥ 2j for j ≥ 2 (Theorem P; e.g. m = 5²·7).

Proof. Let F = F_q (F = Q if n = m). Let N(c) be the number of elements of B that are ≡ c (mod m). If some N(c) is odd let g = the indicator function of {c : N(c) odd}; otherwise let g = 2δ_{c_0} for a cell c_0 with N(c_0) ≥ 2. In both cases g ≢ 0 in F. Read g on the digit box of m. By Theorem P′ (c(m) > 0; the sides are odd primes p | m, and q ∤ 2m) there is a pattern σ such that σg is good over F at every tree level. Sign B so that the elements in the cell c have signs summing to σ(c)g(c) (possible: σ(c)g(c) ≡ N(c) mod 2 and |σ(c)g(c)| ≤ N(c)). The fold of the signed mask to Z_m is σg, so the signing is good by Lemma G and the Corollary. If the digits of m in increasing order satisfy n_1 ≥ 3 and n_j ≥ 2j, use Theorem P in place of Theorem P′ in this argument: its pattern is good at every level, in particular at every tree level (and then c(m) ≥ 2^−k as well, by the same induction, since the tree family has |𝓛_1| ≤ 2^{k−1} at every step). For squarefree m the digit box has distinct odd primes as sides, the j-th smallest is ≥ 2j + 1: this gives (a) (with n = m, F = Q) and (b). ∎

If n is squarefree, B a set and F = Q, then g = the indicator of B and the signing is sign(x) = ∏_{p | n} σ_p(x mod p); at least 2^−ω(n) of the 2^{Σ p} patterns are good.

Which n are covered (coverage.py, coverage.out). Using Theorem 2 (n = p^a q^b), Theorem 3 (n = p r q^b) and Theorem 6 with every admissible choice of q: among the odd n < 200000 only 118125 = 3³·5⁴·7 and 165375 = 3³·5³·7² are covered by nothing. 8326 odd n < 200000 are covered by Theorem 6 and not by Theorems 2, 3; the first are 1155, 1365, 1575, 1785, 1995, 2145, 2205. Examples: 1575 = (5²·7)·3² by Theorem P (digits 5, 5, 7) and also (3²·7)·5² by Theorem P′; 11025 = (3²·7²)·5²: c = 1/16; 3³·5³·7³: not covered.

5. The strong form is false

Strong form: "all signs +" is good or some signing with exactly one sign turned is good.

5.1 On a box. In Z_3 × Z_3 × Z_5 take B = {(0,0,0), (1,0,1), (2,0,2), (0,1,3), (0,2,4)}. All + is degenerate at the level {3} (one point for each value of the third coordinate). Turning the sign of any point with first coordinate 0 makes the first marginal (1, 1, 1); of any point with second coordinate 0, the second marginal (1, 1, 1). Every point has first or second coordinate 0. Two turns suffice (oneset.py; found by grid.c, which also shows that this is the smallest size at which it happens in that box). This is not a counterexample on Z_45, whose levels are different (Z_9 is not Z_3 × Z_3), but it shows the mechanism.

5.2 On a cyclic group. Let p_1, …, p_9 = 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, 29, Π = ∏ p_i = 3234846615, q_i = p_i − 1, Q = ∏ q_i = 1021870080, e = Σ_i Π/p_i − Π = 108169298 > 0 (the sum of the 1/p_i exceeds 1; without 29 it does not). On G = ∏ Z_{p_i} define multiplicities w(x) by the set Z(x) = {i : x_i = 0}, for an integer m ≥ 1:

Z = {i}, 2 ≤ i ≤ 8: w = m q_i Π/p_i; Z = {1}: w = m q_1 (Π/p_1 − e); Z = {9}: w = m q_9 (Π/p_9 − e); Z = {1, 9}: w = m q_1 q_9 e; x = 0: w = 2; all other x: w = 0.

Then for every i the number of elements (with multiplicity) with x_i = 0 is u_i + 2 and with x_i = v is u_i for every v ≠ 0, where u_i = mQΠ/p_i. (Count: the points with zero set Z number ∏{j ∉ Z} q_j; for i ∉ {1, 9} the elements with x_i = v ≠ 0 number (mQ/q_i)(Σ{j ≠ i} Π/p_j − e) = (mQ/q_i)(Π − Π/p_i) = u_i, and similarly in the other cases; strong_cyclic.py checks the formulas by enumeration on small boxes and then the identities for these primes.) The total is N = 2 + mQΠ. Let r be an odd prime dividing N; r ∤ Π because N ≡ 2 (mod p_i). Put n = Π·r, Z_n = G × Z_r, list the N elements with equal points of G consecutive, and give the t-th element the coordinate t mod r in Z_r. This is a multiset B on Z_n, and a set if r ≥ max w.

Proposition S. For this B, the mask with all signs + is divisible by Φ_r, and for every element x of B the mask with the sign of x turned is divisible by Φ_{p_i} for an i with x ≡ 0 (mod p_i). Proof. Each class modulo r contains N/r elements, so B(ζ_r) = (N/r)·Σ_v ζ_r^v = 0. Every element has a coordinate x_i = 0 (w = 0 otherwise); turning its sign changes the count of the class 0 modulo p_i from u_i + 2 to u_i, all classes modulo p_i then have signed count u_i, and the mask vanishes at ζ_{p_i}. ∎

  • m = 1: N = 3305592969257779202 = 2·103·70943447·226188161; r = 103 gives a multiset on Z_n, n = 333189201345 (largest multiplicity on G is 6057480688 > r).
  • m = 3: N = 9916778907773337602 = 2·641·7735396963941761; r = 7735396963941761 ≥ max w = 18172442064 gives a set of N points in Z_n, n = Π·r = 25022822684488282627989015.

Both n are squarefree, so by Theorem 6 these B have good signings. For the set, an explicit one: turn the signs of the two elements over x = 0. The fold to Z_Π is then Σ_Z w_Z·1[zero set = Z] − 2δ_0, whose cube sum at level P with corners 0, 1 is Σ_Z w_Z (−1)^{|P∖Z|} ∏_{j ∉ P ∪ Z} q_j − 2; all 512 of them are ≢ 0 (mod r) (strong_cyclic.out), so the signing is good by Lemma G and the Corollary. (For the multiset with r = 103 four of these cube sums are ≡ 0 mod 103, so this particular test does not decide that signing.)

How small can it be? For a counterexample of this shape (all + bad at one prime r, each single turn bad at a prime p_i with x ≡ 0 mod p_i) counting the elements gives Σ_i (u_i + 2) ≥ N with N ≡ 2 (mod ∏ p_i), which forces Σ 1/p_i ≥ 1 up to a term 2·9/N: nine primes at least. Composite levels d (where the fold is 2δ_c plus cosets) might allow smaller n; I did not exclude that. Where I looked there is none: all sets B ∋ 0 with |B| ≤ 6 in Z_105 (g357_6.out and the run up to size 5), and about 30000 structured sets each in Z_1155 and Z_1575, about 20000 each in Z_3465 and Z_5005 (struct_*.out): all + or one turn is always good (all + alone in about 87% of them, so about 2300 to 4100 sets per group tested one turn). A remark on lead 1 of the brief, derived by hand and not checked by program: if d | d′ are both in D_bad and d′/d is a power of a prime s, then 2(ζ_d^c − ζ_d^{c′}) ∈ s·Z[ζ_d], which forces c_{d′} ≡ c_d modulo d itself (not only modulo its part prime to s); so the class of d′ lies inside the class of d and adds nothing to a covering.

6. What is open, exactly

Conjecture (product patterns on every box). For every box with all sides ≥ 3 and every nonzero g : X → F (F = Q or F_q, q odd, q dividing no side) some pattern σ makes σg good at every level; in fact at least 2^−k of the patterns do.

By §2 and the proof of Theorem 6, the conjecture for the digit boxes (tree levels suffice) gives the statement on signs for every odd n. Theorem P proves it when the sides grow (n_j ≥ 2j). The case that resists is many equal small sides: for sides (3, 3, 3) the step "K = {0}" of Lemma 3 loses 2|𝓛_1|·2^−p = 1 and gives nothing. There the count of the pairs lost is too crude (it charges two sign vectors to every pair (σ_Y, P′), whether or not the kernel of t ↦ class of σ_Y u_t at P′ is of the form s^⊥). A sharper count of the set Lost, or a different argument when all slices are linearly independent, is what is missing. Evidence for the conjecture: §7.

Two further observations, unproved, true in everything computed (§7): - at least half of all signings of a set in a box with sides ≥ 3 are good (equality for two points on a line, and for a full line of four points); - no fixed set of turned signs F ⊆ B pairs every bad signing with a good one in general (fails for the five points of 5.1), and the bad product patterns can span the whole space R^X (so there is no certificate that is linear in σ_1 ⊗ … ⊗ σ_k).

8. Roads that failed or were left (so that they are not tried again)

  • One fixed flip set. "S bad ⇒ S with the signs on F turned is good": such an F exists for every set in 3×5 (size 1, 2 or 4) but for none of the five-point sets of 5.1 (grid_pair2.c). So "half the signings are good" is not a pairing by translation.
  • A linear certificate. A tensor γ with ⟨γ, σ_1⊗…⊗σ_k⟩ ≠ 0 ⇒ σg good would give the count 2^−k at once (a multilinear polynomial of degree k is nonzero on at least 2^−k of the cube). It exists on 3×3 and 3×3×3 in all samples but not on 3×5 (cert.py: the bad patterns span R^15 for some g).
  • Induction on the dimension with only "a good pattern exists" as hypothesis: the step needs one pattern of Y that is good for two functions at once (u_s and the differences of slices), and two functions on Z_3 can have no common good sign vector ((1,1,0) and (1,−1,0)). The count is what makes the step work (Lemma 3).
  • Balanced signings (total unimodularity) for m = p·r^c: the union of the two laminar families {whole, {x} × D} and {Z_p × D} is totally unimodular, so all block sums can be kept in {0, ±1}; for |O| odd and q ≥ 5 that signing is good, but |O| even and q = 3 need a case analysis I did not finish. Superseded by §3.
  • Counting with Littlewood–Offord alone: the signings with S(ζ_d) = 0 number at most C(N, ⌊N/2⌋) for each d (project to a generic real line and use Erdős's antichain argument), so a good signing exists as soon as τ(n)·C(N, ⌊N/2⌋) < 2^N, roughly N > 0.64·τ(n)². True for every odd n but only for large multisets; not needed where Theorem 6 applies.
  • The strong form as a route to the statement: closed by §5.
