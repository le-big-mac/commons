Bad3. Proved in Lean, in A2_sem.lean, none of it using a sorry of the skeleton: - bad3_iff_dvd_F3, bad3_iff_dvd_ZMod3: for 3 ∤ d, Bad3 e ε d ↔ cyclotomic d (ZMod 3) ∣ (P e ε).map (Int.castRingHom (ZMod 3)). (Over F3, Φ_d is the product of X − ζ over the primitive d-th roots, which are distinct; divisibility over ZMod 3 and over F3 are the same.) Since Φ_d is monic, that is "the remainder of the signed mask modulo Φ_d has all coefficients divisible by 3", which is the paper's "S(ζ) ∈ 3·Z[ζ]" (Z[ζ]/3 = (ℤ/3)[X]/(Φ_d)). So Bad3 is the paper's "3-bad at d" for every d prime to 3. That last step (division by a monic polynomial commutes with reduction modulo 3) is by hand, not in Lean. - bad3_of_three_dvd: for 3 ∣ d, d ≠ 0: Bad3 e ε d holds for EVERY signing (no primitive d-th root in characteristic 3). This is not the paper's notion (which is defined for d | n_3 only); no statement uses it: every Bad3 in the fourteen is at a d, p^i * d or 2^j * d that the hypotheses make prime to 3. - d = 0: val3_zero_of_bad3_zero: Bad3 e ε 0 implies that the mask vanishes at 0 ∈ F3 (Mathlib counts 0 as a primitive 0-th root), that is, the signs of the elements with exponent 0 add up to 0 modulo 3; so it is a real condition (one element with exponent 0 is not 3-bad at 0: proved). The converse needs that every nonzero element of F3 has finite order; true, not proved by me in Lean. No statement reaches d = 0. - bad3_mod: for d ∣ n, Bad3 (fun k => e k % n) ε d ↔ Bad3 e ε d: the exponents need not be reduced. - bad3_of_isEmpty, not_bad3_one_elt: the empty family is 3-bad at every d; one element is 3-bad at no d prime to 3. - A second program (ecore.bad3_literal, t0_selftest.out): the definition taken literally in GF(3^r), r the order of 3 modulo d (the signed sum of ζ^(e_k) at every power ζ0^a, gcd(a, d) = 1, of a primitive root ζ0), against "remainder modulo Φ_d divisible by 3": equal on 600,639 signings (41,681 of them 3-bad) for 26 values of d from 1 to 80. "Every" matters: 4,110 of these signings vanish at one primitive root and not at all (d = 8, 11, 13, 16, 20, 22, 26, 28, 40, 44, 55, 56, 77, 80, where Φ_d splits modulo 3).

ExceptionalPair. As Lean has it: w is, as a function, the sum of two indicator functions (a = b allowed), and the additive order of a − b in ZMod n is even. Proved in Lean (A2_sem.lean, A6_dump.lean): - exceptionalPair_iff: for n ≠ 0 the order is n / gcd(n, (a − b).val). - not_exceptionalPair_double, single_two: two equal marks, which is the same function as one mark of multiplicity 2 and nothing else, are NOT an exceptional pair (the order of 0 is 1). That is right: 2X^a is a good signed mask, and Corollary 3 does not except it (2^k divides 0). - sum_of_exceptionalPair: an exceptional pair has exactly two elements. not_exceptionalPair_one: on ZMod 1 nothing is exceptional. auditNotExceptionalOfOdd: for odd n nothing is exceptional. - By the kernel (decide): for n = 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 20 and all a, b, n / gcd(n, (a − b).val) is even iff the power of 2 in n does not divide (a − b).val; for n = 9 never. {0, 1} on ZMod 6 is exceptional, {0, 2} is not. - For n = 0 (ZMod 0 = ℤ; excluded from both final statements by [NeZero n]) the definition would call every pair of different integers exceptional (order 0, which is even): proved for {0, 1}. Harmless.

Outside Lean (t6_exc.out): for every n ≤ 64 and every ordered pair (a, b), 89,440 pairs: "the additive order of a − b, found by repeated addition, is even" equals "2^k ∤ a − b" (2^k the power of 2 in n) in every case; the order equals n / gcd(n, a − b) in every case; and "the pair {a, b} has a good signing" (long division of X^a ± X^b by every Φ_d, d | n) equals "the order is odd" in every case.

Natural-number subtraction and division. i - 1 occurs only as p ^ (i - 1), in S0′, S1′ (which assume 1 ≤ i) and inside live, IsPivot (S2′ assumes 1 ≤ i). p - 1 in S2′ with the rank: p is prime, exact. c % p ^ (i - 1) turns any c into the index of a group (0 for i = 1). 2 ^ j * d, 2 ^ (j + 1), r + 2 ^ j: no subtraction. E1's count is written with two ifs and no subtraction. N1′ is in ℚ ((1 / 2 : ℚ) ^ μ t < 1 / 8, rational division). pos k % M with M = 2^(j+1) ≥ 2. addOrderOf is a natural number (0 for infinite order, which cannot occur for n ≠ 0).

Other things worth knowing before trusting "proved in Lean"

  1. PrintEven.lean prints the filtered sets of T and L as {ε | …}: the decidability and Fintype instances inside them do not show, so two prints could be identical with different instances there. A1_instances.out shows them, and A6_dump.lean prints a structural hash of each of the eight definitions (type and value), the fourteen statements and the six proved lemmas as elaborated in the skeleton (A6_dump.out, the first 28 lines). Run in the finished project it should print the same lines. I did not run it there.
  2. What Lean will certify, if the proofs come: the two final statements, that is, Conjecture E as an "if and only if" for every n ≥ 1. Not the shares of good signings of Corollary 3, and not the paper's number 0.118569 (N1′ says "< 1/8").
  3. The second final statement must not depend on the other thirteen (brief F says so): it is a statement about two monomials. The first rests, in the plan's arrangement, on all of T, M, S0′, S1′, S2′, E1, L, N1′, A′, B.
  4. Bad3 is the paper's "3-bad" only at d prime to 3 (and nonzero). A later statement that used it at a d divisible by 3 would be about something else (true for every signing).
