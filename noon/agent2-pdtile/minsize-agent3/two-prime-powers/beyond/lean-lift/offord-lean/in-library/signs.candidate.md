## The setting

n ≥ 1, and B a finite multiset of elements of Z_n with m elements, counted with multiplicity. A *signing* ε gives each element a sign ±1; its *signed mask* is S_ε(X) = Σ_(b ∈ B) ε_b X^b, a polynomial modulo X^n − 1. The signing is *bad at d*, for d | n with d = 1 included, if the cyclotomic polynomial Φ_d divides S_ε (that is, S_ε vanishes at the roots of unity of order d; at d = 1: the signs add up to 0); it is *bad* if it is bad at some d | n, and *good* otherwise. "Share" means the share of the 2^m signings. 2^k is the highest power of 2 dividing n. β(m) is the supremum, over all ODD moduli n′ and all multisets of m elements of Z_(n′), of the share of bad signings, and c_m = C(m, ⌊m/2⌋)/2^m (a binomial coefficient over 2^m).

"Refereed" always means: by a fresh AI instance, not by a person; the referees were briefed to referee hard, and those of statements 2, 3 and 4 to assume that the text may be wrong. "In Lean" means: statements fixed before any proof of them existed; by my checks (sections 13, 15, 16 and 19) the fixed statements stand character for character in the finished files, and there is no `sorry` and no added axiom. For statement 1 the files build with the whole shared library from nothing (Mathlib's own built files used as they were); for statement 2 the same holds since 28 November (the front page of `lean-lift/union-lean/`), and for statement 5 too (the front page of `lean-lift/offord-lean/`).

## What is open

- **β(m) ≤ 1/√m for even m ≥ 10.** Proved for odd m, for m = 2 (the share is exactly 1/2), for m = 4, 6, 8 (by the exact values) and on moduli that are powers of one odd prime. The largest share·√m known for even m is below 0.94.
- **The limits of β(m)·√m** (whether they exist is not known), and whether one heavy element with a fixed set of further elements is the worst shape for large m. For such a family the share of bad signings is K·√(2/(πm))·(1 + o(1)), with K computed from the other elements; so these give the lower bounds √(2/π)·K. The largest K known are 78764625/67108864 ≈ 1.1737 for even m (found by the first text of section 18) and 337/512 ≈ 0.658 for odd m (mine). Programs, none of them Lean: `lean-lift/even/root/one/` and `lean-lift/even/asym/`.
- **The exact value of β(m) for m ≥ 9.**
- **Lean for 3 and 4.** Their proofs rest on finite computations that Lean has not seen. Statement 5 is what Lean has of them so far: the one-root bound, and their bounds on moduli 2^k.

## What is in print

The searches by fresh instances with the web (one for section 12, two for section 14, one each for sections 16, 17 and 18) found none of the four statements; that is what the searches found, and not proof that they are new. Not to be taken for new, by the same reports:
- in statement 1: the exceptional pairs themselves, the case of three elements, and full support (every residue once, m = n) for even n ≥ 4;
- in statement 2, for full support: pieces of "twice" (asymptotically for the divisors 1 and 2 in Miller's preprint, arXiv:2411.17577; exactly at n = 2p (p an odd prime) in the integer sequence A144926);
- in statement 3: three and four elements on odd n (from the classical lists of vanishing sums of three and four unit complex numbers), and full support on odd n (from a formula of Meckes's paper, arXiv:0902.2472);
- for ONE root of unity, the bound c_m for the share of signings at which a signed sum of m roots of unity vanishes (the classical bound of Erdős for the problem of Littlewood and Offord, as later papers state it; the search knows Erdős's and Kleitman's papers only through two later ones, and I have read neither). From it, for each fixed n, a bound of the order 1/√m follows, with a constant that grows with n (the one-root bound times the number of divisors); what 4 adds is that the factor can be dropped;
- for full support and even n: the order 1/√n (Meckes) and the sharp limit 2√2/√(πn) (Miller); less for odd n.
