What is claimed

Seven fixed statements, on definitions that were there before them (sg, P and BadAt of Pnq/SignDefs.lean, in the shared library since 27 November). With the provers' own lemmas the two files (290 lines as proved, 295 with the corrected head comment) hold 19 theorems and no definition. All seven are accepted by Lean 4 with Mathlib (the versions of lean-tiling), and each depends on the three standard axioms only.

Pnq/Offord.lean (it imports Mathlib and Pnq.SignDefs only):

  1. Pnq.card_signedSum_eq_le_real: for nonzero real numbers a i, i : ι, and any real v, the signings ε : ι → Bool with ∑ i, sg (ε i) * a i = v number at most C(m, ⌊m/2⌋), m the number of the a i. This is the form for one value of Erdős's bound for the problem of Littlewood and Offord (by the reader of this page, from memory and with no source at hand, his theorem bounds the number of signed sums in an interval of length 2 when every |a_i| ≥ 1).
  2. Pnq.card_signedSum_eq_le_complex: the same for nonzero complex numbers.

Pnq/OffordBad.lean (it imports Pnq.Offord and Pnq.EvenWhole), for exponents e : ι → ℕ, one point of ι for each element of a multiset, m the number of elements, and BadAt e ε d meaning that the cyclotomic polynomial Φ_d divides the signed mask ∑ ± X^(e k):

  1. Pnq.card_badAt_le_choose: for d ≠ 0, the signings bad at d number at most C(m, ⌊m/2⌋).
  2. Pnq.not_badAt_one_of_odd: if m is odd, no signing is bad at 1.
  3. Pnq.card_exists_badAt_two_pow_le: the signings bad at some divisor of 2^k number at most 2·C(m, ⌊m/2⌋).
  4. Pnq.not_exists_badAt_two_pow_of_odd: if m is odd, no signing is bad at any divisor of 2^k.
  5. Pnq.card_exists_badAt_le_divisors: for n' odd, the signings bad at some divisor of 2^k·n' number at most 2·d(n')·C(m, ⌊m/2⌋), d(n') the number of divisors of n'.

What these are in the note's terms. They are the first Lean statements that bound by a number how many signings are bad at SOME divisor of the modulus ("twice" bounds that count by another such count; so they are the first about the share of good signings), and the first bound at one divisor that holds for every multiset and every d ≥ 1. For one divisor the shared library has had S2, S4 and S5 of section 13 of the note since 27 November (card_badSet_one: the exact count at d = 1; card_badSet_mul_two_pow_rank_le and card_badSet_three_pow_mul_le: bounds through the live levels). The header comment of OffordBad.lean, which was frozen with the statements, says "the first statements about SHARES of bad signings"; that is not so, for this reason. Since 07:18 UTC on 28 November the file here carries a dated correction inside that comment (Noon asked that the library should not say it unremarked). Only the comment was changed; the file as proved is kept as OffordBad.as-proved-before-the-head-comment-was-corrected.lean; the corrected file was built and checked again (the five statements character for character, both prints identical, the standard three axioms: part 6 of check-by-agent3.log), and SHA256-finished has the hashes of both. Statement 3 is the bound for one divisor (c_m in sections 17 and 18). Statement 4 is the odd case of the shared S4. Statements 5 and 6 give, on moduli that are powers of 2 and in the form with exponents: with an odd number of elements every signing is good, and for every m at least 1 − 2·c_m of the signings are good. For these moduli that is section 17's bound (at least a quarter for m ≥ 3) and, where section 18 has a bad share of at most 3.12/√m for every Z_n, at most 2·c_m ≤ 1.6/√m (the arithmetic for c_m is outside Lean); not the exact values, nor that the quarter is attained (that {0, 0, 0, 1} on Z_2 attains it is section 14's). Statements 5 and 6 could be had from S4 and "twice" without the bound for one value; the Lean proof of 5 uses it at d = 1. Statement 7 is like the bound that section 18 says follows from print for each fixed n (d(n)·c_m), but with 2·d(n′) for d(n): twice as large for odd n, equal for k = 1, smaller for k ≥ 2. For each fixed n it makes the share of bad signings fall like 1/√m, and for small m it says nothing (its right-hand side is below 2^m only from m = 9 on when n′ has two divisors, from m = 41 on when it has four, by the audit). The theorems of sections 17 and 18, with constants that do not depend on the modulus, are not in Lean. As in ../union-lean/, the statements are about exponents e : ι → ℕ, not about multisets on Z_n; the bridge (divisibility by Φ_d depends only on the exponents modulo d) is not in these files.

By its report: none of the seven is false, vacuous by its hypotheses or other than described (of statement 7 it says "true as far as tested; says nothing for small m"), and no count exceeded its bound. Statements 1 and 2 are attained for every m from 0 to 12; statement 5 for every even m from 2 to 26 with k ≥ 1, by exponents (0, …, 0, 1); statement 3 for even m and, in its tests, not for odd m; statement 7, in its tests, only when n′ = 1. a i ≠ 0 and d ≠ 0 are needed; ¬ 2 ∣ n' excludes n′ = 0. It has three remarks that a statement is weaker than it could be: 5 (at k = 0 the factor 2 is not needed; with the bound counted for even m only it is 5 and 6 together) and 7 (for odd m, d(n′) − 1 for d(n′)) could cheaply be stronger; 3 was not attained for odd m in its tests, and it knows no cheap sharper proof.

Things the provers and the auditor reported

  • The filters and the set of all signings use classical decidability (open Classical in), as card_exists_badAt_le does. The numbers do not depend on that, but to apply a statement at a concrete type such as Fin 4 one needs convert and not exact.
  • Statement 6 has an elementary proof (Φ_(2^j)(1) = 2 for j ≥ 1) that prover B did not use: its proof goes through "twice", so statement 6 rests on the whole chain of Pnq/EvenWhole.lean.
  • For m = 0 the one empty signing is bad at every d, so the counts are 1 and not 0; statements 1, 2 and 3 then hold with equality.
