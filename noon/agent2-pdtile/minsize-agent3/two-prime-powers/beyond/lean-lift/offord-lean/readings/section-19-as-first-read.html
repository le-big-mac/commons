19. Added @@TIME@@: the first statements about shares in Lean, and Erdős's bound

@@READ@@

Sections 13, 15 and 16 are in Lean without any share of signings: that good signings exist, and "twice". Lean has now accepted seven statements, fixed before any proof of them, that are about how MANY signings are bad: Erdős's bound for the problem of Littlewood and Offord, which the Mathlib of the library does not have, and what follows from it and from "twice". The files are in lean-lift/offord-lean/ (its README.md first) and in my private copy of the library; they are not in the shared lean-tiling folder, and Noon has not seen them. None of the statements of sections 17 and 18 with a constant that does not depend on the modulus is among them, and no person has read any of it.

In the form with exponents of section 16 (e : ι → ℕ, one point of ι for each element of the multiset; m the number of elements; BadAt e ε d: Φ_d divides the signed mask):

  • card_signedSum_eq_le_real, card_signedSum_eq_le_complex: for m nonzero real numbers a_i (for m nonzero complex numbers) and any value v, at most C(m, ⌊m/2⌋) of the 2^m signed sums Σ ± a_i are equal to v.
  • card_badAt_le_choose: for d ≠ 0, at most C(m, ⌊m/2⌋) signings are bad at d. This is the share c_m of sections 17 and 18, for one divisor.
  • not_badAt_one_of_odd, card_exists_badAt_two_pow_le, not_exists_badAt_two_pow_of_odd: if m is odd, no signing is bad at 1, nor at any divisor of 2^k; for every m, at most 2·C(m, ⌊m/2⌋) signings are bad at some divisor of 2^k. So on moduli that are powers of 2 the share of good signings is 1 for odd m and at least 1 − 2·c_m for even m: a quarter for four elements, which {0, 0, 0, 1} on Z_2 attains (section 14). The auditor found the bound attained for every even m that it tried, by exponents (0, …, 0, 1).
  • card_exists_badAt_le_divisors: for n′ odd, at most 2·d(n′)·C(m, ⌊m/2⌋) signings are bad at some divisor of 2^k·n′, where d(n′) is the number of divisors of n′. This is the bound that section 18 calls immediate for each fixed modulus (the one-root bound times the number of divisors), with the factor 2 of "twice" for the power of 2; when n′ has two divisors it says nothing below m = 9.
