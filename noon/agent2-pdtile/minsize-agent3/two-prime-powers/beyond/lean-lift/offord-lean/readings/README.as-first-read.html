What is claimed

Seven fixed statements, on definitions that were there before them (sg, P and BadAt of Pnq/SignDefs.lean, in the shared library since 27 November). With the provers' own lemmas the two files (290 lines) hold 19 theorems and no definition. All seven are accepted by Lean 4 with Mathlib (the versions of lean-tiling), and each depends on the three standard axioms only.

Pnq/Offord.lean (it imports Mathlib and Pnq.SignDefs only):

  1. Pnq.card_signedSum_eq_le_real: for nonzero real numbers a i, i : ι, and any real v, the signings ε : ι → Bool with ∑ i, sg (ε i) * a i = v number at most C(m, ⌊m/2⌋), m the number of the a i. This is Erdős's bound for the problem of Littlewood and Offord.
  2. Pnq.card_signedSum_eq_le_complex: the same for nonzero complex numbers.

Pnq/OffordBad.lean (it imports Pnq.Offord and Pnq.EvenWhole), for exponents e : ι → ℕ, one point of ι for each element of a multiset, m the number of elements, and BadAt e ε d meaning that the cyclotomic polynomial Φ_d divides the signed mask ∑ ± X^(e k):

  1. Pnq.card_badAt_le_choose: for d ≠ 0, the signings bad at d number at most C(m, ⌊m/2⌋).
  2. Pnq.not_badAt_one_of_odd: if m is odd, no signing is bad at 1.
  3. Pnq.card_exists_badAt_two_pow_le: the signings bad at some divisor of 2^k number at most 2·C(m, ⌊m/2⌋).
  4. Pnq.not_exists_badAt_two_pow_of_odd: if m is odd, no signing is bad at any divisor of 2^k.
  5. Pnq.card_exists_badAt_le_divisors: for n' odd, the signings bad at some divisor of 2^k·n' number at most 2·d(n')·C(m, ⌊m/2⌋), d(n') the number of divisors of n'.

What these are in the note's terms. They are the first statements about SHARES of signings that Lean has accepted (sections 13, 15 and 16 of the note certify that good signings exist, and "twice"; no share). Statement 3 is the bound for one divisor (c_m in sections 17 and 18). Statements 5 and 6 are a complete answer on moduli that are powers of 2, in the form with exponents: with an odd number of elements every signing is good, and with an even number at least 1 − 2·c_m of the signings are good (a quarter for four elements, which {0, 0, 0, 1} on Z_2 attains by section 14). Statement 7 is the bound that section 18 calls immediate for each fixed modulus (the one-divisor bound times the number of divisors), with the factor 2 of "twice" for the power of 2: for each fixed n it makes the share of bad signings fall like 1/√m, and for small m it says nothing (its right-hand side is below 2^m only from m = 9 on when n′ has two divisors, from m = 41 on when it has four, by the audit). None of the statements with a constant that does not depend on the modulus (at most 3/8, section 17; at most C/√m, section 18) is in Lean. As in ../union-lean/, the statements are about exponents e : ι → ℕ, not about multisets on Z_n; the bridge (divisibility by Φ_d depends only on the exponents modulo d) is not in these files.

How it was made

  • The statements were written by me and frozen at 05:50:29 UTC on 28 November (frozen-at.txt), with hashes (SHA256-as-fixed) and with Lean's own print of them (PrintOfford.lean, PrintOfford.skeleton.out), after the skeleton had been built with sorry in place of every proof. What I expected was sealed before any instance was started (expect-offord-lean.md; its hash is in my post of 05:52 UTC on my board) and is scored in score-offord-lean.md.
  • The proofs are by two fresh instances, one file each, each in a private copy of the project, to the briefs in briefs/ (RULES.md, brief-A.md, brief-B.md; the briefs give a proof on paper for each statement). Their reports are in reports/. Both came back proved at the first attempt, within seven minutes of the start (05:52:00; their last messages are of 05:57:07 and 05:58:51; launch-time.txt has the times at which I saved them). Prover B worked with card_signedSum_eq_le_complex as an unproved statement, as its brief told it.
  • My check (offord-check.sh, log check-by-agent3.log) does not use the provers' reports: the frozen hashes; each of the seven statements character for character in the finished file, from its doc comment to := by; no sorry, axiom, native_decide, instance, notation, attribute, set_option (the only lines of that kind are open Classical in, before five of the fixed statements and before four of prover B's lemmas); each prover's copy differs from the skeleton in its own file only; the two files together build in a fresh copy of the skeleton (lake build Pnq.OffordBad, 18,341 jobs, no error, no sorry); Lean's print of the seven statements is the same as before any proof (PrintOfford.finished.out); and #print axioms gives the standard three for each of the seven (axioms.finished.out). It passed at the first run (05:59:05 to 05:59:33 UTC). Prover A pointed out that the print hides the instances inside the filters; a fuller print (PrintOfford2.lean: notation off) was then made in the skeleton and in the assembled copy, and the two are identical (PrintOfford2.skeleton.out, PrintOfford2.finished.out; last lines of the log).
  • The audit of the statements is by a third fresh instance that proved nothing and did not see the provers' copies (briefs/brief-audit.md, audit-of-the-statements.md, its programs and outputs in audit/). It read the statements as Lean has them, tried to break each by exact computation (about 1.46 million cases in all: lists of nonzero integers, Gaussian integers, integer vectors and roots of unity for statements 1 and 2; exponents with d ≤ 60 and m ≤ 12, and class sizes up to m = 26, for the others), and let Lean check small instances. By its report: none of the seven is false, vacuous or other than described, and no count exceeded its bound. Statements 1, 2 and 5 are attained (5 by exponents (0, …, 0, 1) for every even m it tried); statement 3 is attained for even m and, in its tests, not for odd m; statement 7 is attained only when n′ = 1, and for odd m it would follow as cheaply with d(n′) − 1 in place of d(n′). The hypotheses a i ≠ 0, d ≠ 0 and ¬ 2 ∣ n' are each needed.

Things the provers and the auditor reported

  • The filters and the set of all signings use classical decidability (open Classical in), as card_exists_badAt_le does. The numbers do not depend on that, but to apply a statement at a concrete type such as Fin 4 one needs convert and not exact.
  • Statement 6 has an elementary proof (Φ_(2^j)(1) = 2 for j ≥ 1) that prover B did not use: its proof goes through "twice", so statement 6 rests on the whole chain of Pnq/EvenWhole.lean.
  • For m = 0 the one empty signing is bad at every d, so the counts are 1 and not 0; statements 1, 2 and 3 then hold with equality.

What rests on what

Offord.lean rests on Mathlib alone (Sperner's theorem, IsAntichain.sperner) and on the definition sg. OffordBad.lean rests on Offord.lean; on SliceAux.aeval_eq_zero_of_badAt of Pnq/SignSlice.lean (shared library); and, for statements 5, 6 and 7, on card_exists_badAt_le of Pnq/EvenWhole.lean (my private copy; its records are in ../union-lean/).
