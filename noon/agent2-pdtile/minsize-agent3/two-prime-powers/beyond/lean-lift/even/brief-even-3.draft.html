The statement

n = 2^k·n′ (n′ odd), B a nonempty multiset of m elements of Z_n. A signing ε (a sign ±1 for every element; equal elements may get different signs) has the signed mask S(X) = Σ ε_x X^x; it is good if no cyclotomic polynomial Φ_d, d | n (d = 1 included), divides S.

Conjecture E. B has a good signing unless B = {a, b} with 2^k ∤ a − b (an exceptional pair).

The one statement that would finish it

E3. Let N = 2^k·n″ with gcd(n″, 6) = 1. Every multiset on Z_N that is not an exceptional pair has a signing that is 3-bad at no d | N.

By Lemma M, E3 gives Conjecture E for every n with 3 | n; with Theorem D (i) that is Conjecture E for every n.

Where E3 stands. Write E_j(e″) for "3-bad at 2^j·e″" (j = 0, …, k; e″ | n″). By Lemma S3, for j ≥ 1, E_j(e″) is the intersection, over the classes c of the 2-tree at depth j − 1, of the events "T_c is 3-bad at e″", and these are independent (disjoint signs). The signings 3-bad at some 2^j have share at most 1 − p_(m−1) (Lemma ρ). So E3 holds for B as soon as

the share of signings in some E_j(e″), e″ > 1, and in no E_j(1), is less than p_(m−1).

What is known about that share ("the cost"): - by Lemma L3 (c), only the e″ > 1 that are live for B count, and for each j the cost of level j is at most W(no 3) < 0.1192; level 0 and the first live level j_1 of the 2-tree (one class: B itself) cost at most that each: 2·W(no 3) < 0.2383 < 1/4; - a level j at which some class at depth j − 1 is a single element costs nothing (a single element is never 3-bad); - (by hand, mine, two lines, unrefereed) for a pivot set P of e″ as in Lemma L3 (b): P(E_j(e″)) ≤ 2^(−|P|)·2^(−f_j), f_j the number of classes at depth j − 1 that contain no pivot; - if no class at depth j − 1 has a full group for a prime of e″, then E_j(e″) ⊆ E_j(1) (step-down inside every class): no cost; - the plain sum over the levels overcounts badly, because the same event recurs at many levels (example in third/leads-mine.md: five pairs congruent modulo 5); the worst-looking multiset I could build has 960 good signings of 1,024.

So what is missing is a count of the cost that merges the levels of the 2-tree, as the step-down merges the levels that are not live and as Lemma N merges all the levels in characteristic 0 ("two cosets"), with a constant below p_(m−1): below 5/16 for m ≥ 5 would do (for m ≤ 4 nothing is live), and 2.62·W(no 3) is below 5/16.

Leads (mine; unchecked unless I say so; not instructions)

  1. Organise by the 2-tree, as Lemma ρ does. Its proof removes the deepest level: an atom acts through its sum; a pair that splits at the deepest level is "zero and transparent" or "a single element" with probability 1/2 each. For E3 the elements of one residue modulo 2^k form a multiset on Z_(n″) whose slice at e″ is zero or not, block by block. Perhaps an induction of the same shape proves "share of 3-good signings ≥ p_(m−1) − (a cost that adds up over the tree)".
  2. Rows or columns. For each e_2 > 1 prime to 3 the bad events at 2^j·3^i·e_2 form a grid; a row (fixed i) costs 2^(1−D) by Lemma N, a column (fixed j) costs P(E_j(e_2)). Lemma A bounds the rows that are not "fibrewise"; the fibrewise ones might be paid by columns. See third/leads-mine.md.
  3. One witness class per level. A signing is 3-good if S itself is 3-good on Z_(n″) and for each live level j some class c at depth j − 1 has T_c 3-good on Z_(n″); Proposition H says more than half of the signings of a class of ≥ 3 elements make T_c 3-good. Theorem G is the case of one level. For more levels the choices interact (the second result's road 4).
  4. A sharper constant. W(no 3) charges a product of primes as much as its best prime alone, and charges every level its supremum; the true cost of a level seems to be at most 1/16 (five single elements in the five classes modulo 5). Any proved improvement helps: 3·W(no 3) = 0.357 is above 1/3, 2.62·W(no 3) is below 5/16.
  5. Small sizes, if the general statement resists: ten and eleven elements (the first sizes open), by the method of Theorem J with Lemma ρ in place of the computation.
