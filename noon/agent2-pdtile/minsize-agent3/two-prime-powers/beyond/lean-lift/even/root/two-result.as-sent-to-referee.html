0. What is claimed, what is not, whose is what

Notation of the brief: n odd, B a multiset of m elements of Z_n, signs independent and uniform, S_r a sum of r independent signs, c(r) = P(S_r = 0) (0 for odd r), q(r) = max_v P(S_r = v) = C(r, ⌊r/2⌋)/2^r (the brief's c_r), κ = sqrt(2/π) = 0.797885.

Theorem 1 (powers of 3). For every m ≥ 1, every odd n and every multiset B of m elements of Z_n, the share of signings that are bad at some power of 3 (1 included) is - at most 0.9487/√m if m is even, and at most 0.9379/√m if m is even and m > 1000; - at most 0.4331/√m if m is odd (the worst case is m = 3: the share 1/4 is 0.43301/√3), and at most 0.40/√m if m is odd and m > 200; for odd 5 ≤ m ≤ 1000 it is at most (1/2)·q(m − 2), which is attained (m − 2 copies of 0, one 1, one 2, modulo 3), so for these m that is the exact maximum.

Theorem 2 (one prime p ≥ 5). For every prime p ≥ 5, every m ≥ 1 and every multiset of m elements on an odd modulus, the share of signings that are bad at some p^i with i ≥ 1 and not bad at 1 is at most a_p/√m, with a_p = τ_p/(1 − θ_p), τ_p = √p·2^(1−p), θ_p = 0.8165·√p·2^(2−p): a_5 = 0.18109, a_7 = 0.04434, a_11 = 0.00326, a_13 = 0.00089, …; the sum of a_p over all primes p ≥ 5 is at most 0.22964.

Corollary 3 (prime-power modulus; there, the brief's (b)). If n′ is a power of an odd prime, every multiset of m elements of Z_(n′) has bad share at most 1/√m, for every m ≥ 1: at most 0.9487/√m on powers of 3 and 0.979/√m on powers of a prime p ≥ 5 (for odd m: 0.4331/√m and 0.182/√m). By Corollary U, on Z_(2^k·p^a) the share of good signings is at least 1 − 2/√m.

Corollary 4 (every odd modulus, prime-power divisors only). For every odd n and every multiset of m elements of Z_n, the share of signings that are bad at some prime power d | n (d = 1 included) is at most 1.18/√m for even m and 0.67/√m for odd m.

Corollary 5 (at most one prime with a live level). Let B be a multiset of m elements on an odd n in which at most one prime p has a live level (a class modulo p^(i−1) that meets all p classes modulo p^i in it). Then the WHOLE bad share of B is at most 1/√m: at most 0.9487/√m (m even) and 0.4331/√m (m odd) if that prime is 3 or there is none, at most 0.979/√m if it is a prime ≥ 5. In particular every multiset with at most four distinct residues, with any multiplicities, on any odd modulus (one heavy element and up to three other residues, for instance) has bad share at most 0.9487/√m, and so on every Z_n a share of good signings of at least 1 − 1.9/√m.

On the brief's (c), for powers of 3 only. With L_even, L_odd the upper limits of √m·(largest share of signings bad at a power of 3) along even and odd m: κ/2 = 0.39894 ≤ L_odd ≤ 0.40, and 0.9303 ≤ L_even ≤ 0.9379. The lower bound for even m is new data (§6): deeper trees of 3 give K = 9/8, 37/32, 149/128, 597/512 on Z_3, Z_9, Z_27, Z_81 (pairs at 3^i and 2·3^i), the steps being 1/8, 1/32, 1/128, 1/512, which points to 7/6 in the limit (7κ/6 = 0.9309; not proved); and on Z_135 the Z_27 pattern with a pair in each nonzero class modulo 5 has K = 19221/16384 = 1.17316, more than the brief's largest (4773/4096 = 1.16528), so β(m)·√m ≥ about 0.936 for large even m.

Lead 2 (two equal slices make a bad signing of a union, then induction on m) does not close for them as it stands, and I can say where (§5): when the full group of the largest prime p ≥ 5 has no class of one element, pairs use only (p − 1)/2 of the p − 1 equations, and at five equal classes their weight is 0.593·C/√m whatever C. 1. Setting

Fix an odd n, a prime p with p^v ∥ n, and a multiset B. For 0 ≤ i ≤ v let F_i = F_i(B) be the event "bad at p^i": F_0 = {the signs add up to 0}, and for i ≥ 1, by the slice criterion with the root 1,

F_i = {in every class modulo p^(i−1) (a group at level i) the p classes modulo p^i in it (its subclasses) have equal sums of signs}.

A group with an empty subclass is flat only if all its subclass sums are 0. Put X_p(B) = (F_1 ∪ … ∪ F_v) \ F_0 and e_p(m) = the supremum of P(X_p(B)) over all odd n and all multisets of m elements. P(F_0) = c(m) exactly, so the share of signings bad at some power of p (1 included) is c(m) + P(X_p(B)). Sub-multisets of B are taken as multisets on the same Z_n, with the same definitions.

2. The recursion (text three's Proposition 3, for any odd prime)

Lemma 2. Let B have m elements. If B lies in one class modulo p^v, then X_p(B) = ∅. Otherwise let j be the least level with B not in one class modulo p^j, B_1, …, B_k (2 ≤ k ≤ p) the occupied classes modulo p^j, r_1 ≥ … ≥ r_k their sizes, s_t the sum of the signs of B_t, x_t = P(X_p(B_t)), u_t = c(r_t) + x_t. Then

P(X_p(B)) ≤ [k = p]·g(r_1, …, r_p) + ∏_t u_t − ∏_t c(r_t), g(r_1, …, r_p) = Σ over v ≠ 0 of ∏t P(S(r_t) = v).

Proof. Levels i < j: B lies in one subclass of one group, so F_i = {sum 0} = F_0. Level j: B is one group with k occupied subclasses; if k < p one is empty, so F_j = {all s_t = 0} ⊂ F_0; if k = p, F_j \ F_0 = {s_1 = … = s_p ≠ 0}, of share g. Levels i > j: a group at level i is a class modulo p^(i−1), inside one class modulo p^j, so the groups at level i of B are those of the B_t, and F_i(B) = ∩_t F_i(B_t) ⊂ ∩_t (F_0(B_t) ⊔ X_p(B_t)). The events for different t are independent and ∩_t F_0(B_t) ⊂ F_0(B), so the part outside F_0(B) has share at most ∏(c(r_t) + x_t) − ∏ c(r_t). If B lies in one class modulo p^v every F_i is F_0. ∎

Consequences used below. (a) e_p(r) = 0 for r < p (induction: k < p always, and ∏u − ∏c = 0 when all x_t = 0). (b) The right side increases with every x_t, so x_t may be replaced by any upper bound of e_p(r_t). (c) ∏u − ∏c = x_1·∏(s≥2) u_s + c(r_1)·(∏(s≥2) u_s − ∏(s≥2) c(r_s)), and ∏(s≥2) u_s − ∏(s≥2) c(r_s) ≤ Σ(t≥2) x_t·∏_(s≥2, s≠t) u_s. (d) u_t ≤ β(r_t): at most 1/2, at most 3/8 for r_t ≥ 3, and 0 for r_t = 1 (Theorem Q; X_p ⊔ F_0 is a set of bad signings of B_t). (e) For p = 3, k = 2 and r_1, r_2 ∈ {3, 5, 7} the bound may be replaced by Ê(r_1)·Ê(r_2)/2 (text three, Remarks 3.2 and 3.3: on X the signs add up to ±3, so half of X(B_1) ∩ X(B_2) lies in F_0(B)).

4. Theorem 2 (p ≥ 5)

The top term. For p ≥ 5 and all sizes r_1 ≥ … ≥ r_p ≥ 1 with sum m: √m·g(r_1, …, r_p) ≤ τ_p = √p·2^(1−p), with equality for m = p. g = 0 unless all sizes have one parity. For any index t, g ≤ ∏_(t≠t) max_(v≠0) P(S_(r_t) = v) (sum the factor t* over v). - All odd, t* = p (the smallest class, ρ = r_p): g ≤ ∏(t<p) q(r_t). For odd r, q(r + 2)/q(r) = (r + 2)/(r + 3), and with m ≥ r + 2: sqrt((m + 2)/m)·(r + 2)/(r + 3) ≤ sqrt((r + 4)(r + 2))/(r + 3) < 1; so √m·∏(t<p) q(r_t) does not increase when some r_t (t < p) grows by 2, and is at most its value at r_1 = … = r_p = ρ, which is sqrt(pρ)·q(ρ)^(p−1): √p·2^(1−p) for ρ = 1, and for ρ ≥ 3 at most √p·κ·(3/8)^(p−2) ≤ √p·2^(1−p). - All even: max_(v≠0) P(S_r = v) = P(S_r = 2) ≤ 1/4 (checked for r ≤ 2000; ≤ κ/√r < 1/4 beyond), and ≤ κ/√r_1 for t = 1, with m ≤ p·r_1: √m·g ≤ κ·√p·4^(2−p) ≤ √p·2^(1−p).

The contraction. Let a = a_p and suppose e_p(r) ≤ a/√r for all r < m (true for r < p by (a)). Let B have m elements, with its first split. By (c), with x_t ≤ a/√r_t (0 if r_t < p) and c(r_1) ≤ κ/√r_1:

√m·(∏u − ∏c) ≤ a·θ, θ = sqrt(m/r_1)·[∏(s≥2) u_s + κ·Σ over t ≥ 2 with r_t ≥ p of r_t^(−1/2)·∏(s≥2, s≠t) u_s].

  • k = 2: θ = sqrt(m/r_1)·[u(r_2) + κ·[r_2 ≥ p]/√r_2], and r_1 ≥ p ≥ 5 (else both x vanish). r_2 = 1: 0. r_2 = 2: (1/2)·sqrt(7/5) = 0.592. r_2 = 3, 4: (3/8)·sqrt(9/5) = 0.503. 5 ≤ r_2 < p: (3/8)·√2 = 0.530. r_2 ≥ p odd: u(r_2) = x_2 ≤ a/√5 < 1/4, so θ ≤ √2·(1/4 + κ/√5) = 0.858. r_2 ≥ p even (≥ 6): √2·(3/8 + κ/√6) = 0.991. So θ < 1.
  • 3 ≤ k ≤ p: sqrt(m/r_1) ≤ √k. Let j be the number of t ≥ 2 with r_t ≥ p; for these u ≤ 3/8 and r_t^(−1/2) ≤ 5^(−1/2); the other u_s are at most 1/2. The bracket is at most 2^(−(k−1)) for j = 0 and 2^(−(k−1−j))·(3/8)^(j−1)·(3/8 + j·κ/√5) = 2^(−(k−2))·(3/4)^(j−1)·(3/8 + j·κ/√5) ≤ 0.8165·2^(−(k−2)) for j ≥ 1 (the maximum over j is at j = 2: 0.81649). So θ ≤ 0.8165·√k·2^(2−k): 0.7072, 0.4083, 0.2283 for k = 3, 4, 5, decreasing in k.

So for k < p: √m·P(X_p(B)) ≤ a·θ ≤ a; for k = p: √m·P(X_p(B)) ≤ τ_p + a·θ_p = a. Hence e_p(m) ≤ a_p/√m for every m. ∎ (consts.py recomputes every constant of this section and, as a check, the exact recursion tables for p = 5 (m ≤ 100) and p = 7 (m ≤ 36), which stay below a_p: largest Ê_5·√m = 0.139754 = τ_5 at m = 5, largest Ê_7·√m = 0.041340 = τ_7 at m = 7; consts.out.)

Corollary 3. n′ = p^a: bad = bad at some p^i, so the bad share is c(m) + P(X_p(B)): Theorem 1 for p = 3; for p ≥ 5 at most (κ·[m even] + a_5)/√m ≤ 0.979/√m. ∎ Corollary 4. Corollary 5. By the step-down ((F2) of text three: if level i of p is not live and p^i ∥ d, then bad at d implies bad at d/p) a bad signing is bad at 1 or at some d > 1 all of whose prime-power levels are live. If at most one prime p has a live level, such a d is a power of p, so bad = bad at a power of p (or at 1 only, if no prime is live): Theorem 1 or Corollary 3's bound for p ≥ 5 (which holds on any modulus, by Theorem 2). A full group of a prime q needs q distinct residues, so with at most four distinct residues no prime q ≥ 5 is live; B mod n′ has no more distinct residues than B, so Corollary U gives 1 − 2·0.9487/√m on every Z_n. ∎

5. Composite divisors: what is missing, what I tried

Nothing in this section is proved. By the step-down and the sorting of lead 2, a bad signing that is not bad at a prime power is bad at some d = p^a·e with p ≥ 5 the largest prime of d, level a of p live, e > 1 with all primes below p; then every group at level a of p has its p slices equal at a root x of order e. As in Lemma 2 the same x serves all classes of the first split, so the event at a deeper level lies in a product of events of the classes; the count for the deeper levels would then be like §4's, with β in the place of u. I have not carried that count out, because the first split itself is missing:

(M) Constants t_p (p ≥ 5 prime) with a small sum such that for any p disjoint nonempty multisets T_1, …, T_p with m elements in all, on an odd modulus, the share of signings for which the slices s_(T_1)(x), …, s_(T_p)(x) are equal at some root of unity x ≠ 1 of odd order with all prime factors below p is at most t_p/√m. (For x = 1 and "equal and nonzero" this is the top term of §4, with t_p = √p·2^(1−p).)

What lead 2 gives. Two equal slices make a bad signing of T_t ∪ T_u (text three, Lemma G (i)), so with the induction hypothesis f(k) = min(3/8, C/√k) the share is at most ∏ f over disjoint pairs of classes. If some class is a single element this is good enough (text three, Proposition 5: 2^(−(k−1))·∏ β(r_t + 1), no root left). If no class is a single element it is not: for p = 5 and five equal classes, two pairs of size u = 2m/5 give f(u)²; at u = (8C/3)² this is (3/8)², and (3/8)²·√m/C = (3/8)·sqrt(5/2) = 0.593 whatever C is (0.581 with Theorem Q's 47/128 for even sizes; odd pair sizes would help, but with all classes even every pair is even). The same count gives 0.263 for p = 7 (three pairs) and 0.046, 0.019 for p = 11, 13: together 0.92 of the budget C/√m, before the powers of 3 (about 0.95/C) and the deeper levels. So the induction of lead 2 does not close with pairs alone, for any C. The loss is that pairs use (p − 1)/2 of the p − 1 equations. What would do: a bound for THREE OR MORE disjoint multisets with equal slices at a common x that falls faster than a pair's (at x = 1 it is a product of p − 1 binomial factors); or the bounded E|V| of lead 3, which is this statement for a heavy class. A remark on lead 3: a FIXED signing of a decoration can give many integer values: for k primes q take the nonzero multiples of n/q, all with sign +; the value at order ∏(q∈S) q is |U| − Σ(q∈S) q, as many values as the primes have distinct subset sums (checked for 3, 5, 7 on Z_105: the eight values 12, 9, 7, 5, 4, 2, 0, −3; fixedsign.out). So a bound can hold only for the expectation over the decoration's signs, as lead 3 has it. I did not search for a refutation.
