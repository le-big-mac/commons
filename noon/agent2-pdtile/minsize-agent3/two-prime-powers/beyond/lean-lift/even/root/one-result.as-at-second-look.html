1. Setting, and what is taken over

n odd, B a multiset of m elements of Z_n, signs independent and uniform, S(X) = Σ ε_b X^b. For d | n, d > 1: g = rad(d), d₀ = d/g; a cell of d is a nonempty class of B modulo d₀; for a prime p | d the p classes of a cell are its parts by k_b = (b − c)/d₀ modulo p; a cell is p-full if its p classes are all occupied; d is fully live if for every prime p | d some cell of d is p-full. A level i of p is live if some class modulo p^(i−1) has all its p subclasses modulo p^i occupied; for a fully live d the rank j_p(d) ≥ 1 is the position of the level v_p(d) among the live levels of p. From the second text:

  • (T1) The cells carry disjoint sets of signs and P(bad at d) = ∏ over the cells of P(A_c = 0), where A_c is a signed sum of roots of unity, one for each element of the cell (its Lemma 1).
  • (T2) Through p | d: A_c = Σ_t η^t σ_t, where σ_t is the signed sum of roots of unity (of order prime to p) over the class t, and A_c = 0 iff σ_0 = … = σ_(p−1); if the cell is not p-full this means that every σ_t is 0 (its (1)).
  • (T3) (Sperner 1928; Erdős 1945.) A signed sum of r ≥ 1 nonzero complex numbers takes a given value for at most C(r, ⌊r/2⌋) of the 2^r signings: share at most c_r (its Lemma 3).
  • (T4) A bad signing is bad at 1 or at some fully live d > 1 (its Lemma 2).
  • (T5) A fully live d has at least G(d) := max over p | d of 1 + (j_p(d) − 1)(p − 1) cells (its Lemma 5).
  • (T6) A fully live d is determined by its primes and their ranks: d ↦ d* := ∏ p^(j_p(d)) is one to one.

Two consequences (both in its §2, §6). (K1) A cell with R elements has P(A_c = 0) ≤ c_R (T3 for A_c itself), and P(A_c = 0) = 0 if R = 1. (K2) For a p-full cell with classes of sizes r_t, and any one class t₀: P(A_c = 0) = Σ_β ∏t P(σ_t = β) ≤ ∏(t ≠ t₀) c_(r_t) (T2, independence of the classes, T3 for each class).

For d = p^i the roots are all 1: the cells are the occupied classes modulo p^(i−1), the classes are those modulo p^i, and σ_t is the sum of the signs of the class; write P_r(v) = C(r, (r + v)/2)/2^r for the share of the signings of r elements with sum v.

2. The numbers c_r

Put c_r = 1 for r ≤ 0 and f(h) = C(2h, h)/4^h, so that c_(2h) = c_(2h−1) = f(h) for h ≥ 1, f(0) = 1, f(h)/f(h − 1) = (2h − 1)/(2h).

  • (c1) c is non-increasing on the integers, and c_r ≤ 1/2 for r ≥ 1.
  • (c2) c_(x−2) ≤ 2·c_x for every integer x. (x ≥ 3: the quotient is 2h/(2h − 1) ≤ 4/3 with h = ⌈x/2⌉ ≥ 2; x = 1, 2: 1 = 2·(1/2); x ≤ 0: 1 ≤ 2.)
  • (c3) c_r ≤ √(2/(πr)) for r ≥ 1. (h·f(h)² increases, the quotient of consecutive values being (2h + 1)²/(4h(h + 1)) > 1, and tends to 1/π by Wallis's product; so f(h) ≤ 1/√(πh), which is the claim for r = 2h and gives √(2/(π(r + 1))) for r = 2h − 1.)
  • (c4) Product lemma. For integers a_1, …, a_n ≥ 1 with sum A: ∏ c_(a_i) ≤ 2^(−(n−1))·c_(A − 2(n−1)).

Proof of (c4). ρ_h := f(h + 1)/f(h) = (2h + 1)/(2h + 2) increases with h. For a, b ≥ 1: f(a)/f(1) = ∏(h=1)^(a−1) ρ_h ≤ ∏(h=1)^(a−1) ρ_(h+b−1) = f(a + b − 1)/f(b), that is f(a)·f(b) ≤ f(1)·f(a + b − 1). By induction f(h_1)⋯f(h_n) ≤ f(1)^(n−1)·f(H) with H = h_1 + … + h_n − n + 1, for h_i ≥ 1. Take h_i = ⌈a_i/2⌉; then c_(a_i) = f(h_i), f(1) = 1/2, H ≥ 1 and H ≥ A/2 − n + 1 = x/2 with x := A − 2(n − 1). If x ≤ 0, f(H) ≤ 1 = c_x. If x ≥ 1, H ≥ ⌈x/2⌉ and f(H) ≤ f(⌈x/2⌉) = c_x. ∎

Equality holds when all a_i but one are equal to 2 (c_2·c_2·c_L = c_L/4); with ones in place of twos the index on the right is too small by the number of ones (c_1·c_1·c_L = c_L/4 against c_(L−2)/4).

3. One divisor

Let d > 1 be fully live, p a prime of d, k the number of cells of d, and N := k + p − 1 (≥ 3). Choose a p-full cell D; let r_0 ≤ r_1 ≤ … ≤ r_(p−1) be the sizes of its classes. The pieces are the p classes of D and the k − 1 other cells: N pieces, with m elements in all. By (T1), (K1), (K2), for any class t₀ of D,

(*) P(bad at d) ≤ ∏(t ≠ t₀) c(r_t) · ∏ over the other cells of c_(R_cell),

a product of N − 1 numbers c, one for each piece but t₀, each at most 1/2.

Lemma B. P(bad at d) ≤ 2^(−(N−2))·c_⌈m/N⌉ ≤ √(2/(πm))·√N·2^(−(N−2)).

Proof. A largest piece has at least m/N elements. Take for t₀ a smallest class of D other than that piece (if the largest piece is a class of D of least size, all classes of D have that size and another one serves). In (*) the largest piece gives at most c_⌈m/N⌉ and the other N − 2 factors at most 1/2 each. The second inequality is (c3). ∎

Lemma C. P(bad at d) ≤ 2^(−(N−3))·c_(r_1)·c_(m − r_0 − r_1 − 2(N−3)) ≤ 2^(−(N−2))·γ(m, N), where

γ(m, N) := max over 1 ≤ r ≤ (m − 1)/2 of 2·c_r·c_(max(m − 2r, r) − 2(N−3)).

Proof. In (*) with t₀ the class of size r_0, split off the factor c_(r_1). The other N − 2 ≥ 1 factors (the classes 2, …, p − 1 of D, there is one since p ≥ 3, and the k − 1 other cells) have indices adding up to m − r_0 − r_1, and (c4) bounds their product by 2^(−(N−3))·c_(m − r_0 − r_1 − 2(N−3)). For the second inequality put r = r_1: 1 ≤ r_1 ≤ (m − 1)/2 because r_0 ≥ 1 and r_2 ≥ r_1; m − r_0 − r_1 ≥ m − 2r_1 because r_0 ≤ r_1, and m − r_0 − r_1 ≥ r_2 ≥ r_1; then (c1). ∎

Lemma C is attained, for instance by residues (L, 1, 1) modulo 3 with L odd (share c_L/2; N = 3, r_0 = r_1 = 1). It holds for every integer N ≥ 3 that is k + p − 1 for the d at hand, and γ is defined for every integer N ≥ 3.

Monotony. N ↦ 2^(−(N−2))·γ(m, N) and N ↦ √N·2^(−(N−2)) do not increase: by (c2) each term of γ at most doubles when N grows by 1, and √((N + 1)/N) < 2.

4. All divisors: the weights and Theorem 1

A pattern d* is a finite nonempty set of odd primes with a rank j_p ≥ 1 for each; G(d) := max_p (1 + (j_p − 1)(p − 1)), P(d) := its largest prime, and

N(d) := G(d) + P(d) − 1 (odd, ≥ 3), cnt(N) := the number of patterns with N(d) = N.

For a fully live d with pattern d* take p = P(d) in §3: by (T5), N = k + P − 1 ≥ N(d). If P(bad at d) > 0 every cell has at least two elements (K1) and one has at least P, so m ≥ 2(k − 1) + P ≥ N ≥ N(d*).

Count. The patterns with largest prime P and G ≤ g number J(P, g)·∏ over the odd primes q < P of (1 + J(q, g)), J(q, g) = 1 + ⌊(g − 1)/(q − 1)⌋ (the number of ranks allowed); differences in g give cnt(N) (bound.py counts, checked against a plain list of the patterns for N ≤ 19; counts_61.out):

(N = 3: the pattern 3; N = 5: 5, 9, 15; N = 7: 7, 21, 35, 105, 27, 45.)

Tail. With μ(d) = max_p j_p(p − 1) as in sign-four-result.md: N(d) ≥ μ(d) + 1 (if q attains μ, then G + P − 1 ≥ (j_q − 1)(q − 1) + q = μ + 1). So the patterns with N(d) = N are among the odd d with μ(d) ≤ N − 1, which number A(N − 1) ≤ 2^(N−1)·(u + 1)·x^u, u = (N − 1)/2, x < 0.5783 (proof of Lemma W there). Hence cnt(N)·√N·2^(−(N−2)) ≤ 2·√(2u + 1)·(u + 1)·x^u; for u ≥ 31 consecutive such terms have quotient at most x·(1 + 1/31)^1.5 < 0.607, and the sum over N ≥ 63 is below 5.5·10^(−5). With the exact terms for N ≤ 61:

*Σ_h := Σ over all patterns of √N(d)·2^(−(N(d)−2)) < 2.62509, Σ₁ := Σ of 2^(−(N(d)−2)), 1.191896 < Σ₁ < 1.191951.

Proof of Theorem 1. By (T4) a bad signing is bad at 1 or at a fully live d > 1. P(bad at 1) is c_m for even m and 0 for odd m, at most √(2/(πm)) by (c3). For a fully live d, Lemma B with p = P(d) and monotony give P(bad at d) ≤ √(2/(πm))·√N(d)·2^(−(N(d*)−2)); by (T6) different d have different patterns. So the share of bad signings is at most √(2/(πm))·(1 + Σ_h) < 2.893/√m. ∎

√m·β(m) ≤ √(2/π)·([m even] + f₁·Σ_(N ≤ 21) cnt(N)·2^(−(N−2)) + Σ_(N > 21) cnt(N)·√N·2^(−(N−2))).

For M₀ = 3000 the right side is below 0.9590 (m odd) and 1.7569 (m even).

Proof. In γ(m, N): for r ≤ 2, 2c_r = 1 and the index is at least m − 2N + 2, so the term is at most √(2/(π(m − 2N + 2))). For 3 ≤ r ≤ m/9: 2c_r ≤ 2c_3 = 3/4 and the index is at least 7m/9 − 2N + 6: at most (3/4)·√(2/(π(7m/9 − 2N + 6))). 6. Even m: what parity gives at the powers of one prime

For even m the level 0 alone costs c_m ≈ 0.798/√m, so the other divisors matter more. At d = p^i the class sums are integers, and for a signing that is not bad at 1 some full cell has a common value other than 0. Put, for even r ≥ 2, c_r := max over v ≠ 0 of P_r(v) = C(r, r/2 + 1)/2^r; c_2 = c_4 = 1/4, c_r decreases from r = 4 on, c*_r ≤ c_r.

Lemma E. Let m be even, d = p^i fully live with k cells, and L any class modulo p^i, with ℓ elements. Then P(bad at d and not bad at 1) ≤ 2^(−(k+2p−5))·c_ℓ, that is 2^(−(N−2))·2^(−(p−2))·c_ℓ with N = k + p − 1.

Proof. A class with sum 0 has an even number of elements; in a full flat cell with common value v all classes have the parity of v. So if some signing is bad at d, every cell is either even (all its classes even) or odd (full, all classes odd; then the cell has an odd number of elements, p being odd, and v ≠ 0). m is even, so the number F_o of odd cells is even. Let F ≥ 1 be the number of full cells. Bounds for one cell, from §1, where the factor c_ℓ can be kept if L is a class of the cell (choose t₀ ≠ L in (K2)): (n) not full: P(flat) ≤ 1/2, or ≤ c_ℓ; (f) full: P(flat) ≤ 2^(−(p−1)), or ≤ c_ℓ·2^(−(p−2)); (f) full and even: P(flat with v ≠ 0) = Σ_(v ≠ 0) ∏t P(r_t)(v) ≤ ∏_(t ≠ t₀) c_(r_t) ≤ 4^(−(p−1)), or ≤ c_ℓ·4^(−(p−2)).

F_o ≥ 2. Then F ≥ 2, and P(bad at d) ≤ c_ℓ·2^(−e) with e = (k − F − 1) + (p − 1)F if L is in a cell that is not full and e = (k − F) + (p − 2) + (p − 1)(F − 1) if it is in a full one; both are k − 1 + (p − 2)F ≥ k + 2p − 5.

F_o = 0. All cells are even, and a signing that is bad at d and not at 1 has v_D ≠ 0 in some full cell D (if all class sums were 0 the signs would add up to 0). So the share is at most Σ_D P(all cells flat, v_D ≠ 0), with (f*) for D and (n), (f) for the others. If L is in a cell that is not full, each term is at most c_ℓ·2^(−e), e = (k − F − 1) + 2(p − 1) + (p − 1)(F − 1) = k + p − 2 + (p − 2)F, and F·2^(−(p−2)F) ≤ 2^(−(p−2)): the sum is at most c_ℓ·2^(−(k+2p−4)). If L is in a full cell D_L: the term D = D_L is at most c_ℓ·2^(−e) with e = 2(p − 2) + (k − F) + (p − 1)(F − 1) = k + p − 3 + (p − 2)F, each of the F − 1 others with e = 2(p − 1) + (p − 2) + (k − F) + (p − 1)(F − 2) = k + p − 2 + (p − 2)F; the sum is c_ℓ·2^(−(k+2p−5))·2^(−(p−2)(F−1))·(F + 1)/2 ≤ c_ℓ·2^(−(k+2p−5)). ∎

Lemma E′ (p = 3, one cell). Let m be even and d = 3^i fully live with a single cell. Then P(bad at d and not bad at 1) ≤ (17/128)·c_ℓ for each of its three classes, ℓ its size.

Proof. With class sizes ℓ, a, b the event is "the three sums are equal to some v ≠ 0" (the signs add up to 3v), of share Σ_(v ≠ 0) P_ℓ(v)P_a(v)P_b(v) ≤ c_ℓ·Σ_(v ≠ 0) P_a(v)P_b(v); it is empty unless ℓ, a, b have one parity, hence are even. Σ_(v ≠ 0) P_a(v)P_b(v) ≤ c_max(a,b), which is at most c_32 < 0.13172 < 17/128 if a or b is at least 32; for even a, b ≤ 30 the largest value is 17/128, at a = b = 4 (evenconst.py, exact). ∎

Lemma F. Let d = p^i be fully live with k cells, N = k + p − 1, ℓ the size of a largest class modulo p^i and s = m − ℓ. Then P(bad at d) ≤ 2^(−(N−3))·c_ℓ·c_(⌈s/2⌉ − 2(N−3)).

Proof. P(bad at d) is the product over the cells of P(flat); for a cell that is not full this is the product of the P(σ = 0) ≤ c_r over all its classes, for a full cell at most ∏(t ≠ t₀) c(r_t) with t₀ a class of least size, chosen other than L. Call the classes other than the t₀'s factor classes: L is one, and there are n ≥ (k − F) + F(p − 1) ≥ N − 1 of them. A t₀ is no larger than any factor class of its cell, and its cell has a factor class other than L (p − 1 ≥ 2); so the t₀'s together have at most as many elements as the factor classes other than L, whose total is therefore at least ⌈s/2⌉. By (c4) for these n − 1 ≥ 1 classes, (c1), and (c2) for n ≥ N − 1: their product is at most 2^(−(n−2))·c_(⌈s/2⌉ − 2(n−2)) ≤ 2^(−(N−3))·c_(⌈s/2⌉ − 2(N−3)). ∎

The bound for even m. There are at most pk classes, so ℓ ≥ m/(pk). For a prime power pattern p^j put G = 1 + (j − 1)(p − 1), LC(m, N) := 2^(−(N−2))·γ(m, N),

Fb(m, p, k, g) := 2^(−(k+p−3))·max over 0 ≤ s ≤ m − ⌈m/(pk)⌉ of c_(m−s)·min(g, 2·c_(⌈s/2⌉ − 2(k+p−4))),

PPE(m, p, j) := max( max over k = G, …, G + q − 1 of min(LC(m, k + p − 1), Fb(m, p, k, g_k)), LC(m, G + q + p − 1) ),

with q = max(2, p − 2), g_k = 2^(−(p−2)), except g_1 = 17/64 for the pattern 3; for p ≥ 11 simply PPE = LC(m, N(p^j)). Theorem 4. For even m: β(m) ≤ B_E(m) := c_m + Σ over the patterns with at least two primes of LC(m, N(d)) + Σ over the prime powers p^j of PPE(m, p, j) (patterns with N(d) > m contribute 0).

Proof. A bad signing is bad at 1 (share c_m), or bad at a fully live d with two primes or more (Lemma C, as in Theorem 2), or not bad at 1 and bad at a fully live d = p^i. For such a d with rank j and k ≥ G cells: its share is at most LC(m, k + p − 1) (Lemma C) and at most Fb(m, p, k, g_k) (Lemmas E, E′, F with s = m − ℓ ≤ m − ⌈m/(pk)⌉); for k ≥ G + q, LC(m, k + p − 1) ≤ LC(m, G + q + p − 1) by monotony and the convention. ∎

10. What is not proved, and what I am least sure of

  • Not proved: β(m) ≤ 1/√m for even m (the brief's (b)). Here: 1.5529 for every even m, at most 1.2699 in the limit; the families give 0.9364.
