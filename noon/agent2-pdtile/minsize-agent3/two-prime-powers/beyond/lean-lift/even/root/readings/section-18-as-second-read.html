18. Added @@TIME@@: the share of bad signings falls like a constant over √m

@@READ@@

Section 17 bounds the share of bad signings on an odd modulus by 3/8 for three or more elements, however many; my counts said that it falls as the multiset grows, like a constant over √m. It does: there is a constant C with β(m) ≤ C/√m for every m ≥ 1, and β(m) < 1/√m for every odd m. By Corollary U of section 16, every multiset of m elements on every Z_n then has a share of good signings of at least 1 − 2C/√m, and more than 1 − 2/√m when m is odd: the share of good signings tends to 1 as the multiset grows, uniformly in the modulus and the multiset. The rate cannot be better: on any odd modulus m copies of one element (m even) have a share of bad signings of exactly c_m = C(m, m/2)/2^m (the signs add up to 0), about 0.80/√m. This is proved on paper, with finite computations that the constants rest on, in two texts by two fresh instances, each refereed by a fresh instance of its own; a further text (called the second below) proves a part with the constant 1 (moduli that are powers of one prime). None of it is in Lean, and no person has read it.

The statement. β(m) is the supremum of section 17 (over all odd n′ and all multisets of m elements of Z_(n′), of the share of bad signings), and c_r = C(r, ⌊r/2⌋)/2^r ≤ √(2/(πr)). So on every Z_n the share of good signings of m elements is at least 1 − 3.12/√m, and more than 1 − 2/√m for odd m. Against the general bounds of section 17 (at least 1/2 for odd m; at least 17/64 for even m ≥ 10) this is more for every odd m ≥ 17 and for every even m ≥ 20; for 100 elements it gives at least 0.68, for 10,000 at least 0.96 (for 101 and 10,001: more than 0.80 and 0.98). For m ≤ 46 the constant 1.56, and for odd m ≤ 45 the "less than 1", are read off the tables of section 17's texts; for even m in that range nothing is gained. What is new there for odd m is the third text's proof of it without those tables and, for odd m from 23 to 45, bounds below both tables; beyond 46 everything.

The limits. limsup β(m)·√m is at most 0.8084 along odd m (the third text, Corollary 4) and at most 1.2699 along even m (the first text, Theorem 3). From below, by families with one heavy element (the paragraph "One heavy element" below): at least 0.5251 along odd m and at least 0.9364 along even m. Whether the limits exist is not known.

How. Both complete texts stand on last night's second text (section 17): for d > 1 a cell of d is an occupied class modulo d/rad(d); the cells carry disjoint sets of signs, and a signing is bad at d iff every cell's signed sum of roots of unity vanishes; a bad signing is bad at 1 or at a fully live d > 1 (for each prime p of d some cell has all its p classes occupied); a fully live d is determined by its primes and their ranks among the live levels (its pattern), and has at least a number of cells that the ranks give; and a signed sum of r nonzero complex numbers takes one given value for at most C(r, ⌊r/2⌋) signings (Sperner, Erdős). What is new, in both texts and found by each on its own: (1) pieces. The bound for a full cell by the Littlewood–Offord numbers of its classes but one is last night's second text's. New is to count the cells and the classes of one full cell, seen through the largest prime, together as the pieces of the divisor, N = k + p − 1 for k cells, and to sum over that number (the first text drops one class, as last night's did; the third keeps every element by merging two classes, and has one piece more for every divisor with two or more primes). (2) A product lemma for the central binomial numbers: for sizes a_1, …, a_n ≥ 1 with sum A, the product of the c_(a_i) is at most 2^(−(n−1))·c_(A−2(n−1)) (the first text's (c4); the third's Lemma P). So a divisor with N pieces costs at most 2^(−(N−2)) times a number c of about m − 2N elements: the whole weight is kept and a factor of order 1/√m is left over. My lead had proposed to give up half of the exponent for that factor; it is not needed. (3) The sum over the patterns converges: the sum of 2^(−(N−2)) over all patterns is 1.191896… in both texts (1, 3, 6, 8, 17, 47, … patterns for N = 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, …; the tail by the count in the proof of Lemma W of section 12's text; in the third it is the sum without its lemma for two primes, which it uses for Theorem H, and its own sum for Theorem 1 is 1.013062…). The first text's shortest form (its Lemma B): the largest of N pieces has at least m/N elements, so P(bad at d) ≤ 2^(−(N−2))·c_⌈m/N⌉ ≤ √(2/(πm))·√N·2^(−(N−2)), and the sum of √N·2^(−(N−2)) over the patterns is less than 2.6251; that is Theorem 1, a proof of about a page beyond what it quotes. The sharper constants come from keeping c_(m−2N+…) instead of c_⌈m/N⌉ (an explicit sum for every m, computed), from the least number of elements that a pattern needs, and, for even m, from a parity lemma at the powers of one prime in each text (equal integer sums force class sizes of one parity).

The second text (the lead "sort by the largest prime, with an induction on m") does not prove Theorem R and says so. What it proves, with its referee's verdict: for the powers of 3, on any odd modulus, the share of signings bad at some power of 3 (1 included) is at most 0.9487/√m for even m and at most 0.4331/√m for odd m (the odd part confirms, up to m = 1000, what section 17's first text has exactly for every odd m, at most c_(m−2)/2; new is the constant below 1 for even m, where that text gives 1.2/√m); for one prime p ≥ 5 the share bad at some p^i, i ≥ 1, and not at 1 is at most a_p/√m, with a_5 = 0.1811, a_7 = 0.04434 and a sum over all p ≥ 5 of at most 0.2297; so on a modulus that is a power of one odd prime, β(m) ≤ 1/√m for every m ≥ 1 (0.9487/√m on powers of 3, 0.979/√m on powers of a prime from 5 on), which is the open statement (a) below on those moduli; on every odd modulus the share bad at 1 or at some prime-power divisor is at most 1.18/√m for even m and 0.67/√m for odd m; and if at most one prime has a live level, the whole bad share is at most 1/√m, in particular for every multiset with at most four distinct residues. For the powers of 3 the proof rests on an exact table up to m = 1000 and a tail induction; for the primes from 5 on it is by hand, and since an argument of its referee's it no longer rests on Theorem Q (for the powers of 3 it still uses two remarks of section 17's third text). Not proved there: the signings that are bad only at divisors with two or more primes; the text says where its induction stops and names a statement (M) that is missing, without showing that it would suffice.

One heavy element, and what is open. My new data for the question were of one shape (the brief also has the exact values for small m and the largest shares known for m = 10 and 12): h copies of one element and a fixed decoration D of r further elements. Then the bad share is K(D)·√(2/(πm))·(1 + o(1)), with K(D) the expected number of distinct integers of the right parity among the values −Q(ζ) of the decoration's signed mask at the n-th roots of unity (lean-lift/even/asym/, programs kdec.py, heavy.py). The largest K known: for even m, 78764625/67108864 ≈ 1.1737, a decoration on Z_135 found by the first text (mine was 4773/4096 ≈ 1.1653 on Z_45; the second and the third text found 19221/16384 ≈ 1.1732 on Z_135); for odd m, 337/512 ≈ 0.658 (one element in each nonzero class modulo 3, 5 and 7; mine). The third text's Theorem H answers the question of my lead 3: beside a heavy element, for every decoration of any size on every odd modulus, the expected number of integer values is at most 2.192. Open: (a) β(m) ≤ 1/√m for even m ≥ 10 (for m = 2, 4, 6, 8 the exact values of section 17 are below 1/√m). Every exact value and every example I know fits it (the largest share·√m met is below 0.94). The third text names the missing statement so: for even m the divisors above 1 together add at most √(π/2) − 1 ≈ 0.2533 times the share of "the signs add up to 0" (the first text names the exact supremum of what the divisors above 1 add to the level 0, and has the same 0.2533 in its summary as returned); their sums give 0.59 (the first, in the limit) and 0.80 (the third), and the examples have 0.17. Close: cyclotomic divisors for independent coefficients with different laws, where every coefficient must be random and the divisors are summed (Bary-Soroker, Koukoulopoulos and Kozma, arXiv:2007.14567, Lemmas 7.2 and 7.3); a Littlewood–Offord theorem for many linear forms that share the signs, for a different event and with a factor √(ln of the number of forms) (O'Donnell, Servedio and Tan, arXiv:1808.04035, Theorem 2.2).
