0. Summary

Proved here (complete; §1 to §4; every step is either quoted from a refereed text, with its place, or proved below).

  • Theorem 1. For every m ≥ 1, β(m) ≤ Ψ(m), where Ψ(m) is the explicit finite sum of §3: c_m if m is even, plus, for each odd number e > 1 with need(e) ≤ m, a weight 2^(1−k)·c*_(m−2k+2), k = κ⁺(e).
  • Corollary 2 (Conjecture R, with constants). √m·Ψ(m) ≤ 1.82196 for every m (largest at m = 14), so β(m) ≤ 1.822/√m for every m ≥ 1. With the parity refinement for even m (Lemma E and Theorem 1′ of §3a: β(m) ≤ Ψ_e(m) ≤ Ψ(m) for even m): β(m) ≤ 1.6914/√m for every m ≥ 1 (largest at m = 14), by this text and the lemmas it quotes, without Theorem Q. With Theorem Q of the brief for the even m ≤ 18: β(m) ≤ 1.6212/√m for every m ≥ 1 (largest at m = 20).
  • Corollary 3 (odd m: part (b) of the brief). Ψ(m) < 1/√m for every odd m. So β(m) < 1/√m for every odd m, without Theorem Q. The margin is thin at two places: √13·Ψ(13) = 0.99382 (Ψ(13) = 1129/4096) and √15·Ψ(15) = 0.99047 (Ψ(15) = 2095/8192); from m = 17 on √m·Ψ(m) is at most 0.9702 and falls, to the limit 0.8083.
  • Corollary 4 (limits: part (c)). limsup β(m)√m ≤ √(2/π)·(1 + 2W_e) = 1.4398 along even m (Theorem 1′; 1.6062 by Theorem 1 alone) and ≤ √(2/π)·2W⁺ = 0.8084 along odd m, where 2W⁺ = Σ_e 2^(1−κ⁺(e)) = 1.013062… and 2W_e = 0.804505…. (From below: the brief's examples give 0.930 and 0.525; §4 raises the first to 0.936.)
  • Theorem H (lead 3's question). For every decoration D of any size beside a heavy element, on every odd modulus, the expected number of integer values is bounded: E|V| ≤ 1 + 2W′ < 2.192 (2W′ = 1.191896…), and K_other ≤ 2W′.

Not proved. β(m) ≤ 1/√m for even m ≥ 10 (part (b) for even m): my constant along even m is 1.44 in the limit and at worst 1.69 (1.62 with Theorem Q); the truth seems to be about 0.94. The missing statement is in §6, with what I tried. The limits of β(m)√m are not determined, only bounded as above.

1. Setting, and what is quoted

n odd, B a multiset of m elements of Z_n, signs independent and uniform, S(X) = Σ ε_b X^b; "bad at d" means Φ_d | S. c_r = C(r, ⌊r/2⌋)/2^r for r ≥ 1; c_(2j−1) = c_(2j) = g(j) with g(j) := C(2j, j)/4^j, g(0) = 1, g(1) = 1/2. I write c_r = c_r for r ≥ 1 and c_r = 1/2 for r ≤ 0.

For d | n: d_0 = d/rad(d); a cell of d is a nonempty class of B modulo d_0; for b in the cell of c, k_b = (b − c)/d_0 mod rad(d). With ω of order rad(d), A_c = Σ_(b in the cell) ε_b ω^(k_b).

  • (Q1) cells (Lemma 1 of quarter/two/result.md): ε is bad at d iff A_c = 0 for every cell.
  • (Q2) classes through a prime (the same text, (1)): for a prime p | d write rad(d) = p·g″, ω = η·θ, η of order p, θ of order g″. The p classes of a cell are the sets k_b ≡ t (mod p), with sums σ_t = Σ ε_b θ^(k_b) ∈ Z[θ]; A_c = 0 iff σ_0 = σ_1 = … = σ_(p−1) (the minimal polynomial of η over Q(θ) is 1 + X + … + X^(p−1)). A cell is p-full if its p classes are all occupied; d > 1 is fully live if for every prime p | d some cell is p-full.
  • (Q3) descent (its Lemma 2): a bad signing is bad at 1 or at some fully live d > 1.
  • (Q4) one value (its Lemma 3; Sperner 1928, Erdős 1945): for nonzero complex u_1, …, u_r and any complex β, P(Σ ε_i u_i = β) ≤ c_r.
  • (Q5) rank and cells (its §1 and Lemma 5): if a cell of d is p-full, level v_p(d) of the p-tree is live; the rank j_p(d) ≥ 1 is the position of v_p(d) among the live levels of p. A fully live d has at least 1 + (j_p(d) − 1)(p − 1) cells, for every prime p | d. A fully live d is determined by its primes and their ranks (v_p(d) is the j_p-th live level of p).
  • (Q6) how many e (proof of Lemma W in sign-four-result.md): with μ(e) = max_p v_p(e)(p − 1), the number A(t) of odd e with μ(e) ≤ t satisfies A(t)·2^(−t) ≤ (t/2 + 1)·x^(t/2), x < 0.5783.

The cells of d are disjoint sets of signs, so P(bad at d) = ∏ over the cells of P(A_c = 0).

2. Pieces

A piece is a nonempty set of elements of B. Disjoint pieces G_1, …, G_K cover an event E if P(E) ≤ ∏ c_(|G_i|). (Every bound below has this shape; bounds for events on disjoint sets of signs multiply.)

Lemma A (a full cell keeps all its elements). Let a cell of d be p-full, with classes of r_0, …, r_(p−1) ≥ 1 elements, in any order. Then P(A_c = 0) ≤ c_(r_0)·c_(r_1)·…·c_(r_(p−3))·c_(r_(p−2) + r_(p−1)): p − 1 pieces that hold all elements of the cell. Any cell is covered by itself as one piece: P(A_c = 0) ≤ c_R, R its size.

Proof. By (Q2) and the independence of the classes, P(A_c = 0) = Σ_β ∏t P(σ_t = β) ≤ ∏(t ≤ p−3) max_β P(σ_t = β) · Σ_β P(σ_(p−2) = β)·P(σ_(p−1) = β). The last sum is P(σ_(p−2) − σ_(p−1) = 0), and σ_(p−2) − σ_(p−1) is a signed sum of r_(p−2) + r_(p−1) nonzero complex numbers (reversing the signs of one class does not change their distribution). Now (Q4) for each factor. The second sentence is (Q4) for A_c itself. ∎

(The texts drop one class of the full cell; here the last two classes are merged, so no element is lost. For p = 3 and one cell: P(bad at 3) ≤ c_(r_0)·c_(r_1 + r_2) ≤ c_(m−2)/2, attained by (m − 2, 1, 1), m odd.)

Lemma P (the product of the pieces). Let r_1, …, r_K ≥ 1 with sum M. Then ∏ c_(r_i) ≤ 2^(−(K−1))·c*_(M − 2K + 2) =: h_M(K), and h_M(K + 1) ≤ h_M(K).

Proof. g(j + 1)/g(j) = (2j + 1)/(2j + 2) increases with j, so for 1 ≤ a ≤ b: g(a)/g(a − 1) ≤ g(b + 1)/g(b), that is g(a)·g(b) ≤ g(a − 1)·g(b + 1); repeating, g(a)·g(b) ≤ g(1)·g(a + b − 1) for all a, b ≥ 1, and by induction ∏(i ≤ K) g(a_i) ≤ g(1)^(K−1)·g(Σ a_i − K + 1) for a_i ≥ 1. Put a_i = ⌈r_i/2⌉, so c(r_i) = g(a_i). Then Σ a_i − K + 1 ≥ 1 and Σ a_i − K + 1 ≥ ⌈M/2⌉ − K + 1, and g decreases; so the product is at most 2^(−(K−1))·g(max(1, ⌈M/2⌉ − K + 1)). If M − 2K + 2 ≥ 1 then ⌈M/2⌉ − K + 1 = ⌈(M − 2K + 2)/2⌉ ≥ 1 and g(⌈(M − 2K + 2)/2⌉) = c_(M−2K+2); otherwise the factor is g(1) = 1/2. That is h_M(K). Monotony: with r = M − 2K + 2, h_M(K + 1)/h_M(K) = c_(r−2)/(2·cr), and c*(r−2) ≤ 2·c*r: for r ≥ 3, c(r−2)/c_r = g(j − 1)/g(j) = 2j/(2j − 1) ≤ 2 (j = ⌈r/2⌉ ≥ 2); for r ≤ 2 both are 1/2. ∎

For an odd e > 1 with exponents j_p (p | e) and largest prime q put

s(e) = max over p | e of (j_p − 1)(p − 1), κ(e) = s(e) + q − 1, κ⁺(e) = κ(e) if e is a prime power, κ(e) + 1 if e has two or more primes, need(e) = q + 2·s(e), and q + 1 if e has two or more primes and s(e) = 0.

κ(e) is even, κ(e) ≥ μ(e), κ(q^j) = j(q − 1), need(e) ≥ κ(e) + 1. For a fully live d let e(d) = ∏ p^(j_p(d)); by (Q5) the map d ↦ e(d) is injective on the fully live divisors of one multiset. Write κ(d) for κ(e(d)), and so on.

Lemma B. Let d > 1 be fully live. (i) The bad event at d is covered by K ≥ κ(d) pieces that partition B, and P(bad at d) ≤ h_m(κ(d)) = 2^(1−κ)·c*_(m−2κ+2). (ii) If P(bad at d) > 0 then m ≥ q + 2·s(d).

Proof. Let q be the largest prime of d and p a prime of d with (j_p − 1)(p − 1) = s(d). Some cell C* is q-full; by Lemma A it is covered by q − 1 pieces that partition it. By (Q5) there are at least s(d) other cells; each is one piece. The cells partition B, so the pieces do, and K ≥ q − 1 + s(d) = κ(d). By (Q1), independence and Lemma P, P(bad at d) ≤ ∏ c_(|G_i|) ≤ h_m(K) ≤ h_m(κ(d)). (ii) A cell of one element has A_c = ± a root of unity, not 0; so if some signing is bad at d every cell has at least two elements, C* has at least q, and m ≥ q + 2·s(d). ∎

Lemma C (a cell full for two primes). Let p < q be primes of d and let a cell C of d be p-full and q-full. Then the event A_C = 0 is covered by at least q pieces that partition C. If C has exactly q elements, A_C is never 0.

Proof. Write rad(d) = p·q·g‴ and ω = η_p·η_q·θ′ with η_p, η_q, θ′ of orders p, q, g‴. For b ∈ C let s_b = k_b mod p, t_b = k_b mod q; the subclass (s, t) has the sum τ_(s,t) = Σ ε_b θ′^(k_b) ∈ Z[θ′] (0 if the subclass is empty). Seen through q as in (Q2), with θ = η_p θ′: the classes are the columns t ∈ Z_q, σ_t = Σ_s η_p^s τ_(s,t), and A_C = 0 iff all σ_t are equal. Fact F: for x_0, …, x_(p−1) in Q(θ′), Σ_s η_p^s x_s = 0 iff x_0 = … = x_(p−1) (minimal polynomial of η_p over Q(θ′); p ∤ g‴). Every β in Z[η_p θ′] = Z[η_p, θ′] can be written Σ_s η_p^s β_s with β_s ∈ Z[θ′]. Call a row s ∈ Z_p occupied in a column t if the subclass (s, t) is not empty. Every column is occupied somewhere (q-full) and every row is occupied in some column (p-full).

Choose two columns t_1 ≠ t_2 (below). Conditioning on the signs of column t_2, as in Lemma A,

P(A_C = 0) ≤ P(σ_(t_1) = σ_(t_2)) · ∏ over t ≠ t_1, t_2 of sup_β P(σ_t = β).

(α) One column t, any β = Σ η_p^s β_s. By Fact F, σ_t = β iff there is a c with τ_(s,t) = β_s + c for all s. Let O_t be the set of rows occupied in t. If |O_t| = 1: one piece, the column, by (Q4). If 2 ≤ |O_t| ≤ p − 1: take a row s_3 not in O_t; then τ_(s_3,t) = 0 forces c = −β_(s_3), and the event implies τ_(s,t) = β_s − β_(s_3) for every s ∈ O_t: events on disjoint sets of signs, each of probability at most c of the size of its subclass, by (Q4): |O_t| ≥ 2 pieces, the subclasses of the column. If |O_t| = p: P = Σ_c ∏s P(τ(s,t) = β_s + c) ≤ ∏(s ≤ p−3) c(|(s,t)|) · P(τ_(p−2,t) − τ_(p−1,t) = β_(p−2) − β_(p−1)): p − 1 ≥ 2 pieces that hold the column (as in Lemma A).

(β) The pair. σ_(t_1) − σ_(t_2) = Σ_s η_p^s D_s with D_s = τ_(s,t_1) − τ_(s,t_2), a signed sum over the elements of row s in the two columns; by Fact F it vanishes iff all D_s are equal. Let O be the set of rows occupied in t_1 or in t_2. If |O| = 1: one piece, the two columns, by (Q4). If 2 ≤ |O| ≤ p − 1: some row has D = 0 identically, so the event implies D_s = 0 for every s ∈ O: |O| ≥ 2 pieces (the rows of the pair). If |O| = p: Σ_c ∏_s P(D_s = c) gives p − 1 ≥ 2 pieces that hold the pair, as before.

So there are q − 1 groups (q − 2 single columns and the pair), each giving one piece or at least two, and the pieces partition C. The choice. If some column t_0 is occupied in two or more rows, choose t_1, t_2 different from t_0 (q ≥ 5): column t_0 gives at least two pieces. Otherwise every column t is occupied in exactly one row s(t); s is onto Z_p and p ≥ 3, so there are t_1, t_2 with s(t_1) ≠ s(t_2); choose them: |O| = 2 ≤ p − 1 and the pair gives two pieces. Either way at least (q − 2) + 2 = q pieces. Exactly q elements: then every column is one element, we are in the second case, and the event of the pair requires D_(s(t_1)) = 0, a single ± root of unity: impossible. ∎

Lemma B⁺. Let d > 1 be fully live with two or more primes. (i) The bad event at d is covered by K ≥ κ(d) + 1 pieces that partition B, and P(bad at d) ≤ h_m(κ(d) + 1) = 2^(−κ)·c*_(m−2κ). (ii) If P(bad at d) > 0 and s(d) = 0, then m ≥ q + 1.

Proof. q, p, C* as in Lemma B, and p′ ≠ q another prime of d. (i) If C* is p′-full, Lemma C covers C* by q pieces in place of q − 1. If not, some other cell C′ is p′-full and Lemma A covers C′ by p′ − 1 ≥ 2 pieces in place of one. Either way K ≥ κ(d) + 1; then Lemma P. (ii) If m = q, then C* (at least q elements) is all of B and the only cell, so it is p′-full, with exactly q elements; by Lemma C no signing is bad at d. ∎

(Attained: Lemma B (i) by (m − 2, 1, 1) modulo 3 for odd m; for q = 5 it is not attained by integer slices: (m − 4, 1, 1, 1, 1) modulo 5 has c_(m−4)/8 against the bound c_(m−6)/8, because classes of two elements and of one cannot have equal sums. 3. Theorem 1 and the constants

*Ψ(m) := [m even]·c_m + Σ over the odd e > 1 with need(e) ≤ m of 2^(1−κ⁺(e))·c_(m − 2κ⁺(e) + 2).

Theorem 1. β(m) ≤ Ψ(m) for every m ≥ 1.

Proof. By (Q3) a bad signing is bad at 1 (share c_m if m is even, 0 if m is odd: the signs must add up to 0) or at a fully live d > 1. By Lemma B and Lemma B⁺ the share of the signings bad at d is 0 unless m ≥ need(d), and is at most 2^(1−k)·c*_(m−2k+2) with k = κ⁺(d). The fully live d have different e(d), so the sum over them is at most the sum over all odd e > 1 with need(e) ≤ m. ∎

3a. Even m: parity for the powers of one prime (Lemma E, Theorem 1′)

For even m the terms of the prime powers can be lowered. Theorem 1, Corollary 3 and the numbers of §3 do not depend on this section.

Lemma E. Let m be even and d > 1 fully live with a single prime q, so e(d) = q^j and κ = κ(d) = j(q − 1). Then P(bad at d) ≤ max(E1, E2), where E1 := (3/4)·2^(1−κ)·c_(m−2κ) if m ≥ 2κ + 2 and E1 := 0 otherwise, and E2 := h_m(κ + q − 2) = 2^(3−κ−q)·c*_(m−2κ−2q+6).

Proof. rad(d) = q, so in (Q2) θ = 1 and the class sums σ_t are integers, each of the parity of the size of its class. Suppose some signing is bad at d. In a cell that is not q-full every σ_t is 0, so its classes have even sizes. In a q-full cell the σ_t are equal, so its classes have sizes of one parity; call the cell odd or even. An odd full cell has an odd number of elements (q is odd), every other cell an even number, and m is even: the number of odd full cells is even. (a) Two or more odd full cells. Two cells are covered by q − 1 pieces each (Lemma A), every other cell by one, and there are at least 1 + s(d) cells: K ≥ 2(q − 1) + s(d) − 1 = κ + q − 2, and P(bad at d) ≤ h_m(κ + q − 2) = E2 (Lemma P). (b) No odd full cell. Then every class of every cell has even size. In C* let r_0 be the size of a largest class and merge the two smallest classes (Lemma A allows any order; for q = 3 these are the other two). The merged piece has at least 4 and at most 2·r_0 elements. All K ≥ κ pieces have even sizes 2a_i with Σ a_i = m/2 =: A; write a_0 = r_0/2 and a_μ ≥ 2 for the merged piece, a_μ ≤ 2a_0. As in Lemma P, g(a_0)·∏ g(a_i) over the K − 2 pieces other than the merged one is at most g(1)^(K−2)·g(b) with b = A − a_μ − (K − 2) = a_0 + Σ (a_i − 1) ≥ a_0. If b ≥ 2: g(b)·g(a_μ) ≤ g(2)·g(b + a_μ − 2) (lower the smaller of b, a_μ to 2 and raise the other, by g(x)g(y) ≤ g(x − 1)g(y + 1) for x ≤ y). If b = 1: then a_0 = 1, so a_μ = 2 and the same holds with equality. Now b + a_μ − 2 = A − K ≥ 1, so P(bad at d) ≤ g(2)·g(1)^(K−2)·g(A − K) = (3/8)·2^(2−K)·c_(m−2K), and m ≥ 2K + 2. For m ≥ 2K + 2 this expression does not increase with K (c_(r−2) ≤ 2c_r), and K ≥ κ; so P(bad at d) ≤ (3/8)·2^(2−κ)·c_(m−2κ) = E1, and case (b) needs m ≥ 2κ + 2. ∎

*Ψ_e(m) := c_m + Σ over the prime powers e = q^j with need(e) ≤ m of max(E1, E2) + Σ over the other e with need(e) ≤ m of 2^(−κ(e))·c_(m − 2κ(e)) (m even).

Theorem 1′. For even m, β(m) ≤ Ψ_e(m) ≤ Ψ(m).

Proof. As Theorem 1, with Lemma E for the fully live d with one prime. Ψ_e ≤ Ψ term by term: E2 ≤ h_m(κ) by the monotony in Lemma P, and E1 ≤ 2^(1−κ)·c_(m−2κ+2) because c_(r−2) ≤ (4/3)·c_r for r ≥ 4 (c_(r−2)/c_r = 2j/(2j − 1), j = ⌈r/2⌉ ≥ 2). ∎

Constants (const.out, the lines "Theorem 1′"). Ψ_e(m) for m = 2, 4, …, 12: 1/2, 1/2, 69/128, 1041/2048, 1065/2048, 245761/524288. Exactly for m ≤ 161: the largest √m·Ψ_e(m) is 1.69134 at m = 14 (√m·Ψ_e(m) = 1.644, 1.624, 1.691, 1.653, 1.644, 1.621, 1.610, 1.590 for m = 10, 12, …, 24, and smaller after that); with Theorem Q where it is smaller (even m ≤ 18) the largest is 1.62120 at m = 20. 4. Theorem H: one heavy element (lead 3)

Let D be a multiset of r elements of Z_n (n odd), x ∈ Z_n, Q the signed mask of D. V = V(ε) is the set of integers v with Q(ζ) + v·ζ^x = 0 for some ζ with ζ^n = 1. For B = D with h more copies of x, a signing is bad iff the signed sum of the copies lies in V, so P(bad) ≤ c_h·E|V|; the brief's K(D) is the expected number of v ∈ V of one parity.

Theorem H. E|V| ≤ 1 + Σ_e 2^(1−κ(e)) = 1 + 2W′ < 2.192 for every D, x, n. The value at d = 1 always exists and has the parity of r; so K_same(D) ≤ 1 + 2W′ and K_other(D) ≤ 2W′ < 1.192. Hence P(bad) ≤ 2.192·c_h for every multiset with an element of multiplicity h.

Proof. Translate so that x = 0. For each d there is at most one integer v_d with Φ_d | Q + v_d (Φ_d divides no nonzero constant). Let B′ = D with one copy of 0. (Q1), (Q2) hold for arbitrary integer weights in place of signs, and the proof of (Q3) uses only this: if no cell is p-full, some class of every cell is empty, its sum is 0, hence all class sums of all cells are 0 and the weighted mask vanishes at d/p. So if d > 1 is least with Φ_d | Q + v, then d is fully live for B′, and V ⊆ {v_1} ∪ {v_d : d fully live for B′, v_d exists}. For such a d let q, p, C* be as in Lemma B (for B′) and C_0 the cell of 0. "v_d exists" requires A_C = 0 for every cell C ≠ C_0, and A_(C_0) = 0 for some integer weight v of 0. If C* ≠ C_0: C* gives q − 1 pieces and each other cell except C_0 one piece: at least κ − 1 pieces without 0. If C* = C_0: seen through q, the q − 1 classes of C_0 that do not contain 0 must have equal sums: q − 2 pieces (the computation of Lemma A for q − 1 classes); the other cells give s(d) more: again κ − 1 pieces. Each piece costs at most 1/2. So P(v_d exists) ≤ 2^(1−κ(d)); sum over the fully live d as in Theorem 1. ∎

So the answer to lead 3's question is yes: E|V| is bounded by an absolute constant, for decorations of any size. The bound is not sharp: the brief's decoration on Z_105 (one element in each nonzero class modulo 3, 5, 7) has E|V| = 277/256 + 337/512 = 1.740, the largest I know. I did not redo Lemma C with the free weight; it would lower 2.192 somewhat.

Theorem H is not used in Theorem 1.

A lower bound for sup K_same. Chain of pairs: two copies each of 3^(i−1) and 2·3^(i−1), i = 1, …, a, on Z_(3^a) beside the heavy 0. The value at 3^i exists and is new iff the pairs of the levels below i have sum 0 and the two pairs of level i have equal nonzero sums: K_same = 1 + (1/8)(1 + 1/4 + … + 4^(1−a)). Computed: 9/8, 37/32, 149/128, 597/512 for a = 1, 2, 3, 4 (k-structured.out); the formula tends to 7/6. With pairs in the nonzero classes modulo 5 as in the brief: 19221/16384 = 1.17316 on Z_135 (computed), so β(m)√m ≥ about 0.936 along large even m. All far below √(π/2) = 1.2533.

6. What is missing, and what I tried

Missing for part (b): β(m) ≤ 1/√m for even m ≥ 10. In the terms of this text: the union bound over the e is too generous for even m, where the events at different e overlap or are killed by parity. In the limit along even m the sum of Theorem 1′ has 1 (d = 1) + 0.375 (e = 3) + 0.1875 (e = 5, 9) + 0.0625 (e = 15) + …, in all 1.8045, and the truth seems to be about 1.17. The statement that would give part (b) for large even m:

For even m, P(bad at some fully live d, and not bad at 1) ≤ (√(π/2) − 1 − o(1))·c_m = (0.2533 − o(1))·c_m.

(In the searches of §5 the excess of the best share over c_m, divided by c_m, was 0.172, 0.179, 0.169, 0.175, 0.160 at m = 16, 20, 24, 30, 40, where 1/√m allows 0.273, 0.269, 0.266, 0.264, 0.261; examples.out.)

What I see but did not prove (so not claimed): Lemma E uses parity for "bad at d" only. For even m most of what is left overlaps "bad at 1": when all classes are even, "bad at d and not bad at 1" forces some full cell with a nonzero common sum. For e = 3 and a single cell with even classes this gives c_(r_0)·(c_(r_1+r_2) − c_(r_1)c_(r_2)) in place of c_(r_0)·c_(r_1+r_2), and c_(a+b) − c_a c_b is at most 17/128 in the few cases I computed by hand (a, b ≤ 8; largest at a = b = 4); my tests (T5) never met more than 0.281·c_(m−2)/2 for "bad at the first live level of 3 and not at 1" with m even. Doing this for every prime power would lower the even limit from 1.44 to something between 1.1 and 1.3 by my estimate, still above 1; to reach 1 the overlaps between different e must be handled too, as the "distinct values" do in the one shape with a heavy element. I stopped there.

The limits of β(m)√m: between 0.936 and 1.440 along even m, between 0.525 and 0.809 along odd m.

Thin margins. Corollary 3 holds at m = 13 and m = 15 with 0.6 and 1 per cent to spare, so there it depends on every term of Ψ, on the convention c* and on need(e). A referee should recompute Ψ(13) and Ψ(15) by hand from the list of e (for m = 13: e = 3, 9, 27; 5, 15, 45, 25, 75, 225, 675, 135; 7, 21, 35, 105, 63, 315; 11 and its 7 multiples by 3, 5, 7; 13 alone; total 1129/4096). Theorem Q gives β(m) ≤ 1/4 < 1/√m for odd 9 ≤ m ≤ 15 independently.

Roads not taken or failed. (i) A heavy element first, then the rest (lead 3 as given): once Lemma P was there the split was unnecessary, and the union bound for E|V| is no better than the general one. (ii) Lemma C for three primes or with ranks (more pieces for composite e): possible, small gain. (iii) A bound on the set of e that one multiset of m elements can make fully live, to shorten the sum: beyond need(e) ≤ m I found none (the sum over the primes of (p − 1)·(number of live levels) is not bounded by m − 1: five elements can make 3 and 5 live at once).
