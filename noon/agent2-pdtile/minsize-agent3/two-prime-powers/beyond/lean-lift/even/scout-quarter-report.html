Report (the reader)

Report: is the quantitative statement (at most 3/8 bad on odd n; at least 1/4 good on every n) in print?

Judgement: NOT FOUND in what was searched; that is not proof of absence. Nothing found contradicts it. Four pieces are in print or follow from print and should not be claimed as new:

  • One root at a time. For a single root of unity the bound 3/8 (m ≥ 3) is the classical Erdős–Littlewood–Offord bound. The statement's content is the union over all n-th roots.
  • Full support, odd n. The bound follows from the 2009 random-circulants paper, by a sum I worked out (0.3492 < 3/8).
  • Three and four elements, odd n. Both follow from the classical description of vanishing sums of three and four unit complex numbers. For four elements the bad share is exactly 3/8 for every multiset.
  • Full support, n ≤ 51. The listed OEIS counts lie within the bounds.

I did not find, in any vocabulary, a bound for the event "vanishes at some n-th root of unity" that is uniform in n and in a prescribed multiset. I also did not find the constants 3/8, 3/4, 1/4 or 1/2 attached to that event, or the exact value on moduli 3^a.

3.1 The Erdős–Littlewood–Offord bound — for ONE root of unity the bound 3/8 is KNOWN; for "some root" it does not follow

He, Juškevičius, Narayanan, Spiro, "On the reverse Littlewood–Offord problem of Erdős", https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11034 (introduction, on the classical results): - "Littlewood and Offord [8] originally considered the special case of this problem when each v_i is a complex number of norm at least one and showed that the probability that σ_V lies within any open ball of radius one is at most O(n^(−1/2) log n). This result was sharpened in seminal work of Erdős [3] who used Sperner's theorem to prove that this probability is at most C(n, ⌊n/2⌋) 2^(−n), which is sharp when v_i = 1 for all 1 ≤ i ≤ n." (binomial reassembled from the layout) - "The first of these asked for an extension of his upper bound of C(n, ⌊n/2⌋) 2^(−n) for complex numbers of norm at least 1 to vectors of norm at least 1 in arbitrary Hilbert spaces; this was eventually resolved in full by Kleitman [7]". - I did not read Erdős 1945 or Kleitman. My memory, unchecked, is that Erdős proved the real case and Kleitman the complex one.

Juškevičius and Šemetulskis, "Optimal Littlewood-Offord inequalities in groups", https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01085: - "Proposition 4. Under the notation of Theorem 3 and assuming that G is torsion-free for any set A ⊂ G of cardinality k we have P(X_1 ∗ ⋯ ∗ X_n ∈ A) ≤ P(ε_1 + ⋯ + ε_n ∈ (−k, k]), where ε_i are independent. In particular, for any g ∈ G we have P(X_1 ∗ ⋯ ∗ X_n = g) ≤ C(n, ⌊n/2⌋)/2^n." (reassembled)

How it relates: - KNOWN for one k. Fix k. The numbers u_i = ζ^(k·b_i) have modulus 1, and the additive group of Z[ζ] is torsion-free. So P(Σ ε_i u_i = 0) ≤ C(m, ⌊m/2⌋)/2^m, which is 3/8 for m = 3 and 4 and smaller for m ≥ 5. Repeats are allowed, and no parity of n is needed. - The union does not follow. The statement bounds the union over all k. By the brief's own table, the largest bad shares at m = 6 and m = 8 are 11/32 and 39/128, above the one-root bounds 5/16 and 35/128. So no bound for a single linear event gives the statement. - Same type, different event. The group theorems (Theorem 1, Theorem 3, Proposition 4 of the second paper) bound one signed sum hitting a finite set. The bad set here, inside Z[C_n], is an infinite union of sublattices.

3.2 Meckes, "Some results on random circulant matrices" (2009) — the odd-n bound FOLLOWS for full support only

Address: https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.2472.

  • The proof: "Since Φ_m has degree ϕ(m), P_{n,m} is an (n − ϕ(m))-dimensional subspace of the (rational) vector space of polynomials of degree at most n − 1. Therefore some set of n − ϕ(m) coefficients of a polynomial g suffice to determine whether g ∈ P_{n,m}, and so P[f ∈ P_{n,m}] ≤ 2^(−ϕ(m))".

How it relates: - FOLLOWS for full support. Take B to be every residue once, m = n odd. Then P(bad) ≤ Σ_{m|n, m>1} 2^(−ϕ(m)) ≤ Σ over all odd m ≥ 3 of 2^(−ϕ(m)). 3.3 Three and four elements on odd moduli — FOLLOW from the classical short vanishing sums

The four-term fact in print. Finch and Jones, "On the irreducibility of {−1, 0, 1}-quadrinomials", Integers 6 (2006) #A16, http://math.colgate.edu/~integers/g16/g16.pdf: - "Lemma 1. [Dubickas] Let z_1, z_2 and z_3 be complex numbers which lie on the unit circle, and suppose that z_1 + z_2 + z_3 + 1 = 0. Then z_j = −1 for some j."

So four unit complex numbers sum to zero only as two pairs of opposite numbers.

Four elements, n odd: exactly 3/8 (my derivation; not found written anywhere). - If Σ ε_i ζ^(b_i) = 0 with four terms, the terms pair off: ε_i ζ^(b_i) = −ε_j ζ^(b_j). - Then ζ^(b_i − b_j) = −ε_i ε_j is ±1. As ζ has odd order it is 1, so ε_i = −ε_j in each pair. - So every bad signing has sign-sum zero. Conversely every signing with sign-sum zero vanishes at 1. - The bad signings are exactly the 6 of 16 with sign-sum zero, for every four-element multiset and every odd n.

Three elements, n odd: at most 1/4 (same kind of argument). - The three-term source is Table 1 of Poonen–Rubinstein, quoted in the afternoon report: three roots of unity sum to zero only as a rotation of 1, ω, ω². - A ratio ε_i ε_j ζ^(b_i − b_j) equal to a primitive cube root forces ε_i ε_j = 1 when n is odd. - So only +++ and −−− can be bad.

Numerical check of both derivations: every multiset with first element 0, every odd n ≤ 21, floating point with tolerance 1e-9 (946 multisets for m = 3, 5786 for m = 4). All agree.

What print gives beyond that. - The same paper's Theorem 2 decides, sign pattern by sign pattern, which x^a + βx^b + γx^c + δ (a > b > c > 0) vanish at a root of unity: "f(1) = 0 for any other choice of (β, γ, δ)" than its five listed cases, each with parity conditions on the exponents. The m = 4 case of "at least a quarter good" for four distinct exponents could be checked from it by cases. I did not do that. - Fixed larger m could be worked by hand from the tables of minimal vanishing sums: weight up to 12 in Poonen–Rubinstein, up to 21 in Christie, Dykema, Klep, https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11268. That does not give all m.

Asymptotic in the degree. Coefficients that are zero for certain are excluded, so the folded model of the statement is not covered.
  • Bary-Soroker and Kadets, "Generic Manin–Mumford", https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09354: "The probability that f_n is divisible by a factor of Φ is not summable. For example, Prob(X − 1 | f_n) ≍ n^(−1/2)." Full support.
  • Grebennikov and Kwan, "Geometric Littlewood–Offord problems via lattice point counting", https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24699 (abstract only): bounds on P(Σ ξ_i a_i ∈ S) for S "a set of points in convex position, an algebraic variety, or a semialgebraic set". I did not check whether any of its theorems applies to the union of hyperplanes that is the bad set here.
  • Liang, Shi, Yu, Yu, https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05938 (abstract): random regular digraph with independent signs, not circulant.
  • Suvagiya, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17343 (abstract): spectral radius of signings.
  • Chatterjee and Murty on a conjecture of Erdős (search 9), by the search tool's account: true "for 82% of the remaining integers q ≡ 1 (mod 4)". That is a proportion of moduli, not of sign choices. Not opened.
  • MathOverflow 315925, "Lower bound for some sums of roots of unity", https://mathoverflow.net/questions/315925: size of nonzero sums Σ x_k ρ^k, full support, n prime; no answers.
  • MathOverflow 219337, "A case of nested central limits", https://mathoverflow.net/questions/219337: the norm of a random sign cyclotomic integer.
  • A general tool, not from a paper I opened. For search 24 the tool cited arXiv 2611.11966 for "Pr_x[p(x) ≠ 0] ≥ 2^{-d}" for a nonzero multilinear polynomial of degree d. Applied to a circulant determinant this would give a share that decays with the degree, and only where a good signing is known to exist. It does not give a constant.

4. Overall judgement

Theorem Q and the quarter: NOT FOUND.
