The statement

B a finite multiset of m residues modulo n. A signing gives each element a sign ±1; the signed mask is S(X) = Σ_(b ∈ B) ± X^b. Call a signing bad if S vanishes at some n-th root of unity (S is divisible by a cyclotomic polynomial Φ_d, d | n, d = 1 included), and good otherwise.

  • Theorem Q (odd n). If n is odd and m ≥ 3, at most 3/8 of the 2^m signings are bad. (Largest shares for m = 3, …, 8: 1/4, 3/8, 1/4, 11/32, 7/32, 39/128; 3/8 only for m = 4.)
  • With an inequality of the same evening (the number of signings bad on Z_n is at most twice the number bad for the reduction modulo the odd part of n): for every n and every multiset of m ≥ 3 elements, at least a quarter of the signings are good; at least a half when m is odd. The least shares of good signings are 1/2, 1/4, 1/2, 5/16, 9/16, 25/64 for m = 3, …, 8.
  • On moduli 3^a the largest share of bad signings of an odd number m of elements is exactly C(m − 2, (m − 1)/2)/2^(m−1) (half the largest binomial probability for m − 2 signs).

Other clothes for the same thing: - Random polynomials with prescribed exponents. For a polynomial Σ ε_i X^(b_i) with independent uniform signs ε_i and any exponents b_i (repeats allowed), m ≥ 3 terms: the probability that it has a common root with X^n − 1 is at most 3/4, for every n; at most 3/8 if n is odd; at most 1/2 if m is odd. - Vanishing sums of roots of unity with random signs. For any m ≥ 3 roots of unity u_1, …, u_m of odd order: P(Σ ε_i u_i^k = 0 for some k … ) — more plainly, for a fixed multiset of n-th roots of unity ζ^(b_i), the probability that Σ ε_i ζ^(k·b_i) = 0 for SOME k is at most 3/8 (n odd), 3/4 (any n). - Circulants and group rings. A ±1-signed circulant with a prescribed pattern of m nonzero entries per row (repeats allowed: entries are sums of signs) is nonsingular for at least a quarter of the sign choices when m ≥ 3; a random signed element Σ ε_i g^(b_i) of Z[C_n] is a non-zero-divisor of Q[C_n] with probability at least 1/4. - A uniform (in n and in the multiset) anti-concentration statement of Littlewood–Offord type for the event "vanishes at some root of unity".
