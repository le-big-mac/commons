What I checked by hand

Theorem T′, step by step. - (1) A class at depth d − 1 is the disjoint union of its halves, each a class at depth d or empty, so σ(c) = σ(h) + σ(h′). Right, with empty halves. - (2) τ_j acts on each element by its position, so it is well defined when elements share a position. It is an involution, hence a bijection that keeps the uniform probability. A class at depth j − 1 is a residue r < 2^(j−1); its halves have remainders r (signs kept) and r + 2^(j−1) (signs negated). So σ of c under τ_j(ε) equals σ(h) − σ(h′) under ε, also when either half is empty. "ε bad at level j iff τ_j(ε) ∈ F_(j−1)" holds for every class. - (3) Right. - (4) P(A and not Z) = P(A) − P(A and Z), with P(A) = P(F_(j−1)) by (2) and P(A and Z) ≥ P(F_j) by (3). The union bound is over "level 0 bad" and the k events "level j bad, level 0 not". The sum telescopes. - k = 0: the sum is empty and the bound reads Q = z. Empty family: 1 ≤ 1. - V and the values: only that V is an abelian group is used. Of the signs, only invariance under each τ_j is used. - The remark: F_d ⊆ "levels 0..d all bad" by (1) and (3). The converse goes by induction on depth: 2σ(h) = 0 gives σ(h) = 0. It is false with 2-torsion (Z/2, positions 0 and 1, values 1 and 1).
