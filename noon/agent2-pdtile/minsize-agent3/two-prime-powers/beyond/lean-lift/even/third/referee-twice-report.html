Suggestions

S1. A five-line proof of Theorem T, with no hypotheses and a sharper bound. It could replace §2.

Theorem T′. V any abelian group; values may be 0. For 0 ≤ d ≤ k let F_d be the event that every class at depth d has σ = 0 (F_0 is "level 0 is bad"). Then P(level j is bad) = P(F_(j−1)) for 1 ≤ j ≤ k, and Q(B) ≤ 2·z(B) − P(F_k).

Proof. F_0 ⊇ F_1 ⊇ … ⊇ F_k, since a class at depth d − 1 is the disjoint union of its halves, which are classes at depth d or empty. Fix j ≥ 1 and let τ_j negate the sign of every element whose position has remainder ≥ 2^(j−1) modulo 2^j; it is a bijection of the signings. A class c at depth j − 1 has one half h whose signs τ_j keeps and one half h′ whose signs it negates, so σ(c) after τ_j is σ(h) − σ(h′) before. So a signing is bad at level j iff its image lies in F_(j−1), and P(level j bad) = P(F_(j−1)). A signing in F_j has both halves of every class at depth j − 1 equal to 0, so level j is bad and level 0 is bad. Hence P(level j bad, level 0 not bad) ≤ P(F_(j−1)) − P(F_j). Summing over j: Q(B) ≤ z(B) + P(F_0) − P(F_k). ∎

  • Without 2-torsion, F_d is "levels 0, …, d are all bad". So the number of signings bad at 2^j·e equals the number bad at 2^i·e for every i < j.
  • The proof uses only that the position sums are independent and symmetric.
  • τ_1 is Theorem G's twist, so the credit sentence of M1 fits this proof even better.
  • I checked the three facts as sets on 4,900 random families (short_proof_check.py) and as counts on every family of the runs below. Please have it read by someone other than me before it replaces your proof.
