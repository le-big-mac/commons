0. Summary

n = 2^k·n′, n′ odd. B a nonempty multiset of m elements of Z_n. Exceptional pair: m = 2, B = {a, b}, 2^k ∤ a − b. Conjecture E: B has a good signing iff B is not an exceptional pair. (The "only if" is in the brief.)

Proved here, completely:

  • Theorem A. Conjecture E holds for n = 2^k (every k, every multiset). The proof is a construction by the recursion of lead 4; one page.
  • Theorem B. Conjecture E holds for every n when m ≤ 4. For m = 4 a signing is good on Z_n iff it is good for the residues modulo 2^k on Z_{2^k}.
  • Theorem D. (i) If 3 ∤ n, Conjecture E holds (every k, every multiset); the share of good signings is more than 0.76 for m odd and more than 0.011 for m even, m ≥ 4. (ii) If 5 ∤ n and m is odd, B has a good signing; the share is more than 0.13 (at least 1/3 if n = 2^k·3^a). (iii) If n = 2^k·3^a·5^b and m is odd, B has a good signing (share more than 0.014).
  • Five elements, every n (pointed out by the referee). With five elements no prime ≥ 7 has a live level, and the count of Theorem D (iii) applies as it stands; the same count covers odd m for some further sets of primes (end of §5).
  • The tools: Lemma N (for a fixed odd e, a signing that is bad at 2^j·e for some j must satisfy one of at most two affine conditions modulo 2, the same for all j but one) and Proposition P (for every n: the share of signings bad somewhere is at most P_1 + Σ over the live odd e > 1 of 2^(1−D_e), where P_1, the share bad at some 2^j, is 0 for odd m and at most 3/4 for even m ≥ 4).

Not proved here: n even, 3 | n, m even, m ≥ 6; and n even, 15 | n, m odd, m ≥ 7, with 7 | n or 11 | n, or 13 | n together with 17 | n or 19 | n. (For odd n the conjecture is Theorem 4, which this text does not prove again when 3 | n. For m = 5 the proof of Theorem D (iii) applies as it stands. Within the two families, by sharpening (α), Theorem D (i) still applies when the 3-tree of B has no live level, and the count of Theorem D (iii) when m is odd and the odd primes with a live level are 3, 5 and primes ≥ 17, or 3, 5, 13 and primes ≥ 23.) No counterexample in any test. §6 says where the count stops and states the two things that are missing: for odd m a purely combinatorial inequality about ranks modulo 2 (first order suffices in every search); for even m a second-order form of Lemma N.

1. Setting

x ∈ Z_n is written (u_x, w_x) = (x mod 2^k, x mod n′). A root of unity ζ of order 2^j·e (j ≤ k, e | n′) is ξ·η in exactly one way with ξ of order 2^j and η of order e, and ζ^x = ξ^{u_x}·η^{w_x}. A signing ε has the mask S_ε(X) = Σ_x ε_x X^x; it is bad at d if Φ_d | S_ε, i.e. S_ε(ζ) = 0 for the ζ of order d; good if bad at no d | n. N = N(ε) is the set of elements with sign −. "Share" and "probability" refer to the 2^m signings, uniform. c_r = C(r, ⌊r/2⌋)/2^r is the largest probability with which a sum of r independent signs takes one value (c_1 = c_2 = 1/2, c_3 = c_4 = 3/8, non-increasing).

The 2-tree: a class at depth i is a residue class modulo 2^i (of u_x); its two children are at depth i + 1. For an odd prime p, the p-tree, groups, full groups are as in sign-four-result.md §1; level i of p is live if some group at level i is full (the Lean live).

Lemma S (lead 1; sets are enough). Let O be the set of elements of odd multiplicity in B. If O = ∅, 2X^x is a signed mask of B and is good. If O ≠ ∅ is not an exceptional pair, a good signing of the set O gives one of B. If O = {a, b} is an exceptional pair and B ≠ O: if a (or b) has multiplicity ≥ 3, 3X^a ± X^b is good; otherwise some c ∉ O has multiplicity ≥ 2, and with u = v_2(a − c) (u = k if 2^k | a − c), v = v_2(a − b) < k: X^a + X^b + 2X^c is good if u ≥ v and X^a − X^b − 2X^c is good if u < v. Proof. As in the brief. For the last case: X^a + X^b + 2X^c vanishes at ζ = ξη only if ζ^a = ζ^b = −ζ^c, so ξ^{a−b} = 1 and ξ^{a−c} = −1 (the odd parts must be 1), i.e. j ≤ v and j = u + 1: impossible if u ≥ v. X^a − X^b − 2X^c vanishes only if ζ^a = ζ^c = −ζ^b, i.e. j ≤ u and j = v + 1: impossible if u < v. ∎ (Tested: tests1.py sets.)

2. Theorem A: n = 2^k

Lemma R (lead 4). Let n be even, N = n/2, and S(X) = S_0(X²) + X·S_1(X²), so that S_0, S_1 are the signed masks of E = {x/2 : x ∈ B even} and D = {(x − 1)/2 : x ∈ B odd} on Z_N. Then S is good on Z_n iff (a) for every odd e | n and η of order e, with θ = η²: S_0(θ) ≠ η·S_1(θ) and S_0(θ) ≠ −η·S_1(θ); and (b) for no even d | N both S_0 and S_1 are divisible by Φ_d. More precisely S is bad at e iff S_0(θ) = −η S_1(θ); at 2e iff S_0(θ) = η S_1(θ); at 2d (d | N even) iff S_0 and S_1 are both bad at d. Proof. S(±η) = S_0(θ) ± η S_1(θ), and −η has order 2e. If ζ has order 2d with d even, θ = ζ² has order d and [Q(ζ) : Q(θ)] = φ(2d)/φ(d) = 2, so 1, ζ are independent over Q(θ) and S(ζ) = S_0(θ) + ζ S_1(θ) vanishes iff both do. ∎ (Tested with the bad levels compared one by one: tests1.py recursion.)

For n = 2^k condition (a) reads σ_E ≠ ±σ_D, where σ_E = S_0(1), σ_D = S_1(1) are the sums of the signs.

Theorem A. Let n = 2^k. A nonempty multiset B on Z_n has a good signing iff it is not {a, b} with a ≠ b.

Proof. Induction on k. k = 0: all signs + (the mask is m ≠ 0). Let k ≥ 1, B not an exceptional pair, E and D as in Lemma R, on Z_N, N = 2^(k−1).

One half empty, say D: S(X) = S_0(X²) is good on Z_n iff S_0 is good on Z_N (Lemma R with S_1 = 0), and E is an exceptional pair on Z_N iff B is one on Z_n. Induction.

Both halves nonempty, both exceptional pairs on Z_N: E = {x, y}, D = {z, w}, v_E = v_2(x − y), v_D = v_2(z − w), both < k − 1. At ξ of order 2^j: X^x − X^y vanishes iff j ≤ v_E, and X^z + X^w vanishes iff j = v_D + 1 (likewise with the roles exchanged). If v_D ≥ v_E take S_0 = X^x − X^y, S_1 = X^z + X^w: σ_E = 0 ≠ ±2 = ±σ_D, and a common bad level j ≥ 1 would need v_D + 1 ≤ v_E. If v_D < v_E take S_0 = X^x + X^y, S_1 = X^z − X^w: a common bad level would need v_E + 1 ≤ v_D.

Both nonempty, not both exceptional pairs. Let A be one of E, D that is not an exceptional pair and C the other. (a) and (b) are symmetric in the two halves, and (b) holds as soon as the mask of A is good on Z_N. - |C| ≥ 2: sign A well by induction; σ_A ≠ 0. Sign C with all signs + (sum |C|) or with exactly one − (sum |C| − 2 ≥ 0); these two sums have different absolute values, so one of them differs from |σ_A|. - |C| = 1 and |A| even: sign A well by induction; σ_A is even, σ_C = ±1. - |C| = 1 and |A| odd: |A| = 1 would make B an exceptional pair, so |A| ≥ 3. Give A all signs +. This mask is good on Z_N: at ξ of 2-power order it is ≡ |A| modulo (1 − ξ), odd, and (1 − ξ) is a proper ideal containing 2 (at ξ = 1 the value is |A|). And σ_A = |A| ≥ 3 ≠ ±1. ∎

The proof is a program (pow2.py); its output was checked exactly on every nonempty subset of Z_2, …, Z_16 and more (§7).

3. Theorem B: at most four elements, every n

Theorem B. For every n, a multiset B with m ≤ 4 elements has a good signing iff it is not an exceptional pair. Moreover for m = 4 a signing is good on Z_n iff the same signs are good for the residues modulo 2^k on Z_{2^k}; and for three elements (equal ones allowed) at least two of the four signings (first sign +) are good.

Proof. Two elementary facts: (T3) three complex numbers of modulus 1 with sum 0 are z, zω, zω², ω = e^{2πi/3} (from |a + b| = 1: a/b + b/a = −1); (T4) four complex numbers of modulus 1 with sum 0 are two pairs {z, −z} (if a + b ≠ 0, then a + b = −(c + d) fixes the bisector and the half-angle, so {c, d} = {−a, −b}). m = 1: clear. m = 2, not exceptional: X^a + X^b vanishes only if ζ^{a−b} = −1, but a − b has odd order. m = 3: Let B = {a, b, c} (equal elements allowed), δ_b = ε_aε_b, δ_c = ε_aε_c. By (T3) the mask vanishes at ζ = ξη iff (δ_b ζ^{b−a}, δ_c ζ^{c−a}) = (ω^{±1}, ω^{∓1}); comparing the parts of 2-power order and of odd order: (ξ^{b−a}, ξ^{c−a}) = (δ_b, δ_c). The pairs (ξ^{b−a}, ξ^{c−a}), ξ ∈ μ_{2^k}, form a cyclic group, which meets {±1}² in at most two elements. So at most two of the four (δ_b, δ_c) are bad. m = 4: by (T4) the mask vanishes at ζ = ξη iff the four terms split into two pairs with ε_xζ^x = −ε_yζ^y, i.e. ζ^{x−y} = −ε_xε_y; then η^{x−y} = 1 and ξ^{x−y} = −ε_xε_y, so the same pairs cancel at ξ. Hence the signing is bad on Z_n iff its mask vanishes at some ξ ∈ μ_{2^k}, iff it is bad for B mod 2^k on Z_{2^k}; and a multiset of four elements on Z_{2^k} has a good signing by Theorem A. ∎

4. Lemma N: for a fixed odd e, the levels 2^j·e are nested modulo 2

Fix e | n′, η of order e, and a prime 𝔭 of Z[η] above 2. Since e is odd, 2 is unramified in Q(η): there is a discrete valuation v of Q(η) with v(2) = 1, Z[η] ⊂ its ring, residue field κ = Z[η]/𝔭, and for y ∈ Z[η]: y ∈ 𝔭 iff v(y) ≥ 1. Put

β_i = Σ_{x ∈ B} C(u_x, i)·η^{w_x} ∈ Z[η] (0 ≤ i < 2^k), so that Σ_x η^{w_x}(1 + T)^{u_x} = Σ_i β_i T^i; a = the least i with β_i ∉ 𝔭 (a = ∞ if there is none); λ(N) = Σ_{x ∈ N} η^{w_x} mod 𝔭 ∈ κ, for a set N of elements (an F_2-linear function of the indicator of N); c = (β_0/2) mod 𝔭 (defined when a ≥ 1), c′ = c + (β_a mod 𝔭) (when 1 ≤ a < ∞; then c′ ≠ c).

Lemma N. Let ε be a signing, N its set of minus signs, 0 ≤ j ≤ k, and suppose ε is bad at 2^j·e. Then (i) a ≥ 1 if j = 0, and a ≥ 2^(j−1) if j ≥ 1; (ii) λ(N) = c′ if j ≥ 1 and a = 2^(j−1); λ(N) = c in every other case.

Proof. j = 0: 0 = S_ε(η) = β_0 − 2·Σ_{x∈N} η^{w_x}, so β_0 ∈ 2Z[η] ⊂ 𝔭 and λ(N) = c. Let j ≥ 1, ξ of order 2^j, π = ξ − 1. Φ_{2^j}(1 + T) = (1 + T)^{2^(j−1)} + 1 = T^{2^(j−1)} + 2·(…) + 2 is an Eisenstein polynomial for v, so v extends in one way to Q(η, ξ), totally ramified, with the same residue field κ; normalise v_π = 2^(j−1)·v, so v_π(π) = 1, v_π(2) = 2^(j−1); and π^{2^(j−1)}/2 ≡ −1 ≡ 1 modulo π (from the same polynomial). Now S_ε(ξη) = b − 2·n with b = Σ_i β_i π^i and n = Σ_{x∈N} ξ^{u_x} η^{w_x} ≡ Σ_{x∈N} η^{w_x} modulo π. We have v_π(β_i π^i) = i + 2^(j−1)·v(β_i): it is a for i = a, at least i + 2^(j−1) for i < a, at least i for i > a. If a < 2^(j−1), the term i = a has valuation a and every other term a larger one, so v_π(b) = a < v_π(2n): then S_ε(ξη) ≠ 0. This proves (i). If a ≥ 2^(j−1) and b = 2n: modulo π, n ≡ b/2 = Σ_i β_i π^i/2, in which the terms 0 < i < a vanish (β_i/2 is integral), the term i = 0 is c, the term i = a has valuation a − 2^(j−1) and is (β_a mod 𝔭)·1 if a = 2^(j−1) and 0 otherwise, and the terms i > a have valuation ≥ i − 2^(j−1) > 0. ∎

Remarks. 1. (i) says: if a < ∞, no signing whatever is bad at 2^j·e for j > 1 + log_2 a; a = 0 means Σ_x η^{w_x} ∉ 𝔭 ("e is not dangerous at 𝔭", lead 2). 2. a ≥ 2^d iff every class modulo 2^d has its unsigned sum Σ η^{w_x} in 𝔭 (modulo 𝔭, κ[T]/(T^{2^d}) is the group algebra of Z_{2^d} and the classes are a basis). So (i) is the tree form of lead 2. For e = 1: a ≥ 2^d iff every class modulo 2^d has an even number of elements. 3. At most one special level for e. Let a_𝔭 be the number a for the prime 𝔭 and a_min its least value over the primes above 2. By (i) a signing can be bad at 2^j·e only for j = 0 (if a_min ≥ 1) or 2^(j−1) ≤ a_min. For such j, "a_𝔭 = 2^(j−1)" forces a_𝔭 = a_min = 2^(j−1). So with j* = 1 + log_2 a_min (if a_min is a power of 2): for every j ≠ j, bad at 2^j·e implies λ_𝔭(N) = c_𝔭 for all 𝔭; and bad at 2^{j}·e implies λ_𝔭(N) = c′_𝔭 for the 𝔭 with a_𝔭 = a_min and c_𝔭 for the others. Since Z[η]/2 = ∏_𝔭 Z[η]/𝔭:

Corollary N. Let λ_e(N) = Σ_{x∈N} η^{w_x} mod 2 ∈ Z[η]/2Z[η], V_e its image (the F_2-span of the η^{w_x} mod 2), D_e = dim V_e. If a_min = 0 no signing is bad at any 2^j·e. Otherwise there are two elements γ, γ′ of Z[η]/2 (γ with the components c_𝔭; γ′ as in Remark 3) such that every signing that is bad at 2^j·e for some j ∈ {0, …, k} has λ_e(N) ∈ {γ, γ′}. Since λ_e is F_2-linear onto V_e, it takes a given value for at most the share 2^(−D_e) of the signings. Hence the share of the signings bad at 2^j·e for some j is at most 2·2^(−D_e) = 2^(1−D_e), whatever k is.

(The trivial union bound over j gives (k + 1)·2^(−D_e). The bound 2^(1−D_e) is attained: three elements, one in each class modulo 3, not all of the same parity, n = 6: half of the signings are bad at 3 or at 6.)

A second proof of Corollary N, without valuations (the referee's; added after its report). For a class r modulo 2^j write s_j(r) = Σ ε_x η^{w_x} and U_j(r) = Σ η^{w_x}, both over the elements with u_x ≡ r modulo 2^j (the signed and the unsigned sum); β_0 = U_0(0), and Λ = Σ_{x∈N} η^{w_x} ∈ Z[η], so that λ_e(N) = Λ mod 2. (a) Let j ≥ 1. If ε is bad at 2^j·e, then s_j(r) = s_j(r + 2^(j−1)) for every r < 2^(j−1). (This is S0 for p = 2: 1, ξ, …, ξ^(2^(j−1)−1) is a basis over Q(η) and ξ^(2^(j−1)) = −1.) (b) Modulo 2, s ≡ U. So by (a) U_j(r) ≡ U_j(r + 2^(j−1)), and every class modulo 2^(j−1) has its unsigned sum U_{j−1}(r) = U_j(r) + U_j(r + 2^(j−1)) in 2·Z[η] (this is lead 2). (c) Summing (a) over r: β_0 − 2Λ = Σ_x ε_x η^{w_x} = 2·Σ_{r<2^(j−1)} s_j(r). Hence β_0 ∈ 2·Z[η] and λ_e(N) = γ_j := β_0/2 + Σ_{r<2^(j−1)} U_j(r) mod 2. For j = 0, bad at e means β_0 = 2Λ, so λ_e(N) = γ_0 := β_0/2 mod 2. (d) If β_0 ∉ 2·Z[η], no signing is bad at any 2^j·e, by (c). Otherwise let J be the largest j ∈ {1, …, k} such that every class modulo 2^(j−1) has its unsigned sum in 2·Z[η] (J = 0 if k = 0). The property passes from j to j − 1, a class modulo 2^(j−2) being the union of two classes modulo 2^(j−1); so by (b) a signing is bad at 2^j·e only for j ≤ J. For 1 ≤ j < J every class modulo 2^j has its unsigned sum in 2·Z[η], so γ_j = γ_0. Hence every signing that is bad at 2^j·e for some j has λ_e(N) ∈ {γ_0, γ_J}, and the value γ_J, if it differs from γ_0, occurs at the level J only. ∎ (I checked this proof line by line and tested its statements with a program of my own, test_short.py, §7.)

Proposition P apart from its part (2), the sharpenings (α), (β), Lemma V, Lemma Q and Theorem D use only Corollary N and the fact that a signing bad at some 2^j·e forces Σ_x η^{w_x} ∈ 2·Z[η]; the second proof gives both, and with it Theorem D rests on S0 for p = 2, S1, linear algebra modulo 2, the counting in part (2) of Proposition P (translated below) and the sums W, and for m = 2 on Theorem B, without Lemma N. Part (2) of Proposition P is written with Lemma N for e = 1 (the number a and Remark 2). The second proof gives what (2) uses as well: for e = 1 and m even, no signing is bad at 2^j for j > J; those bad at 2^j with j < J have |N| ≡ γ_0; if γ_J = γ_0 the same holds at the level J and P_1 ≤ 1/2; if γ_J ≠ γ_0, the number of elements in the classes r < 2^(J−1) modulo 2^J is odd, so some class modulo 2^J is odd, while every class modulo 2^(J−1) is even: this is the case a = 2^d of (2), with d = J − 1, and (2) goes on as written. Lemma N says more than the second proof (the values at each prime 𝔭 above 2 separately, and the number a), and nothing else in the text needs that.

Lemma V (the rank). Let e > 1 be odd, p a prime with p^i ∥ e, and suppose level i of the p-tree is live and is the l-th live level of p (counted from level 1). Then D_e ≥ l·(p − 1). Proof. Write e = p^i·e″ and η_e = η_{e″}·η_{p^i} (primitive roots of orders e″ and p^i). Then Z[η_e] = Z[η_{e″}][Y]/(Φ_{p^i}(Y)), Y ↦ η_{p^i} (Φ_{p^i} is irreducible over Q(η_{e″}) because φ(e) = φ(e″)·φ(p^i), and both sides are free over Z of rank φ(e)), so Z[η_e]/2 is free over A = Z[η_{e″}]/2 with basis Y^r Z^t, 0 ≤ r < p^(i−1), 0 ≤ t ≤ p − 2, Z = Y^{p^(i−1)}, and Z^(p−1) = 1 + Z + … + Z^(p−2). The element x gives η_e^{w_x} = α_x·Y^r Z^t with α_x = η_{e″}^{w_x} a unit of A, w_x ≡ r + t·p^(i−1) modulo p^i: r is its class modulo p^(i−1) (its group at level i) and t its subclass. Different groups lie in different blocks. In a group with occupied subclasses L take one element from each subclass of L′, where L′ = L if |L| < p and L′ is L without one subclass if |L| = p (the set so chosen is the pivot set of S2 (b) of the plan): these are independent over F_2 (if p − 1 ∉ L′ they are in different coordinates; if p − 1 ∈ L′ some t_0 ≤ p − 2 is not in L′, and the coordinate t_0 isolates the element of subclass p − 1). So D_e ≥ Σ over the occupied groups at level i of min(|L|, p − 1) ≥ (p − 1) + (N_{i−1} − 1), N_{i−1} the number of occupied classes at depth i − 1, and N_{i−1} ≥ 1 + (l − 1)(p − 1) because every live level below i adds at least p − 1 occupied classes (sign-four-result.md, Lemma L, step (4)). ∎

5. Proposition P and Theorem D

Call an odd e > 1 live if for every prime power p^i ∥ e level i of the p-tree is live.

Proposition P (every n, every multiset). The share of signings that are bad somewhere is at most P_1 + Σ over the live e > 1, e | n′, of 2^(1−D_e), where P_1 is the share of signings bad at some 2^j, and (1) P_1 = 0 if m is odd; (2) P_1 ≤ 3/4 if m is even and m ≥ 4; (3) Σ over the live e > 1 of 2^(1−D_e) ≤ 2·W_Π, where Π is the set of odd primes dividing n and W_Π = Σ over the e* > 1 all of whose prime factors are in Π of 2^(−μ(e)), μ(e) = max_p v_p(e*)(p − 1).

Proof. Step-down. Let ε be bad at 2^j·e. If some p^i ∥ e has level i not live, ε is bad at 2^j·e/p (S1 with d = 2^j·e/p^i, p ∤ d). Repeating, ε is bad at 2^j·e′ with e′ = 1 or e′ live. So "bad somewhere" is the union over e′ = 1 and the live e′ of "bad at 2^j e′ for some j", and the live ones cost 2^(1−D_e′) each by Corollary N.

(1) At ξ of 2-power order S_ε(ξ) ≡ m modulo (1 − ξ) (and S_ε(1) ≡ m modulo 2).

(2) Lemma N for e = 1 (𝔭 = (2), κ = F_2, λ(N) = |N| mod 2, β_i = Σ_x C(u_x, i)); m even gives a ≥ 1. If a is not a power of 2 (or a = ∞), every signing bad at some 2^j has |N| ≡ c: P_1 ≤ 1/2. Let a = 2^d. Then every class modulo 2^d is even, some class modulo 2^(d+1) is odd (Remark 2), no signing is bad at 2^j for j > d + 1, the signings bad at 2^j with j ≠ d + 1 have |N| ≡ c (share 1/2), and those bad at 2^(d+1) have, in every class C modulo 2^d, equal sums on the two children (S0 for p = 2), which has probability ∏C q_C, q_C = P(a sum of |C| signs is 0) ≤ 1/2 for each occupied C. If at least two classes modulo 2^d are occupied: P_1 ≤ 1/2 + 1/4. If only one class C_0 is occupied, it holds all of B, its children have odd sizes r, s with r + s = m, so max(r, s) ≥ 3; for j ≤ d "bad at 2^j" forces σ(B) = 0 (the class holding B has one empty child), and "bad at 2^(d+1)" forces σ′ = σ″ for the sums on the two children; so P_1 ≤ P(σ′ = ±σ″) ≤ 2·c{max(r,s)} ≤ 2c_3 = 3/4.

(3) By Lemma V, D_e ≥ max_p (p − 1)·l_p(e), l_p(e) the number of live levels of p up to v_p(e). The map e ↦ e* = ∏ p^{l_p(e)} is injective on the live e (v_p(e) is the l_p-th live level of p) and μ(e*) ≤ D_e. ∎

The numbers (wsum_even.py; exact partial sums up to t = 40 by the product formula A_X(t) = ∏{p ≤ t+1, p ∉ X} (1 + ⌊t/(p − 1)⌋), checked against an enumeration for t ≤ 14, plus the tail bound 5.7·10^(−4) of Lemma W, which applies because the terms are sums over subsets of those of W): W(no 3) < 0.11914, W(no 5) < 0.43241, W({3}) = Σ{l≥1} 4^(−l) = 1/3; and W(all) = 0.8445, W(no 7) < 0.606.

Theorem D. (i) If 3 ∤ n, every nonempty multiset on Z_n that is not an exceptional pair has a good signing. (ii) If 5 ∤ n and m is odd, B has a good signing. (iii) If n = 2^k·3^a·5^b and m is odd, B has a good signing.

Proof of (i), (ii). (i) m = 2: Theorem B. m odd: the share of bad signings is at most 2·W(no 3) < 0.2383. m even, m ≥ 4: at most 3/4 + 2·W(no 3) < 0.9883 < 1. (ii) At most 2·W(no 5) < 0.8649 (and 2/3 if n = 2^k·3^a). ∎

For odd n with 3 ∤ n this is a second proof of Theorem 4 (in the arrangement of the plan of the Lean proof: no contraction of the multiset, only S1; what is different is that the count is modulo 2 instead of over Z). The margin in (i) for even m is 0.0117; it rests on the 3/4 of (2) and on W(no 3) < 1/8.

Two sharpenings that the proof gives as it stands. (α) In (3) only the primes with a live level matter: Π can be taken to be the set of odd primes p such that some level of the p-tree of B is live. So (i) holds for every B whose 3-tree has no live level (whatever n is), and (ii) for odd m whenever the 3-tree or the 5-tree of B has no live level. (β) By Corollary N a live e costs nothing unless it is dangerous (Σ_x η_e^{w_x} ∈ 2·Z[η_e]); the sum in Proposition P may be restricted to the live dangerous e.

Lemma Q (the dangerous levels of one prime; m odd). Let m be odd, p an odd prime, and let p^i be dangerous, the l-th dangerous one among p, p², …, p^i. Then level i of p is live and D_{p^i} ≥ (p − 1)·p^(l−1). Proof. Call a class of the p-tree odd if it holds an odd number of elements of B. In F_2[Y]/(Φ_{p^i}) the sum Σ_x Y^{w_x} is Σ over the groups r and subclasses t of (parity of the subclass)·Y^r Z^t, which is 0 iff in every group the p subclasses have the same parity (Z^(p−1) = 1 + Z + … + Z^(p−2)); as p is odd, that parity is the parity of the class at depth i − 1 that is the group. So p^i is dangerous iff every class at depth i − 1 has all its p children of its own parity. Let o_j be the number of odd classes at depth j; o_0 = 1 because m is odd. An odd class has an odd number of odd children, so o_j ≥ o_{j−1}; and if p^j is dangerous, o_j = p·o_{j−1}. Hence o_{i−1} ≥ p^(l−1), and each odd class at depth i − 1 is a full group at level i (its children are odd, so occupied). So level i is live and, by the count in the proof of Lemma V, D_{p^i} ≥ (p − 1)·o_{i−1}. ∎

Proof of Theorem D (iii). By the step-down and (β) the share of bad signings is at most the sum of 2^(1−D_e) over the live dangerous e = 3^i·5^{i′} > 1. The pure powers of p cost, by Lemma Q, at most δ_p = Σ_{l ≥ 1} 2^(1−(p−1)p^(l−1)): δ_3 = 2^(−1) + 2^(−5) + 2^(−17) + … < 0.53126, δ_5 = 2^(−3) + 2^(−19) + … < 0.12501. The mixed e (i, i′ ≥ 1) cost, as in (3), at most Σ_{a, b ≥ 1} 2^(1−max(2a, 4b)) = Σ_{b ≥ 1} (4b + 2/3)·16^(−b) = 74/225 < 0.32889 (for fixed b: the 2b values a ≤ 2b give 2^(1−4b) each, the others Σ_{a > 2b} 2^(1−2a) = (2/3)·16^(−b)). Together less than 0.98516. ∎

(The same count gives, for odd m: share of bad signings ≤ S(Π) for any set Π of odd primes containing every prime with a live level, where S(Π) = Σ_{p∈Π} δ_p + Σ over the e* with at least two primes, all in Π, of 2^(1−μ(e)). S({3, 5}) < 0.98516 covers every multiset of five elements on every Z_n: a full group of p needs p elements, so with five elements no prime ≥ 7 has a live level. S is above 1 for {3, 5, 7} (1.32) and {3, 5, 11} (1.03); it is below 0.99980 for {3, 5, 13} and below 0.98760 for {3, 5} with all primes ≥ 17. Further values: below 0.99989 for {3, 5, 13} with all primes ≥ 23; 1.0027 for {3, 5, 13, 17}; 1.0006 for {3, 5, 13, 19}. Whose numbers. The remark on five elements and this paragraph are the referee's: the paragraph is in its words, with "for odd m", the definition of S and the reason for five elements put in from elsewhere in its report, and the sentence "Further values" from its table. 0.98516, 1.32 and 1.03 were in my text (wsum_two.py). The other numbers are the referee's (its tW2.py); I checked every row of its table with a program of my own before adopting it (spi.py: the sum over e by the product formula with the tail bound of Lemma W, and for the finite sets again by listing the exponents; as first run, kept as spi.first-version.py, it stopped at t = 60 and printed its bounds rounded to nearest, which made two lower bounds false in the seventh decimal, as the referee found, and left the upper bounds 0.999797 and 0.999891 true but not proved by it; it now goes to t = 120 with exact fractions and rounds lower bounds down and upper bounds up), and found 0.999790 ≤ S({3, 5, 13}) ≤ 0.999797, S({3, 5} with all primes ≥ 17) ≤ 0.987599, S({3, 5, 13} with all primes ≥ 23) ≤ 0.999891, S({3, 5, 13, 17}) ≥ 1.002697 and S({3, 5, 13, 19}) ≥ 1.000636.)

6. What is missing, and the roads that failed

Where the count stops. With 3 | n and m even, the level e = 3 alone can cost 2^(1−2) = 1/2 and e = 1 can cost 3/4 (11/16 for m ≥ 6, see below). With 15 | n and m odd, the bound 2·W_Π of (3) exceeds 1 already for Π = {3, 5} (1.1289; hence Lemma Q in Theorem D (iii)), the count of (iii) exceeds 1 for the sets of primes named in §0, and so can the sum Σ over the live e of 2^(1−D_e) itself: a local search (ranksearch.py) found a support of seven points of Z_945 with sum 33/32 ({141, 207, 318, 363, 490, 614, 786}), and a designed one on Z_315 has 63/64. Two different things are missing.

Missing statement 1 (odd m ≥ 7, 15 | n and further odd primes as in §0; first order would do). Let F be a multiset on an odd Z_{n′} with an odd number of elements, W its support, W_2 the set of its points of odd multiplicity. Then R_d(W_2, W) := Σ over the e > 1, e | n′, that are live for W and dangerous for W_2 (Σ_{w ∈ W_2} η_e^w ∈ 2·Z[η_e]) of 2^(1−D_e(W)) is less than 1.

By Proposition P with (β) this gives a good signing for every multiset with an odd number of elements on every Z_n (take F = the residues modulo n′). In my searches the largest value is 337/512 ≈ 0.658, for 13 points of Z_105 and of Z_315 (ranksum.py: every support with 0 of Z_15 with W_2 = W, |W| odd: at most 81/128; random and structured supports of Z_45, Z_105, Z_135, Z_225, Z_315); the local searches found at most 21/32 (ranksearch.py … d: W_2 = W on Z_105, Z_315, Z_945; ranksum2.py: pairs W_2 ⊆ W on Z_45, Z_105, Z_315). A live dangerous prime p forces an odd number of points of W_2 in every class modulo p and costs 2^(2−p) (1/2 + 1/8 + 1/32 = 21/32 for 3, 5, 7 together), and Lemma Q bounds the prime powers; what I could not bound in general are the e with two or more primes (for them I have only the bound of Lemma V, which ignores that e is dangerous; for e = 15, dangerous means that the parities of the cells of Z_3 × Z_5 are of the form r_s + ρ_t). Not proved; the searches are small.

Missing statement 2 (even m, 3 | n; second order needed). A bound for the share of signings bad at 2^j·3^i for some j that uses the sizes of the classes and not only their parities (Lemma N is the first 2-adic digit: for class sizes (2, 2, 2) modulo 3 the true share at j = 0 is 5/32, the first-order bound 1/4), and likewise at the other small e, such that with the bound for e = 1 the sum over e is below 1 for even m ≥ 6.

Here the first order cannot do: for even m the level e = 1 costs up to 3/4 at m = 4 and, in the open range m ≥ 6, up to 11/16 by the proof of (2) (with the case (r, s) = (3, 3) evaluated exactly: P(σ′ = ±σ″) = 5/8), and a dangerous live e = 3 has 2^(1−D_3) = 1/2. (The 11/16 is the referee's remark. In its enumerations the largest true P_1 is 5/8, 35/64, 63/128 for m = 6, 8, 10, and P_1 = 3/4 occurs only at m = 4; my test_A_share.out has the same values as far as it goes: m = 6 on Z_2 to Z_16, m = 8 on Z_2, Z_4, m = 10 on Z_2.) The truth seems to leave room: truesum.py computes, for n = 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 60, 90, the true shares P_e (bad at 2^j·e for some j) summed over e = 1 and the live e > 1. The largest sum found is 15/16 for even m (m = 6, e.g. {0, 0, 1, 1, 2, 5} on Z_6) and 1/2 for odd m ({0, 1, 2}); exhaustive for small m on n ≤ 30, random and structured up to 11 elements otherwise. The margin (1/16 at m = 6) is thin, and I do not claim that a union bound over e can always be made to close for even m; that it did in these tests is evidence, not proof.

An induction step that I could not prove (a second road to the whole conjecture, by Lemma R as in Theorem A): for n even, B ↔ (E, D) on Z_N with both halves nonempty, not both singletons and not both exceptional pairs, there is a good signing of B in which S_E alone, or S_D alone, has no root of even order dividing N. Tested on 177,115 multisets (n = 4, …, 72, tests2.py mprime): no failure. In Theorem A this is where "|C| ≥ 2 gives two absolute values" is used; with an odd part the other half must avoid ±(a given mask) at every odd root at once.

Roads that failed. 1. Target avoidance with the good half treated as one generalised element. False in that generality: for D = {0, 1} one can prescribe a good T ∈ Z[Z_15] with T(η_3) = 1 − η_3 and T(η_5) = 1 + η_5 (the congruences between the components are compatible), and then neither X^0 + X^1 nor X^0 − X^1 avoids ±T. (Thought through, not computed.) So the signing of the good half must be chosen with the other half in view. 2. n = 2n′ from "more than half". For n = 2n′ the conjecture is: two multisets E, D on the odd Z_{n′} have signings with S_E + S_D and S_E − S_D both good; this follows if more than half of the signings of every multiset of at least three elements on an odd modulus are good. True in my tests (least share 5/8, at four equal elements; tests2.py half), but Theorem 4 gives 0.155 and I could not raise it. For 3 ∤ n′ Theorem D makes it unnecessary. 3. A product instead of a sum over the levels of 2. By Lemma R, with q(B) the share of signings of B with a root of even order: q(B) ≤ r(B) + q(E)·q(D), r(B) the share bad at the odd levels and their doubles. The fixed point needs r ≤ 1/4, which fails for small or even halves. Lemma N replaced this. 4. First order alone. By Lemma N a signing is good as soon as λ_e(N) avoids γ_e, γ′_e for every e. At e = 1 (and at every e modulo which all elements are congruent) the two cosets can be everything (pairs; one element against three); this is why (2) of Proposition P goes back to the integers. 5. A proof of Theorem B's kind for m = 5, 6 (vanishing sums of five or six roots of unity) looked feasible but long; not done. (For m = 5 it is not needed: the count of Theorem D (iii) covers five elements on every Z_n, end of §5.)
