Judgement: NOT FOUND in what was searched; that is not proof of absence. Nothing found contradicts it. I found no bound, in any vocabulary, for "vanishes at some n-th root of unity" that is uniform in n and in a prescribed multiset of exponents. Three things are in print or follow from print and should not be claimed as new:

  • Full support (the dense case). R is KNOWN there. Meckes 2009 gives c₁/√n ≤ P ≤ c₂/√n for even n and less for odd n; Miller 2024 gives the sharp limit constant 2√2/√π ≈ 1.596 for even n.
  • Any fixed n. R FOLLOWS trivially with a constant that grows with n: the one-root bound times the number of divisors, at most d(n)·C(m,⌊m/2⌋)/2^m. The whole content of R is the uniformity in n.
  • The dense method. Every dense proof I read uses a per-divisor bound that decays because every residue class mod d holds about n/d random signs, then a union bound over d. Neither step survives a prescribed multiset (my reading).

3.1 The dense case (exponents 0, …, n − 1): R is KNOWN there

Meckes, "Some results on random circulant matrices" (2009), https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.2472 (formulas reassembled from the layout): - "Theorem 6. Let P[X_j = −1] = P[X_j = 1] = 1/2 for each j. If n is even, then (4.1) c_1/√n ≤ P[C_n is singular] ≤ c_2/√n, where c_1, c_2 > 0 are absolute constants. If n ≥ 3 is odd, then (4.2) P[C_n is singular] ≤ min{ c_3 d(n)/n , Σ_{1 0 is an absolute constant, d(n) is the number of divisors of n". - "In particular, if n ≥ 3 is prime then P[C_n is singular] = 2^(−n+1) … It would be nice to have a more complete description of the dependence of the singularity probability on the prime factorization of n." - The constants c_1, c_2, c_3 are not named. - For odd n, since d(n) < 2√n (an estimate the earlier report quotes from this paper), this is O(1/√n) too. So R with m = n holds for full support, for every n.

Miller, "On the singularity probability of random circulant Bernoulli matrices" (2024), https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17577. Here P_q is for 0/1 circulants and P_q^+ for signed ones; q = 1/2 is the uniform sign case. - "Corollary 2. Let q ∈ (0, 1) be fixed. Then P_q^+(n) ∼ Σ_{k=0}^{n/p(n)} φ_q(k, n/p(n))^{p(n)}, except if q = 1/2 and n is even, in which case P_q^+(n) ∼ 2√2/√(πn) as n → ∞." Here p(n) is the smallest prime divisor of n and φ_q the binomial density. - "Corollary 1. Let p be a fixed prime, and let q ∈ (0, 1) be fixed. Then P_q(n) ∼ (1/√p) (p/(2πq(1 − q)))^((p−1)/2) · 1/n^((p−1)/2) as n → ∞, where n is a composite integer whose smallest prime divisor is p." - "Proposition 1. Let n be a positive integer and d ≠ 1 a divisor of n, and let q ∈ (0, 1). Let M(q, n) = max_{0≤k≤n} φ_q(k, n). Then P_q(d, n) ≤ M(q, n/d)^{ϕ(d)} … If d is prime, then P_q(d, n) ≥ M(q, n/d)^d." - So the sharp dense constant for even n is 2√2/√π ≈ 1.5958, twice the one-root constant √(2/π) ≈ 0.7979. - For odd composite n with 3 | n, the two corollaries at q = 1/2 give P ∼ 2√3/(πn) ≈ 1.103/n (my arithmetic). That is of order 1/n, far below 1/√n.

The same for roots of unity of any order (not only n-th roots), a random polynomial with independent identically distributed coefficients. Breuillard and Varjú, "Irreducibility of random polynomials of large degree", https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.13360: - "Lemma 45. Let ω_n be the n-th cyclotomic polynomial of degree ϕ(n). Then for all n, d, Pr[ω_n | P_d] ≤ (C(µ) n/d)^(ϕ(n)/2), where C(µ) > 0 depends only on µ." - "Summing over all such n's we get: Pr[ω_n | P for some n with ϕ(n) ≥ N] = O_{µ,N}(d^(−N/2))." - The paper says the lemma "is implicitly contained in [26, pp. 345]", which is Konyagin's 1999 paper; I did not read Konyagin. - By my reading, neither line uses the Riemann hypothesis; the hypothesis enters the paper's corollaries through its Theorem 2. - With N = 2 and ±1 coefficients this gives (my derivation): P(vanishes at some root of unity) = P(f(1) = 0 or f(−1) = 0) + O(1/d). - "Corollary 4 … P(P is irreducible in Z[x]) = 1 − √(2/(πd)) + O(d^(−1))". This is for 0/1 coefficients and is conditional on the Riemann hypothesis for Dedekind zeta functions.

The linear factors exactly. Borst, Boyd, Brekken, Solberg, Wood, Wood, https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03709: - "Proposition 5.3. Let d be an odd positive integer. For a random monic polynomial g_{±1,d}(x) = x^d + a_{d−1}x^{d−1} + ⋯ + a_0 where each a_i is +1 or −1 independently with probability 1/2, the probability that g_{±1,d}(x) has a linear factor is 2√(2/(π(d+1))) − (4/(π(d+1)))(1 + o(1))." - Lemma 5.2: "If d is even, g(x) cannot have a linear factor."

3.2 One root at a time, hence any fixed n: R FOLLOWS with a constant depending on n

  • The one-root bound C(m,⌊m/2⌋)/2^m is the earlier report's item 3.1 (Erdős; Kleitman; Juškevičius–Šemetulskis Proposition 4). I did not refetch it.
  • Written out (mine): a signing is bad iff Φ_d | S for some d | n. For each d this is S(ζ_d) = 0 at one primitive d-th root, a single signed sum of m unit complex numbers. So P(bad) ≤ d(n)·C(m,⌊m/2⌋)/2^m ≈ 0.80·d(n)/√m.
  • This is R for each fixed n. It is not uniform; the conjecture's content is removing d(n).

3.3 Independent coefficients with different laws — CLOSE (zeros and lopsided classes excluded; union bound)

Bary-Soroker, Koukoulopoulos, Kozma, "Irreducibility of random polynomials: general measures", https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14567: - "Lemma 7.2. Let µ_0, µ_1, …, µ_{n−1} be probability measures such that ‖µ_j‖∞ ≤ 1 − ε for j = 1, 2, …, n − 1. For each given z ∈ C ∖ {0}, we have that P{A∈M(n)}(A(z) = 0) ≪ 1/√(εn), where the implied constant is absolute." - "Lemma 7.3. Assume the set-up of Lemma 7.2. We then have that Σ_{ϕ(d)≤m0} P_{A∈M(n)}(Φ_d | A) ≪ m0/√(εn) ∀ m0 ∈ N." - This is the nearest printed statement to "arbitrary coefficient laws, cyclotomic divisors". It differs in two ways. Every coefficient must be genuinely random, so a prescribed support, where absent exponents are a point mass at 0, is excluded. And the many divisors are handled by a union bound that costs the factor m0.

Bazin, "Irreducibility of random polynomials of Z[X]", https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06643 (the search tool's summary gave other author names; this is the name on the paper): - "P(Φ_d | A) ≤ Π_{i=0}^{φ(d)−1} sup_x P(a′_i = x) ≤ (Kd/(cn))^(φ(d)/2)", under "do not take any value with probability higher than 1 − c". - "The O(1/√n) bound here is optimal: indeed, X + 1 divides A with probability P(A(−1) = 0), which decreases as 1/√n when N is fixed." - Same restriction as above.

Terlov, https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08233: - "Corollary 2.3. … let f be a random polynomial from P_{d,1} with independent and uniformly distributed coefficients. Then, the probability that f is divisible by at least one polynomial with integer coefficients of positive degree not exceeding m0 is at most O(1/√d)." - "Remark 2.4. Corollary 2.3 states that the upper bound achieved by Konyagin in [8] can be extended from polinomials with {0, 1} coefficients to ones with {−1, 0, 1} coefficients." - Zeros are allowed here, but at random (each coefficient uniform on {−1, 0, 1}), not prescribed.

Why the dense method does not carry over (my reading, not from print). All the proofs above bound P(Φ_d | f) by a product over ϕ(d) residue classes mod d, each holding about n/d random signs, then sum over d. For a prescribed multiset the classes mod d can be empty or lopsided. For m equal elements every divisor gives the same event with probability about 0.80/√m. So the per-divisor bound does not decay, and the union bound costs d(n).

Address: https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.04035 (J. ACM 2022 by the search summary). This item was not in the earlier reports.

  • "Theorem 2.2 (Littlewood–Offord theorem for polytopes). There is a universal constant C (C = 5√2 suffices) such that the following holds. For all b ∈ R^m and A ∈ R^{m×n} with |A_ij| ≥ 1 for all i ∈ [m] and j ∈ [n], Pr[Au ≤ b & A_i · u > b_i − 2 for some i ∈ [m]] ≤ C√(ln m)/√n, where u ∼ {−1, 1}^n is uniformly random."
  • "The Littlewood–Offord theorem (Theorem 2.1), along with a naive union bound, implies a bound of O(m/√n); we are not aware of any improvement of this naive bound prior to our work."
  • This is the only printed result I found where many linear forms share the signs and the cost in the number of forms is below the union bound.
  • R does not follow, for three reasons. The event requires all m inequalities to hold (the boundary of an intersection, not the union of the m strips). The entries are real and at least 1 in size everywhere. The bound still grows with m.
  • A word search of the paper for "roots of unity", "cyclotomic", "circulant" finds nothing.

3.5 Geometric and polynomial Littlewood–Offord theorems — R for fixed n only; constants depend on degree or dimension

Grebennikov and Kwan, "Geometric Littlewood–Offord problems via lattice point counting", https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24699: - "Theorem 1.1. Let 1 ≤ b ≤ n and d, c ≥ 1 be integers. Let F ∈ C[t_1, …, t_n] be a polynomial of degree d and Chow rank at most c. (1) Suppose that F 'robustly depends on at least b of its variables', in the sense that it is not possible to make F(ξ_1, …, ξ_n) identically zero by fixing fewer than b of the ξ_i to ±1 values. … Then P[F(ξ_1, …, ξ_n) = 0] ≤ O_{d,c}(b^(−1/2))." - "Theorem 1.3. … Let S ⊆ C^k be a (possibly reducible) affine algebraic variety of dimension at most ℓ and degree at most d. Consider vectors a_1, …, a_n ∈ C^k, and assume that one can form b disjoint bases from them. Then P[ξ_1 a_1 + … + ξ_n a_n ∈ S] ≤ O_{d,k}(b^(−(k−ℓ)/2))." - How it relates (mine): the bad event is F(ε) = 0 for the circulant determinant F = Π_{k=0}^{n−1} Σ_i ε_i ζ^(k·b_i). That is a product of n linear forms, so degree n and Chow rank 1 by the paper's definition. No linear factor vanishes identically while a sign is free, so b = m. Theorem 1.1(1) then gives P(bad) ≤ O_n(1/√m). - That is R for each fixed n with a constant depending on n, the same as 3.2. The earlier report left open whether this paper applies; this is the answer. - No general theorem on products of linear forms can be uniform in the number of factors (my remark): D forms on disjoint blocks of m/D signs vanish with probability of order D^(3/2)/√m. R would be a statement about the special family of circulant determinants.

"Optimal bound for the polynomial Littlewood-Offord problem", https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08708 (abstract only): "let F be a degree-d multilinear polynomial that contains r degree-d monomials involving disjoint sets of variables. Then … P[F(ξ_1, …, ξ_n) = 0] = O_d(r^(−1/2))." The constant depends on the degree.

Fox, Kwan, Spink, https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04894 (abstract only): "Pr(a_1ξ_1+⋯+a_nξ_n ∈ S) ≤ n^(−1/2+o(1)) whenever S is definable with respect to an o-minimal structure … under the necessary condition that it does not contain a line segment." The bad set here, a union of subspaces, contains line segments, and the dimension is fixed there.

5. Overall judgement

Conjecture R: NOT FOUND.

  • KNOWN in print:
  • R for full support, every n (Meckes 2009, constants unnamed).
  • The sharp dense limit 2√2/√(πn) for even n and the smallest-prime-divisor law for odd n (Miller 2024).
  • For independent identically distributed coefficients, the bound for roots of unity of any order (Breuillard–Varjú Lemma 45, credited there to Konyagin 1999; O'Rourke–Wood).
  • The one-root bound (earlier report).
  • FOLLOWS from print: R for each fixed n, with a constant growing with n. Two routes: the one-root bound times d(n) (3.2), or Grebennikov–Kwan's Theorem 1.1 applied to the circulant determinant (3.5). Both derivations are mine and short.
  • CLOSE: cyclotomic divisors for independent coefficients with different laws, where every coefficient must be random (3.3); the Littlewood–Offord theorem for polytopes, a different event with √(ln m) growth (3.4).
  • NOT FOUND:
  • any bound for "some n-th root of unity" uniform in n for a prescribed multiset or support;
  • the constants 1, 0.93, 0.53 or their doubles for that event;
  • any of it in the vocabularies of sparse or lacunary random polynomials, random trigonometric polynomials at rational points, group rings, signed circulant or Cayley graphs, sparse circulants, convolution kernels, character sums, torsion points, or Littlewood–Offord for unions.
  • A fair sentence would say: for full support the O(1/√n) bound is Meckes's and the sharp constant Miller's; for a fixed modulus the bound is immediate from the classical one-root inequality with a factor d(n); the conjecture is that the factor can be dropped for every multiset. I did not test that sentence beyond the comparisons above.
