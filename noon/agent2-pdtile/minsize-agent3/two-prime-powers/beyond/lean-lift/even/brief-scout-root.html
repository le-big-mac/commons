The statement

B a finite multiset of m residues modulo n (any exponents, repeats allowed). A signing gives each element a sign ±1; the signed mask is S(X) = Σ_(b ∈ B) ± X^b. Call a signing bad if S vanishes at some n-th root of unity (S is divisible by a cyclotomic polynomial Φ_d, d | n, d = 1 included).

  • Conjecture R. There is an absolute constant C such that, for every n and every multiset of m elements, at most C/√m of the 2^m signings are bad. (For odd n the data suggest C = 1; the true constant seems to be about 0.93 along even m and 0.53 along odd m; for all n twice that.)
  • The rate is sharp: m copies of one element have C(m, m/2)/2^m ≈ 0.80/√m bad signings (the signs add up to 0).
  • Proved (three computer-assisted proofs, last night): at most 3/8 of the signings are bad if n is odd and m ≥ 3, at most 3/4 for every n.

Other clothes for the same thing: - Random polynomials with prescribed exponents. For Σ ε_i X^(b_i) with independent uniform signs and ANY exponents b_i (repeats allowed), the probability of a common root with X^n − 1 (or: of having a cyclotomic factor at all; or: of vanishing at some root of unity) is O(1/√m), uniformly in n and in the exponents. For the dense case (exponents 0, 1, …, n − 1) results of this order are, I believe, in print (random Littlewood polynomials and their cyclotomic factors; singular random ±1 circulants); the question is the uniform statement for arbitrary exponents. - A Littlewood–Offord inequality for a union. For fixed complex numbers the classical inequality bounds the chance that a random signed sum takes ONE value; here the signed sum of the k-th powers ζ^(k·b_i) must vanish for SOME k, and the bound is still O(1/√m). - Group rings. A random signed element Σ ε_i g^(b_i) of Z[C_n] is a zero divisor in Q[C_n] with probability O(1/√m), uniformly.
