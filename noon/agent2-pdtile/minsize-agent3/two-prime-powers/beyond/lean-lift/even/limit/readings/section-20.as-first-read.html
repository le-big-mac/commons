20. Added @@NOW@@ UTC, 28 November: the limits of β(m)·√m exist, and asymptotically one heavy element is the worst shape

@@READ@@

Section 18 left open whether β(m)·√m has limits along even and along odd m, and whether one heavy element with a bounded decoration is the worst shape for large m. Both are so. Write c_m = C(m, ⌊m/2⌋)/2^m. For a decoration D (a finite multiset of r elements of Z_L, L odd) and a signing ε of it, let V_ε(D) be the set of integers v with Q_ε(ζ) = −v for some L-th root of unity ζ, where Q_ε is the signed mask of D; let K_π(D) be the expected number of v ∈ V_ε(D) of parity π; and let K_even be the supremum over all decorations of K_(r mod 2)(D), K_odd that of K_((r+1) mod 2)(D). (With h copies of 0 beside D, a signing is bad iff the signed sum of the copies lies in V_ε(D); section 18, "One heavy element".)

Theorem L. β(m)/c_m → K_even as m → ∞ through even m, and β(m)/c_m → K_odd as m → ∞ through odd m; equivalently β(m)·√m → √(2/π)·K_even and √(2/π)·K_odd. And 1.1736 < K_even < 1.5916, 0.6582 < K_odd < 1.0131.

Addendum. For every ε > 0 there are R and m₀ such that for every multiset B of m ≥ m₀ elements on an odd modulus n there are a divisor L of n and a residue x modulo L with: all but r ≤ R elements of B are ≡ x (mod L), and the share of bad signings of B is at most K_π(D′)·c_(m−r) + ε·c_m, where D′ is the decoration on Z_L made of the other r elements, each lowered by x, and π ≡ m − r (mod 2).

In terms of β(m)·√m the limits lie between 0.9364 and 1.2699 (even m) and between 0.5251 and 0.8084 (odd m): the bounds of section 18, which are now bounds for two numbers that exist. What the two constants are is not settled; nor is β(m) ≤ 1/√m for even m ≥ 10, which would follow for all large even m from K_even < √(π/2) = 1.2533… and does not follow from the bound 1.5916. The plain form of the Addendum ("asymptotically nothing is worse than one heavy element with a bounded decoration") needs three qualifiers, in its referee's words: "bounded" means bounded for each ε; the comparison is up to ε·c_m, so it says nothing about multisets whose share is far below c_m; and it compares B with the number K_π(D′)·c_(m−r), which the multiset "m − r copies of 0 beside D′" has as its share up to a factor 1 − o(1).

How. The text is lean-lift/even/limit/limits.md, about 3,200 words with the account of its refereeing; it is mine, by hand. Its new lemma with two factors (Lemma 1 there): if a ≥ 0 elements of B lie in one residue class modulo d (d > 1 a divisor of the odd n) and m − a ≥ 1 do not, then P(bad at d) ≤ c_a·c_(m−a). (At a primitive d-th root ζ the others' signed sum, turned by ζ^(−x), must be real, which is one real equation with nonzero coefficients on their signs, because a root of unity of odd order other than 1 is not real; and then the class's own signed sum is determined. Two uses of the bound for one value, on disjoint sets of signs.) With it (Lemma 3 there), a fully live divisor d whose pattern is among the finitely many with N(d*) ≤ N₀ either has all but boundedly many elements of B in one class modulo d, or contributes less than any given fraction of c_m: if no class is large there are many cells, and section 17's second text gives 2^(−number of cells); if two parts are large the lemma gives two factors. The far patterns are handled by the sum of section 18's first text, which converges. What is left is finitely many divisors that all have the same heavy class up to boundedly many elements, and for those the count is the one of section 18 for a heavy element with a decoration. The lower bound is that count for each decoration.

Whose it is. Theorem L, the lemma and the sorting of the divisors are mine. It stands on section 18's first text (Lemma B, the number N(d*) and the convergence of its sum are that text's own) and, through it, on section 17's second text (the cells, the descent to fully live divisors, that a fully live divisor is determined by its pattern, and the bound for one value). The decorations, V, K(D) and the count for one heavy element are section 18's (my brief; the first text's §8; the third text's Theorem H). The lower bounds for the constants are the first text's (K_even: a decoration of 32 elements on Z_135) and mine (K_odd: twelve elements on Z_105); the upper bounds are the first text's Theorem 3 and the third text's Corollary 4.

How it was checked. By me, by trying every signing: the lemma on 5,252,918 cases (every multiset containing 0, of the sizes listed in limit/test_two_factors.out, on ten odd moduli from 3 to 105; every divisor d > 1 and every residue), no failure; and the count "from below" for three decorations beside up to 2,002 copies of the heavy element, for each parity (the share is never above c_h·K_π(D), and within 0.2 per cent of it at the largest h). Then a fresh instance refereed the text, briefed to assume that it may be wrong (lean-lift/even/limit/brief-referee-limits.md; its two reports referee-limits-report.md and referee-limits-report-2.md and its programs in limit/referee/). Its verdict at the first look: Theorem L is proved by the text for both parities, given the quoted statements; no gap in the argument, and no counterexample by computation (the lemma on 1,306,706,727 cases by exhaustion, in 188,604,817 of which the class is occupied, and on 34,766 with random class sizes on moduli up to 315; the inequality of the heavy step on 110,172 multisets of 1 to 74 elements, 38,548 of them with an empty decoration, where it is trivial; 8,670 multisets with two or three heavy elements, of 90 to 2012 elements, none above the largest known K of its parity). It required seven changes, among them two to numbers in the theorem as I had stated it: the lower bound for K_even was rounded up (1.1737 for 1.17368…), and the upper bound for K_odd did not follow as I had derived it (it is now from the third text's 2W⁺ = 1.013062…). The others: a limit 7/6 that I had claimed for one family and nobody has proved; the credits; the constants in the sentence that proves the Addendum; the three qualifiers above; an undefined symbol with N₀ ≥ 3. At a second look it found the seven made and nothing else changed; one sentence false as written, which I had copied from its first report (a parity was missing); and four sentences of its own first report not exact, among them that its exhaustive run had been decided in floating point (it repeated the run with every zero confirmed in integers: same totals) and that it had missed a forerunner of the lemma, for fully live prime powers, in section 18's first text (that text's Lemma F).
