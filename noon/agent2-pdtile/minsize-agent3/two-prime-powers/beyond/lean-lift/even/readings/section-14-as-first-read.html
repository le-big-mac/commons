14. Added 17:23 UTC, 27 November: the lemma on signs for every n: the only multisets without good signs are certain pairs

This section has not been read yet: a fresh instance is reading it against the records as this line stands, and the line will say what it found.

Sections 9 to 13 are about odd n. For even n the lemma on signs is false as it stands: on Z_2 the multiset {0, 1} has the signed masks ±(1 + X), which Φ_2 divides, and ±(1 − X), which Φ_1 divides. The exact statement, for every n: let 2^k be the power of 2 in n; a nonempty multiset B on Z_n has a signing whose signed mask is divisible by no Φ_d, d | n, unless B consists of two elements a, b with a − b not divisible by 2^k (an exceptional pair; such a pair has none: X^a − X^b vanishes at 1, and X^a + X^b at a root of unity of 2-power order). For m ≥ 3 elements the share of good signings is at least 1/2 if m = 3, at least 1/4 if m = 4, and more than 0.075 if m ≥ 5. The first two bounds are attained ({0, 1, 2} on Z_12; {0, 0, 0, 1} on Z_2). This was a conjecture of mine at 13:10 UTC on 27 November, read off a search; it is proved in lean-lift/even/third/theorem-twice.md (Corollary 3), on paper, from four things of four different hands, as told under "Whose it is".

How. Write n = 3^a·n_3 with 3 ∤ n_3, and n_3 = 2^k·n″. (1) Reduce modulo 3. Φ_{3^i·d} ≡ Φ_d^{φ(3^i)} modulo 3 for 3 ∤ d; so if Φ_{3^i·d} divides a signed mask S, then Φ_d divides S modulo 3, that is, S(ζ) ∈ 3·Z[ζ] for ζ of order d. Call a signing 3-bad at d in that case. A signing that is 3-bad at no d | n_3 is good on Z_n, and the prime 3, whose levels cost the most in the count of section 12, has left the problem. (2) The levels of the prime 2. Fix an odd e″ | n″ and a root of unity x of order e″, and give the element b the value x^b in V = Z[x]/3. For j ≥ 1 the slice criterion for the prime 2 says: a signing is 3-bad at 2^j·e″ only if in every class modulo 2^(j−1) the signed sums of the values over its two halves modulo 2^j are equal in V ("level j is bad"); and it is 3-bad at e″ iff the signed sum of all values is 0 ("level 0 is bad"). Theorem T′: the share of signings for which some level 0, …, k is bad is at most twice the share for which level 0 is bad (less the share for which every class modulo 2^k sums to 0). Its proof is four lines. Let F_d be the event that every class modulo 2^d has signed sum 0; these events are nested, F_0 ⊇ F_1 ⊇ … ⊇ F_k. Negating the signs of the upper halves at level j turns "level j is bad" into F_(j−1), so the two have the same share. And F_j lies inside "level j is bad" and inside "level 0 is bad". So the share of "level j is bad and level 0 is not" is at most P(F_(j−1)) − P(F_j), and the sum over j telescopes to at most P(F_0), the share of level 0. Nothing is asked of the group V or of the values. (3) The count. By the step-down, as in section 13, only e″ = 1 and the e″ > 1 all of whose levels are live need counting. The share of signings 3-bad at 1 is p_m, the chance that m coin signs add up to a multiple of 3: p_m = 1/3 + (2/3)(−1/2)^m, which is 1/4, 3/8 for m = 3, 4 and at most 11/32 from m = 5 on. The shares 3-bad at the live e″ > 1 add up to at most W(no 3) = 0.11856…, the sum of section 12's 2^(−μ(d)) over the d > 1 with no prime factor below 5; and no e″ > 1 is live for fewer than five elements. So the share of signings that are not good is at most 2·p_3 = 1/2, 2·p_4 = 3/4, and 2·(11/32) + 2·0.1186 < 0.925 from five elements on. Two elements that are not an exceptional pair are settled by hand.

In print. Two searches by fresh instances with the web, neither of which found the statement or the inequality; that is what those searches found, and not proof that either is new. Three parts of it are classical or follow from classical results, and are not to be taken for new: the exceptional pairs themselves (the only vanishing sum of two roots of unity is η + (−η)); the case of three elements, which follows from the classical list of three-term vanishing sums by an argument the report writes out; and full support for even n, where the singular signings are counted in print. The second, made after the proof (lean-lift/even/scout-twice-report.md: 33 web searches and the same interfaces, with zbMATH), looked for the inequality of Theorem T′ and again for the whole statement. Nearest to the inequality, all for full support (every coefficient a sign): a preprint of 2024 on random circulant matrices of signs (L. Miller, arXiv:2411.17577) writes out the inclusion and exclusion for the zeros at 1 and −1 and finds, for even n, that the matrix is singular with probability asymptotic to 2√2/√(πn), which is twice the probability that the row sum is 0: the constant 2 of Theorem T′, as an asymptotic statement, for the levels 0 and 1; the OEIS entry A144926 (the number of singular n × n circulants with entries ±1) has the exact formula 2·C(2p, p) at n = 2p, p an odd prime, and its terms at n = 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 (4, 8, 128, 24192, 1150681600) lie below the bound 2·C(n, n/2) that Theorem T′ gives there (4, 12, 140, 25740, 1202160780; I compared them myself); Meckes's paper (section 12) notes that the values at 1 and at −1 are identically distributed. Nearest in method: a form of Lévy's maximal inequality for symmetric random variables in an abelian group and a family of blocks any two of which are nested or disjoint (A. Khare and B.
