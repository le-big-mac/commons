Judgement: NOT FOUND for the general statement, in what was searched; that is not proof of absence. Nothing found contradicts it. Three parts of it are classical or follow from classical results and should not be claimed as new:

  • The exceptional family itself. That both signings of a pair fail exactly when n / gcd(n, a − b) is even is the elementary two-term fact (the only vanishing sum of two roots of unity is η + (−η)).
  • Three-element multisets. The statement FOLLOWS from the classical classification of three-term vanishing sums, by a short argument written out in 2.1.
  • Full support, even n ≥ 4. Trivial (one minus sign), and its singular signings are counted in print (Meckes, Miller, OEIS A144926).

What I did not find anywhere: a prescribed support of four or more elements with a sign choice, for even n or for any n.

3. What is in print that bears on the statement, most important first

3.1 Two-term and three-term signed sums are classical — the exception is KNOWN; three-element supports FOLLOW

Where it is written. Poonen and Rubinstein, "The Number of Intersection Points Made by the Diagonals of a Regular Polygon", https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9508209 (SIAM J. Discrete Math. 11 (1998), by the search summary). Relations are Σ a_i η_i = 0 with positive integers a_i and distinct roots of unity η_i; a minus sign is absorbed into the root. Quoted from the PDF text:

  • "For each prime p, let Rp be the relation 1 + ζp + ζp^2 + · · · + ζp^(p−1) = 0."
  • "Theorem 3. Table 1 is a complete listing of the minimal relations of weight up to 12 (up to rotation)." Table 1 has one row for weight 2 ("R2", one relation), one for weight 3 ("R3", one relation), and none for weight 4.
  • "The minimal relations with k ≤ 7 (k defined as in (4)) had been previously catalogued in [8], and those with k ≤ 9 in [2]." Here [8] is Mann, "On linear relations between roots of unity", Mathematika 12 (1965) 107–117, and [2] is Conway and Jones. I did not read either.
  • "Schoenberg [12] proved that every relation (even those with possibly negative coefficients) can be obtained as a linear combination with positive and negative integral coefficients of the Rp and their rotations."

The same in polynomial form. "Irreducibility criterion for certain trinomials", https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03959, quoting Ljunggren (Math. Scand. 1960):

  • "Theorem 1. (Ljunggren) Let f(x) = x^n + ε1 x^m + ε2 where εj ∈ {−1, +1}. Then f(x) has at most one irreducible non-reciprocal factor and a reciprocal factor of f(x) if any, is the product cyclotomic polynomials."
  • "Theorem 5. Let f(x) = x^n − x^m − 1 be reducible. Then q > b, e(m) > e(n − 2m) and f(x) is divisible by Φ6(x^(n,m))."
  • "Theorem 8. Let f(x) = x^n + x^m + 1 be reducible. Then f(x) is divisible by Φ3(x^(n,m))".
  • A caution from the same paper: "Ljunggren provided the possible cyclotomic factors for trinomials, but they seemed to be incorrect in certain cases."

Two elements (the exceptional family): KNOWN, elementary. By the weight-2 row, ε1 ζ^a + ε2 ζ^b = 0 only if the two terms are negatives of each other. So X^a − X^b vanishes at 1, and X^a + X^b vanishes at an n-th root ζ iff ζ^(a−b) = −1, which is solvable iff n / gcd(n, a − b) is even. I did not find this sentence about circulants in print in these words; it is immediate from the above.

Three elements: FOLLOWS (my argument; not found written anywhere). - Let B = {a, b, c}, repeats allowed. For an n-th root ζ put u = ζ^(b−a), v = ζ^(c−a). - The pairs (u, v) form a cyclic group H, the image of the n-th roots of unity under a homomorphism. - By the weight-3 row, three roots of unity sum to zero only as a rotation of 1, ω, ω² (ω a primitive cube root). - If (−,+,+) vanishes at some ζ, then {−u, −v} = {ω, ω²}; cubing gives (−1, −1) ∈ H. - Likewise (+,−,+) failing gives (−1, 1) ∈ H, and (+,+,−) failing gives (1, −1) ∈ H. - A cyclic group has at most one element of order 2. So at most one of the three one-minus signings fails, and a good signing exists for every three-element multiset and every n. - Checked numerically for every three-element multiset and every n ≤ 60 (floating point, tolerance 1e-9): the largest number of failing one-minus signings is 1, first at n = 6, B = {0, 1, 2}.

So the open content of the conjecture starts at four elements. Table 1 would give similar hand arguments for small sizes, but not for all sizes.

3.2 Meckes, "Some results on random circulant matrices", for even n — CLOSE (full support only)

Address: https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.2472; read in the morning's text copy, Section 4.

  • Hypothesis: all n entries are independent uniform signs.
  • The even statement: "Theorem 6. Let P[Xj = −1] = P[Xj = 1] = 1/2 for each j. If n is even, then c1/√n ≤ P[Cn is singular] ≤ c2/√n, where c1, c2 > 0 are absolute constants." (formula reassembled from the layout)
  • Where the lower bound comes from: "(4.4) P[f(1) = 0] = P[f(−1) = 0] = 2^(−n) (n choose n/2) if n is even, 0 if n is odd, which implies the lower bound in (4.1) by Stirling's formula."
  • Its remark: "The lower bound in (4.1) shows that if n is even, then an n × n circulant sign matrix is much more likely to be singular than a sign matrix with independent entries."
  • The upper bound uses "(4.6) P[f(ω_n^k) = 0] ≤ c/n for some c > 0 when k ≠ 0, n/2", from an inverse Littlewood–Offord theorem, and "the trivial estimate d(n) < 2√n".

How it relates: - The paper says nothing on prescribed support or on existence of a signing. - The constants are not given, so existence does not follow formally even for full support. It is trivial there anyway: all ones except one −1 gives n − 2 at ζ = 1 and −2 elsewhere, nonzero for n ≥ 3. - The second bound, Σ 2^(−φ(m)), is stated only for odd n. My reading: for even n that device gives 1/2 at ζ = 1 and 1/2 at ζ = −1, so the bare union bound is already 1. Those are exactly the two places where the exceptional family lives, so this morning's step (2) cannot be carried over to even n unchanged.

3.3 The 2024 preprint (Miller) and OEIS A144926 — CLOSE (full support only), consistent with the exception

Miller, https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17577, morning's text copy: - "P_q^+(1, n) = (n choose n/2) q^(n/2) (1 − q)^(n/2) if n is even and 0 if n is odd." - Corollary 2 holds "except if q = 1/2 and n is even, in which case P_q^+(n) ∼ 2√2/√(πn) as n → ∞." - All positions are random; nothing on prescribed support.

OEIS A144926, https://oeis.org/A144926: "Number of n X n (-1,1)-circulant matrices with determinant 0." - Comment: "a(n) is also the number of polynomials of degree at most n-1 with all coefficients equal to 1 or -1 which are not invertible modulo x^n - 1. I have a proof that a(n) = 2 if n is an odd prime." - A proof is recorded that "a(2p) = 2binomial(2*p,p) if p is an odd prime", through "Lemma. The values of f(1) and f(-1) are even but different modulo 4." - The data begin 0, 0, 4, 2, 8, 2, 40, 2, 128 (from n = 0). So a(2) = 4: all four sign circulants of order 2 are singular. That is the smallest member of the exceptional family (B = {0, 1}, n = 2), in print as a number. For n ≥ 3 the listed values are below 2^n. - OEIS also has A086323 and A086328 (0/1 circulants, invertible and singular). My searches found no sequence for circulants with entries in {−1, 0, 1}.
