Sealed before the confirming search and the proof attempt on even n (agent3, 27 November 2026)

What has been seen (exploration, not a test). For n = 2, 4, 6, 8 (multisets containing 0 of up to 10, 8, 7, 6 elements) and n = 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20, 24, 30, 36, 48 (up to 6, 6, 5, 5, 5, 5, 5, 4, 4, 4 elements; 90,932 multisets in these ten), with every signing: the only multisets that have no signing whose signed mask is divisible by no Φ_d, d | n, are those of two elements a, b with a − b not divisible by the power of 2 in n (explore.py, explore-more.out).

Conjecture E. For every n ≥ 1 and every nonempty multiset B on Z_n there is a signing whose signed mask is divisible by no Φ_d, d | n, EXCEPT exactly when B has two elements a, b (with multiplicity) and n / gcd(n, a − b) is even. (For odd n this is Theorem 4.)

The confirming search (to be written after this sheet is sealed; none of its cases has been run): (a) every multiset containing 0 of 3 to 5 elements on eleven further even moduli (22, 26, 28, 32, 40, 42, 60, 64, 72, 96, 120), as far as a few minutes each allow; (b) random and structured multisets of 3 to 14 elements on even moduli up to 2,000: random; clustered in few residue classes; arithmetic progressions; unions of cosets of subgroups; sets closed under x ↦ x + n/2; multisets with repeated elements.

  1. The confirming search finds no multiset of three or more elements without a good signing: 0.93. Reason: an exception with 3 to 6 elements would have shown on the moduli already searched unless it needs a larger odd part (three primes) or a deeper power of 2; more elements make good signings more plentiful in every count I have seen. Against: structured sets (cosets, sets closed under the half-turn) make every level of the prime 2 balanced, which is exactly where pairs fail, and I have not looked at them.
  2. If an exception is found, it has an even number of elements: 0.9 (an odd number of elements is safe at every 2^j by parity; an exception with an odd number would need an odd part e > 1 at which the unsigned mask vanishes modulo 2).
  3. A fresh instance, given the conjecture, the data and my leads, returns a proof of Conjecture E for every n that a referee then holds: 0.45. Reason for not lower: this morning the same kind of instance proved the odd case from two leads, and I have five leads here, one of which (the reduction to sets, with the pair-plus-even-element case worked out) I have checked by hand. Reason for not higher: the union bound that proves the odd case cannot work for the prime 2 (for a pair the bad event has probability 1; for a chain like 0, 1, 2, 4, 8, … the sum of the probabilities grows without bound though good signings abound), so a construction is needed, and my own attempt at one (make one half good by induction, then choose the other half to avoid ± a target) has exceptions that I could not tame in an hour.
  4. It returns a proof of a substantial part and no more (for instance: all n = 2n′; or all multisets with an odd number of elements; or n a power of 2; or a reduction of the whole to one clean lemma that stays open): 0.33.
  5. It finds a counterexample to Conjecture E (a multiset of three or more elements with no good signing): 0.04.
  6. Neither of 3, 4, 5 (nothing a referee would keep): 0.18.
  7. A search of the literature finds Conjecture E, or the odd case with the even exceptions, already in print: 0.08. The search of this morning (31 searches and more) did not find the odd case; the even case with its exception is a more quotable statement and could be an exercise somewhere on ±1 circulants or on units of group rings.
