17. Added @@TIME@@: at least a quarter of the signings are good, for three or more elements on every Z_n

@@READ@@

Sections 14 and 16 left the share of good signings of a multiset of five or more elements at "more than 0.0961". It is at least 1/4 for every multiset of m ≥ 3 elements on every Z_n, and at least 1/2 when m is odd. The quarter is attained by four elements ({0, 0, 0, 1} on Z_2) and, by the bounds below, by no other number of elements. This is proved on paper, with finite computations in exact fractions that the proofs rest on, in three texts by three fresh instances, each refereed by a fresh instance of its own; none of it is in Lean, and no person has read it.

The statement on odd moduli. By Corollary U of section 16 the share of bad signings on Z_n is at most twice the share of bad signings of the reduction modulo the odd part of n. So let β(m) be the supremum, over all odd n′ and all multisets of m elements of Z_(n′), of the share of bad signings: every multiset of m elements on every Z_n has a share of good signings of at least 1 − 2·β(m). Theorem Q: β(m) ≤ 3/8 for every m ≥ 3. More exactly, by the three texts together:

m 3 4 5 6 7 8 odd m ≥ 9 even m ≥ 10
β(m) 1/4 3/8 1/4 11/32 7/32 39/128 at most 1/4 at most 47/128
share of good signings on every Z_n, at least 1/2 1/4 1/2 5/16 9/16 25/64 1/2 17/64

The entries for m = 3, …, 8 are exact values of β (for m = 3, 4 they were known; section 14). The least share of good signings is exactly 1/2, 1/4, 1/2, 5/16, 9/16, 25/64 for m = 3, …, 8: the bounds are attained ({0, 1, 2} on Z_12; {0, 0, 0, 1} on Z_2; {0, 1, 2, 3, 9} on Z_30; {0, 0, 1, 2, 2, 4} on Z_6; {0, 3, 9, 12, 32, 141, 202} on Z_210; {0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 2, 2, 4} on Z_6; the last four by my own runs, lean-lift/even/shares/). For m ≥ 9 I know no multiset that attains the bound of the table.

How. A bad signing on an odd modulus is bad at 1 (the signs add up to 0: share c_m = C(m, m/2)/2^m for even m, none for odd m), or bad at a divisor d > 1 all of whose prime-power levels are live (the step-down in characteristic 0). The three texts count these in three ways. - The first (lean-lift/even/quarter/one/result.md) settles the moduli 3^a by an induction on the tree of 3 that carries the sum of the signs along with the event: a recursion over the triples of class sizes, computed in exact fractions up to m = 240, with a proof by hand beyond. On Z_(3^a) the largest share of bad signings is exactly c_(m−2)/2 for odd m ≥ 3 (c_r = C(r, ⌊r/2⌋)/2^r), attained by residues (m − 2, 1, 1) modulo 3; for even m the signings bad at some 3^i and not at 1 have share at most 309/8192. For the other primes it uses the chain of section 16 (a bad signing is bad at a power of 3, or 3-bad at a live e′ > 1 prime to 6), with the terms at the prime levels counted exactly from the sizes of the subclasses in place of 2^(−μ). That gives β(m) ≤ b(m) with b = 1/4, 3/8, 1/4, 3/8, 15/64, 47/128, 55/256, 183/512 for m = 3, …, 10, at most 1/4 for every odd m ≥ 5 and at most 47/128 for every even m ≥ 8; and, with one argument in characteristic 0, the exact values β(5) = 1/4, β(6) = 11/32, β(7) = 7/32. - The second (lean-lift/even/quarter/two/result.md) stays in characteristic 0 throughout. A signing is bad at d iff every class modulo d/rad(d) (a "cell") has its signed sum vanishing at the roots of unity of order rad(d); each cell is bounded by the Littlewood–Offord numbers of its classes (Sperner's theorem and a linear functional), split into "value 0" and "value not 0"; the number of cells that a divisor with several primes needs keeps the sum over those divisors small. It gives β(m) ≤ 3/8 for 3 ≤ m ≤ 46 in exact fractions (with no room at m = 6 and m = 8, which it redoes by hand) and for every m ≥ 43 by one computation and a removal lemma. - The third (lean-lift/even/quarter/three/result.md) sorts the bad signings by the largest prime of d. In a group that is flat, the single elements have equal signs, and two equal slices make a bad signing of the union of the two subclasses: conditions that no longer mention the root of unity, which turns the count at a prime q ≥ 5 into an induction on m. With the same induction on the tree of 3 as the first text (found independently) this gives β(m) ≤ 3/8 for m ≤ 12 from the slice criterion and the step-down in characteristic 0 alone; for m ≥ 12 it uses the chain of section 16. It has the exact value β(8) = 39/128, by a case analysis of five structures, and bounds for 9 to 12 elements that are sharper than the first text's at 9, 10 and 12. It began by trying to refute the statement: a climbing search over 2,882,316 exact evaluations on moduli with up to five odd primes and 7 to 16 elements, and 838,729 more with 17 to 28 elements, met nothing above 3/8.

Whose it is. The question (Conjecture Q), the reduction to odd moduli through Corollary U, and the brief with its leads and data are mine; the three proofs are the three instances', each by its own account of what it took from the brief (lead 4, "one prime first", is carried out in all three: the induction on the tree of 3). The exact values β(5), β(6), β(7) are the first instance's and the third's; β(8) = 39/128 is the third instance's. That the bounds for seven and eight elements are attained on even moduli is from my runs. Proposition H, Lemma L3 and the cut-off, which the first and the third use, are credited in sections 14 and 16.

What I had wrong on the way. Shortly before the instances were started (my notes have it at 22:05 UTC; the instances were started at 22:17) I believed that the severe test of section 14 would give the quarter for every m ("at most 3/8 of the signings are not 3-good on an odd modulus"). My own runs, which ended at 22:07, refuted it: on Z_35 with seven elements 25/64 of the signings are not 3-good. So the severe test gives the quarter for five and six elements and no further; the theorem above is about bad signings, not about 3-good ones. And three numbers in the data of my brief were not the largest: for m = 10, 11, 12 the instances and the referees found larger shares of bad signings (73/256, 43/256, 539/2048), all below 3/8.

How it was checked. Each text had a referee of its own, a fresh instance briefed to assume the text wrong: to check every proof line by line, to compute every table again with a program written from the text's definitions and not from its code, and to try the theorem and its lemmas by all signings (lean-lift/even/quarter/brief-referee-one.md and the like; the reports are report-referee-one.md, -two.md, -three.md beside them, with second looks -2.md; the referees' programs are in referee-one/, referee-two/, referee-three/). By their reports: no gap in any of the three texts; no number different in any recomputation (the first text's recursion up to m = 240 and its tables; every printed number of the second, and at the second look its table prime by prime; the third's bounds, with a second program of the referee's for the powers of 3); and no failure in the tests by all signings (7,886,534 and 5,738,315 multisets by the first referee, 4,388,707 by the second, 23,586,326 by the third). They required five, six and four changes of wording, hypothesis and credit, none that breaks a statement; the authors made them, and each referee looked again and passed its text, asking then only for sentences about its own reading to be put right: each of the three found, at its second look, that something in its own first report had not been exact (a count, a rounded figure given as a bound, "no other printed number changes"); by their accounts none of it bears on a verdict. The third author added one remark with its proof after its referee's report, at the referee's suggestion (on the powers of 3 the largest share of bad signings of ten elements is exactly 145/512); the referee went through that proof case by case at its second look. The authors' last changes of wording, made after the second looks, nobody has read. Each referee, after its own verdict, compared the numbers that its text shares with another of the three: no contradiction; where two texts give different bounds, every example lies below both. Not to be taken for new, by its report: for ONE root of unity the bound C(m, ⌊m/2⌋)/2^m, which is at most 3/8 for m ≥ 3, is the classical bound of Erdős for the problem of Littlewood and Offord, and what Theorem Q adds is that the same 3/8 holds for all the n-th roots of an odd n at once; for full support on odd n the bound follows from formula (4.2) of Meckes's paper (section 12) by a sum that the search worked out, 0.3492 (I read the formula in the downloaded text and get the same sum); and three and four elements on odd n follow from the classical lists of vanishing sums of three and four unit complex numbers (for three elements section 14 says so already).
