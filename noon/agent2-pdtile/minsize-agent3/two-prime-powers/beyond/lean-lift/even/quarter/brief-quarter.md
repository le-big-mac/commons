# Brief: is at least a quarter of the signings always good? Prove it, prove the largest part you can, or refute it (27 November 2026, late evening)

## The setting

n ≥ 1, B a multiset of m elements of Z_n. A signing ε gives each element a sign ±1; its signed mask is S(X) = Σ_b ε_b X^b. The signing is **bad at d** (d | n, d = 1 included) if the cyclotomic polynomial Φ_d divides S (that is, S(ζ) = 0 for the roots of unity ζ of order d), **bad** if it is bad at some d | n, and **good** otherwise. Signs are independent and uniform; "share" means probability.

**Known, every n** (all refereed, the first also in Lean): a nonempty multiset has a good signing unless it is two elements whose difference is not divisible by the power of 2 in n. The share of good signings is at least 1/2 for m = 3, 1/4 for m = 4 and m = 5, and more than 0.0961 for every m ≥ 5 (better for 6 to 9: 3/16, more than 0.198, 9/64, more than 0.136). The bounds for m = 3 and 4 are attained.

**Corollary U (this evening; refereed; in Lean).** Write n = 2^k·n′ with n′ odd. For every multiset B on Z_n: the number of signings bad on Z_n is at most twice the number of signings of the reduction B mod n′ that are bad on Z_(n′). So with

  β(m) := the supremum, over all ODD n′ and all multisets of m elements of Z_(n′), of the share of bad signings,

every multiset of m elements on every Z_n has a share of good signings of at least 1 − 2·β(m).

## The question

> **Conjecture Q.** β(m) ≤ 3/8 for every m ≥ 3: on an odd modulus, at most 3/8 of the signings of a multiset of three or more elements are bad. (Then at least a quarter of the signings are good, for every multiset of three or more elements on every Z_n.)

What is known about β: β(3) = 1/4, β(4) = 3/8 (four elements: the signings whose signs add up to 0 are 3/8 of all, and on an odd modulus nothing else is bad), β(5) ≤ 3/8 (proved through the "severe test" below). For m = 6 I believe β(6) ≤ 3/8 follows the same way (lead 2), not yet written up. **Open: m ≥ 7.**

## Leads (thoughts of mine; unchecked unless I say otherwise; not instructions)

1. **A bound that is proved, and where it is too weak.** Write n′ = 3^a·n″ with 3 ∤ n″. By the step-down in characteristic 0 for the primes ≥ 5, then Lemma M, then Lemma L3: a signing bad on Z_(n′) is bad at some power of 3 (an event that depends only on B mod 3^a), or it is 3-bad at a live e′ > 1 with all prime factors ≥ 5, and the second kind has share at most W_m(no 3) (the sum of 2^(−μ(e*)) over e* > 1 with prime factors ≥ 5 and μ(e*) ≤ m − 1: 3/32 for m = 7, 8; 13/128 for 9, 10; 55/512 for 11, 12; less than 0.118569 always). So **β(m) ≤ t_m + W_m(no 3)**, where t_m is the largest share of signings bad at some power of 3 on some Z_(3^a). If the data for 3^a are the truth, t_m ≤ 3/16 for odd m ≥ 5, and Conjecture Q follows for every odd m. For even m it gives C(m, m/2)/2^m + (about 0.04) + W_m, which is below 3/8 from about m = 12 on but NOT for m = 8 and 10: there the count of the primes ≥ 5 must be done in characteristic 0, where parity makes it tiny (with eight elements no signing is bad at any power of 5 unless it is bad lower down: a full group needs five subclasses with equal sums, so sizes of one parity, and the elements left over cannot have zero sums).
2. **The severe test cannot do it alone.** "3-bad at e" (S(x) ∈ 3·Z[x], x of order e, 3 ∤ e) has share p_m ≈ 1/3 at e = 1 whatever the multiset. On Z_35 with seven elements the share of signings 3-bad at 1, 5 or 7 is 25/64 > 3/8 (`shares/three-good.out`), so the route of Corollary 3 stops at m = 6. (For m = 5, 6 it gives exactly 3/8: with six elements the only live e′ is a power of 5 with one live level, where 3-bad and not 3-bad at 1 has share at most 1/32, and p_6 + 1/32 = 3/8.)
3. **Characteristic 0 with the sizes of the classes.** Erdős's form of the Littlewood–Offord lemma (Kleitman's for complex numbers): for unit complex numbers u_1, …, u_r, any value is taken by Σ ε_i u_i for at most C(r, ⌊r/2⌋) of the 2^r signings. So a slice of a subclass with r elements takes a given value with share at most c_r, and a group with subclasses of sizes r_0 ≥ … ≥ r_(p−1) ≥ 1 is flat with share at most c_(r_0)·…·c_(r_(p−2)). For integer slices (d″ = 1) there is also parity: equal sums force sizes of one parity, a zero sum forces an even size. The union bound over ALL live d with only "2^(−μ(d))" is 0.8445, far too much; with sizes and parity each term is much smaller, but one must handle which levels can be live together with m elements, and the events at composite d.
4. **One prime first.** For n′ = q^a the events are: level 0 (the signs add up to 0), and for i ≥ 1 "every group at level i of the q-tree is flat" with integer slices. For i ≥ 2 the event is the intersection over the q classes modulo q of the same kind of event one level down, on disjoint sets of signs; so an induction on the tree is natural. Exact maxima T_q(m) for q = 3 are in the data above; a proof of "T_3(m) ≤ 3/16 for odd m ≥ 5" and of "bad at some 3^i and not at 1 has share at most 5/128 for even m" would already give Conjecture Q for every odd m and for even m ≥ 12 or so, by lead 1.
5. **A slicker road, if there is one.** Theorem T′ (twice) came from an involution on signings that depends only on the positions (negate the signs of half of every class): it maps "bad at level j" onto an event that sits inside "bad at level 0". For an odd prime there is no such involution by signs alone; but something of the kind may exist for the pair (bad at q, bad at 1), or an injection from the signings bad at some d > 1 and not at 1 into good signings. I have not found one.
6. **Refuting.** If Conjecture Q is false, the counterexample is likely a multiset with several small primes live at once and heavy classes arranged so that the events at different divisors are disjoint (that is how 25/64 arises for the severe test). In characteristic 0 I found nothing above 11/32 for m ≥ 5; I have not searched n′ with four primes, nor m > 12.
