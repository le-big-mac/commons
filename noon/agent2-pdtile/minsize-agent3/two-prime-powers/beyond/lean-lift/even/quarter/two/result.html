What is claimed.

Theorem (Conjecture Q). For every odd n, every m ≥ 3 and every multiset of m elements of Z_n, at most 3/8 of the 2^m signings are bad: β(m) ≤ 3/8 for every m ≥ 3. With Corollary U of union-twice.md: every multiset of three or more elements on every Z_n has a share of good signings of at least 1/4.

More precisely β(m) ≤ T2(m) for 3 ≤ m ≤ 46 (Theorem A; table in §5: 1/4, 3/8, 5/16, 3/8, 5/16, 3/8, 71/256, 47/128 for m = 3, …, 10; at most 5/16 for every odd m from 5 to 45; at most 47/128 for 9 ≤ m ≤ 46), and β(m) < 0.3625 for every m ≥ 43 (Theorem B). So β(m) ≤ 47/128 for every m ≥ 9, and every multiset of nine or more elements on every Z_n has a share of good signings of at least 17/64. For moduli that are powers of one prime the exact maxima are computed for m ≤ 16 (§4).

1. Setting, cells, fully live divisors

n is odd, B a multiset of m elements of Z_n, the signs ε_b are independent and uniform, S(X) = Σ ε_b X^b. For d | n put g = rad(d) (the product of the primes of d) and d_0 = d/g. A cell of d is a nonempty class of B modulo d_0. For an element b of the cell of c (0 ≤ c < d_0) put k_b = (b − c)/d_0 mod g.

Lemma 1 (cells). Let ζ be a root of unity of order d and ω = ζ^(d_0), of order g. Then ε is bad at d iff for every cell A_c := Σ_(b in the cell) ε_b ω^(k_b) = 0.

Proof. S(ζ) = Σ_c ζ^c·A_c with A_c ∈ Q(ω). The degree of Q(ζ) over Q(ω) is φ(d)/φ(g) = d_0, and ζ is a root of X^(d_0) − ω; so 1, ζ, …, ζ^(d_0 − 1) is a basis of Q(ζ) over Q(ω), and S(ζ) = 0 iff every A_c = 0. ∎

A cell seen through a prime p | d. Write g = p·g″ and ω = η·θ with η of order p and θ of order g″ (Chinese remainder theorem). The p classes of the cell are the sets of its elements with k_b ≡ t (mod p), t ∈ Z_p (they are the parts of the cell in the p subclasses of one group at level v_p(d) of the p-tree), and σ_t := Σ_(b in class t) ε_b θ^(k_b) ∈ Z[θ]. Then A_c = Σ_t η^t σ_t, and since 1 + X + … + X^(p−1) is the minimal polynomial of η over Q(θ):

(1) A_c = 0 iff σ_0 = σ_1 = … = σ_(p−1).

The cell is p-full if all its p classes are occupied. If it is not, some σ_t is an empty sum and (1) says: A_c = 0 iff every σ_t = 0. Call d > 1 fully live if for every prime p | d some cell of d is p-full.

Lemma 2 (descent). A bad signing is bad at 1 or at some fully live d > 1.

Proof. Let d be the least divisor at which ε is bad, d > 1, and p | d. Suppose no cell of d is p-full. Then, every cell having A_c = 0, every σ_t of every cell is 0. If p² ∤ d: d/p has the same d_0, the same cells and the root θ in place of ω, and the sum of a cell is Σ_b ε_b θ^(k_b) = Σ_t σ_t = 0. If p² | d: a cell of d/p (a class modulo d_0/p) is the union of the cells c_u = c′ + u·d_0/p of d, u ∈ Z_p; for b in c_u the exponent for d/p is u + p·k_b, and with a root ω′ of order g the sum of the cell is Σ_u ω′^u Σ_(b ∈ c_u) ε_b (ω′^p)^(k_b), where ω′^p has order g″, so each inner sum is Σ_t σ_t = 0 for the cell c_u (with θ = ω′^p; statement (1) does not depend on the choice of the roots). Either way ε is bad at d/p by Lemma 1, against the choice of d. ∎

If a cell of d is p-full, the group at level i = v_p(d) of the p-tree that contains it is full: level i is live for p in the sense of the brief. The rank j_p(d) ≥ 1 is the position of the level v_p(d) among the live levels of p, counted from the top.

2. What a cell costs

c_r = C(r, ⌊r/2⌋)/2^r; c_0 = 1, c_1 = c_2 = 1/2, c_3 = c_4 = 3/8; c_r does not increase with r; and c_a·c_b ≤ c_(a+b) (the sum of a + b signs takes the value x* + y, x, y* the most likely values of the two parts, with share at least c_a c_b).

Lemma 3 (Sperner 1928; Erdős 1945 for real numbers; used for complex numbers). Let u_1, …, u_r be nonzero complex numbers and β a complex number. At most C(r, ⌊r/2⌋) of the 2^r signings have Σ ε_i u_i = β.

Proof. Choose a real-linear λ: C → R with λ(u_i) ≠ 0 for all i (finitely many lines are to be avoided). Replacing u_i by −u_i permutes the signings; so let λ(u_i) > 0 for all i. If two signings with Σ ε_i u_i = β had sets of plus signs A ⊊ A′, applying λ to the difference would give 2·Σ_(i ∈ A′∖A) λ(u_i) = 0. So the sets of plus signs form an antichain, and Sperner's theorem (an antichain of subsets of an r-set has at most C(r, ⌊r/2⌋) members) gives the bound. ∎

Lemma 4 (one class). Let σ = Σ ε_b y_b be the sum of a class of r ≥ 1 elements, the y_b roots of unity of odd order dividing g″. Then - (a) P(σ = β) ≤ c_r for every β; - (b) P(σ = 0) ≤ z(r), where z(1) = 0, z(2) = 1/2, z(3) = 1/4, z(r) = c_r for r ≥ 4; and z(3) = 0 if 3 ∤ g″; - (c) P(σ = β) ≤ cnz(r) for every β ≠ 0, where cnz(1) = 1/2, cnz(2) = 1/4, cnz(r) = c_r for r ≥ 3; and if 3 ∤ g″ a class of two elements never has σ equal to a root of unity, of any order.

Proof. (a) is Lemma 3. Three complex numbers of absolute value 1 with sum 0 are the corners of an equilateral triangle, so the quotient of two of them is a cube root of unity of order 3; a quotient of two numbers ±y, y of odd order dividing g″, has an order dividing 2g″. Hence: (b) for r = 1 a root of unity is not 0; for r = 2, ±y_1 ± y_2 = 0 needs y_1 = y_2 (y_1 = −y_2 is impossible in odd order) and opposite signs, share at most 1/2; for r = 3 the three terms form a triangle, impossible if 3 ∤ g″, and otherwise changing one sign, or two, destroys the sum 0, so at most 2 of the 8 signings have it. (c) r = 1: one sign. r = 2: if y_1 = y_2 the values are 2y_1, 0, 0, −2y_1; if not, the four values ±y_1 ± y_2 are different from one another (equality of two of them would give y_1 = 0, y_2 = 0, y_1 = −y_2 or y_1 = y_2); so each value other than 0 has share at most 1/4. And ±y_1 ± y_2 = ρ, ρ a root of unity of any order, is a sum of three terms of absolute value 1 equal to 0, a triangle; this is impossible if 3 ∤ g″, because the quotient of the two elements of the class, ±y_1 and ±y_2, would itself have to be a primitive cube root of unity, and its order divides 2g″. ∎

Cell values. Fix a prime p; g″ is prime to p, so for p = 3 the case 3 ∤ g″ holds always. For p = 3 put z(3) = 0, for p ≥ 5 put z(3) = 1/4. For a cell with R elements seen through p, with classes of sizes r_t:

  • not p-full: P(A_c = 0) = ∏(occupied t) P(σ_t = 0) ≤ ∏ z(r_t) ≤ vnf_p(R) := z(R) for R ≥ 2, and 0 for R = 1. (One class: z(R). Several classes, each of at least two elements, so R ≥ 4: ∏ z(r_t) ≤ ∏ c(r_t) ≤ c_R = z(R).)
  • p-full: by independence of the classes and (1), with t_0 a class of least size, P(A_c = 0) = ∏t P(σ_t = 0) + Σ(β ≠ 0) ∏t P(σ_t = β) ≤ ∏t z(r_t) + ∏(t ≠ t_0) cnz(r_t), because Σ(β ≠ 0) ∏t P(σ_t = β) ≤ (∏(t ≠ t_0) cnz(r_t))·Σ_β P(σ_(t_0) = β). (This holds for any t_0; a smallest class is a valid choice, not always the best one.) If some class is a single element the first product is 0, and for p = 3 the second is 0 as soon as another class has exactly two elements (Lemma 4 (c): the common value is ± a root of unity). So P(A_c = 0) ≤ vf_p(R) := max(H1_p(R), Z_p(R) + H2_p(R)), where H1_p(R) is the largest ∏ cnz over p − 1 classes with R − 1 elements in all (for p = 3: no class of two), H2_p(R) the largest ∏ cnz over p − 1 classes of sizes ≥ s with R − s elements in all, over s ≥ 2, and Z_p(R) the largest ∏ z over p classes of at least two elements with R elements. vf_p(R) = 0 for R < p.

For G ≥ 1 let BF_p(G, m) be the largest value of ∏ (values of the cells) over all ways of writing m as a sum of G′ ≥ G numbers R_k ≥ 2, one of them (with R_k ≥ p) valued vf_p(R_k) and each other one max(vnf_p(R_k), vf_p(R_k)); 0 if there is no such way. BF_p(G, m) does not increase with G.

3. Composite divisors

Here "composite" means: with at least two different primes. The powers p^i of one prime, i ≥ 2 included, belong to §4.

Lemma 5 (how many cells). Let d > 1 be fully live. The number of cells of d is at least G(d) := max over the primes p | d of 1 + (j_p(d) − 1)(p − 1).

Proof. Every occupied class at depth v_p(d) − 1 of the p-tree contains a cell (d_0 is divisible by p^(v_p(d)−1)). The number N_k of occupied classes at depth k of the p-tree satisfies N_0 = 1, N_k ≥ N_(k−1), and N_k ≥ N_(k−1) + p − 1 if level k is live (the count of sign-four-result.md, Lemma L (4)); below the level v_p(d) there are j_p(d) − 1 live levels. ∎

Proposition 6. For a fully live d > 1 and every prime p | d: P(bad at d) ≤ BF_p(G(d), m).

Proof. By Lemma 1 the cells must all vanish, and they carry disjoint sets of signs: P(bad at d) is the product over the cells of P(A_c = 0). A cell of one element never vanishes. Seen through p, a p-full cell with R elements costs at most vf_p(R), another at most vnf_p(R); some cell is p-full (d is fully live); the numbers of elements add up to m; and there are at least G(d) cells (Lemma 5). ∎

A fully live composite d is determined by its primes and their ranks (j_p). So for every multiset of m elements

Σ over the fully live composite d of P(bad at d) ≤ COMP(m) := Σ over all families (j_p ≥ 1)(p ∈ P), P a set of at least two odd primes ≤ m, of min(p ∈ P) BF_p(max_(q ∈ P) (1 + (j_q − 1)(q − 1)), m),

a finite sum (BF_p(G, m) = 0 for G > 1 + (m − p)/2).

4. Powers of one prime

For d = p^i the cells are the groups at level i of the p-tree, θ = 1, and "bad at p^i" is "in every group at level i the p subclass sums of signs are equal". These events, and "bad at 1" (the signs add up to 0), depend only on the tree of sizes of B for p: the nested partition of the m elements into classes modulo p, p², ….

U_p(m) := the largest share, over all multisets of m elements on all Z_(p^a), of the signings that are bad at some p^i with i ≥ 1 and not bad at 1; T_p(m) the largest share of signings bad at some p^i, i ≥ 0.

Depth. By Lemma 2 of small-multisets.md (delete a digit at a level t at which no group is full; its proof shows that a signing bad at (i, d″) before is bad after at (i, d″) if i < t and at (i − 1, d″) if i ≥ t, with the same d″; the sum of all signs does not change, so for a signing that is not bad at 1 the new level is never 0), a signing that is bad at some p^i, i ≥ 1, and not at 1 stays so when a level that is not live is deleted. Delete one, look again (a deletion merges groups, so which levels are live can change), and repeat until every level is live; the depth drops each time. (small-multisets.md, §3, does it in one pass from the deepest level up.) What is left has only live levels, at most a = ⌊(m − 1)/(p − 1)⌋ of them. So U_p(m) and T_p(m) are maxima over the trees of depth a, classes that do not split at a level allowed (which includes every smaller depth). (The referee's tree program and ptree.py enumerate the same class of trees, so their agreement does not test the depth bound: that rests on the contraction argument alone.)

Computation (ptree.py). For a subtree with r elements and depth a the program computes its distribution: for each pair (s, L), s the sum of the signs and L the set of levels inside the subtree at which every group is flat, the number of signings. A leaf has the binomial numbers; a class with k ≤ p subtrees has s = Σ s_t and L = (the intersection of the L_t, shifted by one) plus the level 1 iff all s_t are equal and (k = p or the common value is 0). All subtrees of all sizes are enumerated, equal distributions merged. At the root the signings with s = 0 or L ≠ ∅ are bad; those with s ≠ 0 and L ≠ ∅ are counted for U_p(m). Proposition 7 (m ≥ 17; a bound, not exact). U_p(m) ≤ PP_p(m), where PP_p(m) is the following number (it is what lo_union2.py computes, by dynamic programming over the groups).

One group with R elements, integer class sums: enf(R) := c_R for even R ≥ 2 and 0 otherwise; ef_p(R) := the largest ∏(t ≠ t_0) c(r_t) over p classes of sizes r_t ≥ 1 of one parity with R elements in all, t_0 a class of least size (0 if there is no such choice); df_p(R) := the same with c′ in place of c, where c′_r = c_r for odd r and C(r, r/2 + 1)/2^r for even r.

G groups with R elements in all (r runs over the numbers of elements of the first group): - P_1 = max(enf, ef_p), P_G(R) = max_r P_1(r)·P_(G−1)(R − r); - E_1 = ef_p, E_G(R) = max_r max(ef_p(r)·P_(G−1)(R − r), P_1(r)·E_(G−1)(R − r)), the largest product of the values of G groups one of which is full; - V_1 = df_p, V_G(R) = max_r [df_p(r)·P_(G−1)(R − r) + P_1(r)·V_(G−1)(R − r)].

Ranks. last_p(j, m) := the least of the largest E_(G′)(m) and the largest V_(G′)(m) over G′ ≥ 1 + (j − 1)(p − 1); it is 0 for a rank needing more groups than m elements allow. nonlast_p(j, m) := the same with ef_p and df_p taken over classes of at least two elements. And

PP_p(m) := max over l ≥ 1 of (Σ_(j < l) nonlast_p(j, m) + last_p(l, m)).

Proof. A group that is not full is flat iff every class sum is 0; then every class is even, and the share is ∏ c ≤ c_R: at most enf(R). A full group is flat with share at most ∏(t ≠ t_0) c(r_t), and equal sums have one parity: at most ef_p(R); it is flat with a common value other than 0 with share at most df_p(R) (c′r is the largest share of a value other than 0). A signing that is bad at some power of p and not at 1 is bad at a live level: take the least i ≥ 1 with ε bad at p^i; ε is not bad at p^(i−1), so by the argument of Lemma 2 some cell of p^i (a group at level i) is p-full. So only live levels are counted, by their ranks. A signing bad at p^i and not at 1 is flat in every group at level i and has a common value β_D ≠ 0 in some full group D (if all were 0 the signs would add up to 0). So with the groups D_1, …, D_G at the level, its share is at most both ∏ e(D_k) and Σ_k δ(D_k) ∏(l ≠ k) e(D_l), where e = enf or ef_p and δ = df_p (δ = 0 for a group that is not full). The first is at most E_G(m), one group being full. The second is at most V_G(m): it is an upper bound for Σ_k δ(D_k) ∏(l ≠ k) e(D_l), as one sees by splitting off the first group, Σ_k δ(D_k) ∏(l ≠ k) e(D_l) = δ(D_1)·∏(l ≥ 2) e(D_l) + e(D_1)·Σ(k ≥ 2) δ(D_k) ∏_(l ≥ 2, l ≠ k) e(D_l), and by induction on G. At the level of rank j there are at least 1 + (j − 1)(p − 1) groups, one of them full; so the share of that level is at most last_p(j, m). If a class at depth i is a single element, no signing is bad at p^(i′) for any i′ > i (the element is alone in a group that is not full, and its sum is ±1 ≠ 0). So if l is the last rank at which some signing is bad, all classes at the levels of rank j < l have at least two elements, the shares of those levels are at most nonlast_p(j, m), and U_p(m) ≤ PP_p(m). ∎

5. Theorem A

Theorem A. For every multiset of m ≥ 3 elements on every odd modulus the share of bad signings is at most

T2(m) := [m even]·c_m + Σ over the odd primes p ≤ m of U_p(m) + COMP(m),

where U_p(m) may be replaced by PP_p(m). With the exact U_p(m) for m ≤ 16 and PP_p(m) for 17 ≤ m ≤ 46 (U_5 exact to m = 20), T2(m) ≤ 3/8 for every 3 ≤ m ≤ 46.

Proof. By Lemma 2 a bad signing is bad at 1 (share c_m for even m, 0 for odd m), or not bad at 1 and bad at a fully live prime power p^i, i ≥ 1 (share at most U_p(m), §4, for each prime p ≤ m; a prime p > m has no live level; the events at p^i depend only on B mod p^(v_p(n)), so U_p(m), defined on moduli that are powers of p, applies; and T_p(m) = [m even]·c_m + U_p(m), the share of level 0 being the same for every multiset), or bad at a fully live composite d (shares add up to at most COMP(m), §3). The values are computed by lo_union2.py with exact fractions (lo2_exact_46.out). ∎

6. Theorem B: every m ≥ 43 at once

Plain values, from Lemma 4 (a) alone: a cell that is not p-full vanishes with share at most c_R if it has R ≥ 2 elements (0 if R = 1), a p-full one with classes of sizes r_t with share at most ∏(t ≠ t_0) c(r_t). A configuration is a way of putting m elements into at least 1 + (j − 1)(p − 1) cells, each either "not full" with R ≥ 2 elements and value c_R, or "full" with p classes of sizes r_t ≥ 1 and value ∏(t ≠ t_0) c(r_t), at least one of them full; φ_p(j, m) := the largest product of the values over all configurations. For fully live d: P(bad at d) ≤ φ_p(j_p(d), m) for every p | d (the proof of Proposition 6 with the plain values), and P(bad at d) ≤ 2^(−j(p−1)) with j = j_p(d) (each cell costs at most 1/2, a full one at most 2^(−(p−1))).

Lemma 8 (removal). φ_p(j, m′) ≤ max(φ_p(j, M), 2^(−(M+p−1)/2)) for all m′ ≥ M.

Proof. Take a configuration with m + 1 elements. If some cell that is not full has at least three elements, or some class of a full cell at least two, remove one element there: the configuration stays one (the same cells, the same full ones); c_(R−1) ≥ c_R; and in a full cell "all but a smallest" is the product of the c's of all classes divided by the largest of them, the product grows by a factor ρ ≥ 1 and the largest c by at most ρ: the value does not decrease. If there is no such cell, every class of a full cell has one element and every other cell two; with K ≥ 1 full cells the value is 2^(−E), E = (p − 1)K + (m + 1 − pK)/2 ≥ (m + p − 1)/2. So φ_p(j, m + 1) ≤ max(φ_p(j, m), 2^(−(m+p−1)/2)); induction on m′, the second term decreasing. ∎

Theorem B. For every m ≥ M the share of bad signings on an odd modulus is at most

Univ(M, t) := c_M + Σ over the d* > 1 with μ(d) ≤ t of min_(p | d) max(φ_p(v_p(d*), M), 2^(−(M+p−1)/2)) + (W − W_t),

where d* = ∏ p^(j_p), μ(d) = max_p j_p·(p − 1), W_t is the sum of 2^(−μ(d)) over the odd d* > 1 with μ(d*) ≤ t and W < W_60 + 3.4·10^(−6) = 0.8444772 (sign-four-result.md, Lemma W and its tail). Univ(43, 30) < 0.36242 < 3/8.

Proof. Lemma 2; level 0 costs at most c_m ≤ c_M; a fully live d > 1 goes to d* = ∏ p^(j_p(d)), one to one; if μ(d) ≤ t its share is bounded by Lemma 8, otherwise by 2^(−μ(d)). tail.py (floating point; the margin is 0.0126): W_30 = 0.844370067, 294,911 values of d*, sum 0.242702, c_43 = 0.119604. The same with exact fractions (test_removal.py 31 60 43 30, a second programme for φ; the term 2^(−(M+p−1)/2) rounded up to 2^(−⌊(M+p−1)/2⌋), W_30 exact, the cited 3.4·10^(−6) as a fraction): Univ(43, 30) ≤ 0.363191 < 3/8. Certified after the referee's report, as it did: the same in fractions with 2^(−1/2) replaced by the larger 70710678119/10^11 (test_removal.py 31 60 43 30 cert, test_removal_cert.out) gives Univ(43, 30) < 0.3624131 (0.362413084 to nine places), so the number in the statement, and "β(m) < 0.3625" in the head, do not rest on floating point. ∎

Theorems A and B give the Theorem at the head: 3 ≤ m ≤ 46 by A, m ≥ 43 by B.
