The statement ("Corollary U")

n = 2^k·n′ with n′ odd. B a finite multiset of residues modulo n, with m elements; B′ its reduction modulo n′. A signing gives each element a sign ±1; the signed mask is S(X) = Σ_(b ∈ B) ± X^b. Call a signing bad on Z_n if S vanishes at some n-th root of unity (S is divisible by a cyclotomic polynomial Φ_d, d | n), and bad on Z_(n′) if, with the same signs, it vanishes at some n′-th root of unity. Then

#{signings bad on Z_n} ≤ 2·#{signings bad on Z_(n′)}.

(With uniform random signs: the probability of vanishing at some n-th root of unity is at most twice the probability of vanishing at some root of unity of odd order dividing n.) The proof is a reflection: for 1 ≤ j ≤ k, negating the signs of the elements whose residue modulo 2^j is ≥ 2^(j−1) maps "vanishes at some root of unity of order 2^j·e, e odd" one-to-one onto "for some odd e, every residue class modulo 2^(j−1) has its partial signed sum vanishing at the roots of order e", and these events are nested.

Other clothes for the same thing: - Circulant matrices. For B = all of Z_n, each residue once: a random n × n circulant matrix with entries ±1 is singular iff the signing is bad. The statement says: P(the ±1 circulant of order n = 2^k·n′ is singular) ≤ 2·P(its "folded" circulant of order n′ is singular), the folded circulant having as entries the sums of the entries over the residue classes modulo n′ (equivalently: the determinant factor ∏(d | n′) of the norms of S(ζ_d) vanishes). More generally for circulants with a prescribed zero pattern or repeated entries. - Group rings. For a random element S = Σ ± g^(b_i) of Z[C_n] with prescribed support: P(S is a zero divisor in Q[C_n]) ≤ 2·P(the image of S in Q[C(n′)] is a zero divisor), C_(n′) the quotient by the Sylow 2-subgroup. - Random polynomials. For a polynomial with coefficients ±1 on prescribed exponents (a Littlewood polynomial if the exponents are 0, …, n − 1): P(it has a common root with X^n − 1) ≤ 2·P(it has a common root with X^(n′) − 1). - For n a power of 2 this reads P(singular) ≤ 2·P(the sum of the signs is 0) = 2·C(m, m/2)/2^m.
