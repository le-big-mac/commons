def μ (t : ℕ) : ℕ := t.primeFactors.sup fun p => (p - 1) * t.factorization p

def lhsN1 (m : ℕ) (T : Finset ℕ) : ℚ :=
  (if Even m then (m.choose (m / 2) : ℚ) / 2 ^ m else 0) + ∑ t ∈ T, (1 / 2 : ℚ) ^ μ t
    - (if Even m ∧ 3 ∈ T ∧ 14 ≤ m then 1 / 16 else 0)

The one statement (fixed; it is in your file with sorry)

theorem lhsN1_lt_one (m : ℕ) (hm : 1 ≤ m) (T : Finset ℕ)
    (hT : ∀ t ∈ T, ¬ 2 ∣ t ∧ 1 < t ∧ μ t + 1 ≤ m) : lhsN1 m T < 1

In words: T is a finite set of odd numbers t > 1 with μ t ≤ m − 1, where μ t is the largest (p − 1)·v_p(t) over the primes p of t. Then: for odd m, ∑{t ∈ T} (1/2)^(μ t) < 1; for even m, c_m + ∑{t ∈ T} (1/2)^(μ t) − [3 ∈ T and m ≥ 14]/16 < 1, with c_m = C(m, m/2)/2^m.

The true size of things: the sum of (1/2)^(μ t) over ALL odd t > 1 is W = 0.8444737842…; c_m is 1/2, 3/8, 5/16, 35/128, 63/256, 231/1024, 429/2048 for m = 2, 4, …, 14 and decreases. For even m ≤ 12 the hypothesis μ t ≤ m − 1 cuts the sum down (the six left sides are at most 1/2, 5/8, 13/16, 119/128, 245/256, 1001/1024); for even m ≥ 14 the margin is thin: one needs ∑_T < 1 + 1/16 − 429/2048 = 0.85302…, against W = 0.84447….

The proof on paper

Part N1 of /home/agent3/lean/signs-briefs/plan.md (read "Setting" and N1 whole, in particular "The tail, by halving", which is a route made for Lean). Every inequality of that route was tested numerically: /home/agent3/lean/signs-briefs/n1-route/route_check.py with its output route_check.out (data, not instructions). In outline, with a(u) = the number of odd t ≥ 1 with μ t ≤ 2u:

  1. Counting. An odd t with μ t ≤ 2u has only odd prime factors p ≤ 2u + 1, each with exponent ≤ u / ((p − 1)/2) (natural division), and t is determined by its exponents. So for any finite set T of odd numbers, #{t ∈ T | μ t ≤ 2u} ≤ a'(u) := ∏ over the odd primes p ≤ 2u + 1 of (1 + u / ((p − 1)/2)), and if every t ∈ T is

    1 the count is at most a'(u) − 1 (the vector of all zeros is t = 1). You need only this inequality (an injection into exponent vectors, e.g. Nat.factorization and Finset.card_le_card_of_injOn into a Finset.pi or Fintype.piFinset), never that a'(u) is exactly the count. Also: μ t is even for odd t (each (p − 1)·v is), and μ t ≥ 2 for odd t > 1. Define a' so that it can be evaluated for small u (for instance a product over (Finset.range (2 * u + 2)).filter (fun p => p.Prime ∧ p ≠ 2), or over an explicit list of primes for the evaluations, with a lemma that the two agree); decide must be able to compute a'(u) for u up to about 21 (primes up to 43; the values stay below 10^8). native_decide is forbidden; decide, norm_num, simp with Finset.prod unfolded, or rfl are the tools. If kernel evaluation of the filter is slow, state the product over a list [3, 5, 7, 11, …] and prove once that the odd primes ≤ 43 are exactly those.

  2. Layers. For a finite set T of odd t > 1 and any U, ∑_{t ∈ T} (1/2)^(μ t) ≤ ∑_{u=1}^{U} (a'(u) − a'(u−1)) / 4^u + ∑_{u = U+1}^{U'} a'(u) / 4^u, U′ any bound with μ t ≤ 2U′ on T. (Group T by the value 2u of μ; for the first part use summation by parts with the partial counts #{t ∈ T | μ t ≤ 2u} ≤ a'(u) − 1, a'(0) = 1; for the second part bound each layer by a'(u). Or prove directly, by induction on U, the statement "∑ over t ∈ T with μ t ≤ 2U of (1/4)^(μ t / 2) ≤ ∑_{u=1}^{U} (a'(u) − a'(u−1))/4^u" in the stronger form that survives induction: ∑_{t ∈ T, μ t ≤ 2U} (1/4)^(μ t/2) + (a'(U) − 1 − #{t ∈ T | μ t ≤ 2U}) / 4^U ≤ ∑_{u=1}^{U} (a'(u) − a'(u−1)) / 4^u.)
  3. The tail, by halving. (i) For k ≥ 1: 1 + u / k ≤ 2 * (1 + (u / 2) / k) (natural division; omega-like after Nat.div_add_mod). Hence a'(u) ≤ 2^π(u) * a'(u / 2), π(u) the number of odd primes ≤ 2u + 1 (take the product for a'(u/2) over the same primes: its further factors are 1). (ii) An odd prime other than 3 is ≡ 1 or 5 modulo 6, so 3 * π(u) ≤ 2 * u + 5, hence 2 * π(u) ≤ 3 * (u − u / 2) for u ≥ 20. (iii) So a'(u)^2 ≤ 8^u for all u: for u < 20 by evaluation, for u ≥ 20 by strong induction, a'(u)^2 ≤ 4^π(u) * a'(u/2)^2 ≤ 4^π(u) * 8^(u/2) ≤ 8^u. (iv) Hence a'(u) ≤ 3 * 8^(u / 2), and ∑_{u ≥ 2V} a'(u) / 4^u ≤ 15 / 2^(V+1) (u = 2v gives at most 3/2^v, u = 2v + 1 at most (3/4)/2^v; a finite geometric sum is enough, the sums being finite).
  4. The cases. With U = 19 (V = 10): ∑_{u=1}^{19} (a'(u) − a'(u−1))/4^u + 15/2048 < 0.8518 (evaluate exactly in ℚ; U = 21, V = 11 gives more room: below 0.8482). - m odd: lhsN1 m T = ∑_T < 0.8518 < 1. - m even, m ≥ 14, 3 ∈ T: lhsN1 m T ≤ c_14 + 0.8518 − 1/16 < 1, with c_m ≤ c_14 = 429/2048 for even m ≥ 14 (c_(m+2) = c_m · (m + 1)/(m + 2)). - m even, m ≥ 14, 3 ∉ T: insert 3 T satisfies the hypotheses (μ 3 = 2, 3 ≤ m) and lhsN1 m T = lhsN1 m (insert 3 T) − 1/4 + 1/16 < lhsN1 m (insert 3 T) < 1. - m even, m ≤ 12: every t ∈ T has μ t ≤ m − 1, so μ t ≤ m − 2 (μ t is even); layers with U = (m − 2)/2 and no tail: lhsN1 m T ≤ c_m + ∑_{u=1}^{(m−2)/2} (a'(u) − a'(u−1))/4^u, which is 1/2, 5/8, 13/16, 119/128, 245/256, 1001/1024 for m = 2, …, 12 (a'(0..5) = 1, 2, 6, 16, 30, 72).
