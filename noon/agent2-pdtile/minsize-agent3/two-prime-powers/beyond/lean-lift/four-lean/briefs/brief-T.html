The one statement (fixed; it is in your file with sorry)

Pnq.card_badSet_three_pow_mul_le (S5): if 7 ≤ Fintype.card ι, live e 3 i = true and rank e 3 i = 1, then (badSet e (3^i)).card * 16 ≤ 3 * 2 ^ Fintype.card ι.

In words: m ≥ 7 elements; level i of the prime 3 is live (some group at level i has all its 3 classes modulo 3^i occupied); then at most 3/16 of all signings are bad at 3^i (the general bound S2 gives 1/4 here). live e 3 0 = false always, so i ≥ 1. Note that badSet e (3^i) has 3^i, not 3^i * 1; S0 and S2 (a) are stated with p^i * d, so you will rewrite with mul_one, taking d = 1 (¬ 3 ∣ 1) and η = 1 (IsPrimitiveRoot.one). With η = 1, classSum e ε 1 (3^i) r is the sum of the signs sg (ε k) over the elements k in the class r (as a complex number).

The proof on paper

Part S5 of /home/agent3/lean/signs-briefs/plan.md (read "Setting", S0, S2 and S5). Let N be the number of occupied classes modulo 3^(i−1), that is, of groups at level i that hold an element.

Case N ≥ 2. There is a pivot set J for level i with 3 elements: take two elements in two different classes of a full group (they leave the third class of that group free), and one element of another occupied group (a group has 3 classes and J has one element in it, so a class of it is free; every other group holds no element of J). Check IsPivot e 3 i J from its definition, then S2 (a) gives card * 2^3 ≤ 2^m, and card * 16 ≤ 2 * 2^m ≤ 3 * 2^m.

Case N = 1. All elements lie in one group, with c = e k % 3^(i-1) the same for all k, and that group is full: its three classes X_t = c + t * 3^(i-1), t = 0, 1, 2, hold all m ≥ 7 elements, each at least one. So one class, call it X, holds at least 3 elements a₁, a₂, a₃ (pigeonhole: 3 classes, 7 elements); let Y be another class, b an element of Y, and Z the third class. A signing bad at 3^i has equal sums on X, Y and Z (S0, with the c of the group and t, t′ ranging over 0, 1, 2). Now count: the map ε ↦ (ε restricted to the complement of {a₁, a₂, a₃, b}) sends the bad signings to at most 2^(m−4) values, and every fibre has at most 3 elements: given the signs outside {a₁, a₂, a₃, b}, the sum on Z is known; the sum on Y must equal it, which fixes the sign of b (the other elements of Y are outside the four); the sum on X must equal it too, which prescribes sg(ε a₁) + sg(ε a₂) + sg(ε a₃), and of the 8 sign patterns of three elements at most 3 have a given sum (the sums −3, −1, 1, 3 are taken by 1, 3, 3, 1 patterns). So card ≤ 3 * 2^(m−4), that is card * 16 ≤ 3 * 2^m. (In Lean it may be easier to bound the bad set by the set of signings with "sum on Y = sum on Z and sum on X = sum on Z", and to count that set through the equivalence (ι → Bool) ≃ ({a₁, a₂, a₃, b} → Bool) × (rest → Bool), or by fixing one coordinate at a time: for each fixed rest, at most 3 of the 16 patterns on the four.)

The hypothesis rank e 3 i = 1 is not needed by this proof; it is there because the assembly applies S5 only in that case. If you find the statement false, say so with the counterexample.
