Verdict

  • I found no statement that is false, vacuous, weaker than the plan means, or different from it in substance, and no definition that is not what is meant. No test disagrees with any of the fifteen: 0 violations of S0 to S5 on 12,400 families with every signing, N1 exact for every m (largest left side 1001/1024, at m = 12), 826,535,767 multisets on 36 odd moduli none without a good signing, 301,284,019 multisets on 72 values of N none without a lift along 2, 571 patterns P on 52 pairs (Z_N, Z_2N) with equal sets of sizes.
  • The parts fit. S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, N1 exactly as stated give A1 by the plan's argument; every hypothesis can be supplied where A1 uses it (checked numerically on 6,750 families, 196 of them using S5; the one-line steps also in Lean). exists_signs_odd with the finished exists_lift_two_aux gives exists_lift_two_all, and that gives sizes_eq_two_all: both derived in Lean in my copy.
  • The three final statements against the paper. exists_signs_odd is Theorem 4's existence statement, clause for clause (not its fractions of good signings, which no Lean statement contains). exists_lift_two_all with two finished theorems is the conclusion of Lemma "lifting along 2" as the paper words it, for every N (derived in Lean). sizes_eq_two_all is the paper's sentence on sizes for s = 2 only; "Z_{Ns} for every s ≥ 1" is not among the fifteen. It follows from sizes_eq_two_all and the finished Lift.sizes_eq (25 lines, compiled in my copy), and I see that a sixteenth statement for it (sizes_eq_mul_all, brief M) was fixed while I worked; I did not audit that one.
  • Remarks, none a defect: S5's 7 ≤ m could be 4 ≤ m (the inequality fails only for m = 3); hrank in S5 and hlive in S2 (b), S2, S5 are not needed for truth; the constant 3/16 of S5 is not attained for m ≥ 7 (the largest share is 5/32); the plan's "thin" margin for even m ≥ 14 is the margin of its bounds (0.0086, and 0.0012 on the route for Lean), the exact margin there is 0.0366 and the tightest case of N1 is m = 12 (23/1024); the paper's numbers 5/32 and 0.961 for even m ≥ 14 are not what the Lean statements contain (S5 has 3/16, N1 has "< 1").

1. The ten definitions

Printed with pp.explicit in A1_print.out. Besides the standard algebraic instances on ℕ, ℤ, ℚ, ℂ and ℤ[X], the instance arguments that carry data are Fintype ι, ZMod.fintype and decidability instances; NeZero, Fact, Nonempty are propositions.

All values that CheckSigns.lean expects in its comments are right (recomputed, a0_checksigns.out).

Natural-number subtraction and division. i - 1 occurs only as p ^ (i - 1); S0, S1, S2 (a), S2 (b), S2 assume 1 ≤ i, and in S5 live e 3 i = true forces i ≥ 1. For i = 0 the definitions give: live false, rank 0, and a pivot set is any set with at most one element. p - 1 is exact (p prime, or a prime factor in μ). m / 2 is used only under Even m. c % p ^ (i - 1) turns any c into the index of a group (for i = 1 it is 0). d = 0 never occurs: ¬ p ∣ d gives d ≠ 0, and the other moduli are 3 ^ i or divisors of a nonzero number.

2. The fifteen statements

In all of them ι : Type u is any finite type (any universe), e : ι → ℕ any exponents (not reduced), ε : ι → Bool, m = Fintype.card ι, and p is any prime, 2 included, unless said.

S0 classSum_eq_of_badAt. p prime, 1 ≤ i, ¬ p ∣ d, η a complex number with IsPrimitiveRoot η d, ε bad at p^i * d; then for all natural c, t, t′ with t, t′ < p: classSum e ε η (p^i) (c % p^(i-1) + t * p^(i-1)) = the same with t′. Plan: the same (d″ ≥ 1 is not a hypothesis, it follows from ¬ p ∣ d). For d = 1, η = 1 and the class sums are integers. False without ¬ p ∣ d (e = (0, 3, 6), all +, p = 3, i = 1, d = 3: sums 3, 0, 0). Tested: 99,425 instances (family, modulus p^i·d, p), 28,910 with p = 2; 16,827 have a bad signing, 1,545,992 bad signings in all, each checked on every group; 7,409 of the instances with a bad signing have d > 1 and 969 a d with two or more primes; levels i = 1 … 6 for p = 2, 1 … 5 for p = 3, 1 … 3 for p = 5, 7, primes up to 13. Class sums in ℤ[X]/(Φ_d), which decides equality in ℂ for every primitive root. 0 violations; the converse (flat on every group ⟹ bad), which is not a statement, also held everywhere.

S1 badAt_of_not_live. p prime, 1 ≤ i, ¬ p ∣ d, ε bad at p^i * d, live e p i = false; then ε is bad at p^(i-1) * d. Plan: the same. False without ¬ p ∣ d (same example: bad at 9, not at 3) and without hlive ((0, 1, 2), all +: bad at 3, not at 1). Tested: 50,088 instances, 6,104 with a bad signing (1,562 with p = 2, 684 with d of two or more primes, levels up to 6). 0 violations.

S2 (a) card_badSet_mul_two_pow_card_le. p prime, 1 ≤ i, ¬ p ∣ d, J a pivot set for (p, i); then (badSet e (p^i * d)).card * 2^J.card ≤ 2^m. Plan: "at most 2^(m − |J|)", the same without subtraction. Tested with the largest pivot set (which implies it for every pivot set): 99,425 instances, equality in 6,216. 0 violations.

S4 badAt_one_iff, card_badSet_one. BadAt e ε 1 ↔ ∑ k, sg (ε k) = 0; and (badSet e 1).card is m.choose (m / 2) if m is even, 0 if odd. Plan: the same, in one part. Both hold for empty ι (1 = C(0, 0)). The first half I proved in Lean; both tested on all 12,400 families.

Occupied classes rank_lt_card_image. ι nonempty, p prime, any j (0 included): (p - 1) * rank e p j + 1 ≤ (univ.image fun k => e k % p^j).card. Plan: N_j ≥ 1 + (p − 1)·rank p j, inside the proofs of S2 and S3. Tested: 310,236 cases (family, p, j ≤ 5), equality in 134,467. False for empty ι (1 ≤ 0).

S2 (b) exists_isPivot. p prime, 1 ≤ i, live e p i = true; then some J : Finset ι is a pivot set with (p - 1) * rank e p i ≤ J.card. Plan: step (b) of the proof of S2. Tested: 49,337 live levels (21,474 with p = 2), equality in 46,032. 0 violations.

S2 card_badSet_mul_two_pow_rank_le. p prime, 1 ≤ i, ¬ p ∣ d, level i live; then (badSet e (p^i * d)).card * 2^((p - 1) * rank e p i) ≤ 2^m. Plan: the same. Tested: 49,337 instances, 10,723 with a bad signing, 6,850 with d of two or more primes; equality in 4,006, at levels 1 to 6 (3,500, 400, 85, 15, 4, 2). 0 violations. It is S2 (a) with S2 (b) (one line, compiled).

S3 rank_lt_card. ι nonempty, p prime, any i: (p - 1) * rank e p i + 1 ≤ m. Plan: the same. Tested with the occupied classes (310,236 cases, equality in 40,240); it follows from them in one line (compiled). False for empty ι.

S5 card_badSet_three_pow_mul_le. 7 ≤ m, live e 3 i = true, rank e 3 i = 1; then (badSet e (3^i)).card * 16 ≤ 3 * 2^m. The modulus is 3 ^ i, not 3 ^ i * 1. Plan: the same. Tested: 2,386 instances in the random families, 1,032 with one occupied group (every triple of class sizes, m = 7 … 14, levels 1, 2, 3) and 2,557 with two or more occupied groups, all signings; the closed count for one group for every triple up to m = 150. 0 violations. The largest share of bad signings for m ≥ 7 is 5/32 (m = 7, sizes 5, 1, 1), for m ≥ 14 it is 0.105; with two or more groups never above 1/8. In these tests equality occurs only at m = 5 (sizes 3, 1, 1), outside the statement, and the inequality fails only at m = 3.

N1 lhsN1_lt_one. m ≥ 1, T a finite set of naturals t with ¬ 2 ∣ t, 1 < t, μ t + 1 ≤ m; then lhsN1 m T < 1 in ℚ. Plan: the same, with μ t ≤ m − 1 written without subtraction and the two parities in one expression. The left side is largest at T = Tmax(m), all odd t > 1 with μ t ≤ m − 1 (a finite set; adding an element never lowers it: the element 3 adds 1/4, or 1/4 − 1/16). Tested exactly: Tmax(m) enumerated for m ≤ 34 (528,767 elements at m = 33, 34; μ of every element recomputed; against a sieve of all odd t < 10^6), the counting formula for m ≤ 2000, and beyond that W < 0.8444737843 with a rigorous tail (a(u)² ≤ 8^u, checked for u ≤ 3000 with the three steps of its induction). Values at Tmax: 1/2, 5/8, 13/16, 119/128, 245/256, 1001/1024 for m = 2, …, 12 (the plan's six; the first 14 values also from Lean's own #eval); 1973/2048 = 0.9634 at m = 14, then decreasing; odd m below W. So no allowed T reaches 1; the least margin is 23/1024 at m = 12. The if is the right way round: without the −1/16 the statement would be false for m = 14, 16, …, 24 (Tmax), and A1 can afford it exactly when 3 ∈ T and m ≥ 14 is even, through S5. hm is needed: lhsN1 0 ∅ = 1 (Lean's #eval).

A1 exists_not_badAt. n with ¬ 2 ∣ n (so n ≠ 0), ι finite and nonempty, any e; then some ε is bad at no d ∣ n (d = 1 and d = n included). Plan: the same. False for empty ι and for n = 0 (both proved in Lean). Tests: with exists_signs_odd.

exists_signs_odd. n ≠ 0 ([NeZero n], also implied by hn), ¬ 2 ∣ n, w : ZMod n → ℕ with some w x ≠ 0; then there are u, v with u x + v x = w x for all x and no cyclotomic d ℤ, d ∣ n, dividing maskW u - maskW v. As text it is the audited Lift.exists_signs with p ^ a * q ^ b replaced by n and the hypotheses on p, q by hn (a5_textdiff.out), and equal to the plan's block; Lift.exists_signs is an instance of it (compiled). Against Theorem 4: the same statement (a signing is a splitting; d = 1 included; n = 1 allowed). Theorem 4's "more precisely" (the fractions of good signings) is not in it. Tested: every multiset containing 0 (every multiset has such a translate) of size up to S on 36 odd moduli from 1 to 3465, 826,535,767 multisets: none without a good signing; "all +" fails for 30,719,538; never more than 6 signings tried. S = 40, 30, 20, 16, 22 for n = 1, 3, 5, 7, 9; 14 for n = 15; 11 for 21; 10 for 27; 8 for 45; 7 for 63; 6 for 75, 81, 105; 5 for 135, 165, 195; 4 for 225, 231, 315, 525; 3 for 735, 945, 1155, 1365, 3465. Eleven moduli with three or more primes (105 … 3465): 258,718,840 multisets. Even sizes from 12 on: n ≤ 15 only (33,350,320 multisets). Control, even n = 2, 4, 6, 10, 12, 30: 36 multisets have no good signing ({0, 1} on each), so hn is needed.

exists_lift_two_all. N ≠ 0, w a nonempty multiset on ZMod N; then some w' : ZMod (N * 2) → ℕ has Lift.reduce w' = w and no cyclotomic d ℤ ∣ maskW w' for d ∣ N·2 with d ∤ N. As text it is the audited Lift.exists_lift_two without its hypotheses on p, q, k, a, b, and equal to the plan's block. Against the paper's Lemma "lifting along 2": "the same number of elements" and "for d ∣ N: Φ_d ∣ A′ iff Φ_d ∣ A" are not in the statement; they follow by the finished sum_reduce and cyclotomic_dvd_reduce_iff (paper_lift2_all in A3_sem.lean is the paper's wording for every N, compiled). Tested literally (each element goes to x or x + N): 72 values of N, 301,284,019 multisets, none without a lift; "all low" fails for 44,298,451. N = 1, 2, 4, …, 256 (32,838,852 multisets; there "all low" always works); every N ≤ 30; 16 values whose odd part has three or more primes (105, 165, 195, 210, 231, 315, 330, 390, 420, 462, 525, 630, 840, 945, 1155, 2310; 237,435,760 multisets; sizes up to 6 for 105, 5 for 210, 4 for 165, 195, 231, 315, 330, 420, 3 for 390, 462, 525, 630, 840, 945, 1155, 2 for 2310).

sizes_eq_two_all. N ≠ 0, P : Finset ℕ with every member dividing N; the set of s > 0 for which some multiset on ZMod (N * 2) of size s has, for every d ∣ N·2 with d ≠ 1, Φ_d ∣ mask ↔ d ∈ P, equals the same set for ZMod N and d ∣ N. As text it is the audited Lift.sizes_eq_two without its hypotheses on p, q, k, a, b. P may contain 1 (no effect, d = 1 is skipped on both sides); the empty multiset is kept out by 0 < s. Against the paper: this is "the sizes are the same on Z_N and on Z_{Ns}" for s = 2 only. Tested: complete census of both groups up to size S for 52 pairs (N = 1 … 30, 32, …, 1155; S from 30 down to 3; for N = 105, 165, 195, 210, 231 S = 4, for 315, 420, 1155 S = 3): 571 sets P with a nonempty side, the two sets equal for every one (881 patterns on the larger group contain a divisor that does not divide N and belong to no P).

3. Do the parts fit?

  • A1 from the parts (plan, part A1; t5_fit.py). For 6,750 families on 27 odd moduli (m = 1 … 16, 750 of them with 13 to 16 elements), with every signing: (i) every signing bad somewhere is bad at 1 or at some d ∈ D; (ii) d ↦ d♭ is injective on D, every d♭ is odd, > 1, with μ d♭ + 1 ≤ m, and #bad(d)·2^μ(d♭) ≤ 2^m; where d♭ = 3 and m ≥ 14 is even (196 times) d is 3^i with level i live and rank 1, and #bad(d)·16 ≤ 3·2^m; (iii) the number of bad signings is at most 2^m·lhsN1 m T < 2^m. 9,423 levels d ∈ D* in all; 0 violations.
  • Each hypothesis can be supplied at its place of use: S1 and S2 are applied to d = p^i·d″ with i = v_p(d) ≥ 1 and p ∤ d″; S5 to the one d with d♭ = 3, where rank e 3 i = 1 and live hold by the definition of D* and ♭, and 7 ≤ m by 14 ≤ m; N1's μ t + 1 ≤ m is S3 for the prime that gives μ(d♭), its 1 ≤ m is Nonempty ι.
  • In Lean, in my copy (A3_sem.lean; all compile, with sorryAx only through the skeleton): S2 from S2 (a) and S2 (b); S3 from the occupied classes; the step of (i) (bad at d, level v_p(d) of p not live ⟹ bad at d/p) from S1; exists_lift_two_all from exists_signs_odd and the finished exists_lift_two_aux; sizes_eq_two_all from exists_lift_two_all (the proof of sizes_eq_two); the paper's wording of the lemma on lifts; the sizes for every s ≥ 1 from sizes_eq_two_all and Lift.sizes_eq.
  • exists_signs_odd from A1 I checked on paper only (ι = Σ x, Fin (w x), e ⟨x, _⟩ = x.val; the signed mask is maskW u - maskW v with u, v the numbers of + and −): I see no gap.

4. Vacuity and strength

  • No hypothesis is contradictory. Each of the fifteen is applied in Lean to data that satisfy its hypotheses (A3_sem.lean: a signing bad at 3; a bad signing at a level that is not live; a pivot set; a live level; seven elements with level 1 of 3 live and rank 1; m = 3, T = {3}; n = 105 and n = 1; N = 210, 8, 1; N = 315, 16).
  • No conclusion is trivially satisfiable. "All +" fails for 30.7 million multisets and "all low" for 44.3 million; S0, S1, S2 were tested on thousands of instances that have bad signings, with equality in S2.
  • [Nonempty ι]: needed in the occupied classes, S3 and A1 (each false for empty ι); not assumed elsewhere and not needed there. hm in N1: needed. hm in S5: as far as tested 4 ≤ m would do (2,039 random instances with m < 7 and 60 with one occupied group and 3 ≤ m ≤ 6: the inequality fails only for m = 3). hlive in S1: needed. hlive in S2 (b), S2, S5 and hrank in S5: not needed for truth as far as tested (50,088 levels that are not live, 361 and 1,040 instances for S5: no failure); harmless, A1 has them. ¬ p ∣ d: needed in S0 and S1; for S2 I found no failure without it (7.9 million small instances). hw, hP: needed (the empty multiset; N = 1, P = {2}). [NeZero n]: implied by hn, needed for the type.
  • Excluded that the paper claims: nothing in the three final statements themselves. Not contained in them: the fractions of Theorem 4, and "every s ≥ 1" (see the verdict).

5. Other things worth knowing before trusting "proved in Lean"

  1. While I worked, ~/lean/signs-check-full.out, signs-all-axioms.out and a sixteenth statement (sizes_eq_mul_all, signs-briefs/brief-M.md) appeared. I read them and used none of them. By that log the seven files are assembled, the fifteen print as in the skeleton and depend on the three standard axioms. That prover A could assemble A1 from the parts as stated is, by that account, Lean's own confirmation of §3.
  2. A4_dump.lean prints a structural hash of each of the ten definitions (type and value) and fifteen statements as elaborated in the skeleton copy (A4_dump.out). Run in the finished project it should print the same lines. I did not run it there. maskW and Lift.reduce have the hashes of the earlier audit.
  3. The Lean proof is not the paper's: no digit is deleted, the level d = 3 costs 3/16 (paper: 5/32) and N1 says only "< 1" (paper: "below 0.961"). The paper's paragraph should not let those numbers pass as formalised.
  4. In sizes_eq_two_all the bound variable P hides the definition Pnq.P; the explicit print shows the finite set, as meant. μ is Pnq.μ everywhere.
  5. Values by #eval rest on Lean's compiler, not the kernel; kernel checks are the 11 decide examples, μ 3 = 2 and the reading lemmas.
  6. The plan's steps I recomputed: the partial sums 1/4, …, 209/256; A(s) up to s = 40; "below 0.8518" for U = 19 (0.851788) against 0.853027; halving step (2) needs u ≥ 20 as stated (it fails for u = 2, 6). In what I recomputed and read of the plan I found no error.

7. How the audit was run

  • Every Python program through ~/bin/cap2g.sh (with -I), every Lean job through lean1.sh in my copy (seven jobs, 5 to 15 seconds each); no program hit the 2 GB cap; no network; nothing posted; nothing is running now. No file under Pnq/, Pqr/, P2q2/ of my copy is newer than the copy; no lake build, update, clean, cache.
  • Departures, all small. (a) About five commands of a second or less (two text edits of my own scripts, the text comparison twice, one test run of t1_parts.py) ran while two of my computations were running, so for a moment there were three processes. (b) Shell tools for reading outputs (tail, grep, awk, cp, sha256sum) were run directly. (c) The searches were run as chains of many short runs (the two A1 chains took about 3 and 12 minutes in all); the longest single computations were the search without hd (t1c_hd.py, 4 minutes, pure Python, longer than I meant), the lifts for N = 105 with 6 elements (192 s) and N = 210 with 5 (139 s), and A1 for n = 105 with 6 elements (128 s).
  • The first runs of t1_parts.py (two batches) were repeated with the final program, which has more moduli and counters; the main A1 chain was run twice (the second time after a correction to the counter of signings tried, which does not affect a result); the outputs kept are the later ones.

Verdict

  • It says what is meant, and I found nothing false, vacuous or weaker in it. As Lean has it, it is Lift.sizes_eq_odd with the argument Odd s removed: the same expression, not only the same text. It is the paper's sentence as worded, in the strong reading (on Z_{Ns} the pattern is exact among all d > 1 dividing Ns).
  • It has no content beyond the fifteenth statement and a finished theorem: it follows from sizes_eq_two_all and Lift.sizes_eq by induction on s (25 lines, compiled; the derived theorem has the same expression as its type). So it is true if those are.
  • No test disagrees: 146 pairs (N, s), 121 of them with s even and not 2, complete census of both groups: 1,214 sets P, the two sets of sizes equal for every one. And 160 pairs (N, s), 106,922,459 multisets on Z_N: every one has a lift to Z_{Ns} of the same size with no new cyclotomic divisor.

1. What it says

For every natural N ≠ 0 (implicit; [NeZero N]), every natural s ≠ 0 (explicit; [NeZero s]) and every finite set P of natural numbers each of which divides N (hP): the set of t for which some w' : ZMod (N * s) → ℕ has ∑ y, w' y = t, 0 < t, and for every natural d with d ∣ N·s and d ≠ 1, cyclotomic d ℤ ∣ maskW w' ↔ d ∈ P, is equal (as a set of natural numbers) to the set of t for which some w : ZMod N → ℕ has ∑ x, w x = t, 0 < t, and for every d ∣ N with d ≠ 1, cyclotomic d ℤ ∣ maskW w ↔ d ∈ P.

  • No subtraction and no division of natural numbers. The group is written ZMod (N * s); its NeZero (N * s) is instNeZeroNatHMul N s (a proposition). d = 0 divides neither order (proved); d = 1 is skipped on both sides; d = N·s and d = N are included.
  • Against Lift.sizes_eq_odd as elaborated: its fifth argument is Odd s, the rest does not mention it, and with it removed the type is the same expression as the type of sizes_eq_mul_all (same hash, same explicit print; checked by a small program in Lean, A5_mul.out). As text the two differ only by the name and (hs : Odd s); with s replaced by 2 the text is that of sizes_eq_two_all. Lift.sizes_eq_odd, sizes_eq_two_all and Lift.sizes_eq_mul are instances of it (three examples, compiled). The hash of sizes_eq_odd is the one of the earlier audit.
  • Against the paper's sentence ("the sizes of the nonempty multisets with exactly a given pattern P of divisors of N are the same on Z_N and on Z_{Ns} for every N and every s ≥ 1"): the same. "Nonempty" is 0 < t; "sizes" are the numbers of elements ∑ w; "the same" is equality of the two sets; N ≥ 1 and s ≥ 1 are the two NeZero. "Exactly the pattern P" on Z_{Ns} is taken among all d ≠ 1 dividing N·s, as in the wording of the Corollary; for P a set of divisors of N that is: exactly P among the divisors of N, and no Φ_d with d ∣ N·s, d ∤ N (proved, pattern_iff). P may contain 1, which changes nothing (proved, erase_one); the paper's patterns are sets of divisors > 1.
  • Fingerprint as elaborated in my copy: type hash 437138964, explicit print 3022 characters.
  • The fixed statement with its comment stands character for character, once, in signs-M/Pnq/SignMul.lean and in signs-all/Pnq/SignMul.lean as they were when I looked (50 lines, no line with sorry, admit, axiom, native_decide). I read nothing else there.

2. Is it true?

  • By derivation. AuditMul.derived in A5_mul.lean proves the same statement from sizes_eq_two_all (for the factors 2 of s) and the finished Lift.sizes_eq (for the odd prime factors), with AllAux.sizes_congr for N * (r * s') = N * s' * r; it compiles, and its only non-standard axiom is the sorry of sizes_eq_two_all in my copy.
  • Census (t6_mul.py; every multiset containing 0 up to size S on Z_N and on Z_{Ns}, exact divisibility): 146 pairs, N in 1 … 10, 12, 15, 16, 18, 20, 21, 24, 25, 27, 30, 36, 45, 60, 105, 210 and s in 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 16, 18, 24; 9,452,498 and 268,967,521 multisets; 1,214 sets P with a nonempty side, the two sets of sizes equal for every one (5,024 patterns on the larger groups contain a divisor that does not divide N and belong to no P). With s even and not 2: 121 pairs, 850 sets P; 57 of these pairs have N not of the form p^n q (438 sets P); 5 have N = 105 or 210 (33 sets P; S = 4 for (105, 4), 3 for (105, 6), (105, 8), (210, 4), 2 for (105, 12)). S is 12 down to 7 for N ≤ 9 with s = 4, 5 for (30, 4) and (36, 4), 4 for (30; 6, 8, 12), 3 for N = 60.
  • Lifts (t6b_liftmul.py; the fact behind the statement, at every size up to the bound and not only as sets of sizes): for every multiset on Z_N a multiset on Z_{Ns} of the same size that reduces to it and has no Φ_d with d ∣ N·s, d ∤ N; all s^(m−1) choices tried before giving up. 160 pairs, N up to 420, s in 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 16, 18, 24; 106,922,459 multisets, none without such a lift; "all low" fails for 5,951,045; at most 35 choices tried. With s even and not 2: 140 pairs, 98,025,663 multisets; 76 of these pairs with N not of the form p^n q (77,633,123 multisets); 24 with the odd part of N of three primes (N = 105, 210, 315, 330, 420; 21,022,824 multisets; sizes up to 5 for N = 105 with s = 4, 6, 8, up to 4 for N = 105 with s = 10, 12 and N = 210 with s = 4, 6, up to 3 for the rest, 2 for (420, 12)); sizes up to 8 for N = 30 with s = 4, 6, 8.

3. Vacuity and strength

  • No hypothesis is contradictory: applied in Lean to N = 105 with s = 12, N = 36 with s = 4, N = 1 with s = 8, any N with s = 1, N = 210 with s = 6 and P = ∅, N = 30 with s = 4 and P = {1, 2, 15}.
  • Not trivial: in the census every one of the 1,214 sets P is nonempty on both sides, and "all low" fails for 5,951,045 multisets.
  • [NeZero s] and [NeZero N]: they say s ≥ 1 and N ≥ 1 and are needed for the statement to be written (the sums run over a finite group).
  • hP: needed. N = 1, s = 2, P = {2}: sizes 2, 4, 6, … on Z_2 against every size on Z_1; N = 3, s = 4, P = {2}; N = 15, s = 4, P = {2, 4} (t6c_hp.out). A member of P that divides neither N nor N·s would do no harm; hP excludes it too.
  • P containing 1: allowed, no effect. P that is the pattern of no multiset: both sets empty.
  • 0 < t: needed. Without it, for N = 1, s = 2, P = ∅ the empty multiset would be on the right and not on the left.
  • s = 1: allowed; the two groups are the same and the statement says nothing (true by rewriting N * 1 = N).
  • Stronger than each audited statement on sizes (they are instances), and no stronger than sizes_eq_two_all with Lift.sizes_eq together.

4. Does it give the paper's sentence?

Yes, by itself: it is that sentence (§1), for every N ≥ 1, every s ≥ 1 and every set P of divisors of N, with the pattern on Z_{Ns} exact among all divisors d > 1 of Ns. Nothing finished has to be added; equal sets of sizes give in particular equal least sizes. The paper's next sentence ("only for odd s … is formalised") is then out of date.
