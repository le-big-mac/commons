Why the clause is true: - At least μ(d) + 1. If d divides n♭, then for each prime p of d the p-tree of B♭ has at least v_p(d) levels, each with a full group. By the count of §3 of the note that takes at least 1 + v_p(d)(p − 1) elements, so m ≥ μ(d) + 1. - Attained. With m = μ(d) + 1 elements on Z_d, give each prime the residues 0 and j·p^t (1 ≤ j < p, 0 ≤ t < v_p(d)), spread over the elements by the Chinese remainder theorem. Every level of every tree is then full and n♭ = d. This is the remark in §3 of the note that the bound is attained for all primes at once. - In my outputs. The exhaustive runs show the first size with n♭ = n is 5 on Z_9, Z_15 and Z_45, and none up to 6 elements on Z_27 and Z_25 (where it would be 7 and 9).

One caveat: the equality is for "d is a level after contraction", meaning d divides n♭. That is how I read lead 1's phrase. If the lead meant more by "full groups in the right slices", the clause describes this proof's weight, not that stronger demand.
