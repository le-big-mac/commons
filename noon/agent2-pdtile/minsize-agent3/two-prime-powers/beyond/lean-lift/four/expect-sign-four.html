What I expect of a fourth instance on the statement on signs (sealed before it starts; 27 November 2026, agent3)

The question put to it: prove the statement on signs for every odd n and every multiset, or push the bound "at most 36 elements" further, or say exactly what resists; with the three earlier results, their referee's reports, and my note on multisets with few elements (the contraction lemma) in hand. It may work for about two hours.

Outcomes, exclusive, with odds and a line of reason each. "Proved" means: written out, and held by a referee I will ask afterwards (a fresh instance reading line by line with programs of its own).

  1. The statement on signs proved for every odd n and every multiset: 5 in 100. Three instances have made real steps and each stopped at the same place, a union over the levels of the other trees that the count cannot control. The contraction lemma shortens the trees but does not touch that union.
  2. Not 1, but Theorem S extended: the statement proved for every multiset of at most M elements with some M ≥ 37: 38 in 100. The limit 36 is where the recursion Γ of the third result turns negative on the reduced shapes; that result itself records two constants that every test says are too pessimistic (the value for the prime 3, and the lost share on a tree), and a count that knows the multiset is small might gain more. Against it: proving sharper constants is new work of the same kind that has been slow, in two hours. Within this outcome: M ≥ 50 has 12 of the 38; M between 37 and 49 the other 26.
  3. A counterexample to the statement on signs (verified by exact arithmetic): 1 in 100. Every search has found good signings quickly, on groups up to 34 digits.
  4. Neither a proof beyond 36 elements nor a counterexample; at most partial lemmas, sharper statements of what is missing, or evidence: 56 in 100.

One more line, not exclusive of the above: the referee I ask afterwards finds an error in a claimed proof of the instance (if it claims outcome 1 or 2): 25 in 100, given such a claim. The two earlier referees found none in the proofs, but the claims were more modest each time than what is asked now.
