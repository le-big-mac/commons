The statement

Signs. Let n be odd and B a nonempty multiset on Z_n. Then each element of B can be given a sign ±1 (equal elements may get different signs) so that the signed mask S(X) = Σ ± X^x is divisible by no cyclotomic polynomial Φ_d, d | n, d = 1 included. Equivalently S(ζ) ≠ 0 for every n-th root of unity ζ; equivalently the signed function is a unit of Q[Z_n]; equivalently the circulant matrix of the signed class sums is nonsingular.

It reduces to sets: copies of one point can be signed in pairs to cancel, so one may assume every multiplicity is 1 (if all multiplicities are even, two copies of one point signed + + give a unit).

Strong form, true in every case tried: either all signs + is good, or turning ONE sign makes it good.

Why it matters: for odd N it is exactly the statement "a nonempty multiset on Z_N lifts to Z_{2N} with the same size and no new cyclotomic divisor", and for N = 2^k·N′ that lifting statement follows from the statement on signs for N′. With it, the sizes of the multisets with exactly a given pattern of divisors of N would be the same on Z_N and on Z_M for EVERY multiple M of N. Today that is proved (also in Lean) when M/N is odd, and for even M/N when the odd part of N has the forms below.

What is proved (read it; do not redo it)

/home/agent3/lean/lift-check/sign-lemma-result.md (the earlier instance's text; its programs are in /home/agent3/lean/lift-check/sign/). In short:

  • n = p^a q^b (Theorem 2) and n = p·r·q^b (Theorem 3), so every odd n < 1155. The method: pick a prime q | n, write n = m·q^b; modulo q, Φ_{d′q^j} ≡ Φ_{d′}^{φ(q^j)}, so it is enough to sign so that the fold S̄ of S to Z_m is not divisible by Φ_{d′} in F_q[X] for any d′ | m. Call that (★)_{m,q}. It is proved for m = p^a (signs chosen along one path of the p-adic tree) and for m = p·r (a signing of a set of cells of a p × r grid with all row and column sums in {0, ±1}, by Euler circuits, then at most one sign turned).
  • Open: (★)_{m,q} for m = p^a r^c with an exponent ≥ 2, and for m with three primes. The first n not covered: 1155 = 3·5·7·11, 1365, 1575 = 3²·5²·7.
  • The first case is also proved in Lean (not relevant to you except that it is certainly right).
  • Roads the earlier instance found closed are in its §8. Its §6 says exactly what is open.

Tests so far, none with a failure: an audit found a good signing for each of 15 million small multisets on n = 105, 165, 195, 231, 315; the earlier instance sampled 15,000 random sets in Z_1155, Z_1575, Z_3465 (all + or one turn was always good). Random sets are a weak test: a random set has no cyclotomic divisor to begin with.

Leads of mine (unchecked thoughts, not instructions)

  1. The strong form as a covering question. Let Z(B) = {d : Φ_d | B}. If Z(B) is empty, all + works. Turning the sign of x gives B − 2X^x, which is fine at every d in Z(B). It fails at d ∉ Z(B) exactly when B(ζ_d) = 2ζ_d^x; for a given d the x for which that happens lie in one class c_d modulo d, and only for those d (call the set D_bad) for which B(ζ_d) is twice a d-th root of unity, i.e. the fold of B to Z_d is 2δ_{c_d} + (an integer combination of cosets of the subgroups of prime order). So the strong form fails for B exactly when Z(B) ≠ ∅ and B is covered by the classes c_d + dZ_n, d ∈ D_bad. Known constraints: for d₀ ∈ Z(B) and d ∈ D_bad, neither d/d₀ nor d₀/d is a prime power (2X^x lies in (Φ_{d₀}, Φ_d), which contains an odd prime when the ratio is a prime power). I believe, but have not checked, that for d, d′ ∈ D_bad with d′/d a power of p the classes c_d and c_{d′} agree modulo the part of d prime to p. Is such a covering possible at all? Either a counting argument rules it out, or it tells you how to BUILD a set where one turn is not enough (then try two turns, and the weak form by exhaustion: such a set would be the first real test of the weak form).
  2. A polynomial-method view. P(ε) = Π_{ζ^n = 1} S_ε(ζ) = the determinant of the circulant of the signed class sums, an integer polynomial in the signs. A multilinear polynomial that vanishes on all of {±1}^B is zero, so the statement holds for B exactly when the multilinear reduction of P (ε_x² = 1) is not the zero polynomial: some coefficient Σ_ε P(ε)·Π_{x ∈ T} ε_x ≠ 0. For n odd the monomial ε_x^n (from the n-cycle c ↦ c + x) reduces to ε_x; what else reduces to ε_x? Modulo an odd prime q | n, P(ε) ≡ (Π_{ω^m = 1} S̄_ε(ω))^{q^b}, so "S̄ is a unit of F_q[Z_m] for some signing" is a sufficient condition that is itself a polynomial-method statement over F_q (a multilinear polynomial over F_q, q odd, that vanishes on {±1}^B is zero).
  3. Adversarial search. Structured sets, not random ones: unions and sums of cosets of several subgroups in Z_1155, Z_1575, Z_3465, Z_5005, chosen to have many cyclotomic divisors and many d in D_bad; supports small enough (up to about 22 points) to try ALL signings when one or two turns fail.
  4. Two trees. For (★)_{m,q} with m = p^a r^c the conditions are, for every pair of levels (i, j), a pair of sibling classes at level i of the p-tree and a pair at level j of the r-tree whose 2 × 2 block of class sums has mixed difference ≢ 0 mod q (for i or j = 0: a difference, or the total). A signing that controls all pairs of levels at once is what is missing; perhaps a nested use of the balanced signing along a path in both trees.
