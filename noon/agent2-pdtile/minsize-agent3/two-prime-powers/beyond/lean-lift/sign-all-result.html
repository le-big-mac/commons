0. Summary

The statement on signs is NOT proved for all odd n. What is proved is a further family, which contains 118125 and 165375 and moves the first n covered by nothing from 118125 to about 6.6·10^18.

Proved (complete proofs below; each lemma tested by programs of my own, §7):

  • Lemma T (lost sign vectors on a tree). T = Z_{p^a}, p an odd prime, x : T → V any vector-valued function (V over a field of characteristic ≠ 2). The sign vectors s ∈ {±1}^T for which Σ s_v x_v ≠ 0 but, at some level i ∈ {1..a}, all sibling classes modulo p^i have equal sums of s·x, number at most (ω + ω² + … + ω^a)·2^{p^a}, ω = 2^{1−p}. (For a = 1 this is the "two sign vectors" of Lemma 3 of the second result.)
  • Lemma S (one tree). For every nonzero c : Z_{p^a} → F at least the fraction single(p, a) of the sign vectors make s·c good at all a + 1 levels; single = 1/2 if a = 1 or p ≥ 5, and 5/8 − λ(3, a) > 7/24 if p = 3, a ≥ 2.
  • Lemma 3T (one step with a whole tree as the new coordinate) and Proposition 5: on the tree box of m (one coordinate Z_{p^a} for each prime power p^a ∥ m, levels = divisors of m, cubes = products of sibling pairs) every nonzero function has at least the fraction Γ(m) of the product patterns good, where Γ is given by a recursion (§5).
  • Theorem 7. If n = m·q^b (q an odd prime not dividing m, b ≥ 0) or n = m, and Γ(m) > 0, then every nonempty multiset on Z_n has a signing divisible by no Φ_d, d | n.
  • Corollary 8 (three primes). Let p_1 < p_2 < p_3 be odd primes and n = p_1^{e_1} p_2^{e_2} p_3^{e_3}. The statement on signs holds for n if e_2 + 1 ≤ (3/8)(2^{p_3 − 1} − 1), or e_3 + 1 ≤ (3/8)(2^{p_2 − 1} − 1), or e_1 + 1 ≤ (7/32)(2^{p_3 − 1} − 1). For n = 3^a·5^b·7^c: whenever a ≤ 12 or b ≤ 22 or c ≤ 4. In particular for 118125 = 3³·5⁴·7 and 165375 = 3³·5³·7² (descent prime 3, m = 5⁴·7 resp. 5³·7², Γ(m) = 1/4).
  • Coverage (coverage2.py, coverage_first.py). With Theorems 2, 3 of the first result: the statement on signs holds for every odd n < 6587407633447265625 = 3⁶·5¹¹·7⁶·11²·13 (about 6.59·10^18). That number is the least odd n covered by nothing here.

Not proved, and what is missing exactly (§6). In Lemma 3T the pairs (s, σ_Y) lost at the levels (i, P′) are counted by a SUM over the levels P′ of the other coordinates; the sum grows with the number L_Y = ∏(a_l + 1) of those levels, while what can be afforded stays below 3/8. What is missing is a bound for the UNION over P′ that does not grow with L_Y. In every case computed the union is small (two trees: least fraction of good patterns exactly 1/4 = 2^−2, and ≥ 13/32 when the slices span more than a line), while the sum does reach L_Y·ω.

No counterexample in any test.

1. Tree boxes

Throughout F is Q, or F_q with q an odd prime; p is an odd prime; "sign vector" on a finite set A means an element of {±1}^A.

Trees. T = Z_{p^a}, a ≥ 1. A class at depth i (0 ≤ i ≤ a) is a residue class modulo p^i (depth 0: all of T; depth a: single points, "leaves"). A class D at depth i − 1 contains exactly p classes at depth i, its subclasses; D with its p subclasses is a group at level i, and two distinct subclasses of one group are sibling classes at level i (1 ≤ i ≤ a). For f : T → V (V a vector space over F) and a class C put f(C) = Σ_{v ∈ C} f(v). f is flat at level i (1 ≤ i ≤ a) if in every group at level i the p subclasses have equal sums f(C); f is flat at level 0 if f(T) = 0. A scalar f : T → F is good if it is flat at no level 0, …, a.

Tree boxes. X = T_1 × … × T_k with T_j = Z_{p_j^{a_j}} (the p_j odd primes; for the lemmas they need not be distinct). A level is a tuple (i_1, …, i_k), 0 ≤ i_j ≤ a_j; there are L(X) = ∏(a_j + 1) levels. A cube at that level chooses, for each j with i_j ≥ 1, two sibling classes C_j ≠ C′_j at level i_j of T_j; its cube sum for g : X → F is

κ(g) = Σ_{x ∈ X} g(x) · ∏_{j : i_j ≥ 1} ( [x_j ∈ C_j] − [x_j ∈ C′_j] ).

g is good at the level if some cube sum is ≠ 0, degenerate at the level otherwise; the functions degenerate at a level form a linear subspace Deg(level). g is good if it is good at every level. (For k = 1 this is the definition for one tree.) A pattern is σ = (σ_1, …, σ_k), σ_j an arbitrary sign vector on T_j (not only products over digits); (σg)(x) = ∏ σ_j(x_j)·g(x); Σ_X is the set of the 2^{Σ p_j^{a_j}} patterns. The box with no coordinate is one point, with one level and one pattern.

Lemma 1T (slices). Let X = T × Y, T = Z_{p^a}, Y a tree box, g : X → F with slices g_v = g(v, ·) and g_C = Σ_{v ∈ C} g_v for a class C of T. Let P′ be a level of Y. (a) g is degenerate at (0, P′) iff Σ_{v ∈ T} g_v ∈ Deg_Y(P′). (b) For 1 ≤ i ≤ a: g is degenerate at (i, P′) iff g_C − g_{C′} ∈ Deg_Y(P′) for all sibling classes C, C′ at level i of T; that is, iff the function v ↦ (class of g_v in Fun(Y)/Deg_Y(P′)) is flat at level i.

Proof. A cube at (i, P′) is a cube κ′ of Y at P′ together with (for i ≥ 1) a sibling pair C, C′; its cube sum is κ′(g_C − g_{C′}), resp. κ′(Σ_v g_v) for i = 0. ∎

Lemma G′ (from the tree box to roots of unity). Let m = ∏ p^{a_p} be odd and identify Z_m with the tree box ∏p Z{p^{a_p}} by x ↦ (x mod p^{a_p})_p. Let d′ | m, d′ = ∏ p^{i_p}, and for each p with i_p ≥ 1 let u_p, u′_p be residues modulo p^{i_p} with u_p ≢ u′_p (mod p^{i_p}), u_p ≡ u′_p (mod p^{i_p − 1}). Then

h(y) = ∏_{p : i_p ≥ 1} ( [y ≡ u_p mod p^{i_p}] − [y ≡ u′_p mod p^{i_p}] )

is a well defined primitive function on Z_{d′} (in the sense of the first result), and for T : Z_m → Z the number ⟨h, T⟩ is the cube sum of T at the level (i_p)_p for the cube (u_p, u′_p)_p.

Proof. h depends on y only through y mod p^{i_p}, and p^{i_p} | d′. Primitive: let p_0 | d′, i_0 = i_{p_0} ≥ 1. Adding t·d′/p_0 (t = 0, …, p_0 − 1) to y does not change y mod p^{i_p} for p ≠ p_0 nor y mod p_0^{i_0 − 1}, and, since d′/p_0 = p_0^{i_0 − 1}·e with e prime to p_0, makes y mod p_0^{i_0} run once through the p_0 classes modulo p_0^{i_0} inside the class of y modulo p_0^{i_0 − 1}. As u_{p_0} and u′{p_0} lie in the same class modulo p_0^{i_0 − 1}, the factor of p_0 sums to 1 − 1 or 0 − 0 over the coset; the other factors are constant on it. So h sums to 0 over every coset of the subgroup of order p_0. Finally ⟨h, T⟩ = Σ{x ∈ Z_m} h(x mod d′) T(x), which is the cube sum. (d′ = 1: h = 1.) ∎

Hence, by the Corollary of the first result: if n = m·q^b and the fold of a signing S to Z_m, read on the tree box of m and reduced modulo q, is good, then S is good (and for n = m: if S is good on the tree box over Q then S is good, by Lemma B(a)). The levels of the tree box correspond one to one to the divisors d′ of m. (These cubes are those of §6 of the first result. The digit cubes of the second result are sums of such cubes over all groups of a level, so "good in the digit box at the tree levels" implies "good in the tree box", not conversely.)

2. Lemma T: lost sign vectors on one tree

Let T = Z_{p^a}, V a vector space over a field of characteristic ≠ 2, x : T → V. For a sign vector s write sx for v ↦ s_v x_v. Put ω = 2^{1−p} and λ(p, a) = ω + ω² + … + ω^a (< 1/(2^{p−1} − 1)).

Lemma T. The set 𝓑(x) of sign vectors s on T such that (sx)(T) ≠ 0 and sx is flat at some level i ∈ {1, …, a} has at most λ(p, a)·2^{p^a} elements.

Proof. Call a class C active if x is not identically 0 on C, and put ψ_C(s) = (sx)(C), which depends only on the signs on C.

Fact 1. If C is active and b ∈ V, at most 2^{|C|−1} sign vectors on C have ψ_C = b. (Take v ∈ C with x_v ≠ 0; changing the sign at v changes ψ_C by ±2x_v ≠ 0, so of each such pair at most one has ψ_C = b.)

Fact 2. Let D be a group at level i with m ≥ 1 active subclasses. At most 2^{|D| − min(m, p−1)} sign vectors on D make the p subclass sums equal ("flat on D"). Indeed, if m < p some subclass is not active, its sum is 0, so every active subclass must have sum 0: by Fact 1 at most ∏{active} 2^{|C|−1}·∏{others} 2^{|C|} = 2^{|D|−m}. If m = p, let C_1, …, C_p be the subclasses and n_j(b) the number of sign vectors on C_j with ψ_{C_j} = b; the number sought is Σ_b ∏j n_j(b) ≤ (∏{j ≥ 2} 2^{|C_j|−1})·Σ_b n_1(b) = 2^{|D|−(p−1)}.

Fact 3. If sx is flat at level i and (sx)(T) ≠ 0, then some group D at level i has all p subclasses active. Indeed (sx)(T) = Σ_D p·β_D, where β_D is the common subclass sum in D; so some β_D ≠ 0, and a subclass with sum β_D ≠ 0 is active.

Let n_i (1 ≤ i ≤ a) be the number of active classes at depth i − 1, i.e. of active groups at level i (n_1 = 1 if x ≠ 0; if x = 0 the set 𝓑 is empty). Every active class contains an active subclass, so n_1 ≤ n_2 ≤ … ≤ n_a. Call level i possible if some group at level i has all p subclasses active. If i is possible and i < a then n_{i+1} ≥ (n_i − 1) + p (one active group contributes p active subclasses, each other at least one).

Flatness at level i is flatness on every group at level i, and the groups are disjoint sets of coordinates. So if i is possible, by Fact 2 the number of s that are flat at level i is at most 2^{p^a}·2^{−(p−1)}·2^{−(n_i − 1)} (one group with m = p, the other n_i − 1 active groups with min(m, p−1) ≥ 1). If i is not possible, no s ∈ 𝓑 is flat at level i (Fact 3).

Let i_1 < … < i_r be the possible levels (r ≤ a). Then n_{i_1} ≥ 1 and n_{i_{l+1}} ≥ n_{i_l + 1} ≥ n_{i_l} + p − 1, so n_{i_l} ≥ 1 + (l − 1)(p − 1), and the number of s ∈ 𝓑 flat at level i_l is at most 2^{p^a}·ω·2^{−(l−1)(p−1)} = 2^{p^a}·ω^l. Summing over l ≤ r ≤ a gives the claim. ∎

Remarks. (1) The first term is attained: x equal to one vector b ≠ 0 on the p leaves of one group at level a and 0 elsewhere; the lost s are the two that are constant on the group. (2) In every case computed (§7) the lost fraction was at most ω itself, not ω + ω² + …; I did not prove that.

3. Lemma S: one tree

Lemma S. Let c : Z_{p^a} → F be nonzero, F = Q or F_q with q an odd prime, q ≠ p. At least single(p, a)·2^{p^a} sign vectors s make sc good (flat at no level 0, …, a), where single(p, a) = 1/2 if a = 1 or p ≥ 5, and single(3, a) = 5/8 − λ(3, a) (> 7/24) for a ≥ 2.

Proof. For a = 1 this is the case k = 1 of Theorem P of the second result (flat at level 1 means: sc constant). Let a ≥ 2 and let w be the number of leaves v with c_v ≠ 0. By Fact 3 of Lemma T (with x = c), an s with (sc)(T) ≠ 0 that is not good lies in 𝓑(c), and 𝓑(c) is empty unless some group has p ≥ 3 active subclasses, which needs w ≥ 3. - w = 1: (sc)(T) = ±c_v ≠ 0 always; every s is good. - w = 2: s is good iff (sc)(T) ≠ 0; changing the sign at an active leaf changes the total by ±2c_v ≠ 0, so at least half of the s are good. - w ≥ 3: fix three active leaves and the signs elsewhere. Among the 8 sign choices on the three leaves at most 3 give total 0: two choices differing in one place give different totals, so the choices with total 0 form a set without two neighbours in the 3-cube; such a set with 4 elements is a parity class, e.g. {+++, +−−, −+−, −−+}, and equal totals on it give c_2 + c_3 = c_1 + c_3 = c_1 + c_2 = 0, hence 2c_1 = 0, which is false (the other parity class is its negative). So at least 5/8 of all s have (sc)(T) ≠ 0, and by Lemma T at most λ(p, a) of all s lie in 𝓑(c): at least 5/8 − λ(p, a) are good. For p ≥ 5 this is

5/8 − 1/15 > 1/2; for p = 3 it is > 5/8 − 1/3 = 7/24. ∎

(Computed: the least fraction is exactly 1/2 also for p = 3, a = 2, 3, 4 in everything tried, §7. I did not prove 1/2 for p = 3, a ≥ 2.)

4. Lemma 3T: one step, the new coordinate being a whole tree

Lemma 2(a), general form (the proof in the second result does not use its special setting). A subspace of dimension d of F^N contains at most 2^d sign vectors: some d coordinates project it injectively to F^d, and the images of sign vectors are sign vectors.

Lemma 3T. Let X = T × Y, T = Z_{p^a}, Y a tree box with L_Y levels. Suppose (H_Y) every nonzero u : Y → F has at least γ·|Σ_Y| patterns σ_Y with σ_Y u good, and (H_T) every nonzero c : T → F has at least γ_T·2^{p^a} sign vectors s with sc good. Then every nonzero g : X → F has at least

min( γ_T·γ , (3/4)·γ − L_Y·λ(p, a) ) · |Σ_X|

good patterns.

Proof. Write a pattern of X as (s, σ_Y). Let g_v be the slices, u_t = Σ_v t_v g_v for t ∈ F^T, and r ≥ 1 the dimension of the span of the slices.

Case r = 1. Then g_v = c_v·h with h : Y → F nonzero and c : T → F nonzero. A cube sum of (s, σ_Y)g at a level (i, P′) is the product of ((sc)(C) − (sc)(C′)) (resp. (sc)(T) for i = 0) and a cube sum of σ_Y h at P′. So (s, σ_Y)g is good iff sc is good on T and σ_Y h is good on Y: at least γ_T 2^{p^a}·γ|Σ_Y| patterns.

Case r ≥ 2. K = {t : u_t = 0} has dimension p^a − r ≤ p^a − 2, so it contains at most 2^{p^a − 2} sign vectors. Let 𝒜 be the set of pairs (s, σ_Y) with σ_Y u_s good on Y. By (H_Y) each s ∉ K gives at least γ|Σ_Y| pairs in 𝒜: |𝒜| ≥ (3/4)·2^{p^a}·γ|Σ_Y|. The slices of (s, σ_Y)g are s_v σ_Y g_v and their sum is σ_Y u_s. By Lemma 1T(a), a pair in 𝒜 is good at every level (0, P′); so a pair in 𝒜 is good unless (s, σ_Y)g is degenerate at some (i, P′) with i ≥ 1. Fix σ_Y and P′, let V = Fun(Y)/Deg_Y(P′) and x_v = the class of σ_Y g_v in V. By Lemma 1T(b), (s, σ_Y)g is degenerate at (i, P′) iff sx is flat at level i; and (s, σ_Y) ∈ 𝒜 gives (sx)(T) = class of σ_Y u_s ≠ 0. So s ∈ 𝓑(x), and by Lemma T there are at most λ(p, a)·2^{p^a} such s. Summing over the |Σ_Y| patterns and the L_Y levels P′, at most L_Y·λ(p, a)·2^{p^a}|Σ_Y| pairs of 𝒜 are not good. ∎

Lemma 3′ (a = 1; Lemma 3 of the second result with Y a tree box). Let X = Z_p × Y, Y a tree box with L_Y levels, F = Q or F_q with q ∤ 2p, and (H_Y) as above. Then every nonzero g has at least min( γ/2 , γ − L_Y·2^{1−p} )·|Σ_X| good patterns.

Proof. The proof of Lemma 3 of the second result, with Lemma 1T in place of its Lemma 1 (for a = 1 the sibling classes are any two points) and 𝓛_0 = 𝓛_1 = the levels of Y; it uses of Y only that the degenerate functions at a level form a subspace and that patterns act linearly. In full: 𝒜 as above. If a pair of 𝒜 is degenerate at (1, P′), all s_v σ_Y g_v have one class b modulo Deg_Y(P′), p·b is the class of σ_Y u_s ≠ 0, so b ≠ 0, and the class of σ_Y u_t is ⟨s, t⟩b for all t. If K ≠ 0: for κ ∈ K, 0 = ⟨s, κ⟩b, so s ∈ K^⊥; by Lemma 2(d) at least 2^{p−1} sign vectors are outside K ∪ K^⊥, each with at least γ|Σ_Y| good pairs. If K = 0: |𝒜| ≥ 2^p γ|Σ_Y|, and for fixed (σ_Y, P′) two lost s, s′ have s^⊥ = s′^⊥, so s′ = ±s (Lemma 2(e)): at most 2·L_Y·|Σ_Y| pairs are lost. ∎

Why the argument "lost pairs have s ∈ K^⊥" of the second result is not used for a ≥ 2: with several groups it is false (the lost s satisfy one relation per group, Σ_D β_D·⟨κ_D, s_D⟩ = 0, not ⟨κ, s⟩ = 0). The split by the rank r replaces it, at the price of the factor 3/4.

5. The recursion, Theorem 7, coverage

For a finite set S of pairs (p, a) with distinct odd primes p (the tree box of m = ∏ p^a) define Γ(S) by: Γ({(p, a)}) = single(p, a); and for |S| ≥ 2

Γ(S) = max over (p, a) ∈ S with G′ = Γ(S ∖ {(p, a)}) > 0 of min( single(p, a)·G′ , (3/4)·G′ − L′·λ(p, a) ), and, if a = 1, also of min( G′/2 , G′ − L′·2^{1−p} ),

where L′ = ∏_{(r, c) ∈ S ∖ {(p, a)}} (c + 1); Γ(S) = −1 if there is no such pair. Write Γ(m) for the tree box of m. Only Γ > 0 carries meaning.

Proposition 5. Let m be odd with Γ(m) > 0 and F = Q or F_q with q an odd prime not dividing m. Every nonzero g on the tree box of m has at least Γ(m)·|Σ| good patterns.

Proof. Induction on the number of trees: Lemma S for one tree; for more, Lemma 3T (with γ_T = single(p, a) from Lemma S and γ = G′ from the induction) or Lemma 3′ for the pair (p, a) that attains the maximum. ∎

Theorem 7. Let m be odd with Γ(m) > 0, q an odd prime not dividing m, b ≥ 0, and n = m·q^b (or n = m). Every nonempty multiset B on Z_n has a good signing (one divisible by no Φ_d, d | n).

Proof. As for Theorem 6 of the second result. F = F_q (F = Q if n = m). N(c) = number of elements of B that are ≡ c (mod m). If some N(c) is odd, g = indicator of {c : N(c) odd}; otherwise g = 2δ_{c_0} with N(c_0) ≥ 2. g ≠ 0 in F. By Proposition 5 there is a pattern σ with σg good on the tree box of m over F. Sign B so that the elements of the cell c have signs summing to σ(c)g(c) (same parity as N(c), absolute value ≤ N(c)). The fold of the signed mask to Z_m is σg, and the signing is good by Lemma G′ and the Corollary of the first result (Lemma B(a) for n = m). ∎

Corollary 8. Let p_1 < p_2 < p_3 be odd primes, n = p_1^{e_1} p_2^{e_2} p_3^{e_3}. The statement on signs holds for n if (i) e_2 + 1 ≤ (3/8)(2^{p_3 − 1} − 1), or (ii) e_3 + 1 ≤ (3/8)(2^{p_2 − 1} − 1), or (iii) e_1 + 1 ≤ (7/32)(2^{p_3 − 1} − 1).

Proof. (i) q = p_1, m = p_2^{e_2} p_3^{e_3}, the tree of p_3 last: p_2 ≥ 5, so G′ = single(p_2, e_2) = 1/2, L′ = e_2 + 1, and (3/4)G′ − L′λ(p_3, e_3) > 3/8 − (e_2 + 1)/(2^{p_3 − 1} − 1) ≥ 0; the other term is 1/4. (ii) the same with the tree of p_2 last. (iii) q = p_2, m = p_1^{e_1} p_3^{e_3}, the tree of p_3 last: G′ = single(p_1, e_1) > 7/24, (3/4)G′ > 7/32 ≥ (e_1 + 1)/(2^{p_3 − 1} − 1) > L′λ(p_3, e_3). (If an exponent is 0, Theorem 2 of the first result applies.) ∎

For 3^a·5^b·7^c: (iii) gives a ≤ 12, (i) gives b ≤ 22, (ii) gives c ≤ 4. 118125 = 3³·5⁴·7 and 165375 = 3³·5³·7²: q = 3, m = 5⁴·7 resp. 5³·7²; Γ(m) = min(1/2·1/2, 3/8 − 5/64) = 1/4 resp. min(1/4, 3/8 − 4·(1/64 + 1/4096)) = 1/4.

Coverage. coverage2.py (output coverage2.out): every odd n < 200000 is covered by Theorem 2, 3, 6 or 7; 118125 and 165375 by Theorem 7 only. coverage_first.py (output coverage_first.out) finds the least odd n covered by none of them:

N* = 6587407633447265625 = 3⁶·5¹¹·7⁶·11²·13 ≈ 6.59·10^18.

How that search is complete. Monotonicity: λ(p, a) decreases and single(p, a) does not decrease when p grows, and each candidate in the recursion is nondecreasing in G′; so if the trees (p_1, a_1), …, (p_k, a_k), taken in some order, give a positive value, the trees (p′_1, a_1), …, (p′_k, a_k) with distinct primes p′_j ≥ p_j give, in the same order, a value at least as large. Theorems 2 and 3 depend on the exponents only. Hence if n′ = ∏ p′_j^{e_j} (p′_1 < … < p′_k) is covered by nothing, so is n = ∏ P_j^{e_j} ≤ n′ with P_j the j-th odd prime. So the least uncovered n is supported on an initial segment 3, 5, 7, … of the odd primes, and the program enumerates all exponent vectors (e_1, …, e_k), e_j ≥ 1, with n below the least uncovered n found so far (1315750 vectors). Every vector below N* was covered by Theorem 2, by Theorem 3, or by the recursion of Γ along the order of increasing primes for some choice of q (so Theorem 6 is not needed for the bound). For N* itself the full recursion Γ (all orders, all q) and the digit recursion of the second result give nothing.

So: the statement on signs holds for every odd n < 6587407633447265625.

6. What is open, exactly

The missing statement. In the proof of Lemma 3T (case r ≥ 2), for a fixed σ_Y let Lost(σ_Y, P′) be the set of s with (s, σ_Y) ∈ 𝒜 and (s, σ_Y)g degenerate at some (i, P′), i ≥ 1. The proof uses

|∪{P′} Lost(σ_Y, P′)| ≤ Σ{P′} |Lost(σ_Y, P′)| ≤ L_Y·λ(p, a)·2^{p^a}.

The second inequality is sharp up to the tail of λ. The first is where everything is lost: L_Y = ∏(a_l + 1) grows with the exponents of the other primes, and the step needs L_Y·λ(p, a) < (3/4)γ ≤ 3/8. What would finish the statement on signs for all odd n is

(U) a bound for the number of pairs (s, σ_Y) ∈ 𝒜 that are lost at SOME level (i, P′), i ≥ 1, by θ·|Σ_X| with θ depending on p only (for instance θ = 2^{1−p}, or anything with θ < (3/4)γ after the induction is rearranged), for every g whose slices span more than a line;

or, what (U) would give, the count itself:

Conjecture (tree boxes). On every tree box with k trees (distinct odd primes, any depths), over Q and over F_q for q odd and prime to the box, every nonzero g has at least 2^−k·|Σ_X| good patterns.

By Theorem 7's proof the conjecture (existence of one good pattern is enough) gives the statement on signs for every odd n, with n = m and F = Q. Why the sum cannot be repaired by sharper constants: for g = 1_D ⊗ h (D one group of p leaves) the SAME sign vectors (constant on D) are lost at every level P′ at which σ_Y h is good, so the sum is L_Y·ω while the union is ω; this g has rank one and is harmless, but the sum of the lost fractions over the levels of Y also reached (c + 1)·ω among all the functions tested (§7), while the fraction of good patterns never went below 1/4, and not below 13/32 when the slices spanned more than a line.

The first shapes not covered. N* = 3⁶·5¹¹·7⁶·11²·13; with three primes, 3^a·5^b·7^c with a ≥ 13, b ≥ 23 and c ≥ 5 (the least is about 3·10^26). In all of them every prime but the largest ones is highly repeated.

Two sharper constants, observed and not proved (they would move N*, not remove it): single(3, a) = 1/2 for a ≥ 2 (Lemma S gives > 7/24), and λ(p, a) = 2^{1−p} (Lemma T gives ω + … + ω^a).
