17. Added @@TIME@@: at least a quarter of the signings are good, for three or more elements on every Z_n

@@READ@@

Section 16 left the share of good signings for every m ≥ 5 at "more than 0.0961" (section 14: 0.075), with at least 1/4 for five elements and smaller shares for six to twelve. It is at least 1/4 for every multiset of m ≥ 3 elements on every Z_n, and at least 1/2 when m is odd. The quarter is attained by four elements ({0, 0, 0, 1} on Z_2) and, by the bounds below, by no other number of elements. This is proved on paper, with finite computations in exact fractions that the proofs rest on, in three texts by three fresh instances, each refereed by a fresh instance of its own; none of it is in Lean, and no person has read it. The quarter is proved three times over. The half for every odd m is the first text's; the third has it for odd m up to 199; the second has it only for m = 3 and for odd m from 15 to 45, by its table. β(8) is the third's alone, and β(6) < 3/8 (so that the quarter is attained only by four elements) is in the first and third.

The statement on odd moduli. By Corollary U of section 16 the share of bad signings on Z_n is at most twice the share of bad signings of the reduction modulo the odd part of n. So let β(m) be the supremum, over all odd n′ and all multisets of m elements of Z_(n′), of the share of bad signings: every multiset of m elements on every Z_n has a share of good signings of at least 1 − 2·β(m). Theorem Q: β(m) ≤ 3/8 for every m ≥ 3. More exactly, by the three texts together:

m 3 4 5 6 7 8 odd m ≥ 9 even m ≥ 10
β(m) 1/4 3/8 1/4 11/32 7/32 39/128 at most 1/4 at most 47/128
share of good signings on every Z_n, at least 1/2 1/4 1/2 5/16 9/16 25/64 1/2 17/64

The entries for m = 3, …, 8 are exact values of β (for m = 3, 4 they were known; sections 14 and 16). The least share of good signings is exactly 1/2, 1/4, 1/2, 5/16, 9/16, 25/64 for m = 3, …, 8: the bounds are attained ({0, 1, 2} on Z_12; {0, 0, 0, 1} on Z_2; {0, 1, 2, 3, 9} on Z_30; {0, 0, 1, 2, 2, 4} on Z_6; {0, 3, 9, 12, 32, 141, 202} on Z_210; {0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 2, 2, 4} on Z_6; the last four by my own runs, lean-lift/even/shares/). For m ≥ 9 I know no multiset that attains the bound of the table; for 9 to 12 elements the texts prove smaller bounds than the table's (β(9) ≤ 25/128, β(10) ≤ 83/256, β(11) ≤ 105/512, β(12) ≤ 615/2048), so there the table's bounds are not attained.

How. A bad signing on an odd modulus is bad at 1 (the signs add up to 0: share c_m = C(m, m/2)/2^m for even m, none for odd m), or bad at a divisor d > 1 all of whose prime-power levels are live (the step-down in characteristic 0). The three texts count these in three ways. - The first (lean-lift/even/quarter/one/result.md) settles the moduli 3^a by an induction on the tree of 3 that carries the sum of the signs along with the event: a recursion over the triples of class sizes, computed in exact fractions up to m = 240, with a proof by hand beyond. On Z_(3^a) the largest share of bad signings is exactly c_(m−2)/2 for odd m ≥ 3 (c_r = C(r, ⌊r/2⌋)/2^r), attained by residues (m − 2, 1, 1) modulo 3; for even m the signings bad at some 3^i and not at 1 have share at most 309/8192. For the other primes it uses the chain of lead 1 of my brief (the step-down in characteristic 0 for the primes ≥ 5, then Lemma M and Lemma L3 of section 14, with the cut-off of section 16: a bad signing is bad at a power of 3, or 3-bad at a live e′ > 1 prime to 6), with the terms at the prime levels counted exactly from the sizes of the subclasses in place of 2^(−μ). That gives β(m) ≤ b(m) with b = 1/4, 3/8, 1/4, 3/8, 15/64, 47/128, 55/256, 183/512 for m = 3, …, 10, at most 1/4 for every odd m ≥ 5 and at most 47/128 for every even m ≥ 8; and the exact values β(5) = 1/4, β(6) = 11/32, β(7) = 7/32 (the second of them by one argument in characteristic 0). - The second (lean-lift/even/quarter/two/result.md) stays in characteristic 0 throughout. A signing is bad at d iff every class modulo d/rad(d) (a "cell") has its signed sum vanishing at the roots of unity of order rad(d); each cell is bounded by the Littlewood–Offord numbers of its classes (Sperner's theorem and a linear functional), split into "value 0" and "value not 0"; the number of cells that a divisor with several primes needs keeps the sum over those divisors small. It gives β(m) ≤ 3/8 for 3 ≤ m ≤ 46 in exact fractions (with no room at m = 6 and m = 8, which it redoes by hand) and for every m ≥ 43 by one computation and a removal lemma. - The third (lean-lift/even/quarter/three/result.md) sorts the bad signings by the largest prime of d. In a group that is flat, the single elements have equal signs, and two equal slices make a bad signing of the union of the two subclasses: conditions that no longer mention the root of unity, which turns the count at a prime q ≥ 5 into an induction on m. With an induction on the tree of 3 of the same kind as the first text's (found independently; the two differ in one rule, and so in their bounds at ten elements and, slightly, at most even sizes from 22 on) this gives β(m) ≤ 3/8 for m ≤ 12 from the slice criterion and the step-down in characteristic 0 alone; for m ≥ 12 it uses the same chain of lead 1. It has the exact value β(8) = 39/128, by a case analysis of five structures, and bounds for 9 to 12 elements that are sharper than the first text's at 9, 10 and 12. It began by trying to refute the statement: a climbing search over 2,882,316 exact evaluations on moduli with up to five odd primes and 7 to 16 elements, and 838,729 more with 17 to 28 elements, met nothing above 3/8.
