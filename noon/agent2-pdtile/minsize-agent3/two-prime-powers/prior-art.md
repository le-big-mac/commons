# Least size of a multiset on Z_{pⁿq^m} with exactly a given set of cyclotomic divisors, m ≥ 2, and Claim U: what is in print

*26 November 2026. A prior-art reading by a fresh reader. Quotations are exact (formulas retyped from the PDF
text); "p." is the page of the version named. Everything I worked out myself (least sizes, a proof, checks by
machine) is in `sealed-derivations.md` beside this file and in the folder `prior-art-tools/sealed/`; do not
open either before your predictions are scored. This file says of that material only what the brief allows.*

## 0. In short

1. **Claim U is not in print that I can find**, neither as a least size, nor as a lower bound, nor in an
   equivalent form (long fibres, grids, cuboids, clusters). No paper I saw asks for the least size of a multiset
   whose divisors among the Φ_d, d | pⁿq^m, are exactly an up-set, and none states the structure of the
   multisets whose divisors contain a general up-set.
2. **I found no counterexample in print and none myself.** The sealed file exists and **contains a proof** of
   Claim U for M with two prime factors.
3. **The closest result in print is Kiss–Łaba–Marshall–Somlai, Proposition 6.3 with Lemma 6.2** (long fibre
   decomposition for one full block {L : N | L | M}, with nonnegative coefficients when there are two primes).
   It implies Claim U at once when D has a single minimal element p^a q^b with a, b ≥ 1, and it is the tool for
   the general case. The same paper iterates Proposition 6.3 over several blocks in the proof of its
   Proposition 9.5, which is the method a proof of Claim U needs; it does so for another purpose and does not
   state the result. The grid-level fact under Proposition 6.3 (a nonnegative two-dimensional "cyclotomic array"
   of any side lengths is a positive sum of fibres) is older: Coppersmith–Steinberger 2006 state it and credit
   Steinberger.
4. **Special cases that are in print:** D = {M} (value p + q; Lam–Leung Theorem 3.3 with the non-divisor
   clause of Kiss–Malikiosis–Somlai–Vizer Theorem 4.9 = Malikiosis Theorem 5.2); D = all d not dividing one c
   with c = M/p (Ma's Lemma as in Malikiosis Lemma 10.2, KMSV Lemma 4.10); M a prime power (KLMS Lemma 1.1).
   Your case m = 1 is your own theorem.
5. **Three prime factors:** the formula of Claim U fails for M = 30, D = {30}, and the witness is in print:
   Lam and Leung's six-term sum (Example 6.7) against the formula's ten. Łaba and Marshall (Example 6.3) print
   the Claim-U construction for D = {pq, pr, qr, pqr} with a lower bound of the largest term only.
6. **Beyond up-sets, for exact patterns on pⁿq^m with m ≥ 2, nothing is in print that I can find**: no
   formula, no table, no structure theorem. With non-divisors not prescribed, KLMS settle MIN(S) = FIB(S) when
   one of the two exponent sets has at most two elements (so for every S when min(n, m) ≤ 2: M = 36, 72, 100,
   108, 225) and when |S| ≤ 3, and show it false in general.
7. I made **41 requests** (26 fetches, 15 general web searches). Three sites refused or failed; de Bruijn 1953
   and Steinberger 2008 could not be opened.

## 1. The question in the field's words

| yours | theirs |
|---|---|
| the pattern D | S_A (the set of s with Φ_s \| A), restricted to s \| M; "zero set" in the Fuglede papers; "root patterns" in Malikiosis |
| an up-set generated by one N | a "block" or "cluster" {s : N \| s \| M} (KLMS p. 35: "our blocks are the clusters B_l := {s ∈ D(M) : N_i \| s \| M_i}"; Łaba–Marshall Definition 3.1, p. 9: "A subset C ⊂ S_A is called a cyclotomic divisor cluster") |
| a coset of the subgroup of order M/c | a "c-grid" Λ(x, c) (KLMS p. 8: "a D-grid is a coset of DZ_N ≃ Z_{N/D} in Z_N") |
| a coset of the subgroup of order p^α | a "p^α-fiber on scale M", a "long fiber" F_{i,α} (KLMS Definition 6.1, p. 17); a "P-fiber chain" for P a top run of scales (Łaba–Londner 2022, Definition 8.2) |
| a coset of order p^α q^β | the sumset of two long fibres (KLMS p. 18: "Λ = F_{1,α₁} ∗ F_{2,α₂}") |
| least size with divisors only | MIN(S) (KLMS (1.3), p. 1) |
| least size among sets fibred on each scale | FIB(S) (KLMS p. 3) |
| "exactly D" | no name. KLMS p. 2: "To force an increase in size, we need additional assumptions on A, S, or both." Their additional assumptions are q ∤ \|A\|, (T1), (T2), an "unsupported divisor" (Definition 1.5, p. 4), never a full list of non-divisors |

An up-set of the divisor lattice of pⁿq^m is a union of top blocks; its complement is a staircase, and your
"maximal divisors not in D" are the corners of the staircase. A search for the words up-set, upper set,
order filter, down-set, lower set, order ideal, upward or downward closed, divisor-closed and hereditary in
the texts on disk (31 papers and one set of slides) finds none of them.

## 2. Kiss, Łaba, Marshall, Somlai (read in full)

## 3. Łaba and Marshall (read in full)

I. Łaba, C. Marshall, *Vanishing sums of roots of unity and the Favard length of self-similar product sets*,
arXiv:2202.07555v3 (15 December 2022), Discrete Analysis 2022:19, 31 pp. v3 is the latest version and is the
journal's. Read in full, all sections (the drawings of Section 7 not seen).

- Theorem 1.2, p. 3 (= Proposition 5.1, p. 16): "Assume further that q ∤ A(1). Then we have the lower bound
  A(1) ≥ p^{E_p}, where E_p = |{α₁, ..., α_k}|." *A bound:* one term of Claim U's sum, when q ∤ |A|.
- Sections 2, 3 (Proposition 2.1, Lemmas 3.2, 3.3, 3.4, 3.6) build sets of large values for the Favard
  problem from clusters of divisors. *Not relevant* to least sizes.
- Lemma 4.3, Lemma 4.4, p. 15: the two-prime positivity and restriction to grids. *Tools.*
- Section 6.1, p. 18: an example with Φ_{p¹⁰q¹⁰} Φ_{pq} | A and "|A| = p² + q²", a sum of two products of
  fibres. Section 6.2, p. 18: the long fibre, and "Translates of such fibers in both directions can be added
  to construct more complicated examples." *The upper-bound construction of Claim U is of this kind; no
  minimality is claimed.*
- Lemma 6.1, p. 19; Lemma 6.2, p. 20; Proposition 6.3, p. 21: "p_i^ℓ | A^{m₀}[∆] for any m₀-cuboid ∆" when
  Φ_{m₀p_i^{α_j}} | A for ℓ exponents.
- Corollary 6.4, p. 22: "there exists α₀ with 0 ≤ α₀ < α₁ such that Φ_{m*} ∤ A, where m* = m₀p_i^{α₀}. Then
  |A| ≥ p_i^ℓ." *A bound that uses a non-divisor:* for a maximal non-member c = p^u q^v of an up-set it gives
  |A| ≥ p^{n−u} and |A| ≥ q^{m−v}, against the term M/c = p^{n−u}q^{m−v} of Claim U.
- Corollary 6.5, p. 22: "Assume further that Φ_{p_i m} | A for i = 1, ..., I, but Φ_m ∤ A. Then A(1) ≥
  p₁ ... p_I." *A bound that uses a non-divisor:* |A| ≥ pq for a maximal non-member with both exponents below
  the top. Remark 6.1, p. 23, says a stronger version follows from Proposition 6.3 and omits it.
- Example 6.3, p. 23, three primes: for {pq, pr, qr} ⊂ S_A and (|A|, pqr) = 1, "|A| > max(pq, pr, qr)", and
  "The following example shows that the lower bound in (6.10) is essentially optimal up to a multiplicative
  constant. Define A ⊂ Z_M, where pqr | M, so that A = (a ∗ F_p ∗ F_q) ∪ (a′ ∗ F_q ∗ F_r) ∪ (a″ ∗ F_p ∗ F_r).
  […] Then (6.8) holds, and |A| = pq + pr + qr". *This is the construction of Claim U for the up-set generated
  by pq, pr, qr in print, with three primes; the lower bound proved is the largest term, and the sum is not
  claimed to be least.*
- Section 6.4, p. 19: "Our current methods are not sufficient to extend Theorem 1.2 to the case when more
  than two distinct prime factors are allowed."
- Section 7 (Lemma 7.2 and its Claim, pp. 26–28): sets of at most ten elements, by the tables of minimal
  vanishing sums. *Not relevant.*

Nothing in the paper gives or contradicts Claim U.

## 4. Lam and Leung (read in full)

T. Y. Lam, K. H. Leung, *On vanishing sums of roots of unity*, arXiv:math/9511209v1 (13 November 1995, the
only version; listed by arXiv as "On vanishing sums for roots of unity"), J. Algebra 224 (2000) 91–109. Read
in full, 18 pages. The journal version was not compared.

- Main Theorem, p. 2 (= Theorem 5.2, p. 12): "the weight set W(m) is exactly given by N p₁ + ··· + N p_r."
- Theorem 3.1, p. 6: "N G ∩ ker(ϕ) = Σ_j g_j (N G₀ ∩ ker(ϕ))", G₀ the subgroup of square-free order: a
  nonnegative vanishing sum splits over the cosets of G₀ (the restriction to grids).
- **What they say for two prime factors.** Theorem 3.3, p. 7: "(2) If r = 2, N G ∩ ker(ϕ) = N P₁ · σ(P₂) +
  N P₂ · σ(P₁)." (credited: "This result can be traced back to the work of de Bruijn [DeB: §3]"). Corollary
  3.4, p. 8: "Let m = p^a q^b, where p, q are primes. Then, up to a rotation, the only minimal vanishing sums
  of m th roots of unity are: 1 + ζ_p + ··· + ζ_p^{p−1} = 0, and 1 + ζ_q + ··· + ζ_q^{q−1} = 0." p. 8: "In the
  case r ≤ 2, (3.3) implies that all minimal elements in N G ∩ ker(ϕ) are symmetric."
  *One scale only: the divisor Φ_m. With the non-divisor clause of Section 5 this is Claim U for D = {M}.
  Nothing on several divisors at once.*
- Three or more primes. Example 2.5, p. 5 (after Rédei): the sum (α + ··· + α^{p−1})(β + ··· + β^{q−1}) + γ +
  ··· + γ^{ℓ−1} = 0 is minimal and not a rotated fibre. Theorem 4.8, p. 11: "For any minimal element x ∈ N G ∩
  ker(ϕ), we have either (A) x is symmetric, or (B) r ≥ 3 and ε(x) ≥ ε₀(x) ≥ p₁(p₂ − 1) + p₃ − p₂ > p₃."
  Theorem 6.5, p. 15: the minimal asymmetric element of that weight is unique up to rotation. Remark 5.3,
  p. 13: for |G| = 30 the element (5.5) has no representation with nonnegative total weights.
  Example 6.7, p. 16: "x(G) = t(h + h²) + g + g² + g³ + g⁴ = z⁵ + z⁶ + z¹² + z¹⁸ + z²⁴ + z²⁵, with weight
  (p₁ − 1)(p₂ − 1) + p₃ − 1 = 6."
  *This is the witness that Claim U's formula fails for M = 30, D = {30}: the multiset {5, 6, 12, 18, 24, 25}
  of Z₃₀ has six elements, and I checked by exact division that Φ₃₀ is its only divisor among the Φ_d,
  d | 30, d > 1. The paper does not say which other Φ_d divide it. Your formula gives 2 + 3 + 5 = 10.* The
  same sum is (5) on p. 6 of Poonen–Rubinstein and the line "6, (R₅ : R₃), 1" of their Table 1, p. 7.

## 6. The answers

**1. Is Claim U in print?** Not that I can find, in any of the forms you list. Is its lower bound in print?
Only for the special cases of Section 0, item 4, and, by two lines that nobody printed, for one-generator
up-sets from KLMS Proposition 6.3. Is it false? I found no counterexample in print and none myself; the
sealed file contains a proof. Three prime factors: it fails at M = 30, D = {30}, by a witness in print
(Section 4); what else I found for three primes is in the sealed file.

**2. Beyond up-sets.** For exact patterns on pⁿq^m with m ≥ 2 I found no formula, no bound that uses a full
list of non-divisors, no table of small cases and no structure theorem. What exists:
- divisors only: KLMS Theorem 7.6, Proposition 7.2, Corollary 7.3 (MIN(S) = FIB(S) in those cases) and
  Section 8 (false in general; the smallest groups named there are Z_{p⁴q⁴} and Z_{p⁵q³} with p = 2, q = 3,
  pp. 28–29);
- one non-divisor: Łaba–Marshall Corollaries 6.4, 6.5; KLMS Proposition 5.1; KLMS Theorem 9.4 (with (T2));
  the non-divisor clause of KMSV Theorem 4.9;
- structure: one scale (de Bruijn, Lam–Leung), one block (KLMS Proposition 6.3; Coppersmith–Steinberger for
  the grid), one prime (Ma's Lemma). Nothing for a general set of scales, and KLMS Section 8 shows that
  multisets with many divisors need not be fibred scale by scale.

**The same question for sets.** Nothing in print that I can find for exact up-set patterns. The nearest is
KMSV Proposition 3.8(a), p. 10 (one scale, Z_{pq}). The sealed file says something on sets.
