Z_36 = 2²·3²: the smallest multiset known for each of the 256 patterns

Made by make_tables2.py. 'size' is the size of the smallest multiset known with exactly that pattern. 'proved least by' names the programmes that proved that nothing smaller exists: G is my solver gen.py (CP-SAT, exact integers; 60 seconds a pattern, 400 for sixteen patterns); R is the referee's programme (referee/milp.py: another formulation, solved by HiGHS, whose proofs of optimality are in floating point; run by the referee, with a limit of 120 seconds a pattern, of which none took more than 44); C marks a pattern where the referee also gave its formulation to CP-SAT, which proved in exact integers that nothing smaller exists; an empty cell means that no programme proved the size least. 'by theorem' gives a value from a theorem where there is one: (S) the least size by Theorem S of the note, for an up-set; (N) for a pattern that uses only the divisors of 2²·3 or only those of 2·3², the lower bound that the theorem for Z_{pⁿq} of minsize.pdf gives by reduction, which is the least size because a multiset of that size is on file in every such row; the program stops if a smaller multiset turns up or if a value proved by a programme differs from it. 'up-set' marks the patterns closed under multiples. 'bound' is the lower bound of Corollary P (the sum of M/c over the corners of the largest up-set inside the pattern), which for an up-set is the value of Corollary U. The multisets themselves are in witnesses-36.jsonl, each checked by exact division.
