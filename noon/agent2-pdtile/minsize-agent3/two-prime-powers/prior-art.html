0. In short

  1. Claim U is not in print that I can find, neither as a least size, nor as a lower bound, nor in an equivalent form (long fibres, grids, cuboids, clusters). No paper I saw asks for the least size of a multiset whose divisors among the Φ_d, d | pⁿq^m, are exactly an up-set, and none states the structure of the multisets whose divisors contain a general up-set.
  2. I found no counterexample in print and none myself. The sealed file exists and contains a proof of Claim U for M with two prime factors.
  3. The closest result in print is Kiss–Łaba–Marshall–Somlai, Proposition 6.3 with Lemma 6.2 (long fibre decomposition for one full block {L : N | L | M}, with nonnegative coefficients when there are two primes). It implies Claim U at once when D has a single minimal element p^a q^b with a, b ≥ 1, and it is the tool for the general case. The same paper iterates Proposition 6.3 over several blocks in the proof of its Proposition 9.5, which is the method a proof of Claim U needs; it does so for another purpose and does not state the result. The grid-level fact under Proposition 6.3 (a nonnegative two-dimensional "cyclotomic array" of any side lengths is a positive sum of fibres) is older: Coppersmith–Steinberger 2006 state it and credit Steinberger.
  4. Special cases that are in print: D = {M} (value p + q; Lam–Leung Theorem 3.3 with the non-divisor clause of Kiss–Malikiosis–Somlai–Vizer Theorem 4.9 = Malikiosis Theorem 5.2); D = all d not dividing one c with c = M/p (Ma's Lemma as in Malikiosis Lemma 10.2, KMSV Lemma 4.10); M a prime power (KLMS Lemma 1.1). Your case m = 1 is your own theorem.
  5. Three prime factors: the formula of Claim U fails for M = 30, D = {30}, and the witness is in print: Lam and Leung's six-term sum (Example 6.7) against the formula's ten. Łaba and Marshall (Example 6.3) print the Claim-U construction for D = {pq, pr, qr, pqr} with a lower bound of the largest term only.
  6. Beyond up-sets, for exact patterns on pⁿq^m with m ≥ 2, nothing is in print that I can find: no formula, no table, no structure theorem. With non-divisors not prescribed, KLMS settle MIN(S) = FIB(S) when one of the two exponent sets has at most two elements (so for every S when min(n, m) ≤ 2: M = 36, 72, 100, 108, 225) and when |S| ≤ 3, and show it false in general.
  7. I made 41 requests (26 fetches, 15 general web searches). Three sites refused or failed; de Bruijn 1953 and Steinberger 2008 could not be opened.

1. The question in the field's words

yours theirs
the pattern D S_A (the set of s with Φ_s | A), restricted to s | M; "zero set" in the Fuglede papers; "root patterns" in Malikiosis
an up-set generated by one N a "block" or "cluster" {s : N | s | M} (KLMS p. 35: "our blocks are the clusters B_l := {s ∈ D(M) : N_i | s | M_i}"; Łaba–Marshall Definition 3.1, p. 9: "A subset C ⊂ S_A is called a cyclotomic divisor cluster")
a coset of the subgroup of order M/c a "c-grid" Λ(x, c) (KLMS p. 8: "a D-grid is a coset of DZ_N ≃ Z_{N/D} in Z_N")
a coset of the subgroup of order p^α a "p^α-fiber on scale M", a "long fiber" F_{i,α} (KLMS Definition 6.1, p. 17); a "P-fiber chain" for P a top run of scales (Łaba–Londner 2022, Definition 8.2)
a coset of order p^α q^β the sumset of two long fibres (KLMS p. 18: "Λ = F_{1,α₁} ∗ F_{2,α₂}")
least size with divisors only MIN(S) (KLMS (1.3), p. 1)
least size among sets fibred on each scale FIB(S) (KLMS p. 3)
"exactly D" no name. KLMS p. 2: "To force an increase in size, we need additional assumptions on A, S, or both." Their additional assumptions are q ∤ |A|, (T1), (T2), an "unsupported divisor" (Definition 1.5, p. 4), never a full list of non-divisors

An up-set of the divisor lattice of pⁿq^m is a union of top blocks; its complement is a staircase, and your "maximal divisors not in D" are the corners of the staircase. A search for the words up-set, upper set, order filter, down-set, lower set, order ideal, upward or downward closed, divisor-closed and hereditary in the texts on disk (31 papers and one set of slides) finds none of them.

2. Kiss, Łaba, Marshall, Somlai (read in full)

G. Kiss, I. Łaba, C. Marshall, G. Somlai, Lower bounds for mask polynomials with many cyclotomic divisors, arXiv:2507.11672v2 (22 March 2026), 44 pages; Advances in Mathematics 494 (2026) 110932. I read v2 from first page to last (text of the PDF; the four figures are diagrams I did not see). v2 is the latest arXiv version; its comment says "Minor revisions, update of author information; accepted for publication by Advances in Mathematics". I fetched the journal version from the Corvinus University repository and compared: the labels of all theorems, propositions, lemmas, corollaries, examples, questions and remarks are the same, and I compared by eye Lemma 6.2, Proposition 6.3, Proposition 7.2, Corollary 7.3 and the closing remark of Section 8, which are word for word the same. Page numbers below are v2's.

Every statement for two primes or for general M, and how it bears on Claim U or on an exact least size. "Divisors only" means that no Φ_d is required not to divide, so the statement is a bound for every pattern containing S and settles no exact pattern by itself.

statement hypotheses and conclusion bearing
Lemma 1.1, p. 3 S = {p^{α₁}, …, p^{α_m}}: "MIN(S) = FIB(S) = p^m" one prime; gives Claim U for M = pⁿ (the size is a multiple of p^m)
Theorem 1.2 = 7.6, pp. 3, 22 "1 ≤ |S| ≤ 3, and … lcm(S) has at most two distinct prime factors. Then MIN(S) = FIB(S)" divisors only; no exact least size
Theorem 1.3, p. 3 "There exist finite and nonempty sets S … such that MIN(S) < FIB(S) and lcm(S) has two distinct prime factors" the S of the examples are not up-sets (Section 8 below); does not contradict Claim U
Lemma 1.9, Theorems 1.10–1.12, p. 5 unsupported divisors under (T1), (T2) not about least sizes of patterns
Proposition 2.2, p. 9 general M: Φ_N | A ⇔ all N-cuboid sums vanish ⇔ A mod N is an integer combination of N-fibres tool (de Bruijn–Rédei–Schoenberg)
Lemma 2.3, p. 9 "Assume that Φ_N | A, where N has two distinct prime factors p₁, p₂. Then A mod N is a linear combination of N-fibers with nonnegative weights" one scale; with a non-divisor clause (Section 5) it is Claim U for D = {M}
Lemma 2.4, p. 10 "Φ_N | A if and only if Φ_N | (A ∩ Λ) for every D(N)-grid Λ in Z_M" tool
Proposition 3.2, p. 10 general M: "FIB(S) = min_σ FIB(S, σ)", FIB(S, σ) = ∏ p_i^{E_i(S,σ)} evaluates the fibred bound; divisors only
Proposition 4.1, p. 12 general M: "(Truncations) … there exists a multiset A′ ∈ M(Z_{M′}) satisfying (i) A′(1) = A(1), (ii) For every N = … with Φ_N(x) | A(x), we have Φ_{N′}(X) | A′(X)" removes unused scales; keeps divisors, says nothing of non-divisors, so it cannot be used on exact patterns as it stands
Lemma 4.4, Corollaries 4.5, 4.6, pp. 13–14 the digit-deleting maps keep the divisors at the other scales tool for 4.1
Proposition 5.1, p. 15 general M: "Suppose that Φ_N | A and that p ∈ P(N) with p^α || N. Then at least one of the following holds. (1) Φ_N Φ_{N/p} ··· Φ_{N/p^α} | A. (2) … there exists some a ∈ A such that A_{ν,a} are nonempty for all ν" a non-divisor below N forces p nonempty grids; a bound of the kind |A| ≥ p, far below Claim U
Lemmas 5.2, 5.3, Corollary 5.4, pp. 16–17 flat cuboids tools
Lemma 6.2, p. 18 "Φ_L(X) | F_{i,α}(X) if and only if p_i^{n_i−α+1} | L | M" the divisors of a long fibre; with 6.3 below
Proposition 6.3, p. 18 "(Long fiber decomposition) Let M = ∏ p_i^{n_i}, and let N | M satisfy N = ∏ p_i^{n_i−α_i+1} with 1 ≤ α_i ≤ n_i. Let A ∈ M(Z_M), and assume that Φ_L(X) | A(X) for each N | L | M. Then, there exist polynomials P_i(X) ∈ Z[X] such that A(X) = P₁(X)F_{1,α₁}(X) + ··· + P_K(X)F_{K,α_K}(X) mod X^M − 1. Moreover, if A ∈ M⁺(Z_M) and K = 2, then we may assume that the polynomials P₁(X) and P₂(X) each have non-negative coefficients." the closest result. With Lemma 6.2 it implies Claim U when D has one minimal element p^a q^b, a, b ≥ 1 (two lines); it is the structure theorem for one block. It does not treat a union of blocks, and no corollary about non-divisors is drawn
Theorem 7.1, p. 19 (= Łaba–Marshall Theorem 1.2) two primes, "q ∤ |A|. Then |A| ≥ p^{E₁} = FIB(S, 1) ≥ FIB(S)" for an up-set it gives one term of the sum of Claim U (the corner with the largest power of q), and only when q ∤ |A|
Proposition 7.2, p. 20 two primes; for all α′ < α in EXP₁(S), β′ < β in EXP₂(S): "if p^α q^β ∈ S and p^{α′} q^{β′} ∉ S, then {p^α q^{β′}, p^{α′} q^β} ∩ S ≠ ∅. Then |A| ≥ FIB(S)" divisors only. Its hypothesis (7.1) fails for some up-sets: in p³q³ take S generated by pq³, p²q², p³q and (α, β) = (2, 2), (α′, β′) = (1, 1). So even MIN(S) for such an up-set is not given by a stated theorem of the paper
Corollary 7.3, p. 20 "|EXP_i(S)| ≤ 2 for some i ∈ {1, 2}. Then |A| ≥ FIB(S)" divisors only; covers every S when min(n, m) ≤ 2
Propositions 7.4, 7.5, pp. 21–22 Φ_{p^{m₁}} ··· Φ_{p^{m_r}} Φ_{p^α q^β} Φ_{q^γ} | A with the exponents in one of two orders: "|A| ≥ p^r q min(p, q)" divisors only; these S are not up-sets
Lemma 7.7, p. 23 general M, "s_j | D(s_{j+1})": "MIN(S) ≥ ∏ min_{i: p_i | s_j} p_i = FIB(S)" divisors only, a chain of scales; not up-sets
Theorem 7.8, p. 24 general M with p_K > ··· > p₂ > p₁^{n₁}: "|A| ≥ p₁^{E₁}" divisors only
Example 8.1, p. 25 three primes, 2 + p₂ = p₃, S = {p₁p₂, p₃}: MIN(S) = p₃ < FIB(S) three primes; S is not an up-set
Proposition 8.2, p. 26 "M = pⁿq^m with n ≥ 9 and m ≥ 6, and let p = 2, q = 3. Then there exists a set A ⊂ Z_M such that Φ_{pⁿ}Φ_{p^{n−1}}Φ_{p^{n−2}}Φ_{q^m}Φ_{q^{m−1}}Φ_{q^{m−2}}Φ_{pq} | A and |A| = p³q³"; p. 27: "There is no such construction with |A| equal to 2² · 3³ or any of its divisors" divisors only; S is not an up-set (Φ_{pq} with the top prime powers); the remark on 2²·3³ is a statement of non-existence at given sizes, given without proof

Proposition 8.4, p. 28 "M = p⁴q⁴, p = 2, q = 3. There exists a set A ⊂ Z_M such that Φ_pΦ_{p²}Φ_{p³}Φ_qΦ_{q²}Φ_M | A and |A| = p³q² = 72" a construction; not an up-set; its exact pattern is not stated
Lemmas 8.5, 8.6, Theorem 8.7, p. 29 odd p, q: a set of size p^a q^b with Φ_p ··· Φ_{p^a} Φ_q ··· Φ_{q^b} Φ_M | A the same

None of these gives Claim U; none contradicts it. What would have to be checked to pass from Proposition 6.3 to Claim U is how the decomposition for one block restricts to the remaining blocks; that is done in the sealed file.

3. Łaba and Marshall (read in full)

I. Łaba, C. Marshall, Vanishing sums of roots of unity and the Favard length of self-similar product sets, arXiv:2202.07555v3 (15 December 2022), Discrete Analysis 2022:19, 31 pp. v3 is the latest version and is the journal's. Read in full, all sections (the drawings of Section 7 not seen).

  • Theorem 1.2, p. 3 (= Proposition 5.1, p. 16): "Assume further that q ∤ A(1). Then we have the lower bound A(1) ≥ p^{E_p}, where E_p = |{α₁, ..., α_k}|." A bound: one term of Claim U's sum, when q ∤ |A|.
  • Sections 2, 3 (Proposition 2.1, Lemmas 3.2, 3.3, 3.4, 3.6) build sets of large values for the Favard problem from clusters of divisors. Not relevant to least sizes.
  • Lemma 4.3, Lemma 4.4, p. 15: the two-prime positivity and restriction to grids. Tools.
  • Section 6.1, p. 18: an example with Φ_{p¹⁰q¹⁰} Φ_{pq} | A and "|A| = p² + q²", a sum of two products of fibres. Section 6.2, p. 18: the long fibre, and "Translates of such fibers in both directions can be added to construct more complicated examples." The upper-bound construction of Claim U is of this kind; no minimality is claimed.
  • Lemma 6.1, p. 19; Lemma 6.2, p. 20; Proposition 6.3, p. 21: "p_i^ℓ | A^{m₀}[∆] for any m₀-cuboid ∆" when Φ_{m₀p_i^{α_j}} | A for ℓ exponents.
  • Corollary 6.4, p. 22: "there exists α₀ with 0 ≤ α₀ < α₁ such that Φ_{m} ∤ A, where m = m₀p_i^{α₀}. Then |A| ≥ p_i^ℓ." A bound that uses a non-divisor: for a maximal non-member c = p^u q^v of an up-set it gives |A| ≥ p^{n−u} and |A| ≥ q^{m−v}, against the term M/c = p^{n−u}q^{m−v} of Claim U.
  • Corollary 6.5, p. 22: "Assume further that Φ_{p_i m} | A for i = 1, ..., I, but Φ_m ∤ A. Then A(1) ≥ p₁ ... p_I." A bound that uses a non-divisor: |A| ≥ pq for a maximal non-member with both exponents below the top. Remark 6.1, p. 23, says a stronger version follows from Proposition 6.3 and omits it.
  • Example 6.3, p. 23, three primes: for {pq, pr, qr} ⊂ S_A and (|A|, pqr) = 1, "|A| > max(pq, pr, qr)", and "The following example shows that the lower bound in (6.10) is essentially optimal up to a multiplicative constant. Define A ⊂ Z_M, where pqr | M, so that A = (a ∗ F_p ∗ F_q) ∪ (a′ ∗ F_q ∗ F_r) ∪ (a″ ∗ F_p ∗ F_r). […] Then (6.8) holds, and |A| = pq + pr + qr". This is the construction of Claim U for the up-set generated by pq, pr, qr in print, with three primes; the lower bound proved is the largest term, and the sum is not claimed to be least.
  • Section 6.4, p. 19: "Our current methods are not sufficient to extend Theorem 1.2 to the case when more than two distinct prime factors are allowed."
  • Section 7 (Lemma 7.2 and its Claim, pp. 26–28): sets of at most ten elements, by the tables of minimal vanishing sums. Not relevant.

Nothing in the paper gives or contradicts Claim U.

4. Lam and Leung (read in full)

T. Y. Lam, K. H. Leung, On vanishing sums of roots of unity, arXiv:math/9511209v1 (13 November 1995, the only version; listed by arXiv as "On vanishing sums for roots of unity"), J. Algebra 224 (2000) 91–109. Read in full, 18 pages. The journal version was not compared.

  • Main Theorem, p. 2 (= Theorem 5.2, p. 12): "the weight set W(m) is exactly given by N p₁ + ··· + N p_r."
  • Theorem 3.1, p. 6: "N G ∩ ker(ϕ) = Σ_j g_j (N G₀ ∩ ker(ϕ))", G₀ the subgroup of square-free order: a nonnegative vanishing sum splits over the cosets of G₀ (the restriction to grids).
  • What they say for two prime factors. Theorem 3.3, p. 7: "(2) If r = 2, N G ∩ ker(ϕ) = N P₁ · σ(P₂) + N P₂ · σ(P₁)." (credited: "This result can be traced back to the work of de Bruijn [DeB: §3]"). Corollary 3.4, p. 8: "Let m = p^a q^b, where p, q are primes. Then, up to a rotation, the only minimal vanishing sums of m th roots of unity are: 1 + ζ_p + ··· + ζ_p^{p−1} = 0, and 1 + ζ_q + ··· + ζ_q^{q−1} = 0." p. 8: "In the case r ≤ 2, (3.3) implies that all minimal elements in N G ∩ ker(ϕ) are symmetric." One scale only: the divisor Φ_m. With the non-divisor clause of Section 5 this is Claim U for D = {M}. Nothing on several divisors at once.
  • Three or more primes. Example 2.5, p. 5 (after Rédei): the sum (α + ··· + α^{p−1})(β + ··· + β^{q−1}) + γ + ··· + γ^{ℓ−1} = 0 is minimal and not a rotated fibre. Theorem 4.8, p. 11: "For any minimal element x ∈ N G ∩ ker(ϕ), we have either (A) x is symmetric, or (B) r ≥ 3 and ε(x) ≥ ε₀(x) ≥ p₁(p₂ − 1) + p₃ − p₂ > p₃." Theorem 6.5, p. 15: the minimal asymmetric element of that weight is unique up to rotation. Remark 5.3, p. 13: for |G| = 30 the element (5.5) has no representation with nonnegative total weights. Example 6.7, p. 16: "x(G) = t(h + h²) + g + g² + g³ + g⁴ = z⁵ + z⁶ + z¹² + z¹⁸ + z²⁴ + z²⁵, with weight (p₁ − 1)(p₂ − 1) + p₃ − 1 = 6." This is the witness that Claim U's formula fails for M = 30, D = {30}: the multiset {5, 6, 12, 18, 24, 25} of Z₃₀ has six elements, and I checked by exact division that Φ₃₀ is its only divisor among the Φ_d, d | 30, d > 1. The paper does not say which other Φ_d divide it. Your formula gives 2 + 3 + 5 = 10. The same sum is (5) on p. 6 of Poonen–Rubinstein and the line "6, (R₅ : R₃), 1" of their Table 1, p. 7.

  • R. D. Malikiosis, On the structure of spectral and tiling subsets of cyclic groups, arXiv:2005.05800v3 (22 March 2022; Forum Math. Sigma 10 (2022) e23). 6. The answers

1. Is Claim U in print? Not that I can find, in any of the forms you list. Is its lower bound in print? Only for the special cases of Section 0, item 4, and, by two lines that nobody printed, for one-generator up-sets from KLMS Proposition 6.3. Is it false? I found no counterexample in print and none myself; the sealed file contains a proof. Three prime factors: it fails at M = 30, D = {30}, by a witness in print (Section 4); what else I found for three primes is in the sealed file.

2. Beyond up-sets. For exact patterns on pⁿq^m with m ≥ 2 I found no formula, no bound that uses a full list of non-divisors, no table of small cases and no structure theorem. What exists: - divisors only: KLMS Theorem 7.6, Proposition 7.2, Corollary 7.3 (MIN(S) = FIB(S) in those cases) and Section 8 (false in general; the smallest groups named there are Z_{p⁴q⁴} and Z_{p⁵q³} with p = 2, q = 3, pp. 28–29); - one non-divisor: Łaba–Marshall Corollaries 6.4, 6.5; KLMS Proposition 5.1; KLMS Theorem 9.4 (with (T2)); the non-divisor clause of KMSV Theorem 4.9; - structure: one scale (de Bruijn, Lam–Leung), one block (KLMS Proposition 6.3; Coppersmith–Steinberger for the grid), one prime (Ma's Lemma). Nothing for a general set of scales, and KLMS Section 8 shows that multisets with many divisors need not be fibred scale by scale.

The same question for sets. Nothing in print that I can find for exact up-set patterns. The nearest is KMSV Proposition 3.8(a), p. 10 (one scale, Z_{pq}). The sealed file says something on sets.
