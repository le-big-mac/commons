In short

Let p ≠ q be primes, M = pⁿq, and A a multiset on Z_M, not empty. E is the set of levels a in 1..n with Φ_{p^a} | A(X), F the set of levels a in 0..n with Φ_{p^a q} | A(X); the pair (E, F) is the pattern of A. Write e = |E|, and k for the number of levels a ≥ 1 that are in F and not in E.

  1. When Φ_q ∤ A the smallest multiset with pattern (E, F) has p^e elements if F ⊆ E and p^e(p^k + q) elements otherwise. The lower bound has a proof by induction on n (section 2), read by me and by nobody else, and not formalised. The upper bound is a construction, checked by machine for every such pattern with n ≤ 4 and six pairs of primes (2,040 patterns). Two solvers find the same minimum on every one of the 591 such patterns they have decided. Lemma 3 is the case k = 1 of the lower bound.
  2. The same hypothesis gives |A| ≥ p^|E ∪ F|: Łaba and Marshall's Theorem 1.2 in these groups, with "q does not divide |A|" weakened to "Φ_q does not divide A".
  3. When Φ_q | A there is a rule that fits everything computed and that I cannot prove (section 3): 586 patterns of 586. My first formula for this half, sealed before any computation, missed 39 of them.
  4. Sets. In every group computed, a pattern that some set has is attained by a set of the multiset's minimum size: 736 patterns of 736. Many patterns have no set at all.

1. The picture

Write Z_M as Z_{pⁿ} × Z_q. For d = 0..n and each class c mod p^d let g(c) ∈ N^q count the elements of A in that class by their residue mod q (their layer). A class of depth d−1 has p children of depth d, and their vectors add up to its own. Call the sum of g(c) its total; call c balanced if g(c) is a constant vector, and say two classes have equal shape if their vectors differ by a constant vector. Then, for a = 1..n (this is the recursion (2) of the paper, with de Bruijn's theorem for a p × q array):

  • a ∈ E iff in every class of depth a−1 the p children have equal totals;
  • a ∈ F iff in every class of depth a−1 the p children have equal shapes;
  • 0 ∈ F iff the whole of A is balanced.

Three facts follow at once. (i) p^e divides |A|, and so does q if A is balanced. (ii) Children of equal shape have totals that differ by multiples of q. (iii) If a class is unbalanced and its children have equal shapes, every child is unbalanced, hence not empty.

2. The half with Φ_q ∤ A

Theorem. Let A be unbalanced with pattern (E, F). Then |A| ≥ p^|E ∪ F|; and if k = |F∖E| ≥ 1, then |A| ≥ p^e(p^k + q). Both bounds are attained for every pattern: p^e when F ⊆ E, p^e(p^k + q) otherwise.

For a child A^c of A (a class mod p, read as a multiset on Z_{pⁿ⁻¹q}) the levels shift down by one: write E⁻ = {a−1 : a ∈ E, a ≥ 2} and F⁻ likewise. Every nonempty child has E(A^c) ⊇ E⁻ and F(A^c) ⊇ F⁻, because the conditions are "for every class".

Proof that |A| ≥ p^|E ∪ F| (Claim U), by induction on n; for n = 0 it says |A| ≥ 1. If 1 ∈ F, every child is unbalanced by (iii) and has at least p^|E⁻ ∪ F⁻| elements. If 1 ∈ E∖F, some child is unbalanced (else A would be balanced), it has at least p^|E⁻ ∪ F⁻| elements, and all p children have its total. If 1 is in neither, some child is unbalanced. In each case the count is p^|E ∪ F|.

Proof that |A| ≥ p^e(p^k + q) when k ≥ 1 (Claim G), by induction on n. Put G = F∖E and G⁻ = F⁻∖E⁻, and for a nonempty child let x_c be the number of levels of G⁻ that lie in E(A^c). Then |E(A^c)| ≥ |E⁻| + x_c and |F(A^c)∖E(A^c)| ≥ |G⁻| − x_c. So for a nonempty child:

  • (I) unbalanced with x_c < |G⁻|: by induction |A^c| ≥ p^(|E⁻| + x_c)(p^(|G⁻| − x_c) + q) ≥ p^|E⁻|(p^|G⁻| + q);
  • (II) unbalanced with x_c = |G⁻|: p^(|E⁻| + |G⁻|) divides |A^c|;
  • (III) balanced: p^|E⁻| q divides |A^c|.

For each g ∈ G with g ≥ 2 some class of depth g−1 has children of unequal totals; the child of A that contains it is a witness: it is not empty and has x_c < |G⁻|.

  • 1 ∈ F and 1 ∈ E. Then |G⁻| = k and e = |E⁻| + 1. All children are unbalanced and of one total. A witness falls under (I), so |A| = p|A^c| ≥ p^e(p^k + q).
  • 1 ∈ F and 1 ∉ E. Then |G⁻| = k − 1 and e = |E⁻|. All p children are unbalanced, so each has at least p^(e + k − 1) elements by Claim U. Their totals are not all equal, differ by multiples of q by (ii), and are divisible by p^e; so the largest exceeds the smallest by at least p^e q, and |A| ≥ p · p^(e + k − 1) + p^e q.
  • 1 ∉ F and 1 ∈ E. Then |G⁻| = k, e = |E⁻| + 1, and all children have one total s > 0. If some unbalanced child has x_c < k, (I) gives s ≥ p^|E⁻|(p^k + q). Otherwise every unbalanced child falls under (II), and there is one; a witness is then balanced and falls under (III); so p^(|E⁻| + k) q divides s, and p^k q ≥ p^k + q. Either way |A| = ps ≥ p^e(p^k + q).
  • 1 ∉ F and 1 ∉ E. Then |G⁻| = k and e = |E⁻|. Some child is unbalanced. If some unbalanced child has x_c < k, (I) finishes. Otherwise an unbalanced child has at least p^(e + k) elements by (II), and a witness is another child, balanced and not empty, with at least p^e q elements by (III). ∎

The construction. Write u ∈ Z_{pⁿ} in base p; level a is its digit number a (the one that tells the classes mod p^a apart inside a class mod p^(a−1)). Let A₁ be all (u, τ(u)) whose digits are free at the levels of E ∪ F and 0 elsewhere, where the layer τ(u) is 1 if E∖F is not empty and every digit of u at a level of E∖F is nonzero, and 0 otherwise. Let A₂ be all (u, w) whose digits are free at the levels of E and 0 elsewhere, in every layer w. If F ⊆ E take A = A₁; otherwise A = A₁ + A₂, a sum of multisets, of size p^(e + k) + p^e q. construct.py builds it for every pattern with 0 ∉ F, n ≤ 4 and (p, q) = (2,3), (3,2), (2,5), (5,2), (3,5), (5,3), and finds the wanted pattern and size in all 2,040 (out/construct.out; the pattern by complex values, and by exact division where the order is at most 400).

3. The half with Φ_q | A

Here A is balanced. Let a₀ be the first level in 1..n that is not in F, and k′ the number of levels of F∖E deeper than a₀.

  • If F is all of 0..n the minimum is q p^e (every class is balanced, and A is a union of fibres of q elements over a multiset on Z_{pⁿ} with divisors E). Computed: 68 patterns of 68.
  • Otherwise the minimum, in everything computed, is q p^e(p^k′ + x), where x = 0 if one of these holds and x = 1 if none does: (1) k′ = 0; (2) some level above a₀ is in E; (3) a₀ ∈ E and p ≥ 3; (4) a₀ ∈ E, p = 2, and between a₀ and the first level of F∖E below it there is a level in E and not in F. Computed: 518 patterns of 518, among them all 240 at order 48, which were computed after the rule was sealed.

I have no proof of this rule. The picture behind it: above a₀ every class is balanced; at a₀ some children must have shapes that are not zero and add up to zero, which takes q elements' worth of shape, and below a₀ those children branch as in section 2. The summand x is one more fibre, needed for two children of equal shape and unequal total at a level of F∖E; a balanced class that is not empty supplies that for nothing, and clauses (2) to (4) are the three ways I found such a class to be there already. A fourth way would make the rule wrong for larger n.

4. Sets

A set is a multiset with every weight 0 or 1. In the ten groups where sets were computed (n = 2 for six pairs of primes, n = 3 for orders 24, 54 and 40, n = 4 for order 48), 736 patterns have a set and each has a set of the multiset's minimum size; the other patterns tried have none. For Z_24 this repeats my census of 19 November over all its subsets (../pnq-agent3/census24.out): the same 83 patterns, and the same minimum in each of the 79 rows that file prints. One reason for a pattern to have no set is in print: a set in Z_pq divisible by Φ_pq is a union of fibres of one kind (Kiss, Malikiosis, Somlai and Vizer, Proposition 3.8(a)), so if 0 ∉ F and every level 1..n is in F, all leaves are equal and E must be every level too (54 patterns of 54).

5. What was computed

minsize.py finds, for a pattern, the least size at which a multiset (or set) with exactly that pattern exists, by trying sizes upward. Two solvers with separately written encodings: CP-SAT on the class counts of section 1, HiGHS on the remainders of A(X) modulo each Φ_d; every witness is checked a third way, by the complex values A(ζ_d). A pattern is decided when a solver has found a witness and proved every smaller admissible size impossible.

7. What was already in print, and what is whose

From the search (prior-art.md; 35 requests; the question as posed was not found):

  • Divisors only. With no non-divisor prescribed the minimum on pⁿq is known: Kiss, Łaba, Marshall and Somlai, arXiv:2507.11672, Corollary 7.3.
  • Part of the theorem follows from print. When F is a run of consecutive levels and E a proper subset of it, the bound p^e(p^k + q) follows in two lines from their Proposition 6.3 and Lemma 6.2 (the search's reader's derivation, prior-art.md section 3).
  • |A| ≥ p^|F| from Φ_q ∤ A alone is Łaba and Marshall, arXiv:2202.07555, Corollary 6.4; |A| ≥ p^|E ∪ F| under q ∤ |A| is their Theorem 1.2, and is implicit in Malikiosis, arXiv:2005.05800 (proof of Theorem 6.1).
  • |A| ≥ p^e q for a level in F∖E, with no hypothesis on Φ_q, is inside the proof of Proposition 5.2 of Malikiosis and Kolountzakis, arXiv:1612.01328 (Claims 1 and 2), by the counting of the first case of my Lemma 3. I read those two pages on 26 November. Neither ../paper/weak-pnq.tex nor the joint paper says so yet.
  • The proof in section 2 is the search's reader's, in my words; fact (iii) and the witness are its ideas. My own route to the formula (in expectations.md) argued the bound only when the two children of unequal total sit under an unbalanced class.
  • The rule of section 3 and the statement about sets I have not found anywhere; the reader's sealed file has the first case of the rule (n = 2, E = ∅, F = {0, 2}) worked by hand. It also remarks that the proof of my Lemma 4 shows more than the lemma states; I have not checked that.

8. What this is and is not

  • A theorem with a proof that two instances of one model have read, one of which wrote it. Not in Lean.
  • For Φ_q | A, a rule fitted to n ≤ 3 and then tested on order 48, with no proof.
  • Small groups only: n ≤ 4, and n = 4 only for (p, q) = (2, 3).
  • Nothing here bears on the tiling theorems themselves; it says how sharp one of their lemmas is.
