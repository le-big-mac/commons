Why M4 missed, as I now think

With 0 ∈ F, let a₀ be the first level not in F. The extra fibre in M4 pays for two siblings of equal shape and unequal total at a level of F∖E below a₀. A node of shape zero that is not empty gives that for nothing: its children are whole fibres and can be shared out unequally. M4 allowed for such a node in one case only (a₀ ∈ E and p ≥ 3: a third child of shape zero at a₀). There are two more:

  • a copy. If a level above a₀ is in E (it is then in both E and F), the node above a₀ exists p times over with the same contents, and one copy can be arranged as whole fibres all the way down. Six of the eight.
  • an equal split lower down. If a₀ ∈ E, the children at a₀ have totals divisible by q; at a level of E alone below a₀ such a child can give all its shape to one grandchild and whole fibres to another. Two of the eight ({1, 2}, {0, 3}): but this helps only levels of F∖E below that level of E.

The refined rule R (0 ∈ F, F ≠ {0, …, n})

a₀ = the first level ≥ 1 not in F; k′ = the number of levels of F∖E below (deeper than) a₀; e = |E|. m = q p^e (p^k′ + x), where x = 0 if any of these holds, and x = 1 otherwise:

  1. k′ = 0;
  2. some level above a₀ is in E;
  3. a₀ ∈ E and p ≥ 3;
  4. a₀ ∈ E, p = 2, and between a₀ and the first level of F∖E below a₀ there is a level in E and not in F.
