1. What is claimed

Terminology is that of Kiss, Londner, Matolcsi and Somlai, Functional tilings and the Coven–Meyerowitz tiling conditions (arXiv:2411.03854, Discrete Analysis 2025:18), below [KLMS]. A set A ⊂ Z_M pd-tiles Z_M if there is f : Z_M → R with f(0) = 1, f ≥ 0, f̂ ≥ 0 and 1_A ∗ f = 1. Their Problem 1.1 asks whether every set that pd-tiles Z_M tiles it properly. It is known for prime powers [KLMS, Prop. 4.1] and for M = pq (Kadir and Fan, arXiv:2607.02149, July 2026, who ask about Z_{pⁿq} and Z_{pqr}). It is false in some non-cyclic groups (Kiss–Londner–Matolcsi–Somlai, A lonely weak tile, arXiv:2410.04948).

Claim 1 (computer-assisted). Every set that pd-tiles Z_M satisfies the Coven–Meyerowitz conditions (T1) and (T2), read relative to Z_M (Section 2), and therefore tiles Z_M, for the following M, in two tiers:

  • 2 ≤ M ≤ 200: established by two different encodings of the search, neither using Proposition 3 (the counts encoding for all 199 moduli, the residues encoding for 113 of them including 144);
  • 201 ≤ M ≤ RANGE_MAX and M = EXTRA_MODULI: established by one model only, which relies on Proposition 3 and on its implementation (tested on small cases by me, and by Yonder on 588 explicit tiles at fifteen moduli from 180 to 2025, thirteen of them in this tier; Section 7).

In neither tier is a solver's "infeasible" a certificate. The range includes M = 144 = 2⁴·3², the smallest modulus covered by [KLMS, Theorem 1.6], where functions f, g exist that pd-tile each other while violating (T2), and M = 2025, the next one. Claim 1 is the part of [KLMS] Problem 1.2 that concerns the set A, as well as Problem 1.1, on this range; it settles open problem (1) of Kadir–Fan for every p^n q and pqr in the range, and Propositions 1–2 are a computational answer to their open problem (2).

Claim 2 (proved by hand, Section 6; machine-checked for several prime pairs). Let p ≠ q be primes and M = p⁴q². No set of p²q elements of Z_M has its mask polynomial divisible by all of Φ_q, Φ_{p⁴}, Φ_{p⁴q}, Φ_{p³q²}, Φ_{p²q²}; and none has it divisible by all of Φ_{p²}, Φ_{p³}, Φ_{q²}, Φ_{pq}, Φ_{pq²}, Φ_{p²q}. Consequently, for the functional pd-tiling (f, g) of [KLMS, Theorem 1.6] (any p < q < p²): neither f nor g is the averaged autocorrelation h_A of a set, and neither is a pd-tiling complement of a set. [KLMS] leave this open ("in principle, it could be possible that one of the functions originates from a set").

I do not know whether either claim is already known to the authors or others. I searched and found nothing, which is weak evidence.

A remark due to Yonder, checked by me (Section 6, end): the other half of [KLMS] Problem 1.2, read literally — that every pd-tiling complement F of a set also satisfies (T1) — already fails at M = 6.

2. The criterion

For A ⊂ Z_M write Z_A = {d | M, d > 1 : Φ_d(X) divides A(X)}. For a set Z of divisors d > 1 of M let H(Z) be {0} together with all elements of Z_M of order d, d ∈ Z (a union of step classes), and let D⁺(Z) be the Delsarte parameter D⁺(H(Z)) of [KLMS, Def. 2.4]: the maximum of Σ_x h(x) over step functions h with h(0) = 1, h ≥ 0, h = 0 off H(Z), ĥ ≥ 0. Let k(Z) be the product of p over the prime powers p^a in Z.

Proposition 1. For every A ⊂ Z_M: (i) D⁺(Z_A) ≤ |A|; (ii) A pd-tiles Z_M if and only if D⁺(Z_A) = |A|; (iii) k(Z_A) divides |A|.

Proof. (i) Let v = |1̂A|²/|A|². Then v(0) = 1, v ≥ 0, v vanishes exactly on the elements whose order lies in Z_A, and v̂ = (M/|A|²)·(1_A ∗ 1{−A}) ≥ 0, with Σv = v̂(0) = M/|A|. Let h be admissible for D⁺(Z_A). The supports of h and v meet only in 0, so Σ_x h(x)v(x) = 1. By Parseval Σ_x h(x)v(x) = (1/M)Σ_ξ ĥ(ξ)·conj(v̂(ξ)) ≥ (1/M)ĥ(0)v̂(0), all terms being nonnegative. Hence Σh ≤ M/Σv = |A|. (This is the easy half of the duality [KLMS, Prop. 2.6].) (ii) If 1_A ∗ f = 1 with f as in the definition, replace f by its average over the maps x ↦ rx, r a unit; this is again a pd-tiling complement, because the zero set of 1̂_A is invariant under units. Now f̂ is a step function vanishing wherever 1̂_A ≠ 0 (other than at 0), and f̂(0) = M/|A|. So h = (|A|/M)·f̂ is admissible for D⁺(Z_A): h(0) = 1, h ≥ 0, ĥ = |A|·f(−·) ≥ 0, and Σh = ĥ(0) = |A| f(0) = |A|. With (i), D⁺(Z_A) = |A|. Conversely, if h is admissible with Σh = |A|, put f = (1/|A|)·ĥ(−·); then f ≥ 0, f(0) = Σh/|A| = 1, f̂ = (M/|A|)h ≥ 0 vanishes where 1̂_A does not (off 0) and f̂(0) = M/|A|, so 1̂_A f̂ = M δ₀, i.e. 1_A ∗ f = 1. (iii) The Φ_s, s ∈ Z_A a prime power, are distinct monic irreducibles dividing A(X) in Z[X], so A(X) = R(X)·ΠΦ_s(X) with R ∈ Z[X], and A(1) = R(1)·ΠΦ_s(1) = R(1)·k(Z_A). ∎

The argument for (ii) is the one in the proof of [KLMS, Prop. 4.1]; I only state it for general M.

(T1) and (T2) for a subset of Z_M. Throughout, (T1) for A ⊂ Z_M means k(Z_A) = |A|, and (T2) means that every d | M with at least two prime factors, all of whose exact prime-power factors lie in Z_A, lies in Z_A. These are exactly the conditions [KLMS] define for functions on Z_M (the paragraph after their Theorem 1.2, where S_F is restricted to prime powers dividing M), specialised to 1_A. They imply the conditions of Coven and Meyerowitz for A regarded as a set of integers in {0,…,M−1}: their set S_A consists of all prime powers s with Φ_s | A(X), whether or not s divides M, but the product of Φ_s(1) over all of them divides A(1) = |A| (as in Prop. 1(iii)), so once k(Z_A) = |A| no prime power outside the divisors of M can occur, and S_A is the set of prime powers in Z_A. The converse fails (Catalpa): A = {0, 1} ⊂ Z_3 has A(X) = Φ_2(X), so it satisfies Coven–Meyerowitz's conditions as a set of integers, while k(Z_A) = 1 ≠ 2 and A does not tile Z_3. Only the implication is used.

D⁺ is monotone in Z. So A pd-tiles if and only if Z_A contains some minimal pattern of size n = |A|: a set Z₀ with D⁺(Z₀) = n such that removing any one element makes D⁺ smaller.

Proposition 2. Fix M. Suppose that for every minimal pattern (Z₀, n) one of the following holds: (a) impossible: k(Z₀) does not divide n; (b) safe: k(Z₀) = n, and Z₀ contains every d | M with at least two prime factors all of whose exact prime-power factors lie in Z₀; (c) searched: there is no set A ∋ 0 with |A| = n, Z_A ⊇ Z₀, violating (T1) or (T2). Then every set that pd-tiles Z_M satisfies (T1) and (T2) and tiles Z_M.

Proof. Let A pd-tile; translate so that 0 ∈ A. Z_A contains a minimal pattern Z₀ of size n = |A|. Case (a) contradicts Prop. 1(iii), since k(Z₀) | k(Z_A). In case (b), k(Z_A) is a multiple of k(Z₀) = n dividing n, so k(Z_A) = n, which is (T1), and the prime powers of Z_A are exactly those of Z₀, so (T2) for A follows from the closure of Z₀. In case (c) there is nothing to prove. Finally (T1) and (T2) imply that A tiles Z_M, by the standard complement taken with period M (this self-contained form of the step is Yonder's). For a prime power s = p^a | M put t(s) = M/p^{v_p(M)} and let B(X) = ΠΦ_s(X^{t(s)}), the product over the prime powers s | M that are not in Z_A. B has nonnegative integer coefficients and B(1) = M/k(Z_A) = M/|A|. Since Φ_s(X^t) = Π_{e | t}Φ_{se}(X) when p ∤ t, every d | M, d > 1, has Φ_d | A or Φ_d | B: if all exact prime-power factors of d lie in Z_A then Φ_d | A, by (T2) or because d is itself one of them; otherwise some exact factor s of d is not in Z_A, d/s divides t(s), and Φ_d | Φ_s(X^{t(s)}). So A(X)B(X) vanishes at every M-th root of unity other than 1, hence is c·(1 + X + … + X^{M−1}) modulo X^M − 1 with c = A(1)B(1)/M = 1. Therefore B is a set and A ⊕ B = Z_M. ∎

Proposition 3 (forbidden differences). Let Z₀ be a pattern with D⁺(Z₀) = n and let h be any optimal function for it. If A is a set with |A| = n and Z_A ⊇ Z₀, then f = ĥ/n is a pd-tiling complement of A, and so f(a − a′) = 0 for all distinct a, a′ ∈ A. Consequently no two elements of A differ by an element of order e, for any e such that some optimal h has ĥ > 0 on the elements of order e.

Proof. h is admissible for D⁺(Z_A) with Σh = n, so f is a pd-tiling complement by the converse half of Prop. 1(ii) (ĥ is even, being a step function). For a ∈ A, Σ_{a′∈A} f(a − a′) = 1; the term a′ = a is f(0) = 1 and the others are nonnegative. ∎

Two further consequences are used below. Covering equations: with f as in Proposition 3, Σ_{a∈A} f(z − a) = 1 for every z ∈ Z_M; this is 1_A ∗ f = 1 written out, linear in the indicator of A, with exact rational coefficients once h is fixed. Class-size caps: the elements of A in one residue class mod D (D | M) differ pairwise by elements whose orders divide M/D and are not forbidden; so they form a clique in a Cayley graph on Z_{M/D} and their number is at most ⌊D⁺⌋ for that set of orders in Z_{M/D} [KLMS, Prop. 2.5].

So a positive answer to Problem 1.1 for a given M follows from a finite list of minimal patterns and, for those that are neither impossible nor safe, a search over sets, in which Proposition 3 may be used to cut the search down. (Proposition 3 and its consequences need only D⁺(Z₀) = |A| and Z₀ ⊂ Z_A, not minimality.) This is a sufficient test, not quite a decision procedure: if a search did return a set, that set would pd-tile and violate (T1) or (T2). It would then fail to tile if it violated (T1): if A ⊕ B = Z_M, every Φ_s with s | M a prime power divides A or B, so k(Z_A)·k(Z_B) ≥ Π_{s | M}Φ_s(1) = M = |A||B| ≥ k(Z_A)·k(Z_B) by Prop. 1(iii), whence k(Z_A) = |A| (Catalpa, Yonder). It would also fail to tile if it violated (T2) and M has at most two prime factors: a tile of Z_M tiles Z, |A| divides M, and the third part of the Coven–Meyerowitz theorem gives their (T2), which is ours once (T1) holds. Otherwise it would need a direct tiling test. No search has returned a set.

3. The computation

  1. Scan (lp.py, scan.py). D⁺(Z) for every set Z of divisors, by an exact simplex method over the rationals. The transform of the indicator of the elements of order d, evaluated at an element of order e, is the Ramanujan sum c_d(M/e).
  2. Minimal patterns (minimal.py) and their classification as impossible / safe / to be searched. The last kind comes in two flavours: T1-short (k(Z₀) < n) and T2-open (k(Z₀) = n but a required product is missing from Z₀).
  3. Search over sets (sat.py, OR-tools CP-SAT, exact integer arithmetic). Variables x₀,…,x_{M−1} ∈ {0,1}, x₀ = 1, Σx = n; Φ_d | A for d ∈ Z₀; and a violation: either for some prime p the number of prime powers p^a with Φ_{p^a} | A is smaller than the exponent of p in n, or for some d with two or more prime factors all exact prime-power factors s of d have Φ_s | A while Φ_d ∤ A. (If n has a prime factor not dividing M, (T1) cannot hold and any set with the zeros is a violation.) Three models were run: - residues: Φ_d | A is stated as A(X) mod Φ_d(X) = 0; - counts: Φ_d | A is stated through the numbers of elements of A in residue classes (Section 6, Facts 1–2, and their analogue for three or more primes: in every box of side lengths p_i all mixed differences of the count function vanish); - full: the counts model plus the three consequences of Proposition 3 — forbidden differences (the set of forbidden orders is computed exactly in lp.py: for each order e, maximise ĥ(e) over the optimal face of the linear program), class-size caps, and the covering equations for one optimal h. All three also carry the constraint that the number of prime powers p^a with Φ_{p^a} | A is at most the exponent of p in n (Prop. 1(iii)). It was added late: the full model's results for 2–200 and the first 176 moduli of the counts model were obtained without it, everything later with it. The three describe the same question and are satisfiable for the same sets, but behave very differently in the solver: patterns on which the residues model runs for ten minutes or times out are typically settled by the full model in under a second. The extra constraints of the full model are valid only because D⁺(Z₀) = |A| and Z₀ ⊂ Z_A; they are not part of the definition of the question. Note what this means for cross-checking (Yonder's point): the full model is the counts model with constraints added, so wherever the counts model says "infeasible" the full model must agree, and that agreement is worth nothing. The independent pair is residues and counts. The full model's results stand on their own only where the other two were not run, and there they rest on Proposition 3 and on its implementation.

6. Two lemmas

Let M = p^α q^β and A ⊂ Z_M. For 0 ≤ a ≤ α, 0 ≤ b ≤ β write N_{a,b}(r, t) for the number of x ∈ A with x ≡ r (mod p^a) and x ≡ t (mod q^b); N_{a,0}(r) and N_{0,b}(t) when only one modulus is in play.

Fact 1. Φ_{p^a} | A ⟺ N_{a,0}(r + i·p^{a−1}) = N_{a,0}(r) for all r, i.

Fact 2. For a, b ≥ 1: Φ_{p^a q^b} | A ⟺ for all r, t, i the difference N_{a,b}(r + i·p^{a−1}, t′) − N_{a,b}(r, t′) is the same for all t′ ∈ t + q^{b−1}Z. The condition is symmetric in the two primes (it says N(i, j) = u(i) + w(j) on each box), so it may equally be read with p and q exchanged.

Fact 3. If every element of A is divisible by q, A = qA′ with A′ ⊂ Z_{M/q}, then for d | M: Φ_d | A ⟺ A′ vanishes at the primitive (d/gcd(d,q))-th roots of unity. (For d = q this reads A′(1) = 0, which never holds for nonempty A′.)

Fact 4. k(Z_A) divides |A| (Prop. 1(iii)). More generally, for a finite set of nonnegative integers with polynomial A(X), the product of Φ_s(1) over all prime powers s with Φ_s | A(X) divides A(1).

Proofs. Facts 1, 2: view N = N_{a,b} as a function on G = Z_{p^a} × Z_{q^b}. Φ_{p^a q^b} | A says that N̂ vanishes at every character of G of order p^a q^b, i.e. at every character that is nontrivial on both K_p = p^{a−1}Z_{p^a} and K_q = q^{b−1}Z_{q^b}. The characters trivial on K_p span the K_p-invariant functions, likewise for K_q, so the condition is N = F₁ + F₂ with F₁ K_p-invariant and F₂ K_q-invariant. On a coset of K_p × K_q with coordinates (i, j), F₁ depends on j only and F₂ on i only; so the condition is N(i, j) = u(i) + w(j) on every such coset, which is Fact 2. Fact 1 is the case of one prime. Fact 3: A(X) = A′(X^q), and ζ ↦ ζ^q maps the primitive d-th roots of unity onto the primitive (d/gcd(d,q))-th roots. ∎

Lemma A. Let p ≠ q be primes, M = p⁴q², A ⊂ Z_M with |A| = p²q. Then A(X) is not divisible by all of Φ_q, Φ_{p⁴}, Φ_{p⁴q}, Φ_{p³q²}, Φ_{p²q²}.

Proof. Suppose it is. Step 1. By Fact 2 for p⁴q, N_{4,1}(r + i p³, j) − N_{4,1}(r, j) = δ does not depend on j ∈ Z_q. Summing over j and using Fact 1 for p⁴ gives qδ = 0. So N_{4,1}(r + i p³, j) = N_{4,1}(r, j), and N_{3,1}(r, j) = p·N_{4,1}(r, j) ∈ pZ. Step 2. Fix j and let ε := N_{3,1}(r + i p², j) − N_{3,1}(r, j); ε ∈ pZ by Step 1. By Fact 2 for p³q², η(t) := N_{3,2}(r + i p², t) − N_{3,2}(r, t) depends only on t mod q; summing over the q values of t in the class j mod q gives ε = q·η. So ε ∈ pqZ. We claim ε = 0. Within each coset c of p²Z in Z_{p³} the p values N_{3,1}(·, j) are multiples of p that are congruent to one another mod pq, so their sum is p·x_c + pq·m_c, where x_c ∈ pZ is the smallest of them and m_c ≥ 0 is an integer, zero exactly when the values on c are all equal. Summing over the cosets and using Φ_q | A (the class j mod q has p² elements): p² = p·Σx_c + pq·Σm_c = p²k + pq·m, i.e. p = pk + qm with integers k, m ≥ 0. If m > 0 then p | m, so qm ≥ pq > p, impossible. So m = 0, every coset is constant, and ε = 0, η = 0, whence N_{2,1}(r, j) = p·N_{3,1}(r, j) ∈ p²Z and N_{2,2}(r, t) = p·N_{3,2}(r, t) ∈ pZ. Step 3. By Fact 2 for p²q², θ(t) := N_{2,2}(r + i p, t) − N_{2,2}(r, t) depends only on t mod q, and θ ∈ pZ. Summing over a class, N_{2,1}(r + i p, j) − N_{2,1}(r, j) = qθ ∈ pqZ; it is also in p²Z by Step 2, hence in p²qZ. If it were nonzero, some N_{2,1}(·, j) would be ≥ p²q = |A|, putting all of A in one class mod q; but Φ_q | A makes the q classes mod q equally populated. So N_{2,1}(r + i p, j) = N_{2,1}(r, j) and N_{1,1}(r, j) = p·N_{2,1}(r, j) ∈ p³Z. Step 4. Φ_q | A gives Σ_{r ∈ Z_p} N_{1,1}(r, j) = |A|/q = p², which is not a multiple of p³. ∎

History of this lemma. My first version assumed p < q, listed Φ_{p³q} as a sixth hypothesis, and used a cruder count in Step 2. The Step 2 above, which needs only p ≠ q, is due to the referee pass of Section 7. Catalpa and Yonder each observed that the proof never uses Φ_{p³q}.

Lemma A′ (Yonder; proof checked by me). Let p < q. With M and A as in Lemma A, A(X) is not divisible by all of Φ_q, Φ_{p⁴}, Φ_{p³q}, Φ_{p³q²}, Φ_{p²q²}. So Φ_{p⁴q} can be traded for Φ_{p³q}.

Proof. Suppose it is. (a) By Fact 1 for p⁴, N_{3,0}(r) = p·N_{4,0}(r) ∈ pZ. (b) By Fact 2 for p³q, ε = N_{3,1}(r + i p², j) − N_{3,1}(r, j) is the same for all j, and ε = q·η as in Step 2 above. Summing over j: N_{3,0}(r + i p²) − N_{3,0}(r) = qε ∈ q²Z, and it lies in pZ by (a), so in pq²Z. Were it nonzero, some class mod p³ would hold at least pq² > p²q = |A| elements. So ε = 0 = η, N_{2,1} = p·N_{3,1} and N_{2,2} = p·N_{3,2} ∈ pZ. (c) As in Step 3, N_{2,1}(r + i p, j) − N_{2,1}(r, j) = qθ with θ ∈ pZ; were it nonzero, some N_{2,1}(·, j) would be at least pq > p², but the class j mod q has p² elements. So it is 0, and N_{1,1}(ρ, j) = p²·N_{3,1}(r, j) for every r ≡ ρ (mod p). (d) Σ_ρ N_{1,1}(ρ, j) = p², so for each j there is exactly one ρ_j with N_{3,1}(r, j) = 1 for r ≡ ρ_j (mod p) and 0 otherwise. Hence N_{3,0}(r) = #{j ∈ Z_q : ρ_j ≡ r (mod p)}; these p numbers are multiples of p by (a) and sum to q, so p | q. ∎

Not proved: the solvers say that both Φ_{p⁴q} and Φ_{p³q} can be dropped at once, i.e. that Φ_q, Φ_{p⁴}, Φ_{p³q²}, Φ_{p²q²} already suffice: infeasible at (p, q) = (2, 3) in Yonder's HiGHS model, and at (2, 3), (2, 5), (2, 7), (3, 2), (3, 5) in my CP-SAT counts model, which timed out at (5, 2); each of the four is necessary at (2, 3) and (2, 5) (sets exhibited and verified). Neither of us has a hand proof of the four-hypothesis statement.

Lemma B. Let p ≠ q be primes, M = p⁴q², B ⊂ Z_M with |B| = p²q. Then B(X) is not divisible by all of Φ_{p²}, Φ_{p³}, Φ_{q²}, Φ_{pq}, Φ_{pq²}, Φ_{p²q}.

Proof. Suppose it is. As in Step 1 above, Φ_{p²} and Φ_{p²q} give N_{2,1}(r + i p, j) = N_{2,1}(r, j), so N_{1,1}(r, j) ∈ pZ; symmetrically Φ_{q²} and Φ_{pq²} give N_{1,1}(r, j) ∈ qZ. Write N_{1,1} = pq·e with e a nonnegative integer function on Z_p × Z_q of total p. By Fact 2 for pq, e(r, j) = u(r) + w(j). If w is not constant, there are j, j′ with e(r, j) − e(r, j′) = w(j) − w(j′) a nonzero integer, the same for all r; say it is ≥ 1. Then e(r, j) ≥ 1 for all r ∈ Z_p, so column j alone has total ≥ p: e is 1 on that column and 0 elsewhere. If w is constant, e(r, j) = e(r) and the total is q·Σ_r e(r) = p, impossible as q ∤ p. So all of B lies in one class mod q; translating, B = qB′ with B′ ⊂ Z_{p⁴q}, |B′| = p²q. By Fact 3, B′ is divisible by Φ_{p²}, Φ_{p³} (unchanged), Φ_q (from Φ_{q²}) and Φ_p (from Φ_{pq}). By Fact 4, p·p·p·q divides |B′| = p²q, which is false. ∎

(An earlier version of Lemma A assumed p < q and used a cruder count in Step 2. The argument above, which needs only p ≠ q, is due to the referee pass described in Section 7.)

Corollary. Let p < q < p², M = p⁴q², and let f, g be the functions of [KLMS, Theorem 1.6]. Reading the two tables there, f vanishes exactly on the elements of order d for d ∈ {p, q, p⁴, p³q, p⁴q, p²q², p³q²}, and g exactly for d ∈ {p², p³, q², pq, p²q, pq², p⁴q²} (the two lists are complementary among the 14 divisors of M other than 1); both are eigenfunctions of the Fourier transform, so the same lists describe the zeros of f̂ and ĝ; and f̂(0) = ĝ(0) = p²q. Hence: (i) there is no set A with h_A = f (it would have |A| = p²q and Z_A equal to the first list: Lemma A), and no set B with h_B = g (Lemma B); (ii) g is not a pd-tiling complement of any set A (1_A ∗ g = 1 forces |A| = p²q and 1̂_A = 0 wherever ĝ ≠ 0, so Z_A contains the first list: Lemma A), and f is not a pd-tiling complement of any set (Lemma B).

Machine checks. lemma_check.py (CP-SAT, six-hypothesis lists, which contain the five of Lemma A): both lemmas infeasible for (p, q) = (2, 3), (2, 5), (2, 7), (3, 5). The referee pass's own models: (3, 2) and (5, 2) as well, and a solver-free exhaustive search at (2, 3). Which single hypotheses can be dropped, at (2, 3) and (2, 5): Lemma A fails without Φ_q, Φ_{p⁴}, Φ_{p³q²} or Φ_{p²q²} (sets exhibited) and survives without Φ_{p⁴q} or without Φ_{p³q}; Lemma B fails without Φ_{p³} or Φ_{pq} and survives dropping any one of the other four, so its hypotheses are not minimal either.

Remark (Yonder; verified by me in exact arithmetic). [KLMS] Problem 1.2 asks whether, for a set A that pd-tiles and any pd-tiling complement f of it, "both A and F" satisfy (T1) and (T2). For F this already fails at M = 6: A = {0, 3} and f = (1, ¾, ¼, 0, ¼, ¾) satisfy f(0) = 1, f ≥ 0, f̂ = (3, 3⁄2, 0, 0, 0, 3⁄2) ≥ 0 and 1_A ∗ f = 1, while F(1) = 3 and both Φ_2 and Φ_3 divide F, so the product over S_F is 6 ≠ F(1). This f is the complement that the linear program attaches to the T1-short minimal pattern ({6}, n = 2). The same happens whenever a T1-short minimal pattern lies inside the zero set of a genuine tile. The authors may well regard this as outside what they meant (their Problem 1.3 assumes (T1)); we have not looked for whether it is noted anywhere. Claim 1 concerns the set A only.
