Section 6

Facts 1 and 2. Correct. The key step — characters of Z_{p^a}×Z_{q^b} of order p^a q^b are exactly those nontrivial on both K_p and K_q, so the condition is N ∈ {K_p-invariant} + {K_q-invariant} — is right, and the converse direction (separable on every coset ⇒ such a decomposition) holds because u and w may depend on the coset. Fact 3. Correct; the map ζ ↦ ζ^q from primitive d-th roots to primitive (d/gcd(d,q))-th roots is onto. Fact 4. Correct (Gauss's lemma gives R ∈ Z[X]).

Lemma A. Each step holds. - Step 1: δ independent of j by Fact 2 at p⁴q; summing over j and Fact 1 at p⁴ gives qδ = 0. ✓. - Step 2: ε = q·η from Fact 2 at p³q², ε ∈ pZ from Step 1, so ε ∈ pqZ (uses p ≠ q). The counting argument: on a coset of p²Z in Z_{p³} the p values are x_c + pq·k_i with min k_i = 0; summing over the p² cosets against the class total p² gives p = pk + qm; m > 0 forces p | m and qm ≥ pq > p. ✓. Then N_{2,1} ∈ p²Z, N_{2,2} ∈ pZ. ✓ - Step 3: the difference lies in pqZ ∩ p²Z = p²qZ (again p ≠ q); nonzero would put all of A in one class mod q. ✓ - Step 4: p² is not a multiple of p³. ✓ - A remark, not an error: the proof never uses Φ_{p³q}. Its only appearance is "ε does not depend on j", and the argument then fixes j anyway. So what is proved is the stronger statement with five hypotheses (Φ_q, Φ_{p⁴}, Φ_{p⁴q}, Φ_{p³q²}, Φ_{p²q²}), for all primes p ≠ q. That matches your machine observation that the lemma survives dropping Φ_{p³q}; it does not explain the other survivor (dropping Φ_{p⁴q}), which would need a different Step 1.

Lemma B. Each step holds. - "Symmetrically Φ_{q²} and Φ_{pq²} give N_{1,1} ∈ qZ": this uses Fact 2 with the roles of p and q exchanged (differences in the q-direction constant along Z_p). That is legitimate because the criterion is "N(i,j) = u(i)+w(j) on each coset", which is symmetric; it would be worth one clause saying so, since Fact 2 is stated asymmetrically. - e = u(r) + w(j) with total p: w non-constant ⇒ a full column of ones and nothing else; w constant ⇒ q | p. ✓ - B = qB′, then Fact 3 gives Φ_{p²}, Φ_{p³}, Φ_q (from q²), Φ_p (from pq) dividing B′, and Fact 4 gives p³q | p²q. ✓ All six hypotheses are used.

Section 2

Proposition 1. Correct. (i): v̂ = (M/|A|²)(1_A ∗ 1_{−A}), Σv = M/|A|, supports meet only at 0, Parseval with all terms nonnegative. (ii): averaging over units keeps f a complement because the zero set of 1̂_A is unit-invariant; both directions check, including the constants. (iii) is Fact 4.

Proposition 2. Correct. Case (b) needs "the prime powers of Z_A are exactly those of Z₀", which follows from k(Z_A) = k(Z₀); you say so.

Proposition 3. Correct, and slightly more general than stated: it needs only D⁺(Z₀) = n, not minimality. The class-size cap also checks: if Y − Y lies in a unit-invariant set H, the unit-average of (1/|Y|)·1_Y ∗ 1_{−Y} is admissible for D⁺(H) and sums to |Y|.

The one sentence that is wrong as written (Section 2, the paragraph defining (T1) and (T2) for subsets of Z_M): "These agree with the conditions of Coven and Meyerowitz for A regarded as a set of integers in {0,…,M−1}." What the paragraph shows is one direction: your (T1) and (T2) imply theirs. The other direction fails: A = {0, 1} ⊂ Z_3 has A(X) = Φ_2(X), so as a set of integers S_A = {2} and Coven–Meyerowitz's (T1) and (T2) hold (it tiles Z with period 2), while k(Z_A) = 1 ≠ 2 and A does not tile Z_3. Nothing downstream uses the false direction — Proposition 2 uses only "yours ⇒ theirs", and Claim 1 is stated in your (stronger) sense, which is also the sense [KLMS] use for functions (their S_F is restricted to prime powers dividing M, p. 3). I would replace "agree with" by "imply", and perhaps say that the Z_M-relative conditions are the ones meant throughout.

A sentence that is right but unproved (end of Section 2): "it would then fail to tile if it violated (T1)". For your Z_M-relative (T1) this needs a line: if A ⊕ B = Z_M then every prime power s | M has Φ_s | A or Φ_s | B, so k(Z_A)·k(Z_B) is a multiple of ∏_{s | M} Φ_s(1) = M = |A||B|, and with k(Z_A) | |A|, k(Z_B) | |B| this forces k(Z_A) = |A|.
