The question

Kiss, Londner, Matolcsi and Somlai (Functional tilings and the Coven–Meyerowitz tiling conditions, arXiv:2411.03854, Discrete Analysis 2025:18) say that a set A ⊂ Z_M pd-tiles Z_M if there is f : Z_M → R with f(0) = 1, f ≥ 0, f̂ ≥ 0 and 1_A ∗ f = 1 (such a set is called a pd-tile below, as in Kadir and Fan), and ask (Problem 1.1) whether every pd-tile tiles Z_M. They prove it for M a prime power. Kadir and Fan (arXiv:2607.02149, July 2026) prove it for M = pq and ask (open problem 1) about Z_{pⁿq} and Z_{pqr}. Kiss, Marshall, Matolcsi and Somlai (arXiv:2611.13593, November 2026) is the most recent paper on the subject that we found; it is about weak complements of sets that already tile. The question of the smallest size of a set with prescribed cyclotomic divisors — the principle behind items 1 and 2 — is the subject of Kiss, Łaba, Marshall and Somlai (arXiv:2507.11672), which we learned of only after the proofs were written; the fresh instance read it in full on 25 November and set its bounds beside ours (prior-art-agent3.md, section 3.3).

What is claimed, in the order we would suggest reading it

One document for items 1, 2 and 2a: paper/weak-tiles.pdf — Noon and Yonder, Weak tiles of Z_{pⁿq}, Z_{pqr} and Z_{p²q²} are tiles: three short proofs, checked in Lean, twenty-one pages: the three theorems, the general facts they rest on, the formal statements. If you read one thing, read that.

  1. Theorem, checked in Lean 4 (agent3; group side of the formal proof by agent2). For primes p ≠ q and every n ≥ 1, a set A ⊆ Z_{pⁿq} that tiles its complement weakly (f ≥ 0, f(0) = 1, 1_A ∗ f = 1) tiles Z_{pⁿq}; in particular every pd-tile of Z_{pⁿq} tiles. → pnq-agent3.md (the written proof, about four pages, no computation) and lean-tiling/ (items 1, 2 and 2a in one buildable Lean folder; the two libraries for items 1 and 2 have 5,834 lines; first complete build 09:46 UTC, 20 November). The engine of the proof is one remark — a (weak) tile has the smallest cardinality among all sets whose mask polynomial has at least the same cyclotomic factors Φ_d, d | M — together with a clique bound and two counting lemmas proved by induction on n.

  2. Theorem, checked in Lean 4 (agent2). For distinct primes p, q, r, a set A ⊆ Z_{pqr} that tiles its complement weakly (f ≥ 0, f(0) = 1, 1_A ∗ f = 1; no positive definiteness) tiles Z_{pqr}. In particular every pd-tile of Z_{pqr} tiles. → paper/weak-pqr.pdf (ten pages, written the way the formal proof goes; start here), lean-pqr/ (2,061 lines of Lean with Mathlib; the README has the exact formal statement, how to re-check it in a few minutes, and what such a check does and does not establish) and pqr-agent2.md (the written proof, about four pages). Besides the remark of item 1 it uses one observation about sets A with Φ_{pqr} | A(X): cut along any coordinate of Z_p × Z_q × Z_r, the slices are 0/1 matrices that all have the same doubly-centred part. What the Lean check leaves to you: whether the four-line formal definitions say what you mean by weak tiling and tiling, and whether the result is new.

Neither proof uses positive definiteness, only the two bounds (S) and (C), which hold for weak tilings and are theorems in Lean for every cyclic group, as is the Coven–Meyerowitz theorem "(T1) + (T2) ⇒ tile" (lean-tiling/).

2a. Theorem, checked in Lean 4 (agent2; one lemma is agent3's). Added 13:30 UTC, 20 November. For primes p ≠ q, a set A ⊆ Z_{p²q²} that tiles its complement weakly tiles Z_{p²q²}; so tiles, pd-tiles and weak tiles of Z_{p²q²} coincide (itself a Lean theorem since 26 November: P2q2.tile_iff_pd_iff_weak_p2q2). [KLMS] say of M = p^α q^β "we do not know"; this is the smallest such case beyond pⁿq. → Part III of the joint paper, or paper/weak-p2q2.pdf (the same as a six-page standalone note), p2q2-proof-agent2/p2q2-agent2.md (the working note, with the scripts and the certificate file), lean-tiling/P2q2/ (11,134 lines when it was accepted at 13:22 UTC, of which 9,132 are generated from a file of certificates; 28 more since 26 November). The proof has a different shape from items 1 and 2: (i) a weak tile for which neither p nor q is a zero lies in pZ or qZ (a mass argument: 1/p + 1/q < 1), which reduces it to item 1; (ii) if some weak complement vanishes outside pZ, the p pieces of A are weak tiles of Z_{pq²} with a common complement and therefore share a tiling complement; (iii) for each of the remaining 192 zero patterns a linear programme in nine unknowns with p, q as parameters has an explicit optimal certificate (two regimes: q < p², q > p²), which either contradicts k | |A|, or gives (T1) and (T2), or produces the complement of (ii), or leaves two patterns for a counting lemma and one for a non-integrality. What the Lean check leaves to you: the same two things as above. Literature for this item, searched at 14:10 UTC: no proof found; closest are [Kiss–Marshall–Matolcsi–Somlai, arXiv:2611.13593, §6.4] (complements of two-prime set tiles that satisfy (T1) satisfy (T2)) and [Kiss–Łaba–Marshall–Somlai, arXiv:2507.11672] (smallest size of a set with given cyclotomic divisors). Added 26 November, found by the fresh instance: spectral sets of Z_{pⁿq²} were already known to tile (Kiss–Malikiosis–Somlai–Vizer, Analysis & PDE 2020; this from the paper's abstract, which is all that it or we have read of it), so no new case of Fuglede's conjecture follows.

2b. Claimed theorem, computer-assisted, NOT checked in Lean (agent2). Added 08:59 UTC, 21 November. For all primes p ≠ q, a set A ⊆ Z_{p³q²} that tiles its complement weakly satisfies (T1) and (T2) and tiles Z_{p³q²}. → paper/weak-p3q2.pdf (ten pages: the lemmas with proofs, what the computer has to establish, how, and what was checked), p2q2-proof-agent2/towards-paqb-agent2.md (the working note, in the order things were found), p2q2-proof-agent2/next-case-p3q2/ (all scripts, certificates, outputs, and two referee reports). The proof: the two reductions of item 2a; two more of the same kind at the other end (the set is a union of cosets of a subgroup of prime order, or a weak complement is constant on one); three counting lemmas for the sets built of cosets of both subgroups of prime order, which is all that escapes the four reductions; and, to decide which case each of 1,215 zero patterns is in, a linear programme certified with p and q as symbols on ten pieces of the (p, q) plane. How far to trust it: less than items 1 to 2a. The lemmas were refereed by one fresh instance and the certificates by another, which recomputed them with its own solver at 933 prime pairs and found no error affecting the theorem; an independent program re-proves every certificate; agent3 confirmed five of the groups by an unrelated search. But one real gap was found on the way (by a numerical comparison, not by the author), nothing here is formalised, and no human has read it. 3. A criterion and a computation (agent2). → flat/flatness.md. A set A pd-tiles Z_M if and only if D⁺(Z_A) = |A|, where Z_A = {d | M : Φ_d | A(X)} and D⁺ is the Delsarte parameter of [KLMS] for the corresponding union of step classes (this is the argument of [KLMS, Prop. 4.1], stated for general M). So for each M the question reduces to finitely many patterns and a search over sets. Carried out for every M ≤ 300 and for M = 324, 400, 432, 576, 648, 784, 2025: no pd-tile fails (T1) or (T2). This is computer-assisted and not certified (the searches end in a constraint solver's "infeasible"); the document states in two tiers how much independent support each modulus has.

  1. The same for weak tiling (agent2). → weak-agent2.md. A set is a weak tile iff a second Delsarte value D^w(Z_A) ≥ D⁺(Z_A) equals |A|; by duality, iff no nonnegative function with at least its Fourier zeros is lighter than it; and iff a real combination of its residue-class counts is identically 1 (this last form is checked in Lean for every M). Computation: for every M ≤ 300 and for M = 324, 400, 432, 576, 648, 784, 2025 no weak tile fails (T1) or (T2); so for those M tiles, pd-tiles and weak tiles coincide. Computer-assisted, not certified.

  2. Two lemmas about [KLMS, Theorem 1.6] (agent3 and agent2, independently). → flat/flatness.md, Section

  3. For M = p⁴q², p ≠ q: no set of p²q elements has A(X) divisible by Φ_q, Φ_{p⁴}, Φ_{p³q²}, Φ_{p²q²}, and none by Φ_{p²}, Φ_{p³}, Φ_{q²}, Φ_{pq}. Consequently neither of the functions f, g of that theorem is the averaged autocorrelation h_A of a set, nor a pd-tiling complement of a set — a possibility the paper leaves open. The proofs are fifteen lines.

What has been checked, honestly stated

  • Items 1, 2 and 2a are formal proofs (lean-tiling/): Lean 4's kernel accepts the three theorems, in the weak-tiling form, with no axioms beyond its standard three. agent3 rebuilt the folder from scratch (Mathlib's prebuilt files apart) under a second account on 20 November (the first two libraries in the morning, all three at 13:43 UTC; the logs are in this folder), and again on 26 November with the 28 lines added that day (lean-tiling-rebuild-26nov-agent3.log: the build completes and all 59 lines of the three check files print the three standard axioms). This removes the possibility of a wrong step in those three proofs. It does not check the meaning of the definitions (four lines, quoted in the README) or novelty, and it says nothing about items 2b–5.
  • Every proof above has been re-derived line by line by at least one other agent and by "referee" instances started for the purpose with instructions to find an error and with their own code. Their reports are in this folder, unedited (review-agent1.md, review-agent3-flatness.md, review-agent3-pqr.md, pnq-agent3/, pqr-agent2/referee/, flat/referee1/, flat/referee2/). They found no wrong step; they did find, and we corrected, a number of overstatements, one of which (counting three search models as independent when two were) changed how item 3 is stated.
  • Exhaustive checks: item 2 on every subset of Z₃₀ and of Z₄₂; item 1 on every subset of ten groups up to Z₂₈ (143 million sets) and, for its lemmas, by integer programming in sixteen groups up to M = 250 [numbers corrected by agent3 at 07:58 UTC from the table in pnq-agent3.md, Section 6; by enumeration the premise of its Lemma 4, where the weight lies, is met by only 91 sets]; item 5 at (p, q) = (2, 3) by exhaustive search without a solver.
  • All of these readers are the same model as the authors. That removes shared context and shared code. It does not remove shared habits of thought, and it is not peer review.
  • We made mistakes along the way and the documents say where: a search model with a missed case (caught by testing against brute force), a remark credited to one of us that turned out to be in arXiv:2611.13593, several wrong explanations withdrawn in place.
  • Novelty: not established. On 20 November agent2 ran five web searches (pd-tiling, weak tiling, "pd-flat", citations of Kadir–Fan, three-prime results) and looked at arXiv:2212.05513, 2410.04948, 2411.03854, 2607.02149 through a tool that summarises and by searching their text, and at the abstract of 2611.13593. (Until 26 November this line said "read the text of". It was not a reading.) On 25 November a fresh instance with the web, briefed by agent3, searched arXiv by title and abstract, read 2411.03854, 2607.02149, 2611.13593 and 2507.11672 in full, and found no paper that proves, or gives in a few lines, any of the three theorems. It put its confidence that no proof is on arXiv at about five chances in six, and did not try Google Scholar, MathSciNet or zbMATH (prior-art-agent3.md). It also found that spectral ⇒ tile was already known in all three groups (Malikiosis–Kolountzakis; Shi; Kiss–Malikiosis–Somlai–Vizer), so no new case of Fuglede's conjecture follows, and that our lower bounds are close to bounds of Łaba–Marshall and of Kiss–Łaba–Marshall–Somlai, which the paper now credits. None of the papers proves any of the three statements. arXiv:2411.03854 (Section 4) says of pd-tile ⇒ tile: "We do not know whether the analogous statement holds true for M = p^α q^β"; Kadir–Fan list Z_{pⁿq} and Z_{pqr} as open in July 2026. That is still not a specialist's knowledge of the field, and it cannot see unpublished work.

If you have ten minutes

Read the first two pages of paper/weak-tiles.pdf (the definitions, the three theorems, how the proofs go) and the two definitions and theorem statements at the top of lean-tiling/README.md, and decide whether they say what you would mean by "weak tile" and "tile"; that is the one thing the proof assistant cannot check. If you have Lean installed, lean-tiling/ rebuilds in a few minutes. What remains uncertain is not correctness but novelty.
