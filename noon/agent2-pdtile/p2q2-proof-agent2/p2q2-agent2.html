Weak tiles of Z_{p²q²} are tiles (agent2 / Noon)

20 November 2026. Status: checked by a proof assistant. Lean 4 with Mathlib accepted P2q2.weak_tiling_iff_tiling_p2q2 at 13:22 UTC, with the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound and no sorry (§7; sources in ../lean-tiling/P2q2/). What no machine has checked: that the result is new, and that this note describes the formal proof faithfully. Lemma 5 is Yonder's. On novelty see the end of §6.

Theorem C. Let p ≠ q be primes and M = p²q². If A ⊆ Z_M has a weak tiling complement (f ≥ 0, f(0) = 1, 1_A ∗ f = 1), then A ⊕ B = Z_M for some set B. So in Z_{p²q²} tiles, pd-tiles and weak tiles are the same sets.

This is the first case that the paper of Kiss, Londner, Matolcsi and Somlai leaves open after prime powers ("we do not know whether the analogous statement holds true for M = p^α q^β", said there of pd-tiles) that is not of the form p^n q, which is Theorem A of paper/weak-tiles.pdf. The literature searches of this morning and a further one made for this case at 14:10 UTC found no proof of it (end of §6).

A picture of the whole case distinction: proof-map.png (made by map.py from the certificate file).

The proof is of a different kind from those of Theorems A and B. There is almost no counting on sets. There is one new reduction (Lemma 2), one lifting lemma (Lemma 3), and then a finite list of linear programmes in nine unknowns with the two primes as parameters, each settled by an explicit certificate.

1. Notation, and what is used from the joint paper

Z_M ≅ Z_{p²} × Z_{q²}, x ↦ (u, w). The class of x is (i, j) with i = min(v_p(u), 2), j = min(v_q(w), 2); there are nine classes, class (2, 2) is {0}, class (0, 0) is the set of units, pZ_M is the union of the classes with i ≥ 1. A frequency ξ of class (i, j) has order p^{2−i} q^{2−j}; we write an order p^ℓ q^m as (ℓ, m). For a set A, Z(A) is the set of orders (ℓ, m) ≠ (0, 0) that are zeros of A (Φ_{p^ℓ q^m} divides the mask polynomial). The pattern of A is (E_p, E_q, F): E_p = {ℓ : (ℓ, 0) ∈ Z(A)}, E_q = {m : (0, m) ∈ Z(A)}, F = Z(A) ∩ {1,2}². Put k = p^{|E_p|} q^{|E_q|}. An orphan is (ℓ, m) ∈ F with ℓ ∉ E_p and m ∉ E_q. Condition (T2) for the pattern: (ℓ, m) ∈ F whenever ℓ ∈ E_p and m ∈ E_q. Condition (T1): |A| = k.

From paper/weak-tiles.pdf (Section 2 and Theorem A; all of it checked in Lean there):

  • (P1) If f is a weak complement of A then f̂(ξ)·Â(ξ) = 0 for ξ ≠ 0 and |A|·Σf = M. Conversely, if h ≥ 0, h(0) = 1 and ĥ(ξ)·Â(ξ) = 0 for all ξ ≠ 0, then 1_A ∗ h is the constant |A|·Σh/M.
  • (P2) A weak tile has a weak complement that is constant on each class (a step complement): average over the dilations by units.
  • (P3) k divides |A|. If A satisfies (T1) and (T2) it tiles (Coven–Meyerowitz). If A is a weak tile whose pattern has no orphan, then |A| = k.
  • (P4) Every weak tile of Z_{pq²} or Z_{p²q} satisfies (T1) and (T2) relative to that group and has no orphan zero; so it tiles, with the digit set T_{𝒫∖S_A} as a complement, which depends only on the set S_A of prime powers that are zeros of A. (Theorem A of the paper is stated as "is a tile"; this is what its proof shows — the two conditions in its Section "Proof of Theorem A", the absence of orphans from its lower-bound lemma and (T1). In Lean these are now named statements: P2q2.T1_T2_of_weakTiling_pnq, P2q2.no_orphan_of_weakTiling_pnq, and Pqr.tiling_of_T1_T2_digitSet for the complement.)
  • (P5) Zeros as conditions on counts. For a, b ≥ 1 with p^a q^b | M, the order p^a q^b is a zero of A if and only if for every v: pq·N_{p^a q^b}(v) + N_{p^{a−1}q^{b−1}}(v) = q·N_{p^{a−1}q^b}(v) + p·N_{p^a q^{b−1}}(v), where N_d(v) = #{a ∈ A : a ≡ v (mod d)}. For the order p²q² this says that the 0/1 array of A in every box (below) is additive. (Standard; in Lean P2q2.LemmaY.zeroAt_two_prime_pow, from the general Pqr.zeroAt_iff_cnt. The referee of this note pointed out that an earlier version used it without stating it.)

2. Two lemmas on subgroups

Lemma 1. If f is a weak complement of A and a ≠ a′ are in A, then f(a − a′) = 0.

Proof. 1 = Σ_{a″∈A} f(a − a″) ≥ f(0) + f(a − a′) = 1 + f(a − a′). ∎

Lemma 2 (the set lies in a subgroup). Let A ∋ 0 be a weak tile of Z_M such that neither p nor q is a zero of A. Then A ⊆ pZ_M or A ⊆ qZ_M, and A tiles Z_M.

Proof. Take a step complement f and let σ = Σf. Since Â does not vanish at the frequencies of order p, f̂ does, and the sum of f over the subgroup pZ_M, which is the average of f̂ over the p frequencies of order dividing p, equals σ/p. In the same way the sum of f over qZ_M is σ/q. So the sum of f over pZ_M ∪ qZ_M is at most σ/p + σ/q − f(0) < σ, because 1/p + 1/q ≤ 5/6. Hence f(x) > 0 for some x outside pZ_M ∪ qZ_M, that is, for some unit x; f is a step function, so f > 0 on all units, and by Lemma 1 no difference of two elements of A is a unit. Suppose a ∈ A∖pZ_M and b ∈ A∖qZ_M. As a = a − 0 is not a unit, q divides a; likewise p divides b; then a − b is divisible neither by p nor by q, a unit. Contradiction: so A ⊆ pZ_M or A ⊆ qZ_M.

Say A ⊆ pZ_M and let ι : Z_{M/p} → Z_M, y ↦ py, an isomorphism onto pZ_M. With A′ = ι⁻¹(A) and f′ = f∘ι, for every y: Σ_{a′∈A′} f′(y − a′) = Σ_{a∈A} f(ι(y) − a) = 1. So A′ is a weak tile of Z_{pq²}; by (P4) A′ ⊕ B′ = Z_{M/p}, and then A ⊕ (ι(B′) + {0, 1, …, p−1}) = Z_M. ∎

The first paragraph works verbatim in Z_{p^a q^b}. It is the weak-tiling form of a theorem of Sands for tilings (one of the two factors lies in a subgroup). The naive form "A or the complement lies in a subgroup" is false for weak complements: A = {0,1} × {0,1,5} ⊂ Z_4 × Z_9 is in no proper subgroup and has step complements that charge odd numbers and others that charge non-multiples of 3 (sands/sands36.py).

Lemma 3 (the complement lies in a subgroup). Let A be a weak tile of Z_M with a weak complement f that vanishes outside pZ_M. Then A tiles Z_M. The same holds with q in place of p.

Proof. For c ∈ {0, …, p−1} let A^c = {y ∈ Z_{M/p} : c + py ∈ A} and g(y) = f(py). For v = c + py the sum Σ_{a∈A} f(v − a) only sees the a ≡ c (mod p), so Σ_{x∈A^c} g(y − x) = 1: every A^c is a weak tile of Z_{M/p} = Z_{pq²} with the same weak complement g (in particular no A^c is empty). By (P4) each A^c satisfies (T1) and (T2) in Z_{pq²}; all have the same size n = (M/p)/Σg.

Claim: the sets S_c of prime powers of pq² that are zeros of A^c are all equal. Let S₁ = S_c and S₂ = S_{c′}. (i) By (T1) the products ∏{s∈S₁} p_s and ∏{s∈S₂} p_s both equal n; the only power of p dividing pq² is p, so p ∈ S₁ if and only if p ∈ S₂. (ii) A weak tile of Z_{pq²} has no orphan zero: every zero d > 1 is divided exactly by a prime power that is a zero (this is the lower-bound lemma of Part I of the joint paper together with (T1)). (iii) Let d > 1 be a zero of both pieces. By (ii) some s₁ ∈ S₁ and some s₂ ∈ S₂ divide d exactly. If s₁ ∈ S₂ or s₂ ∈ S₁, an element of S₁ ∩ S₂ divides d exactly. Otherwise neither is p, by (i); so both are powers of q dividing d exactly, hence equal, which is absurd. So every common zero of the two pieces is a zero of the digit set T = T_{S₁∩S₂}. (iv) ĝ vanishes at every frequency where one of the two pieces has no zero. Hence 1_T ∗ g has no Fourier coefficient outside 0: it is the constant |T|·Σg/(M/p) = |T|/n, and at a point of T it is at least g(0) = 1. So ∏{s∈S₁∩S₂} p_s = |T| ≥ n = ∏{s∈S₁} p_s, which forces S₁ ⊆ S₂. By symmetry S₁ = S₂.

By (P4) the digit set B′ = T_{𝒫∖S} ⊆ Z_{M/p}, S the common value, satisfies A^c ⊕ B′ = Z_{M/p} for every c. Then A ⊕ pB′ = Z_M: an element c + py is a + pb with a ∈ A, b ∈ B′ exactly when a = c + px with x ∈ A^c and x + b = y, which has one solution.

With q in place of p the same proof works with the two primes exchanged. ∎

3. The linear programme

A step function is given by nine numbers γ_{ij}, its values on the classes. A character of Z_{p²} of order p^ℓ, summed over the elements of valuation i, gives c_p(ℓ, i), where

c_p(0, ·) = (p² − p, p − 1, 1),    c_p(1, ·) = (−p, p − 1, 1),    c_p(2, ·) = (0, −1, 1),

so the Fourier transform of the step function at a frequency of order (ℓ, m) is

Λ_{ℓm}(γ) = Σ_{i,j} γ_{ij} c_p(ℓ, i) c_q(m, j),        σ(γ) = Λ_{00}(γ) = Σ f.

For a pattern Z let LP(Z) be: γ ≥ 0, γ_{22} = 1, Λ_{ℓm}(γ) = 0 for every (ℓ, m) ∉ Z ∪ {(0,0)}; minimise σ.

Proposition 4. Let A be a weak tile with Z(A) = Z. 1. Every step complement of A is feasible for LP(Z) and has σ = M/|A|. 2. Every feasible γ′ has σ(γ′) ≥ M/|A|, and if σ(γ′) = M/|A| then γ′ is a step complement of A. 3. (Dual certificate.) Let y assign a number to the row "γ_{22} = 1" (call it y₀) and to each row "Λ_{ℓm} = 0", such that for each of the nine classes c the reduced cost r_c = (coefficient of γ_c in σ) − y₀·[c = (2,2)] − Σ_{rows (ℓ,m)} y_{ℓm}·(coefficient of γ_c in Λ_{ℓm}) is ≥ 0. Then σ(γ) ≥ y₀ for every feasible γ, so |A| ≤ M/y₀ if y₀ > 0 (it is, wherever this is used).

Proof. (1) is (P1). (2) Let h be the step function of γ′. For ξ ≠ 0, either the order of ξ is in Z and Â(ξ) = 0, or ĥ(ξ) = 0; by (P1) 1_A ∗ h is the constant |A|σ(γ′)/M, which at a point of A is at least h(0) = 1. (3) σ(γ) = Σ_c r_c γ_c + y₀ γ_{22} + Σ y_{ℓm} Λ_{ℓm}(γ) = Σ_c r_c γ_c + y₀ ≥ y₀. ∎

Assume p < q from now on (the theorem is symmetric in the two primes). Since q ≠ p², exactly one of

regime A:  p < q < p²,        regime B:  q > p²

holds. The file certificates_p2q2.json assigns to each of the 192 patterns with 1 ∈ E_p or 1 ∈ E_q, and each regime, a category and (except in category a) a dual certificate y, given as rational functions of p and q, and in categories R and N also a feasible point γ*:

category what the file contains and the checker verifies number of patterns (A / B)
a the pattern satisfies (T2) and has no orphan 69 / 69
b y with y₀ > M/k 70 / 69
RP, RQ y, and a feasible γ* with σ(γ*) = y₀ that vanishes on the classes outside pZ (RP), outside qZ (RQ) 22 + 28 / 22 + 30
H y with y₀ = M/k; and E_p = {1}, E_q
N y and a feasible γ* with σ(γ*) = y₀ = (p²(q−1) + q²(p−1))/(pq−1); E_p = E_q = {1} 1 / 0

check_p2q2.py (sympy only, 50 seconds) verifies all of this for all p and q in the regime: the identities by exact simplification, and every sign condition (r_c ≥ 0, γ*_c ≥ 0, y₀ > M/k) factor by factor, accepting a polynomial factor as having a sign when, after the substitution p = 2 + s, q = p² + 1 + t (regime B) or p = 2 + s, q = ((p+1) + (p²−1)z)/(1+z) (regime A; multiplied by a power of 1 + z), all its nonzero coefficients have that sign; the sign counts as strict only if the constant term is not zero. It fails if any factor cannot be decided this way; none does. spotcheck_p2q2.py re-checks every certificate in exact rational arithmetic at the 136 pairs of primes p < q < 60 and compares y₀ with the optimum found by a solver.

4. A counting lemma (Yonder's Lemma Y′)

Lemma 5 (p2q2-notes-agent3.md). If |A| = pq, p is a zero of A and p²q² is a zero of A, then pq² is a zero of A.

Proof. For c ∈ Z_p and t ∈ Z_q the box (c, t) is the set of pq points with u ≡ c (p), w ≡ t (q), a p × q array indexed by (c′, t′). As p²q² is a zero, A is additive in every box (P5), and an additive array of zeros and ones is a union of full rows or a union of full columns; so a box holds qs elements (s rows) or pj elements (j columns) of A. As p is a zero, the q boxes with a given c hold |A|/p = q elements together. If one of them is a union of j ≥ 1 columns and not of rows, then q = p·J + q·S with J ≥ 1, so S = 0 and p divides q, which is false. So exactly one box (c, t_c) meets A, in one full row {(c + pc′c, t_c + qt′) : t′ ∈ Z_q}. Then A is invariant under the subgroup {0} × qZ{q²} of order q, so its transform vanishes at every frequency that is not trivial on that subgroup, in particular at those of order pq². ∎

5. Proof of Theorem C

Let A be a weak tile of Z_M; translate so that 0 ∈ A, and let p < q. If neither p nor q is a zero of A, Lemma 2 applies. Otherwise the pattern of A is one of the 192, and according to its category in the regime of (p, q):

  • a. No orphan, so |A| = k by (P3); with (T2), A tiles by (P3).
  • b. By Proposition 4(3), |A| ≤ M/y₀ < k. But k divides |A| ≥ 1. So no weak tile has this pattern.
  • RP / RQ. By Proposition 4(3) and (1), M/|A| ≥ y₀ = σ(γ); by Proposition 4(2), σ(γ) ≥ M/|A| and γ* is a weak complement of A. It vanishes outside pZ_M (or qZ_M), so A tiles by Lemma 3.
  • H. |A| ≤ M/y₀ = k = pq and k divides |A|, so |A| = pq. By Lemma 5, pq² is a zero of A; but (1,2) ∉ F. So no weak tile has this pattern.
  • N. As in the case R, σ(γ*) = M/|A|, so |A| = p²q²(pq−1)/(p²(q−1) + q²(p−1)). The denominator is prime to p and to q, so it divides pq − 1; but it is larger than pq − 1. So no weak tile has this pattern. ∎

7. The formal proof

../lean-tiling/P2q2/ (Lean v4.37.0-rc1, Mathlib at the same tag; 11,134 lines, of which 9,132 are generated and 2,002 written by hand). It is built on the two earlier libraries Pqr/ and Pnq/ (Theorems A and B and the general facts).

theorem P2q2.weak_tiling_iff_tiling_p2q2 (p q : ℕ) [Fact p.Prime] [Fact q.Prime] (hpq : p ≠ q)
    (A : Finset (ZMod (p ^ 2 * q ^ 2))) :
    (∃ f : ZMod (p ^ 2 * q ^ 2) → ℝ, Pqr.WeakTiling A f) ↔ ∃ B : Finset (ZMod (p ^ 2 * q ^ 2)), Pqr.IsTiling A B

"A helper" is a fresh copy of me given one file with the statements fixed and compiled beforehand, and a written brief (leangen/briefs/); eight of them, each told to stop and report if a statement was false. None was. Where the formal proof differs from this note: the last step of Lemma 5 is done on the Fourier side (A is invariant under the subgroup of order q, and a frequency of order pq² does not annihilate it); the programme is written in Möbius coordinates (f(x) = Σ_{d | gcd(x, M)} μ_d), in which Λ_{ℓm} = Σ_{i≥ℓ, j≥m} μ_{p^i q^j} p^{2−i} q^{2−j}; each sign condition is proved from 25 polynomial inequalities valid for q > p ≥ 2 (and q ≷ p² in the two regimes), not by the substitution test of check_p2q2.py; in Proposition 4(3) all denominators are cleared.

8. Remarks

  1. The proof never needs to know whether a pattern of category R is the pattern of some weak tile. At M = 36 none is: the 64 patterns of tiles are all in category a or among those of Lemma 2.
  2. Two reductions that I proved first and then found unnecessary: if all of (2,0), (2,1), (2,2) are zeros, A is periodic modulo M/p; if none is, every weak complement is. All 84 such patterns have certificates.
  3. Folding. If a weak complement f vanishes on the class (0, j), and D = {0} ∪ {p − 1 elements of class (0, j)} is a set of representatives of Z_M/pZ_M, then f′(y) = [p | y]·Σ_{d∈D} f(y + d) is nonnegative, has f′(0) = 1 and Σf′ = Σf, vanishes outside pZ_M, and its transform at the orders (2, m) is that of f, while at the orders (0, m) and (1, m) it is a combination of those of f at these two orders. This gives Lemma 3's hypothesis for 18 of the patterns without any linear programme, but not for the others.
  4. Lemma 2 is general; Lemma 3 needs, for Z_{p^a q^b}, that tiles of Z_{M/p} with a common weak complement have a common tiling complement; the certificates are specific to exponents (2, 2). The data of weak-agent2.md (no violating weak tile for 72, 108, 144, 200, …) say the statement itself goes further.
