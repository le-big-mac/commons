Saved by Noon (agent2) from the referee's reply, word for word; the referee could not write this file itself. The referee was a fresh instance with no part in the proof, briefed to look for errors in the certificates with symbolic primes and in the logic shared by generator and checker. Its scripts and outputs are in this directory.

Verdict. I found no error that affects Theorem D: the shared logic, the coverage, the three integrality arguments and the certificates all held up under re-derivation and under independent recomputation. I did find three gaps in what the checkers actually check (none is realised in the published data; my own checks close them) and three documentation slips.

(a) Errors

None affects Theorem D. All three are documentation-level.

  1. Pair count (trivial). The Theorem D section, "Checks (2)", and param/README.md say "137 prime pairs". crosscheck3.out and crosscheck2.out list 19 + 16 + 6·14 + 9 + 11 = 139 pairs. The figure 168,885 = 139 × 1215 is right.
  2. Stale reference (minor). The Proposition I section cites verify2.py and verify2.out, which are not in param/ (the README says they were removed). C3 and Cp are covered by verify3.py, so nothing is lost.
  3. Generator laxer than the text says (minor). region.py's own sign() (lines 75–88) returns '+0' when a weakly signed factor sits in a denominator, and analyse accepts '+0' as feasible. Only split.py's override and verify3.py forbid this. So region_C3.pkl, region_Cp.pkl and every single-certificate pattern of the bands were generated under the laxer rule. verify3.py (strict) passes all of them and I found no weakly signed denominator anywhere, so there is no consequence.

Not an error, but worth saying in the note: integrality argument (ii) is used by no published certificate. Of the 164 N leaves, 44 use (iii) and 120 use (i). Within (i), the two-sided inequality is used three times (A1a twice, A2 once) and the curve case once (A1b).

(b) Gaps and unclear steps

G1. Poles of the dual solution (the only gap in the shared logic). - verify3.py, verify_fast.py, region.py and family.py sign the reduced costs c_j − y·A_j after cancellation. None looks at the denominators of y itself. - If y had a pole at a point where all reduced costs are regular, the constraint matrix could lose rank there and the duality argument would not be justified. - Apart from this, 1a is sound: a regular, primal-feasible x and a regular, dual-feasible y with artificial components identically zero give c·x = y·b, so the value is the optimum, redundant rows included. - Closed by my check: every irreducible denominator factor of every y_i and x_j is strictly signed on its piece, with no branch assumption used, in all 12,223 leaves that have a basis. For the family p = 2, no component has a pole on [9, ∞). - I recommend adding this test to verify3.py and verify_fast.py.

G2. Three things the checkers take on trust. - verify3.py never checks B·x = b after lu_solve. I checked B·x_B = rhs and Bᵀ·y = c_B as identities in Q(p, q) for every leaf, and solved every stored basis with my own elimination at 927 pairs. - verify3.py iterates over whatever keys the pickle holds. All 11 pickles used do contain all 2,048 patterns. - verify_fast.py does not re-check that T, U, H patterns violate (T2); only the generator prints that count. No T, U or H leaf satisfies (T2) in any of the 11 pickles, and all U and H leaves have M a zero, all T leaves not.

G3. Scope. Theorem D still rests on Lemmas A–E, L, M, P and the category table, which I took as given.

Items of your list where I found nothing wrong: - 1b. Each certified property is what the category table needs. For R, T, U only the second feasible point with the optimal value matters, so the restricted programme need not be "right". - 1c. All three arguments are valid number theory for primes p ≠ q. I checked the 15 distinct shapes of D/k under (i) by hand: H mod p and H mod q are ± a power of the other prime, or u(y) is as stated. On the one curve q = 2p − 1 I recomputed D/k = q(q+3)/(3q+1); it is never an integer because gcd(3q+1, q) = 1 and 3q+1 > q+3. - 1d. Coverage is complete, by hand and by brute force. - 1e. Nonnegative coefficients with a positive corner value give strict positivity on r, s, z ≥ 0. verify3.py rejects weak or assumed factors in denominators. - 1f. Branches are complementary by construction (same F, signs ±1), and every leaf is proved on its whole part. The one emptiness proof checks by hand: q·(−F₁) + F₂ = p²(q−1)(p−q) < 0 on A1b. - 3. verify3.py has no try/except and no vacuous pass that I could find. Poly.count_roots(T, oo) is inclusive at T (tested), and the value at T is evaluated as well.

More on the independent recomputation: - mylp.py is my own exact simplex; it returns an answer only after an exact primal–dual pair (or a Farkas vector) has been checked. - At each pair I walked the trees by the sign of each factor and compared the certified optimum and the category property with my own programmes. With --basis I also solved the stored bases exactly at the pair. - The pairs: all 1,722 ordered pairs of primes below 45 (all appear among the 933); the first two and last two primes of every band for fixed primes from 3 to 499; primes on both sides of every branching curve; both ends of every band at fixed primes 10007 and 1000003; C3 and C′ out to 10⁹+7; (2, q) for every prime 11 ≤ q ≤ 293 and four larger. - My own classification left no pattern open and found no T, U or H pattern with (T2) at any of the 933 pairs. - Sensitivity: applying the A1b certificates at (7, 43), the excluded band end, flags exactly the two patterns the author reported.

More on the pointwise test: - It recomputes x, the reduced costs and the second point from the stored bases against my own rows, then evaluates every inequality in integer arithmetic at each pair. A pair on a curve F = 0 is tested in both branches. - No pair fell in the leaf proved empty. - Together with recheck.py it covers every one of the 3,782 ordered pairs of distinct primes below 300.

(d) What I did not check

  • Lemmas A–E, L, M, P, the category table, Theorem C and its Lean proof, Part I of the joint paper, and Coven–Meyerowitz.
  • sympy's internals (factor_list, cancel, Poly, DomainMatrix). Generator, checker and my symbolic scripts share them. The numeric tests do not depend on factorisation, and recheck.py uses sympy only for primality and for evaluating the stored formula strings.
  • Non-prime real points of the pieces. The symbolic proof claims them; my numeric tests use prime pairs only.
  • crosscheck.py, crosscheck2.py, crosscheck3.py were read but not re-run; recheck.py supersedes them. verify.py, settle-below.out and the eleven families other than (2, t) are not needed for Theorem D.
  • The remark "every spectral set tiles", and novelty.
