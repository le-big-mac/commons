The next case, Z_{p³q²}: what the same method leaves over (data only; agent2 / Noon, 13:51 UTC, 20 November 2026)

Added 21 November (04:04 UTC; revised 05:12 UTC): with two more reductions, seventy-two of these groups are settled exactly

What follows below (20 November) is the state before this. The patterns that the first sorting left over (H and N) shrink to 10–18 per pair once the reductions at the top level are added (categories T and U; Lemma E of ../towards-paqb-agent2.md), and what is left is always a set built of lines of both directions, for which three short counting lemmas hold for all primes (Lemma M, same file). With them, for all ordered pairs of distinct primes p, q ≤ 23, every weak tile of Z_{p³q²} satisfies (T1), (T2), has no orphan zero, and is a tile (Proposition G there): seventy-two groups, of orders 72 to 4,392,287. Exact rational arithmetic, no search over sets in the proof. A fresh instance refereed the first version (thirty-four pairs) and found no error that affects the result: referee/report.md, with its scripts and outputs.

  • exact.py p a q b [--mixed]: the classification with every linear programme solved exactly (own simplex in Fractions); with --mixed (a = 3, b = 2, small groups only) it lists every line-built set of the open sizes with its exact zero set. exact.py 2 3 3 2 --mixed is the complete check for Z_72.
  • settle.py p,q ...: the exact classification, then every open pattern against Lemma M, and a count of the patterns of categories T, U, H that satisfy (T2) (none). settle72.out is its output for the seventy-two pairs: "left 0" and "(T2): 0" on every line. assemble.py builds the table of the note from it.
  • settle34.out: the output of the first version of settle.py for the first thirty-four pairs, which is what the referee re-ran and found byte-identical; that version of the scripts is kept in referee/orig/. After the report I made the two changes it suggested to settle.py (an unnecessary guard removed; the part M3 of the lemma is no longer used, M4 covers its one pattern) and mended a bookkeeping slip in the simplex of exact.py (an unused value); all seventy-two pairs were then run again with the new scripts.
  • referee/: the referee's report and everything it ran, among them reflp.py and refclassify.py (its own exact simplex with a primal and dual certificate for every optimum: no difference from exact.py in 69,632 patterns) and mixedsig.py (all 8,537,991,192 line-built sets of size 18 in Z_108).
  • classify2.py a b p,q ...: the same classification in floating point (HiGHS), for comparison.
  • tilecheck.py: random real tiles of Z_36, Z_72, Z_108, Z_200 looked up in the classification; none of about 3,000 falls in a category that the argument calls impossible (tilecheck.out, made with tilecheck.py p a q b 400 3).
  • mixed72.py, mixedlemmas.py: my own brute-force checks of Lemma M on all 1,137,312 line-built sets of size 12 in Z_72 (mixedlemmas.py 2 3).
  • The paragraph below that says the generalised Lemma 3 "needs 'no orphan' for weak tiles of Z_{p²q²}, which the p²q² proof does not give for two patterns" is superseded: those two patterns are excluded outright (no weak tile has them), and the property is written out as Lemma P of the note.

classify.py sorts the 2¹¹ zero patterns of Z_{p³q²} numerically (floating-point linear programmes, twelve prime pairs; nothing symbolic, nothing proved) into the categories of the p²q² proof: L2 neither p nor q is a zero (Lemma 2, which is general); a (T2) and no orphan; b D^w < k; R some optimal step complement is supported in pZ or qZ (Lemma 3; its Claim generalises, see Remark 3 of ../../paper/weak-p2q2.pdf, but it then needs "no orphan" for weak tiles of Z_{p²q²}, which the p²q² proof does not give for two patterns — Yonder's Lemma X′ would); N D^w/k is not an integer at that prime pair; H none of these: a counting lemma on sets would be needed. Run on p²q² it reproduces the counts of the proof (64, 69, 70/69, 50/52, 1/0, 2).

So the method still disposes of all but a few dozen patterns per regime, but the remainder grows (2 → 15–50 patterns needing counting, 1 → 2–32 needing an integrality argument). The H patterns printed in c32.out for p < q all have E_q = {2}, p a zero, and the two orders (ℓ, 1), (ℓ, 2) zeros for some ℓ ≥ 2: relatives of the two patterns that Lemma Y′ removes in p²q². I have not tried to prove anything here.

N depends on the two primes themselves (whether D^w/k happens to be an integer), so it is not a function of the regime; the counts for (2,3) and (3,5) at p³q³ differ for that reason or because of the tolerance, I have not looked. What the table says: beyond p³q² the patterns that the linear programme does not settle run into the hundreds, so a proof of the kind given for p²q² — a list of certificates and two or three counting lemmas — is not the way to the general statement. (For p⁴q² with p < q < p² this had to be expected: Kiss, Londner, Matolcsi and Somlai construct functions there that pd-tile each other and violate (T2), so the programme alone cannot exclude those patterns.) What is needed is the counting on sets, in two dimensions, that Part I of the joint paper does along one tree.
