What to prove. 1. S_bound_two (general M): copy the proof of Pqr.S_bound (Pqr/SCBounds.lean) with the weak complement g and the set A₁; at a frequency ξ ≠ 0 with ft g ξ ≠ 0 both hat A₁ ξ = 0 and hat A₂ ξ = 0, hence ZeroAt A₁ (ord ξ) and ZeroAt A₂ (ord ξ), hence ZeroAt A' (ord ξ). (|A₁| = |A₂| = M / ∑ g is not needed.) 2. T1_T2_of_weakTiling_pnq: extract from the proofs in Pnq/Main.lean and Pnq/Final.lean (reuse their lemmas; tiling_of_tree_T1_T2 itself proves T1 A and T2 A on the way to applying Pqr.tiling_of_T1_T2). 3. no_orphan_of_weakTiling_pnq: let d ∣ p^n q, d ≠ 1, ZeroAt A d. If d is a prime power (p^ℓ with ℓ ≥ 1, or q) then s = d is in SA A and divides d exactly. Otherwise d = p^ℓ * q with ℓ ≥ 1, and one has to show that p^ℓ or q is a zero of A. In tree terms: level ℓ ∈ F; if 0 ∉ F (q not a zero) and ℓ ∉ E (p^ℓ not a zero), lemma3 gives size ≥ p^e (p + q), while (T1) on the tree (from step_structure) gives size = p^e when 0 ∉ F: contradiction. 4. SA_eq_of_common_complement_pnq: Let S₁ = SA A₁, S₂ = SA A₂. By (T1) and Pqr.card_mul_sum (|A| ∑ g = M) the two products ∏{s ∈ S_i} s.minFac are equal (both are M / ∑ g). The prime powers of p^n q are p, …, p^n and q; comparing the q-part of the two products, q ∈ S₁ ↔ q ∈ S₂. Claim: every common zero d ≠ 1 of A₁ and A₂ is a zero of T = digitSet M (S₁ ∩ S₂), i.e. (Pqr.zeroAt_digitSet) some s ∈ S₁ ∩ S₂ divides d exactly. Indeed by (3) there are s₁ ∈ S₁ and s₂ ∈ S₂ dividing d exactly; if s₁ ∈ S₂ or s₂ ∈ S₁ we are done; otherwise neither is q (q is in both or in neither), so both are powers of p dividing d exactly, hence equal (the exact power of p in d is unique), a contradiction. By S_bound_two, |A₁| ≤ |T| = ∏{s ∈ S₁ ∩ S₂} s.minFac (Pqr.card_digitSet); as |A₁| = ∏_{s ∈ S₁} s.minFac and every factor is ≥ 2, S₁ ∩ S₂ = S₁, i.e. S₁ ⊆ S₂. By symmetry S₂ ⊆ S₁. 5. exists_common_complement_pnq: if the index type is empty take B = ∅; otherwise fix c₀ and take B = digitSet M (primePowers M \ SA (A c₀)); for every c, SA (A c) = SA (A c₀) by (4), and Pqr.tiling_of_T1_T2_digitSet with (2) gives IsTiling (A c) B.
