1. Verdict

I found no error: Lemmas 1–3, Proposition 4, Lemma 5, the five-category argument of §5, the certificate file and the checker survived every attack I made, by hand and by independent computation (own code; exact arithmetic for the certificates; about 3 million linear programmes on actual sets). What I found are gaps of citation and exposition, none of which changes a statement, and one looseness in check_p2q2.py that the shipped certificate file does not exercise.

2. Errors that break the proof

None found.

For the record, the places where I expected a break and why there is none:

  • Lemma 2. The subgroup sums σ/p, σ/q are right (the annihilator of pZ_M is the p frequencies of order dividing p); "f > 0 on one unit ⇒ on all units" needs the step complement of (P2), which is used; the three-line argument "a ∉ pZ, b ∉ qZ ⇒ a − b is a unit" needs 0 ∈ A, which is assumed. The reduction is right for both subgroups (Z_{M/p} = Z_{q²·p} and Z_{M/q} = Z_{p²·q} are both instances of Theorem A).
  • Lemma 3, Claim. I re-derived it: equal sizes plus (T1) force S_c, S_c′ to differ exactly by q ↔ q²; the sum of g over q²Z_{M/p} is Σg/q²; n = q, Σg = pq; then ĝ vanishes at all five non-trivial orders p, q, q², pq, pq² of Z_{pq²}, so g ≡ 1 and Σg = pq². Each of the five is justified. The q-version is the same proof with the primes exchanged and nothing in it uses p < q. The assembly A ⊕ pB′ is right; it uses that B′ depends on S only (Proposition CM (2)).
  • Λ formula. c_p(1,·) = (−p, p−1, 1) and c_p(2,·) = (0, −1, 1) are right (derived by hand, confirmed numerically, §4.1).
  • Proposition 4. (1) and (2) are (P1) and its converse; (2) does not even need A to be a weak tile. (3) is weak duality; it divides by y₀, which is positive wherever it is used.
  • §5. "k divides |A|" is Proposition CM (1) with k = ∏_{s∈S_A} p_s and is used correctly in b and H. "No orphan" is literally the hypothesis of Proposition CM (3). "p < q without loss of generality" is legitimate because the file certifies all 192 patterns in the asymmetric coordinates (not orbits under exchanging the primes), Lemma 3 is proved for both primes, and Lemma 5 is invoked only with p as the prime that is a zero (it is true in either orientation, §4.9). Every one of the 192 patterns has an entry in each regime (no missing, no duplicate, no extra entry). Regimes A and B are exhaustive: q ≠ p² for primes.
  • Category N. gcd(p²(q−1)+q²(p−1), pq) = 1 and p²(q−1)+q²(p−1) ≥ p²+q² > pq−1. Right.
  • Sign test. Regime B: (s,t) ↦ (2+s, p²+1+t) is onto {p ≥ 2, q ≥ p²+1}. Regime A: q = ((p+1)+(p²−1)z)/(1+z) runs over [p+1, p²−1) for z ∈ [0,∞); a prime q with p < q < p² satisfies p+1 ≤ q ≤ p²−1 and q = p²−1 only for (2,3), which is s = 0 (any z). So every prime pair is covered, including p = 2, (2,3) and (2,5). Multiplying by (1+z)^deg does not change a sign.
  • The checker checks what §3 says. It requires y to have exactly the rows of LP(Z), loops over all nine classes for the reduced costs, tests γ₂₂ = 1, γ ≥ 0, Λ(γ) = 0 on every row, σ(γ) = y₀ and the support for RP/RQ/N, uses the strict test in category b, and fails on a missing entry. Category H has no primal point and needs none. The one soft spot is G4.

3. Gaps that do not break the proof

G1. Lemma 5 and the Claim of Lemma 3 use facts that are not among (P1)–(P4). §1 lists what is imported from the paper; the following are used and are not in that list:

  • (a) in Z_{p²q²}: Φ_{p²q²} | A ⇔ A is additive in every box (Lemma 5, first step, direction ⇒);
  • (b) in Z_{p²q²}: Φ_{pq²} | A ⇔ for every t the p×q array (c,t′) ↦ #{a : u ≡ c (p), w = t+qt′} is additive (Lemma 5, last step, direction ⇐);
  • (c) in Z_{pq²}: Φ_{pq²} | A ⇔ for every class t mod q the array (u,t′) is additive, and Φ_{pq} | A ⇔ the p×q array of counts is additive (Claim of Lemma 3, direction ⇒ twice).

(c) is the lemma on the level conditions (E_ℓ), (F_ℓ) in Part I of the paper (label Y:lem:cyclo), with the primes exchanged; the paper states it without a written proof ("standard") and says it is formalised. (b) follows from it applied to the image of A in Z_{pq²}, which is a multiset, while the lemma is stated for sets. (a) is not in the paper at all: the paper only treats Z_{pⁿq}. All three are true (kernel of evaluation at a primitive root of unity of order p^a q^b, by linear disjointness of the two cyclotomic fields) and I tested them on 7,934 random instances without a disagreement (§4.11), but the note must state them and prove or cite them; as written Lemma 5 rests on an unstated lemma.

G2. (P4) is not what Theorem A says. Theorem A, and the headline Lean theorem Pnq.weak_tiling_iff_tiling_pnq, say "every weak tile of Z_{pⁿq} is a tile". Lemma 3 needs more: each piece satisfies (T1) and (T2) relative to pq², and the digit set T_{𝒫∖S} is a complement. That is what the proof of Theorem A shows (paper, "Proof of Theorem A"; in Lean the composition of exists_stepTiling_tree, step_structure, T2_of_tree and the (T1) conversion inside tiling_of_tree_T1_T2, with Pqr.tiling_of_T1_T2_digitSet for the complement), but no single named statement says it. Either cite the proof, or add the one-line corollary. (Lemma 2 only needs the weak form.)

G3. Lemma 3, one clause missing. "Every A^c is a weak tile" needs A^c ≠ ∅; it follows from the identity just derived (an empty sum is not 1) but is not said, and the equal size n is used right after.

G4. check_p2q2.py accepts expressions that are undefined on the boundary of a regime. sign() returns '+0' when a denominator factor is only weakly signed, and nonneg() accepts '+0'. I ran it: sign(1/(p-2),'A') = '+0', sign(1/(p-2),'B') = '+0', sign(1/(q-p-1),'A') = '+0', sign(1/(q-p**2-1),'B') = '+0', all accepted as non-negative, although they are undefined at p = 2, at (2,3), at (2,5), which are prime pairs of the regime. So the test "r_c ≥ 0" or "γ*_c ≥ 0" passes for an expression with a pole exactly at p = 2. I could not build a complete wrong certificate that gets through (since Σ_c r_c = M − y₀, a pole in a reduced cost drives y₀ to −∞ near the boundary, which the tests on y₀ then reject), but the soundness of the checker should not rest on such an argument. The shipped file does not exercise the hole: every denominator that occurs in it (raw or after reduction) is a product of p, p±1, q, q±1, pq−1 and p²−2pq+q, and each of these is strictly signed on both regimes (§4.6); and all entries evaluate without a division by zero at (2,3), (2,5) and 1,110 other prime pairs. Fix: require a strict sign for every denominator factor.

G5. The note's description of the sign test is weaker than the code. "accepting a polynomial factor as positive when … all its coefficients are positive" is not enough for the strict inequality of category b (s·t has positive coefficients and vanishes at p = 2). The code does the right thing (it also requires a non-zero constant term for a strict sign, and category b demands strict); the sentence in §3 should say so.

G6. Smaller points about the checker. (i) It feeds strings from the JSON to sympy.sympify, which evaluates them: the file has to be trusted (I parsed it with a whitelist parser instead). (ii) An unknown key in gamma (e.g. "7,7") is silently ignored — harmless, tested. (iii) The lookup is sensitive to the order of the pairs in F — conservative (a reordered entry is reported as a failure).

G7. Proposition 4 (3) should say y₀ > 0 before "so |A| ≤ M/y₀" (true in every use: y₀ > M/k, y₀ = pq, y₀ = σ(γ*) ≥ 1).

G8. Bookkeeping. (i) Z_36 has 64 zero patterns of tiles, not 63: the note's count leaves out A = Z_36 itself (category a); so 54 + 10. (ii) sands/consist36.py reads the certificates from /home/agent2/p2q2/final/certificates_p2q2.json, not from the shipped folder (today the two files have the same SHA-256).

G9. A remark, not a gap: the R branch cannot be tested on examples. No tile in any of my samples (all 383,806 tiles of Z_36; 36,473 of Z_100; 46,003 of Z_225; 73,793 of Z_196) has a pattern of category RP or RQ, so "γ* is a weak complement of the tile" was never exercised on a set. The R certificates are verified only as statements about the linear programme (4.5, 4.6, 4.10), and Lemma 3 only on tiles with a-patterns (4.8). This agrees with Remark 7.1 and with the proof, which never claims that such sets exist. Of the 50 R patterns of regime A, at all 858 pairs tested: 5 have M/y₀ not a multiple of k (no weak tile, by divisibility alone); 32 have M/y₀ a proper multiple of k, and 11 have M/y₀ = k but violate (T2) (a weak tile with such a pattern would, by Lemma 3, be a tile violating the necessary conditions of Coven and Meyerowitz); only two are excluded by nothing: E_p = {1}, E_q = {2}, F = {(1,2),(2,1)} and the same with (1,1) added (y₀ = pq; γ* = 1 at 0 and on class (1,2), 1/q on classes (1,0) and (2,0)). Regime B: 52 = 2 + 36 + 12 + 2, the same two patterns. For these two the proof rests on Lemma 3 alone, and Proposition 4 (2) says that every set of size pq with exactly that zero set is a weak tile; at M = 36 there is no set of any size up to 9 with either zero set (t14_special.py). (A sketch of mine, outside the brief: these two are empty as well. With γ* as complement, Lemma 1 puts each of the p pieces inside one class mod q with q distinct residues mod q², so the p×q arrays that decide Φ_{p²q} have a single non-zero column and cannot be additive. t15_sketch.py confirms it on every set of that shape for (p,q) = (2,3), (2,5), (3,2) — 576, 25,600 and 5,832 sets — and on 60,000 random ones each for (3,5) and (2,7): p²q is a zero of none. If so, and granting Lemma 3 and the necessity of (T1), (T2) for tiles, no R pattern is the pattern of a weak tile, and Lemma 3 is only ever applied to an empty family. That does not weaken the proof; it explains why this branch cannot be probed with examples.)

  • Lemma 3, Claim: a new proof, steps (i)–(iv). This replaces the argument I refereed in §2. It is correct: (i) equal size and (T1) give p ∈ S₁ ⇔ p ∈ S₂; (ii) no orphan zero for a weak tile of Z_{pq²} is what the paper's proof of Theorem A gives (F ⊆ E when the single prime is not a zero; nothing to prove when it is); (iii) a common zero d of two pieces is then exactly divisible by an element of S₁ ∩ S₂ (two different powers of q cannot both divide d exactly), so it is a zero of T = T_{S₁∩S₂}; (iv) 1_T ∗ g is the constant |T|/n ≥ g(0) = 1, so ∏{S₁∩S₂} p_s ≥ ∏{S₁} p_s and S₁ ⊆ S₂. The q-version is the same with "the only power of q dividing p²q is q". The statement of the Claim is unchanged, so 4.4 and 4.8 still test it. Step (ii) tested on every tile of Z_12 (88), Z_18 (551), Z_20 (924) and on 50,030 tiles of Z_50: no orphan zero (t17_revision.py).
