20 November 2026, 14:17 UTC. A framework and four small lemmas, not a theorem. Written in one sitting, read by nobody, not in Lean. Notation as in p2q2-agent2.md; here M = p^a q^b with a, b ≥ 1, orders are written (ℓ, m) for p^ℓ q^m, Z(A) is the set of zeros of A, S_A the prime powers among them. For a weak tile A let

𝒫(M): every weak tile of Z_M satisfies (T1) and (T2) relative to M and has no orphan zero (every zero d > 1 is exactly divisible by some s ∈ S_A).

𝒫(M) implies that weak tiles of Z_M are tiles (Coven–Meyerowitz). 𝒫 holds for prime powers, for p^n q (Part I of the joint paper) and for p²q² (Theorem C together with Lemmas B and C below, which carry the three properties through the two reductions of its proof). (Added 21 November: written out as Lemma P in the last section; and for the seventy-two groups Z_{p³q²} with p, q ≤ 23, Proposition G there.)

Added 14:36 UTC. Kiss, Łaba, Marshall and Somlai (arXiv:2507.11672, Theorem 1.6, as Yonder reports it from a summary; neither of us has read the paper) prove that a set with (T1) and (T2) in a cyclic group of two-prime order has no unsupported divisor. If their notion covers "no orphan" as used here, the third property in 𝒫 follows from the first two and need not be carried through the induction.

The statement that is missing

(I3)(M): every weak tile A ∋ 0 of Z_M for which p or q is a zero has a weak complement vanishing outside pZ_M, or one vanishing outside qZ_M.

Conditional theorem. If 𝒫(M/p), 𝒫(M/q) and (I3)(M) hold, then 𝒫(M) holds. So weak tile ⇒ tile for every M = p^a q^b would follow from (I3) for all such M, by induction.

For tiles (I3) is true: a tile with p ∈ S_A has the digit-set complement T_{𝒫∖S_A} ⊆ pZ_M. For p²q² it is what the certificates of categories RP, RQ give, pattern by pattern; I have no proof of it that is not a list.

Proof of the conditional theorem. Let A ∋ 0 be a weak tile. If neither p nor q is a zero, Lemma A gives A ⊆ pZ_M (or qZ_M) and A/p is a weak tile of Z_{M/p}; Lemma B carries the three properties from A/p to A. Otherwise (I3) gives a weak complement f vanishing outside pZ_M, say. The pieces A^c are weak tiles of Z_{M/p} with the common complement g = f(p·), so they have (T1), (T2), no orphan by 𝒫(M/p); by Lemma D they have the same S; Lemma C carries the three properties to A. ∎

A sharper form of the missing statement (added 16:57 UTC)

Proposition F (the simple fold). Let A be a weak tile of Z_M with step complement f, let p be a zero of A, and suppose that for every m ≥ 1 for which f̂ does not vanish at the order q^m, the order pq^m is a zero of A (in particular: suppose that q^m a zero ⇒ pq^m a zero). Then f′(u, w) = [p | u]·Σ_{δ=0}^{p−1} f(u + δ, w) is a weak complement of A vanishing outside pZ_M.

Proof. f′ ≥ 0, Σf′ = Σf. Its transform at a frequency ξ = (ξ_u, ξ_w) is (1/p)·Σ_i f̂(ξ + i·e)·D(ξ + i·e), with e the frequency (p^{a−1}, 0) and D(η) = Σ_{δ<p} e(δη_u/p^a). If the order of ξ is (ℓ, m) with ℓ ≥ 2, all ξ + ie have that order and Σ_i D(ξ + ie) = p, so f̂′(ξ) = Λ_{ℓm}. If ℓ ≤ 1, D vanishes at all ξ + ie except the one with p-part 0, where it is p; so f̂′(ξ) = Λ_{0m}. Hence f̂′·Â vanishes off 0: at orders (ℓ ≥ 2, m) as for f; at (0, m) because Λ_{0m} ≠ 0 forces q^m to be a zero; at (1, m) by the hypothesis (for m = 0: p is a zero). So 1_A ∗ f′ is the constant |A|Σf′/M = 1. At a point of A it is at least f′(0) = 1 + (p − 1)·f(1, 0) ≥ 1; so f′(0) = 1. ∎

(Tested numerically at M = 36 in both orientations, foldtest.py: 1,484 folds of random optimal step complements of random tiles whose pattern satisfies the hypothesis; every fold is a weak complement inside pZ; about one complement in ten charged numbers prime to p before folding.)

So the normalisation takes care of itself, and (I3) follows from the lowest level of (T2):

(L1)(M): for every weak tile A of Z_M: if p and q^m are zeros then pq^m is a zero; and if q and p^ℓ are zeros then p^ℓ q is a zero.

Conditional theorem, second form. 𝒫(M/p), 𝒫(M/q) and (L1)(M) imply 𝒫(M). (A weak tile not inside a coset of pZ_M or qZ_M has p or q as a zero; with (L1) Proposition F gives a complement in the corresponding subgroup.)

What a smallest counterexample to (L1) looks like. Let 𝒫 hold for all proper divisors and let A be a weak tile of Z_M with p and q^m zeros and pq^m not a zero. If q were a zero and the q-half of (L1) held for A, Proposition F for q and Lemmas C, D would give (T2) for A, hence the zero pq^m. If q were not a zero and some step complement vanished outside pZ_M, the same. So either (α) p and q are both zeros and both halves of (L1) fail for A, or (β) q is not a zero, m ≥ 2, every step complement charges qZ_M ∖ pZ_M, and every class of A modulo q is equidistributed modulo p (tool 1 below). At p²q² the 66 patterns that allow (α) or (β) are: 51 of category b (50 when q > p²), the 2 of category H (type β; Lemma Y′ is (L1) for them), the 1 of category N (none when q > p²), and 12 (14 when q > p²) of category RQ, for which the programme has an optimal complement in qZ that is not a fold; the other 126 patterns with p or q a zero (among them all 69 of category a and 38 of category R) need nothing but Proposition F. (l1check.py.) So what is missing in general is a proof, by counting on sets, that (α) and (β) do not occur.

Two remarks of Yonder's on (L1) (message board, 17:07 UTC; unchecked by anyone else). (1) The conditions at the orders (0, m) and (1, m) involve only the masses f(q^j Z_M) and f(pq^j Z_M), and they are term for term the relations of Part I of the joint paper for Z_{q^b} × Z_p, except that the innermost mass f(pq^b Z_M) is only ≥ 1; the three bounds of Part I need no more. Hence: if q^m is a zero, pq^m is not, and above m the orders q^{m′} and pq^{m′} are zeros together, then |A|·min(p, q) ≤ p^a q^{e_q}; with k | |A| this is a contradiction when every power of p is a zero. So (L1) holds at the topmost failing level for weak tiles with E_p = {1, …, a}. (2) In shape (α) with ℓ = m = 1 a counterexample charges some class (i, 0), i ≥ 1, and some class (0, j), j ≥ 1; then every class of A modulo q^j lies in one class modulo q^{j+1} or in one class modulo p, and symmetrically.

Four lemmas

Lemma A (= Lemma 2 of the p²q² note; general). If neither p nor q is a zero of a weak tile A ∋ 0 of Z_M, then A ⊆ pZ_M or A ⊆ qZ_M. (A step complement has mass σ/p on pZ_M and σ/q on qZ_M, hence positive mass on the units.)

Lemma B (a set inside pZ). Let A ⊆ pZ_M and A′ = A/p ⊆ Z_{M/p}. Then (ℓ, m) is a zero of A iff ℓ ≥ 1 and (ℓ − 1, m) is a zero of A′ [with (0, m) read as q^m, and (0, 0) never a zero], or ℓ = 0 and (0, m) is a zero of A′. Consequently, if A′ satisfies (T1), (T2) and has no orphan relative to M/p, so does A relative to M.

Proof. A(X) = A′(X^p), and for a primitive d-th root ζ, ζ^p is a primitive root of order d/p if p | d and of order d otherwise. So E_p(A) = E_p(A′) + 1, E_q(A) = E_q(A′), and (ℓ, m) ∈ F(A) iff (ℓ − 1, m) ∈ F(A′) when ℓ ≥ 2, iff m ∈ E_q(A′) when ℓ = 1. (T1): |A| = |A′|. (T2) and "no orphan" follow by reading these equivalences in the two directions. ∎

Lemma C (pieces with the same S). Let A ⊆ Z_M and let its pieces A^c ⊆ Z_{M/p} (c ∈ Z_p) all satisfy (T1), (T2), no orphan relative to M/p, with the same set S′ of prime-power zeros. Then A satisfies (T1), (T2), no orphan relative to M, with S_A = {p} ∪ {p^{ℓ+1} : p^ℓ ∈ S′} ∪ {q^m ∈ S′}.

Proof. A(X) = Σ_c X^c A^c(X^p). The pieces have equal size, so p is a zero. If p² | d then Φ_d(X) = Φ_{d/p}(X^p) and d is a zero of A iff d/p is a zero of every piece. If p ∤ m′ and m′ is a zero of every piece, then m′ and pm′ are zeros of A. Hence S_A contains the set displayed; its product of primes is p·|A^c| = |A|, and the product over S_A divides |A|: equality, and (T1). (T2) at (ℓ, m): if ℓ ≥ 2, (ℓ − 1, m) is a zero of every piece by their (T2); if ℓ = 1, q^m is. No orphan: a zero (1, m) is supported by p; a zero (ℓ, m) with ℓ ≥ 2 gives the zero (ℓ − 1, m) of every piece, which is supported by p^{ℓ−1} or q^m in S′. ∎

Lemma D (a common weak complement forces the same S; general form of the Claim in Lemma 3). Let A₁, A₂ be weak tiles of Z_{M′}, M′ = p^{a′} q^{b′}, with a common weak complement, both satisfying (T1) and without orphan. Then S_{A₁} = S_{A₂}.

Proof. Both have n = M′/Σg elements, and by (T1) n = p^{α_i} q^{β_i} with α_i, β_i the numbers of powers of p and of q in S_i; so these numbers agree. Put S₀ = S₁ ∩ S₂ and let P_i, Q_i be the powers of p, of q in S_i ∖ S₀; |P₁| = |P₂| = x, |Q₁| = |Q₂| = y. Let d > 1 be a zero of both sets; some s₁ ∈ S₁ and s₂ ∈ S₂ divide d exactly. If neither lies in S₀ ∪ P₁ ∪ P₂, both are powers of q dividing d exactly, hence equal and in S₀: impossible. So every common zero is a zero of the digit set of S₀ ∪ P₁ ∪ P₂, and likewise of the digit set of S₀ ∪ Q₁ ∪ Q₂. By the bound (S) for two sets, n ≤ p^{2x}·∏{S₀} and n ≤ q^{2y}·∏{S₀}, while n = p^x q^y·∏_{S₀}. So q^y ≤ p^x ≤ q^y, and x = y = 0. ∎

Partial tools for (I3) (none of them enough)

  1. Exactly one of the two primes is a zero. Let q be a zero, p not, and A ⊄ a coset of pZ_M, with a step complement f. Summing the tiling identity over a coset of pqZ_M ("box" (c, t)), with N(c, t) the number of elements of A in it and R_c the number in the class c modulo p, gives f(pZ_M ∖ qZ_M)·(R_c − q·N(c, t)) = 0 for all c, t. So either f already vanishes outside qZ_M — and (I3) holds with this f — or every class modulo p is equidistributed over the classes modulo q (and then pq is a zero).
  2. Folding along other classes. Proposition F shifts along (1, 0). Shifting instead along p − 1 elements of a class (0, j) on which f vanishes gives a function whose transform at (0, m), (1, m) is Λ_{0m} for m ≤ j and a combination of Λ_{0m}, Λ_{1m} with nonzero coefficients for m > j (computed for a = b = 2 only).
  3. Dilation. f_p(y) = Σ_{px = y} f(x) is supported in pZ_M with transform f̂(pξ); it is a complement when p is a zero, f vanishes on the nonzero elements of order p, and Z(A) is closed under d ↦ pd.
  4. What the programme says at p³q² (next-case-p3q2/, numerical): of the 1,536 patterns with p or q a zero, all but 26–54 per regime are settled as in p²q² (category a, or D^w < k, or an optimal complement in a subgroup); the rest would need counting lemmas (15–50) or integrality arguments (2–32). So (I3) cannot be read off the programme alone in general: the sets have to enter, as they do through Lemma Y′ at p²q².

Reductions at the top level (added 21 November, 03:45 UTC)

Still no theorem. These are the mirror images of what the sections above do at the bottom of the divisor lattice (the classes modulo p and q); here the subgroups of order p and q take their place. Written by me alone, read by nobody; tested numerically (toptest.py, toptest.out: random tiles of Z_36, Z_72, Z_108 in both orientations, 1,062 to 1,788 step complements each, no failure of any statement below).

Let P = (M/p)·Z_M and Q = (M/q)·Z_M be the subgroups of order p and q, K = P + Q. For a step complement f write x, y, z for its values on P∖{0}, on Q∖{0} and on K∖(P ∪ Q): the classes (a − 1, b), (a, b − 1), (a − 1, b − 1).

Lemma E. Let A be a weak tile of Z_M and f a step complement of A.

  1. f ≤ 1 everywhere.
  2. Λ_{ab}(f) = 1 − x − y + z. So if M is not a zero of A, then x + y = 1 + z.
  3. (The complement is constant on P.) If x = 1, then A meets every coset of P at most once, and the image A′ of A in Z_M/P ≅ Z_{M/p} is a weak tile, with step complement f′ = (the push-forward of f)/p. The zeros of A among the orders (ℓ, m) with ℓ < a are exactly the zeros of A′. If A′ satisfies (T1) and (T2) relative to M/p, then p^a is not a zero of A, and A satisfies (T1) and (T2) relative to M.
  4. (The set is a union of cosets of P.) If A + P = A, the image A′ of A in Z_{M/p} is a weak tile, with step complement the push-forward of f, and Z(A) = Z(A′) ∪ {(a, m) : 0 ≤ m ≤ b}. (T1), (T2) and "no orphan" pass from A′ to A.
  5. (Lines.) If M is a zero of A, then A meets every coset of K in a union of cosets of P or in a union of cosets of Q.
  6. Consequently one of four things holds: (i) A + P = A or A + Q = A; (ii) some step complement of A is 1 on all of P, or on all of Q; (iii) M is a zero of A and A is mixed: one coset of K meets A in a union of cosets of P that is not a union of cosets of Q, and another the other way round; then every weak complement vanishes on (P ∪ Q)∖{0}; (iv) M is not a zero of A, and every step complement has x > 0, y > 0 and x + y = 1 + z; then A meets every coset of P and every coset of Q in at most one point. In cases (i) and (ii) the question whether A tiles is a question in Z_{M/p} or Z_{M/q}, by 3 and 4.

Proof. (1) For a ∈ A and any g: 1 = Σ_{a′∈A} f(a + g − a′) ≥ f(g). (2) A character of order M is non-trivial on P and on Q; its sums over P∖{0}, Q∖{0} and K∖(P ∪ Q) are −1, −1 and 1, and it sums to zero over every class (i, j) with i ≤ a − 2 or j ≤ b − 2. (3) For a ∈ A and g ∈ P∖{0}: 1 = Σ_{a′} f(a + g − a′) ≥ f(g) = 1, so f(a − a′ + g) = 0 for all a′ ≠ a and all g ∈ P; with Lemma 1 of the p²q² note, a − a′ ∉ P. For v ∈ Z_M the sum of f′ over the differences of the image of v and the points of A′ is (1/p)·Σ_{g∈P} (1_A ∗ f)(v + g) = 1, and f′(0) = (1 + (p − 1)x)/p = 1. A character of order (ℓ, m) with ℓ < a is trivial on P, so it takes the same values on A and on A′. If A′ has (T1), then |A| = |A′| is the product of the primes of the prime-power zeros of A′; a further zero p^a of A would multiply the product dividing |A| by p. So the prime-power zeros of A are those of A′, all with ℓ < a, and (T1), (T2) for A are the statements for A′. (4) Here x = 0 by Lemma 1, so the push-forward f′ has f′(0) = 1, and 1_{A′} ∗ f′ at the image of v is Σ_{a∈A} f(v − a) = 1. A character of order (a, m) is non-trivial on P, so its sum over every coset of P, and hence over A, is zero; the other orders are as in 3. S_A = S_{A′} ∪ {p^a} and |A| = p·|A′| give (T1); (T2) at (a, m) holds because every (a, m) is a zero, and elsewhere it is inherited, like "no orphan". (5) As M is a zero, the indicator of A on every coset of K, written as a p × q array, has the form α_u + β_v (Lemma L in the last section); an array of zeros and ones of that form is a union of rows or a union of columns. (6) If M is a zero and A is neither a union of cosets of P nor one of cosets of Q, 5 gives the two cosets of (iii), and then A − A contains P∖{0} and Q∖{0}, where every weak complement vanishes. If M is not a zero, 2 gives x + y = 1 + z. If y = 0 for some step complement, then x = 1 + z ≥ 1, so x = 1 by 1: case (ii). Likewise if x = 0. Otherwise x, y > 0 for every step complement, and Lemma 1 gives the last statement. ∎

In 3 I do not get "no orphan" for A: a zero (a, m) of A whose q^m is not a zero would be one, and nothing above excludes it. (It is excluded if the theorem of Kiss, Łaba, Marshall and Somlai quoted at the top says what I think it says, since A then has (T1) and (T2).)

Proposition F′ (the top fold; the mirror image of Proposition F). Let f be a step complement of A, and let f^↑ be f with its value on each class (a − 1, j), 0 ≤ j ≤ b, replaced by its value on (a, j). Then f^↑ ≥ 0, f^↑(0) = 1, f^↑ is constant on the cosets of P, and its transform is

Λ^↑{ℓm} = Λ{ℓm} + (p − 1)·Λ_{am} for ℓ < a, Λ^↑_{am} = 0.

Hence: if Λ_{a0}(f) = 0 (for instance if p^a is not a zero of A), and if for every m ≥ 1 with Λ_{am}(f) ≠ 0 all the orders (ℓ, m) with ℓ < a are zeros of A, then f^↑ is a weak complement of A equal to 1 on P, and Lemma E.3 applies.

Proof. The classes (a − 1, j) and (a, j) together are P × W_j, with W_j the class j of Z_{q^b}, and (a, j) is {0} × W_j. So f^↑ − f = Σ_j d_j·1_{(P∖{0}) × W_j} with d_j = f_{a,j} − f_{a−1,j}, and its transform at an order (ℓ, m) is c·D_m, where c = p − 1 for ℓ < a, c = −1 for ℓ = a, and D_m depends on m only. A function constant on the cosets of P has no transform at the orders (a, m); so Λ_{am} − D_m = 0, which gives D_m and the formula. Under the hypothesis Λ^↑ vanishes at every order other than (0, 0) that is not a zero of A, and Σf^↑ = Λ^↑_{00} = Σf; so 1_A ∗ f^↑ is the constant |A|·Σf/M = 1. ∎

Proposition F moves the mass of f into pZ_M when the zeros of A are closed under (0, m) ↦ (1, m) where it matters; F′ makes f constant on P when they are closed under (a, m) ↦ (ℓ, m), ℓ < a, where it matters. Neither hypothesis is known for weak tiles.

What a smallest counterexample looks like, with both ends. Let every weak tile of every proper divisor group have (T1), (T2) and no orphan, and let A be a weak tile of Z_M that does not tile. Then A lies in no coset of pZ_M or qZ_M, no weak complement of A vanishes outside pZ_M or outside qZ_M, and at the top A is of kind (iii) or (iv) of Lemma E.6: either M is a zero and A is mixed, or M is not a zero and every step complement charges both P∖{0} and Q∖{0}, so that A is a partial permutation in every coset of K. At the bottom the shapes (α), (β) above apply. (Kinds (i) and (ii) are excluded because they give (T1) and (T2) for A, which is enough to tile.) In the random samples of toptest.out mixed tiles occur at Z_72 and Z_108 (4 of 155, 2 of 100, 2 of 86 tiles with M a zero) and not at Z_36; Z_72 is the smallest cyclic group in which a tiling need not have a periodic factor.

Two things tried on the same day that do not work. (a) Tijdeman's argument (replace A by pA when p does not divide |A|) for a rational complement f = B/N: the coefficients of A(X^p)·B(X) are ≡ N modulo p and average N, which forces them to be N only if N < p. (b) The norm inequality of ../t2why-agent2/: real, but its exponents are φ(d) and it is not uniform in p and q.

Z_{p³q²}: seventy-two groups settled, and what all p and q would still need (21 November; first version 04:03 UTC, revised 05:12 UTC after a referee's report)

A result for particular groups and a route for the general case; no theorem for all p and q. Exact rational arithmetic throughout; no search over sets is used in the proof. Written by me in one night. A fresh instance with no part in it refereed the first version (thirty-four pairs) for an hour with instructions to break it and found no error that affects the result; its report is next-case-p3q2/referee/report.md, word for word, and what it checked is summarised under "Checks" below. Everything it asked for is done in this revision: the property 𝒫 for Z_{p²q²} is written out (Lemma P), the lines lemma is stated here, the categories cite what they use, and Lemma M says "line-built" where it does not mean "mixed". The revision also extends the computation from thirty-four to seventy-two pairs and states the stronger conclusion that the referee pointed out. Nobody but the referee and me has read it.

Proposition G (computer-assisted, exact). Let p ≠ q be primes, both at most 23 (seventy-two ordered pairs). Then every weak tile of Z_{p³q²} satisfies (T1) and (T2), has no orphan zero, and is a tile. The groups have orders from 72 to 4,392,287.

Three of these orders (72, 108, 200) are within the range M ≤ 300 of Claim 1w, which rests on a constraint solver; the others were out of reach of any search over sets.

Lemma L (lines). Let d = p^ℓ q^m with ℓ, m ≥ 1 be a zero of a multiset A in Z_M, and let N(c) be the number of elements of A congruent to c modulo d. On every coset of the subgroup of order pq of Z_d, written as a p × q array, N(u, v) = α_u + β_v with integers α_u, β_v ≥ 0: the array is a sum of full rows and full columns with multiplicities. If A is a set and d = M, the array has entries 0 and 1 and is a union of rows or a union of columns.

Proof. Φ_d divides A exactly when (X^{d/p} − 1)(X^{d/q} − 1)·A(X) vanishes modulo X^d − 1 (the product contains every cyclotomic factor of X^d − 1 other than Φ_d), that is, when N(c) − N(c + d/p) − N(c + d/q) + N(c + d/p + d/q) = 0 for all c. On a coset this gives N(u, v) = N(u, v₀) + N(u₀, v) − N(u₀, v₀); take u₀ where N(·, v₀) is least, α_u = N(u, v₀) − N(u₀, v₀) and β_v = N(u₀, v). An array of zeros and ones of this form has all α_u = 0 or all β_v = 0. ∎ (This is the two-prime case of the theorem of Lam and Leung on vanishing sums of roots of unity.)

Lemma P (the property 𝒫 for Z_{p²q²}). Every weak tile of Z_{p²q²} satisfies (T1) and (T2) relative to that group and has no orphan zero.

Proof. Follow the proof of Theorem C (p2q2-agent2.md, Section 5) through its cases. If neither p nor q is a zero, A lies in a coset of pZ or qZ and A/p (or A/q) is a weak tile of Z_{pq²} or Z_{p²q}, which has the three properties by (P4); Lemma B carries them to A. If the pattern of zeros is of category a, (T2) and "no orphan" hold by definition and (T1) by the bound (S). In categories b, H and N there is no weak tile. In categories RP and RQ the pieces are weak tiles of Z_{pq²} or Z_{p²q} with a common complement; they have the three properties by (P4) and the same S by the Claim of Lemma 3, and Lemma C carries the three properties to A. ∎ (So no weak tile has a pattern of category RP or RQ either: such a pattern violates (T2) or has an orphan.)

How the proposition is proved. Every weak tile A has a set Z of zeros; |A| = D^w(Z) exactly (Proposition 4 of p2q2-agent2.md: a step complement is a feasible point of the programme, and a feasible point with sum M/|A| is a step complement), and the product k of the primes of the prime-power zeros divides |A|. Lemma M (line-built sets in Z_{p³q²}). Call A ⊆ Z_M, M = p³q², line-built if it is the union of i ≥ 1 cosets of P and j ≥ 1 cosets of Q that lie in different cosets of K. (By Lemma E.5 a set with M as a zero that is not a union of cosets of P and not a union of cosets of Q is line-built; the mixed sets of Lemma E.6 are among them.) Let X ⊆ Z_M/P ≅ Z_{p²q²} and Y ⊆ Z_M/Q ≅ Z_{p³q} be the sets of these cosets. For a character χ of order (ℓ, m): χ(A) = 0 if (ℓ, m) = (3, 2); χ(A) = q·χ(Y) if ℓ = 3 and m ≤ 1; χ(A) = p·χ(X) if ℓ ≤ 2 and m = 2; and χ(A) = p·χ(X) + q·χ(Y) if ℓ ≤ 2 and m ≤ 1. In the last case the counts modulo p^ℓ q^m satisfy N^A = p·N^X + q·N^Y. Then:

  • M0. |A| = ip + jq. So |A| ≠ pq; and if |A| = p²q, then j = pj′ and i = q(p − j′) with 1 ≤ j′ ≤ p − 1.
  • M2. If |A| = p²q and p, p³q and p²q² are zeros of A, then A + K = A. (Then A is a union of cosets of P.)
  • M4. If |A| = p²q, then q and pq² are not both zeros of A.

Proof. A character of order (ℓ, m) is trivial on P exactly when ℓ ≤ 2 and on Q exactly when m ≤ 1, and its sum over a coset of a subgroup on which it is not trivial vanishes; a coset of P or of Q lies in one class modulo p²q. That is the dictionary. (M0) If ip + jq = pq then p divides j, so jq ≥ pq. If ip + jq = p²q then p divides j, and i ≥ 1 gives j′ < p.

(M2) As p is a zero, every class c modulo p holds pq = p·N^X(c) + q·N^Y(c) elements; so N^Y(c) = p·t_c and N^X(c) = q(1 − t_c), with t_c ∈ {0, 1}. By the dictionary p³q is a zero of Y, the top order of Z_{p³q}; so by Lemma L the set Y meets every coset of the subgroup of order pq of Z_{p³q} in a union of cosets of its subgroup of order p or of its subgroup of order q. Such a coset lies in one class modulo p. A class with t_c = 1 holds p points of Y, a sum of multiples of p and multiples of q; a positive number of cosets of order q would have to be a multiple of p and hold at least pq points. So these p points are one coset of the subgroup of order p, which is K/Q: the cosets of Q in A fill whole cosets of K. In the same way p²q² is a zero of X, the top order of Z_{p²q²}, and a class with t_c = 0 holds q points of X: one coset of the subgroup of order q, which is K/P. So A + K = A.

(M4) As q is a zero, every class t modulo q holds p² = p·N^X(t) + q·N^Y(t) elements; so N^Y(t) = p·s_t and N^X(t) = p − q·s_t ≥ 0. Let pq² be a zero of A, hence of X. The class t modulo q is a coset of the subgroup of order pq of Z_{pq²}, and by Lemma L the counts of X modulo pq² on it are a sum of α full rows, of q points, and β full columns, of p points: p − q·s_t = αq + βp. If β = 0, q divides p. So β = 1, α = 0 and s_t = 0, for every t; and Y is empty. ∎

(If p < q the first two lines of the proof of M4 already give s_t = 0: a line-built set of p²q elements then does not have q as a zero at all. The first version used this as a separate part M3; M4 covers every case where it was used.)

For the seventy-two pairs every pattern of category H has |A| = pq or |A| = p²q and falls to M0, M2 or M4 (next-case-p3q2/settle.py; the column "left" of the table); a set excluded through M2 would be a union of cosets of P, which category H has already ruled out. The two patterns that needed Lemma Y′ in the proof for Z_{p²q²} are of the same kind: there too M is a zero, |A| = pq, and a union of cosets of P and of Q in different cosets of K cannot have pq elements.

Signs. A polynomial is shown positive on a piece by substituting the parametrisation, multiplying by the power of (1 + z) that clears the denominator, and finding all coefficients ≥ 0 and the value at r = s = z = 0 positive. A rational function is signed through its irreducible factors.

Case distinctions. Where no single basis is feasible on a whole band, the generator takes the polynomial factor F whose sign it could not decide and treats the two parts F ≥ 0 and F ≤ 0 of the band separately (and again inside them, to depth two at most): every point lies in one of the parts. In a part, F counts as known with its weak sign and may not occur in a denominator. A part in which the generator finds no prime pair is closed by a proof that it has no point at all: a combination Σ mᵢ·sᵢFᵢ of the assumed quantities sᵢFᵢ ≥ 0, with multipliers mᵢ that are polynomials with coefficients ≥ 0, that is negative on the whole band.

(3) The categories agree with the pair-by-pair computations of Propositions G and H wherever both exist.

Its own exact simplex, which answers only after checking an exact primal and dual pair, at 933 prime pairs of its own choosing, among them every ordered pair of primes below 45, the first and last two primes of every band for fixed primes up to 499, primes on both sides of every branching curve and band ends at 10007 and 1000003: 1,126,305 certificate checks, no mismatch, and its own classification agrees with settle72.out in every column for all 72 pairs. Coverage by brute force: each of the 184,470 ordered pairs of distinct primes below 3,000 lies in exactly one piece (or is one of the six direct pairs, or in the family). Every certificate evaluated in integer arithmetic at 183,088 prime pairs (about 222 million leaf evaluations), no failure; with the previous item this covers every pair of distinct primes below 300. Category N by brute force: no integer at 168,409 pairs. A mutation test: certificates read under the wrong band name are rejected by verify3.py. The three integrality arguments re-derived as number theory; the fifteen shapes that D^w/k takes under argument (i) checked by hand. It notes that argument (ii) is used by no certificate (44 of the 164 leaves of category N use (iii), 120 use (i), one of them with a curve), and that it did not re-read the lemmas or the category table, and that sympy's factoring and field arithmetic are common to my generator, my checker and its symbolic scripts (its numerical tests do not depend on them).

What the referee found missing, and what was done. (G1) Neither checker looked at the denominators of the dual solution: a pole of y inside a piece would break the duality argument even if every reduced cost stayed regular. (G2) Neither checked that the solution returned by the linear algebra satisfies its equations, that the file of certificates holds all 2,048 patterns, or (in the one-parameter checker) that the patterns of categories T, U, H violate (T2). The referee made all four checks itself and found nothing. They are now part of verify3.py and verify_fast.py, and both were run again on everything: ten pieces, 12,224 leaves, no failure; twelve families, 14,580 certificates, no failure (verify3.out, verify_fast.out). (a3) The generator region.py accepted a weakly signed factor in a denominator where the text and the checker forbid it; no certificate depends on it, and the generator now forbids it too (the published certificates were made before that change and pass the strict checker). The count of pairs and a stale file name in the previous section are corrected.

What this rests on, and what could be wrong. Theorem C and Part I of the joint paper (both checked in Lean); the theorem of Coven and Meyerowitz; Lemmas A to E, L, M, P (short proofs, refereed once); the logic of the categories (refereed once); and, new here, about two thousand lines of Python: the generators (plp.py, family.py, region.py, split.py, empty.py) and the checkers (verify_fast.py, verify3.py, crosscheck*.py), which use sympy for factoring polynomials, for arithmetic in the field Q(p, q) and for isolating real roots. A wrong certificate would have to pass a checker that shares no code with its generator and a numerical comparison at several pairs in its piece; a mistake in the logic shared by generator and checker (what a certificate has to show; which ends of a band are covered; the three integrality arguments) would pass both, and the one such mistake found so far, the band ends, was found by the numerical comparison. That logic is what a referee should attack first.
