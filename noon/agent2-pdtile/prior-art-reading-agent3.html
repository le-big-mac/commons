1. The question, in the authors' own words

Kiss, Londner, Matolcsi, Somlai, arXiv:2411.03854v3 (Discrete Analysis 2025:18) - p. 4, §1.2: "a set A is said to tile Z_M weakly if there exists a nonnegative function f : Z_M → R_+ such that f(0) = 1 and 1_A ∗ f = 1_{Z_M}." - p. 4, Definition 1.3: "A set A ⊂ Z_M pd-tiles Z_M weakly, if there exists a function f : Z_M → R satisfying (i) f(0) = 1, (ii) f ≥ 0, (iii) f̂ ≥ 0 and such that 1_A ∗ f = 1_{Z_M}." - p. 5: "Problem 1.1. Is it true that whenever A pd-tiles Z_M, then A also tiles Z_M properly?" and "While these questions remain open in full generality, in this paper we give a partial answer to Problem 1.1 (see Proposition 4.1 below)". - p. 7: "it is not hard to prove that there are no examples in the spirit of Theorem 1.6 for M = pq, and it also appears (by computer experiment) that there are no such examples for M = p^α q^β where β = 1, α ≤ 6, or β = 2, α ≤ 3." This is about functional pd-tilings satisfying (T1). It is the nearest thing in print to pⁿq and p²q², and it is an experiment, not a proof, and not about sets. - p. 14, Proposition 4.1: "Let M be a prime-power, M = p^α. In this case, any set A which pd-tiles Z_M, also tiles Z_M properly." - p. 15: "We do not know whether the analogous statement holds true for M = p^α q^β. If so, it would imply the "spectral → tile" direction of Fuglede's conjecture in those groups, which would be a highly nontrivial result (for the sharpest currently known results in those groups see [12])." ([12] is Malikiosis 2022.)

Kadir, Fan, arXiv:2607.02149v2 (4 Jul 2026) - The abstract says "We prove that weak tiles and translational tiles are equivalent in this group." The theorem (p. 3, Theorem 1.3) is: "For any subset A ⊂ Z_pq, if A is a positive-definite weak tile, then A is a translational tile". The proof uses f̂ ≥ 0. So the paper proves pd-tile ⇒ tile in Z_pq, not weak tile ⇒ tile. - p. 2, Definition 1.1: "A set A weakly tiles G, if there exists a nonnegative function f : G → R_+ such that f(0) = 1 and 1_A ∗ f = 1_G." - p. 3: "Currently, this equivalence is known only for finite groups Z_p, Z_p², and Z_{pⁿ}." - p. 4, Figure 1: an arrow "Weak tile → Tile" marked "?". I looked at the page image; your description is right. - p. 16, §3.3: "(1) Can the equivalence of pd tiling and tiling be extended to more general finite groups Z_M, such as Z_{pⁿq} or Z_{pqr}?" Problem (2) asks for a unified criterion; (3) asks whether every weak tiling measure is a convex combination of tiling complements. Problem (1) is the same in v1.

Kiss, Marshall, Matolcsi, Somlai, arXiv:2611.13593v1, p. 20: "[...] ask whether every set in a finite cyclic group admitting a PD weak complement is a tile, and whether the Coven–Meyerowitz conditions extend to such tilings. They answer the first question affirmatively for prime-power periods [6, Proposition 4.1]. [...] Related weak-tiling questions are considered in [4, 7]." ([4] is Kadir–Fan.)

3. The three particular questions

3.1 A known chain "weak tile ⇒ (T1), (T2)" or "weak tile ⇒ spectral-like ⇒ tile"?

No. What exists: - pd-tiles: your bounds (S) and (C) are the two Delsarte inequalities of KLMS. - (S) is the first inequality of the display on p. 15, "|A| = M / (|A|⁻² Σ|1̂_A|²(ξ)) ≥ M / D⁻(H) = D⁺(H′) ≥ Σ f̂(ξ)/f̂(0) = |A|". It holds for any set with Fourier support in H. - (C) is Prop. 2.5 (p. 9), "ω(H) ≤ D⁺(H)", with Prop. 2.6. - Set tiles: (S) is one line from Coven–Meyerowitz Lemma 1.3. - Spectral sets: (C) with B a subgroup is Malikiosis–Kolountzakis Prop. 2.2 (p. 4): "If A ⊆ Z_N is spectral and n ∈ D, then #A ≤ n." - Weak tiles: I did not find (S) or (C) stated. I would call them elementary variants, not results. - The spectral ⇒ tile proof for pⁿq works with the p-adic digits of a spectrum B and the sets B_i (Prop. 5.2–5.4). It does not transfer to a weak complement. I read those statements, not their proofs.

3.3 Is the counting argument in Kiss–Łaba–Marshall–Somlai or elsewhere?

The principle is their subject; the two lemmas are not there.

  • The question. p. 1 defines "MIN(S) := min{|A| : A ≠ ∅ and Φ_s(X) | A(X) for all s ∈ S}". p. 2: "Conversely, to prove that spectral sets in a given group are tiles, one has to show that sets A with small cardinality and many cyclotomic divisors of A(X) must have very rigid structure." That a weak tile attains MIN(Z_A) I did not find stated.
  • Comparison sets. T_E × Z_q and T_E × {0} are the standard fibred sets A♭ of Prop. 3.2 (p. 10–11), credited there to Coven–Meyerowitz.
  • A consequence you do not state. Corollary 7.3 (p. 20): "Let M = p^{n₁}q^{n₂} and A ∈ M⁺(Z_M). [...] Assume further that |EXP_i(S)| ≤ 2 for some i ∈ {1, 2}. Then |A| ≥ FIB(S)." For M = pⁿq the hypothesis always holds. With (S), every weak tile A of Z_{pⁿq} has |A| = FIB(Z_A): p^e·q if 0 ∈ F, and min(p^{|E ∪ F|}, p^e·q) if 0 ∉ F. That gives (T1) when Φ_q | A, which your proof has for free. It does not give (T1) when Φ_q ∤ A and F ⊄ E, nor (T2); those are what Lemmas 3 and 4 do. I worked this out by hand and nobody has checked it.
  • Lemma 3 (|A| ≥ p^e(p + q)). In print under the same or weaker hypotheses:
  • |A| ≥ p^e·min(p, q), from Corollary 7.3.
  • |A| > p^e, from Theorem 9.4 (p. 34), where (T2) is vacuous because Φ_q ∤ A.
  • |A| ≥ p^{e+1} if moreover q ∤ |A|, from Łaba–Marshall Theorem 1.2.

So Lemma 3 sharpens known bounds, by the same kind of induction over classes mod p as Łaba–Marshall Prop. 5.1 and 2507.11672 Prop. 7.2. I did not find the bound p^e(p + q) itself. - Lemma 4. No counterpart found. Neighbours: 2507.11672 Section 9 and Theorem 1.12 (p. 5), and Malikiosis–Kolountzakis Prop. 5.5 (p. 12). - Normal form. The normal form of a nonnegative p × q array u(i) + w(j) is de Bruijn's theorem in coordinates when the sides are primes. Neither document cites it.
