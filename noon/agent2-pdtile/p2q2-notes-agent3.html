Notes towards Z_{p²q²}: two lemmas that dispose of the four hard patterns of Z_36 (agent3 / Yonder)

20 November 2026, 10:40 UTC. Not a theorem about p²q². Two short lemmas with complete proofs, valid for all primes p ≠ q, and a statement of what they do and do not settle. Read by nobody else; not in Lean.

Setting

Z_M ≅ Z_{p²} × Z_{q²}, x ↦ (u, w). For c ∈ Z_p and t ∈ Z_q the box (c, t) is the set of pq points with u ≡ c (mod p) and w ≡ t (mod q), indexed by (c′, t′) through u = c + pc′, w = t + qt′. For a set A let N(c, t) = |A ∩ box (c, t)|. The count criteria (checked against exact division for M = 36 in census36-agent3/crit36.py):

  • Φ_p ∣ A iff the row sums Σ_t N(c, t) are equal; Φ_q ∣ A iff the column sums are equal; Φ_{pq} ∣ A iff the p × q array N is additive.
  • Φ_{pq²} ∣ A iff for every t the p × q array (c, t′) ↦ #{a : u ≡ c (p), w = t + qt′} is additive.
  • Φ_{p²q} ∣ A iff for every c the p × q array (c′, t) ↦ #{a : u = c + pc′, w ≡ t (q)} is additive.
  • Φ_{p²q²} ∣ A iff in every box the p × q array of zeros and ones (c′, t′) ↦ [point ∈ A] is additive; by the lemma on additive 0/1 arrays in Noon's pqr-agent2.md (I6), that means: in every box, A is a union of full rows (c′ fixed) or a union of full columns (t′ fixed).

Two consequences used below. (a) If Φ_{pq²} ∣ A, then for each t the numbers N(c, t), c ∈ Z_p, are congruent mod q: an additive array with q columns has row sums that differ by multiples of q. (b) If Φ_{p²q²} ∣ A, every N(c, t) is a multiple of q (rows) or a multiple of p (columns).

Lemma X

If |A| = pq, Φ_q ∣ A and Φ_{pq²} ∣ A, then N(c, t) = 1 for all c, t. Hence Φ_p ∣ A and Φ_{pq} ∣ A.

Proof. Each column of N sums to |A|/q = p. By (a) the p entries of column t are all ≡ r_t (mod q), so p·r_t ≡ p, and r_t ≡ 1 because p is invertible mod q. So every entry is at least 1, and p entries that are at least 1 and sum to p are all 1. ∎

Lemma Y

If |A| = pq, Φ_q ∣ A and Φ_{p²q²} ∣ A, then for every t exactly one box (c_t, t) meets A, in a single full column {(c_t + pc′, t + qt′t) : c′ ∈ Z_p}. Hence Φ{p²} ∣ A and Φ_{p²q} ∣ A, and Φ_p ∤ A.

Proof. Fix t. The boxes (c, t), c ∈ Z_p, hold p elements of A between them (Φ_q). A box that is a union of j full columns holds pj elements; one that is a union of s full rows holds qs. So p = p·(Σ j) + q·(Σ s) over the boxes of this t, where a full box is counted as columns. Then q·Σ s is a nonnegative multiple of p that is at most p; it is not p, since q ∤ p; so Σ s = 0 and Σ j = 1. That is the first assertion. The p points of the column have each c′ once, so for every c the count #{a : u = c + pc′} does not depend on c′ (Φ_{p²}), and (c′, t) ↦ #{a : u = c + pc′, w ≡ t} = [c = c_t] does not depend on c′, so it is additive (Φ_{p²q}). The row sums of N are p·#{t : c_t = c}, which cannot all equal q; so Φ_p ∤ A. ∎

By the symmetry p ↔ q there are two more: Lemma X′ (|A| = pq, Φ_p, Φ_{p²q} ⟹ N ≡ 1) and Lemma Y′ (|A| = pq, Φ_p, Φ_{p²q²} ⟹ single full rows, Φ_{q²}, Φ_{pq²}, and Φ_q ∤ A). Note that Y and Y′ together say: no set of pq elements has Φ_p, Φ_q and Φ_{p²q²}.

What they settle at M = 36, and what they do not

The census of all 2³⁵ subsets (census36-agent3/) leaves exactly four patterns that are not accounted for by divisibility or by the inequality |A| ≥ k(p + q) for sets with an orphan zero (which at M = 36 is an observation from the census, true for 37 of the 39 orphan patterns; it is proved only for pⁿq). They are the patterns in which the weak-tiling bound allows a set of k = 6 elements and no such set exists:

pattern excluded by
{2, 3, 18} (no Φ_6) Lemma X with (p, q) = (2, 3): Φ_3 and Φ_18 at size 6 force Φ_6
{4, 3, 18} (no Φ_2) the same: they force Φ_2
{4, 3, 36} (no Φ_12) Lemma Y: Φ_3 and Φ_36 at size 6 force Φ_12
{2, 3, 36} Lemma Y: Φ_3 and Φ_36 at size 6 exclude Φ_2

So for Z_36 every pattern is now accounted for by a stated argument or a stated bound, but only in the sense that the bounds were computed by a linear program for this one modulus. For a theorem about p²q² one still needs, uniformly in p and q: (i) the upper bounds themselves, for each kind of pattern, as cliques or competitor sets or step-identity relations on two trees; (ii) a proof of the lower bound k(p + q) for sets with an orphan zero where it holds, and the right statement when both primes have prime-power zeros, where it is false and the truth at 36 is 3k; (iii) whatever the other values of (p, q) add, since Noon finds that the list of critical patterns depends on the two primes. None of that is done.
