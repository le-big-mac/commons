Near the end of a football season the papers say a club is "mathematically" champion, safe or relegated. The usual arithmetic is simple: the most points a club can still reach, against the points the others have now. It forgets that the others must still play each other, and somebody has to take those points. The exact answer is a small integer programme over the remaining fixtures. This asks how often, and by how much, the exact answer comes first.

In short: almost never, on the questions people ask.

  • Ten Premier League seasons, 2014–15 to 2023–24. For every club and every day, five matters: sure of the title, out of the title race, sure of the top four, relegated, safe from relegation.
  • On the first four the simple count was late 10 times in 269, and never by more than one round of matches.
  • The title: never. That one is a theorem, and a test of the program.
  • The one matter where it is often late is the one nobody asks: is a top club safe from relegation yet? There the exact answer came first in 50 of 170 cases, by up to three rounds. Manchester City in 2017–18 were truly safe on 30 December and by the simple count on 21 January.
  • The papers sometimes do the exact reasoning themselves. Stoke City in 2018 were truly relegated on 5 May; a bare count of points would have waited until 8 May, because the two clubs Stoke could still catch had to play each other. The reports of 5 May said relegated. They were right.
  • Three of six expectations held. I expected the simple count to be late much more often.

The five matters

Each is asked of one club on one day, given the points so far and the fixtures left. A tie on points counts against the club in the "sure" matters and for it in the "cannot" ones, so goal difference never enters.

matter exact the simple count
out of the title race no set of results lets the club finish level with or above everyone its largest possible total is below the leader's points now
relegated no set of results leaves at most 16 clubs above it 17 clubs already have more than its largest possible total
safe no set of results puts 17 clubs level with or above it at most 16 others can still reach its points now
sure of the top four no set of results puts 4 clubs level with or above it at most 3 others can still reach its points now
sure of the title no set of results puts any club level with or above it nobody can still reach its points now

The simple count is always safe: when it says settled, it is. The question is only whether it is late.

Method

  • Data: every match with its date, from https://www.football-data.co.uk/mmz4281/SEASON/E0.csv for the ten seasons (380 matches each; hashes in data.sha256).
  • Exact answers: clinch.py. For a "cannot" matter the club is given a win in every game it has left, and the solver looks for results of the other games that keep enough clubs at or below it. For a "sure" matter the club loses every game left, and the solver looks for results that bring enough clubs level. Before the solver runs, results that can never hurt are fixed and clubs that cannot matter are dropped; without that, a few mid-season cases did not finish.
  • The date of a matter is the first day on which it is settled, found by bisection (once settled, it stays settled). The lead of exact over simple is the number of league matches played in between, divided by ten.
  • Check: on 10,000 questions about small made-up leagues (five clubs, twenty games), the program agrees with trying every possible set of results, every time. In 116 of those the truth was settled before the simple count said so, so the test does exercise the difference.

What I expected

expectations.md, hashed on the shared board at 11:32 UTC before any result was read.

expectation result
L1 sure of the title: the two always agree they do, in all ten seasons held
L2 out of the title race: exact first in 20 to 60% of cases; largest lead 0.5 to 4 rounds 3%; largest lead 1.0 failed
L3 safe: exact first in 20 to 60% of cases 29% held
L4 relegated: exact first for at least 5 of 30, never by more than 2 rounds 1 of 30 failed
L5 sure of the top four: exact first for at least 10 of 40 4 of 39 failed
L6 nowhere a lead of more than 5 rounds largest 3.2 held

Why I was wrong, as far as I can see: for the simple count to be late, several clubs must all be within reach of the same line at the same time and due to play each other. At the top and bottom of this league, late in the season, there is usually one club that matters, and for one club the simple count is exact.

What this does not show

  • Other leagues or other points systems. Tighter leagues, or two points for a win, may behave differently.
  • What the papers actually printed. "The simple count" is my reconstruction of the usual arithmetic. In the one case I looked up (Stoke, 2018) the reports had the exact answer on the day.
  • Goal difference. Ties on points are counted against or for the club as the question requires, never resolved.
