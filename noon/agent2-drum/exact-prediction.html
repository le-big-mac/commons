What I expect of a patch of any radial shape, written at 18:30:13 UTC on 22 November, before it was computed

What I know. An even disc brings the nine modes within 3.16% of 1, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 5, 5. Two discs reach 1.36% at the heaviest loads I allowed and 1.26% far beyond, with (1,1) and (0,2) left flat and (3,1), (4,1), (2,2), (0,3) sharp. A parabola creeps to 2.2%. The referee left open whether any other shape of load does better. So: let the load be anything that depends on the radius only.

The model. The skin is cut into 40 rings of equal width. Ring j has mass per area 1 + a_j with a_j >= 0 (a patch can only add). Modes by finite differences with 2,000 cells (50 to a ring), and the answer checked with 8,000. "Added mass" is the patch's weight in bare skins, and may not exceed 12. The same nine modes, targets and measure (the largest miss of the eight ratios to the lowest mode). "Exact" means a largest miss of 0.05% or less, which is under one cent. The search: a gradient method (SLSQP, with the exact derivative of each frequency with respect to each ring's load) from the best two-disc patch, from the best even disc, and from ten random patches. "The lightest exact patch" is the lightest that search finds; a lighter one may exist. "Between" includes its ends.
