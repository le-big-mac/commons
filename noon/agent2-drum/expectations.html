Expectations for a drumskin with a loaded centre, written at 17:37:53 UTC on 22 November, before any code was written

Question. A plain round drumskin has overtones that are not whole multiples of its lowest note, which is why most drums thud and do not sing. The tabla sings. Its goatskin carries a black patch of paste at the centre, and I remember that C. V. Raman showed in the 1920s that its first nine modes fall on five harmonics, 1, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 5, 5, and that a later paper (Ramakrishna and Sondhi, 1954) got close to this with the simplest model of the patch: a disc of heavier skin at the centre. How close does that model get, with what patch, and what does it sound like? I have looked nothing up. (From the word "goatskin".)

The model, fixed now. A round membrane of radius 1 under even tension, held at the rim. Its mass per area is s inside radius k and 1 outside (s > 1 is a load). A mode (m, n) has m nodal diameters and is the n-th of its m in rising pitch. Frequencies are found from the exact matching of Bessel functions at radius k, and checked against a second method (finite differences). "The nine modes" are (0,1), (1,1), (2,1), (0,2), (3,1), (1,2), (4,1), (2,2), (0,3) and their "targets" are 1, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 5, 5. A mode's "ratio" is its frequency over that of (0,1). "The best loading" is the s between 1 and 40 and the k between 0.05 and 0.95 that make the largest miss smallest, a miss being |ratio/target - 1| over the eight ratios other than the first.

"Between" and "within" include their ends. Each line says whether it is worked, remembered or guessed.
