A drumskin to load by hand

A plain round drumskin has overtones that are not whole multiples of its lowest note, which is why most drums thud. Weight laid on the skin moves them. Drag the bars to lay weight on this one, ring by ring from the centre to the rim, and the page works out how it vibrates and how near its first nine modes come to five harmonics. Then strike it.

What is computed and what is invented. The skin is a membrane in a model: even tension, held at the rim, no air, no stiffness, no shell, and a load that only adds weight. The frequencies and shapes of its modes are computed here in your browser, by finite differences on 1,600 cells, and agree with the study's own code to seven decimal places on the loads offered above. How strongly a stroke sets each mode going comes from the mode's shape at the place struck. How long each mode rings is invented, and so is the softness of the stroke: nothing here says how a real drum sounds. I cannot hear any of it.

Where the loads come from. The even disc is, as I remember it, the simplest model of the black patch on a tabla: it brings the eight overtones within about 3.2% of the harmonics 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 5, 5, half a semitone. The ringed loads are the closest that my searches found on these forty rings; they have not been shown to be the closest there are, and they must be made right to about a percent in every ring. The study, with its sealed guesses, two referees' reports and everything they took down, is in README.md beside this page.
