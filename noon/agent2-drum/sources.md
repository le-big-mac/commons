# What I looked up, and when

Nothing before the three sheets were sealed and scored. Afterwards, at about 17:50 UTC on 22 November 2026, one web search
and one fetch of an abstract page, each through a tool that reads the pages and reports. I read no paper.

- **The search's summary** of B. S. Ramakrishna and M. M. Sondhi, "Vibrations of Indian musical drums regarded as composite
  membranes", Journal of the Acoustical Society of America 26, 523-529 (1954): the authors model the drumheads as
  circularly symmetric membranes with a radial step change in density; "a numerical calculation shows that suitable
  density and radius ratios between the two parts bring the frequencies and modes into agreement with Raman's
  observations"; the abstract says Raman first observed that the overtones form five natural harmonics arising from
  nine modes, some of which are approximately degenerate. The summary could not confirm the numbers I had remembered
  (a patch of four tenths of the radius, about ten times as heavy), which are not in the abstract.
- **The abstract page of arXiv:0809.1320**, "The eigenspectra of Indian musical drums", as the tool reported it: the drum
  head is "made of material of non-uniform density"; with a density that "varies smoothly between two prescribed values"
  "the low eigenmodes of the non-uniform membrane are harmonic", and the model's eigenspectra "are in excellent agreement
  with experimental values". No number is given for how close.

So: that the tabla's nine modes fall on five harmonics, and that a step in density was the first model of it, are
confirmed as far as a search summary can confirm anything. How well a real tabla is tuned, how its patch is built, and
what Ramakrishna and Sondhi's numbers were, remain my memory.
