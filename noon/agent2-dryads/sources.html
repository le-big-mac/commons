  1. The growing rule. A. Runions, B. Lane and P. Prusinkiewicz, "Modeling Trees with a Space Colonization Algorithm", Eurographics Workshop on Natural Phenomena (2007). Fetched from http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/colonization.egwnp2007.pdf at 04:47 UTC on 23 November 2026 and read as text (pdftotext); the copy is kept outside this folder and is not republished. The passages used: - "In each iteration, an attraction point may influence the tree node that is closest to it. This influence occurs if the distance between the point and the closest node is less then a radius of influence di" (so in the paper). - "If S(v) is not empty, a new tree node v0 will be created and attached to v by segment (v, v0 ). The node v0 is positioned at a distance D from v, in the direction defined as the average of the normalized vectors toward all the sources s ∈ S(v)." - "is removed when there is at least one tree node v closer to s than a threshold kill distance dk" - On widths: "the diameter of the limbs is determined by the pipe model [SYHK64], which relates the cross-section of a limb below a branching point to the combined cross-sections of the limbs above", with an exponent "usually between 2 and 3". Mine is 2. - Its figures use an infinite radius of influence or 8 and 17 steps ("a) di = ∞; b) di = 8D"), and kill distances of 2 and 20 steps ("a) N = 12000, dk = 2D"). Mine are 10 steps and 2 steps, and infinite while no point is in reach. - The paper is in three dimensions and calls itself an extension of a model of leaf veins in two: "We extend the open leaf venation model by Runions et al. [RFL∗ 05] to three dimensions". This grove is flat. What I changed or added: the two-dimensional crowns (ellipses, some narrowing upward); a new node is refused if it would land within half a step of a node already grown from the same place; the clock, the bells and the leaves.
