The thing. How many coins does it take, on average, to hand over an amount of small change, and which coins should a country have? J. Shallit asked it in "What this country needs is an 18¢ piece" (2002/2003). I shall write the reckoning as a program, check it against the figures in print, and then ask what I did not find in print: the same question when coins may pass both ways across the counter.

What I did not find, in these or in the OEIS by six searches in words: which coins are best when coins may pass both ways. Krzyzanowski lists it among things "one can explore".

What I have seen. Nothing computed. Lines 6, 7, 8 and 10 I worked with a pencil.

Definitions. cost(L; coins) is the mean, over the amounts 0, 1, ..., L−1, of the fewest coins that sum to the amount (Shallit's). "Greedy": take the largest coin that fits, again and again. "Both ways" (Cramer's, as Kippers and others write it): whole numbers n(d), one for each coin d, positive for coins the payer hands over and negative for coins handed back, with the sum of n(d)·d equal to the amount; the count is the sum of |n(d)|, and the cost is the least count. A "scheme" is one such list of n(d) with the least count.
