Sources, and what each is about

Fetched on 23 November 2026 into ~/meander-data/ (one folder each; not published here). I read the parts named, and give what each is about, because the same word ("sinuosity") is used for different measures.

  1. Camporeale, Perona, Porporato, Ridolfi (2005), "On the long-term behavior of meandering rivers", Water Resources Research 41, W12403. Read in full. Long simulations of three flow models (the simplest is that of Ikeda, Parker and Sawai, 1981, "IPS") with neck cutoffs, ten sets of hydraulic values, started from a nearly straight line. What it reports of the long-run state, scaled by D0 = H0/(2 Cf0): mean wavelength about 13.4 D0, mean wavelength along the stream about 25 D0, mean absolute curvature about 0.2/D0, a belt 40 to 50 D0 wide. "The tortuosity is defined as the ratio of the river length to the linear distance between its endpoints." The paper does not give its long-run mean. In counting the operations of its neck search (Appendix C) it takes 6 for what it calls "the maximum tortuosity occurred during the simulations"; whether 6 was measured or is a round figure for the count, it does not say. Cutoff: "we adopted a threshold equal to 1.5 times the river width" in the text, and "dc = 1.5b" in Appendix C, where b is the half-width; the two differ by a factor of two. Its Table 2 gives τ^(1/3) for 44 reaches of real rivers: 1.18 to 1.47, and one of 1.88. Its claim: "The ensuing statistical long-term behavior of meandering rivers proves to be universal and largely unaffected by the details of the fluid dynamic processes". And of the P of this study, two lines after defining it: "it can be shown that the parameter P has also a negligible influence on equation (12)". My Table 2 does not bear that out for the wavelength, which goes from 10.1 D at P = 2 to 6.0 D at P = 6; I do not know how much P their ten runs spanned. (The referee found that I had left this sentence out.)
  2. Frascati (2009), doctoral thesis, University of Padua (chapter 4 is Frascati and Lanzoni, 2009, J. Geophys. Res. 114, F02002). Read: abstract, sections 3.3 and 3.4, chapter 4 with its tables. Thirteen long runs each of the IPS model and a fuller one ("ZS"), cutoff when two nodes come within "some critical distance (say 2.2B0∗ )" (B0∗ is the half-width: the thesis has "constant width 2B0∗"), and fifteen reaches of real rivers. Its Table 4.4, "Mean values attained by the morphologic variables", gives the reach tortuosity: IPS 3.5, ZS sub-resonant 3.2, ZS super-resonant 2.8, natural 2.5.
  3. Schwenk, Lanzoni, Foufoula-Georgiou (2015), "The life of a meander bend", J. Geophys. Res. Earth Surf. 120, 690–710. Read: sections 1, 2 and the head of 4. One run of the IPS model ("HIPS") with values for the Beatton River (half-width 35 m, friction 0.0036, α = 10, valley slope 0.0067), a 40 km reach for 30,000 years, first node fixed. "Neck cutoffs are identified when two nodes of stream centerline are separated by a distance less than or equal to 2B." (B is the half-width.) "The mean tortuosity, or ratio of river length to the distance between its ends, approached a steady average value of 4.46 after 3000 years". Its Figure 5 caption: "The upstream boundary condition fixes the first centerline node in place, leading to the formation of the spiral pattern at the upstream boundary." For its statistics of loops it keeps only those "occurring a downstream distance greater than 20% of the entire reach length"; it does not say whether the tortuosity leaves that part out. At the free end: "The downstreammost curvature value c(s = end) requires a linearly interpolated centerline node downstream of the reach", which on the line of the last segment gives no curvature there. (The second referee found that I had left this out.)
  4. Stølum (1996), "River meandering as a self-organization process", Science 271, 1710–1713. Not read: I did not find a copy I could fetch. What I have is second hand. Hooke (2002, item 5): "in an unconstrained state, will be a critical sinuosity of 3.14 (pi)", and "the critical value for a reach will depend on the valley width and will only be 3.14 in unconstrained meanders, rather less in confined meanders". An encyclopedia page (item 7) says the measure is river length over straight distance.
  5. Hooke (2002), "River meander behaviour and instability: a framework for analysis", Trans. Inst. Br. Geogr. Read: the passages on Stølum's model, with their surroundings. A review; about the River Bollin and others.
  6. Liverpool and Edwards (1995), "The dynamics of a meandering river", arXiv:cond-mat/9608080 (Phys. Rev. Lett. 75, 3016). Read in full. A different model: migration by local curvature, a smoothing term and noise, with oxbows. It says nothing of 3.14.
  7. "Sinuosity", an encyclopedia page. Read the paragraph on π: the claim "has not been borne out by later studies, which found an average value less than 2", citing a newspaper column of 2015 about a crowd-sourced list of rivers (reported elsewhere as 258 rivers, mean 1.94; I have not seen the list).

  8. Li and others (2023), "High-resolution modeling of meander neck cutoffs", Frontiers in Earth Science. Read: abstract, introduction, methods. About one cutoff in detail; nothing on long-run sinuosity. Not used.

  9. Fisk (1944), the maps of ancient courses that courses.png is drawn after. What I saw: one sheet and the key, as pictures on a page of the Public Domain Review ("Ancient Courses: Harold Fisk's Meander Maps of the Mississippi River (1944)"), fetched on the evening of 23 November. The sheet's own title block, as I read it from the picture: "Geological Investigation, Mississippi River Alluvial Valley. Ancient Courses, Mississippi River Meander Belt, Cape Girardeau, Mo. – Donaldsonville, La. In 15 sheets. Sheet 7. Office of the President, Mississippi River Commission, Vicksburg, Miss., 1944. To accompany report of Harold N. Fisk, Ph.D., consultant, Louisiana State University, Baton Rouge, La., dated 1 Dec. 1944", and at its corner "Plate 22, Sheet 7". Its key numbers the stages from 20 (1944), left white, back through 19 (1880), 18 (1820) and 17 (1765) to 1 and then J to A, in greens, reds, blues, yellows, pinks and oranges, plain, hatched and stippled, and has a sign for a "neck cut-off following indicated stage". I have not read the report.
