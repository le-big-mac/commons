1. Verdict

The numbers reproduce, but one headline number is wrong: corner up is a local minimum from exactly 1/6 (0.1667), not 0.168, which is produced by the 0.01 rad test circle. Three other sentences say more than the numbers do: "about 0.245 / also stable", "less than 50 trillionths", and the grid test behind "never an edge up" at 0.5. The physics criterion, the ring algebra, (3 − √2)/7, the 0.22689 takeover and "never an edge up" itself all survived what I tried.

2. Errors and overreach, most serious first

E1. "Corner up is stable from a density of 0.168" (summary bullet, table rows 1–2). - Found: the threshold is exactly 1/6, where the corner-up water line reaches the three nearest vertices and turns from a triangle into a hexagon. My algebra for d ≤ 1/6 gives curvature (√3/2)((6d)^(1/3) − 1) per rad², zero at 1/6; above 1/6 the extra terms start at third order. - Evidence, my 60-digit circle test: threshold 0.1678578 at radius 1e-2, 0.1667846 at 1e-3, 0.1666785 at 1e-4, 0.1666666678 at 1e-8. - Evidence, the author's own is_local_min with only r changed: 0.16904 (0.02), 0.16784 (0.01), 0.16702 (0.003), 0.16678 (0.001), 0.16671 (0.0003). - Why the beam check could not catch it: the beam and face-up thresholds move with r² (0.211332 at r = 0.01 against 0.211325); this one moves linearly in r. - The hollow is narrow: at 0.168 a direction 0.02 rad away is already lower than corner up; at 0.170 one 0.05 rad (2.9°) away is. - Should say: "from 1/6 (0.1667), where the corner-up water line becomes a hexagon; just above that the hollow is under 0.6° wide until 0.168 and under 3° at 0.17." Table rows: "up to 1/6", "1/6 to 0.2113".

E2. "0.2269 to about 0.245: corner up; also stable: what is left of the tilted attitudes"; "about 0.25 to 0.5". - Found: two different things are lumped together. - A strict minimum tilted towards an edge, up ∝ (1,p,p). It exists up to d = 0.237690 (tilt 27.25°) and ends by a fold with transverse curvature still +0.011; it does not merge into corner up. - An arc of exact ties left from the ring, around the towards-a-face direction: cos φ + sin φ ≤ 2d/tan θ. Its half-width is 21.8° at 0.23, 11.9° at 0.235, 6.4° at 0.24, 2.7° at 0.245, and zero at exactly d = 1/4. sliver.out already prints this 0.2500. - The arc is neutral, not stable: h is flat along it and falls beyond both ends. From 1/4 on, the beam-like tilt (1,t,0) is a saddle; I found nothing but corner up at 0.25, 0.26, 0.28, 0.30. - Evidence: in results.json every "also stable" entry at 0.24 and 0.245 equals the ring formula to 1e-15 with azimuth inside the arc; the diagonal one appears only at 0.23 and 0.235. - Should say: "0.2269 to 0.2377: corner up; also stable, a tilt of 18–27° towards an edge. Up to exactly 0.25 a shrinking arc of the old ring stays level (neutral). 0.25 to 0.5: corner up only."

E3. "lower than the others by less than 50 trillionths … until corner up overtakes at 0.22689." - Found: the advantage reaches 75 trillionths at the takeover. It is 6.3e-14 at 0.2266, 3.2e-12 at 0.2267, 2.2e-11 at 0.2268, 4.5e-11 at 0.22685, 7.3e-11 at 0.22689 and 7.5e-11 at 0.226892677. - No saved output shows the figure: sliver.out prints "−0.000" millionths on every line. - It is resolvable: the author's double-precision height() gives the same differences as my 60-digit arithmetic (−2.247e-11 both at 0.2268). No component is near TINY, and the bisection error enters at second order. - Should say: "by less than 80 trillionths", with the number printed.

E4. "At no density on the grid is [edge up] a local minimum." - Found: true, but at 0.5 the registered test says so by where its 36 points fall. On the 0.01 rad circle h is lower only within ±2.4° of the towards-a-corner direction; the nearest test point is 2.0° off. - Control: rotating the 36 points by 5–9° makes is_local_min([1,1,0], 0.5) return True (at 7°, also at 0.495). With 360 points it never passes. - The claim stands on other evidence: my curvature scan from 0.020 to 0.500 in steps of 0.001 never has both curvatures positive. Roll curvature is negative below 9/32; towards-corner curvature is negative from about 0.125, and exactly 0 at 0.5, where h − h_edge = −θ³/48 (algebra; numerics −0.0208333 θ³). The author's −0.16 millionths at 0.02 rad matches. - Should say: rest the sentence on the curvatures, not on the 36-point test.

E5. Scorecard. - F6 and F7 use different conventions. F6 is left open because the ring ties lopsided with symmetric attitudes. F7 is failed by calling the sliver "the same tilt", which treats the ring as not lopsided; on that convention F6 fails. If the ring counts as lopsided, F6 holds and on the fine scan F7 holds too (lopsided → diagonal plane → corner up). Consistent tallies are 4 held / 3 failed (the stated intent) or 6 / 1 (generous letter). On the registered 0.005 grid F7 fails either way. - F2 "smoothly": the verdict is right but the evidence shown is not (the grid tilt jumps 0 → 9°). The closed forms show it: tan²θ = 12d(1 − d) − 2 to d = 1/4, then sin 2θ = 16d/(9 − 16d) to 9/32. The tilt is continuous, with slope continuous at 1/4 and infinite at both ends. It holds as "continuous and monotone" and fails as "differentiable". - F3 "for none above" and F1 "not above": true only for the densities searched (to 0.5), which is what the letter says; the README's first bullet (face up above 0.7887) contradicts them otherwise. - F4: right, but "regular hexagon" is not computed; extra.py prints 'hexagon' from 1/6 < d < 5/6. My section at 0.5 has six sides of 0.707107 (at 0.3 they alternate, about 0.46 and 0.96).

E6. Statics read as behaviour. - README: "the lowest is where a jostled cube ends most surely"; "does not care which way it leans". - Found: nothing here is dynamic, and the margins are not stated. At 0.22 the ring is 1.6e-4 of a side below face up and 1.2e-3 below corner up. The tie is exact only for a perfect cube; any non-uniformity picks a direction. - Ice: the sentence is safe for the ideal cube (0.92 behaves as 0.08; metacentric margin +0.051 sides), but nothing is shown for real ice. - Should say: add the margins and "neutral" to "What this does not show".

E7. Figure. - The top panel joins 0.225 to 0.23 with a sloping line, but the lowest attitude jumps from an 18° tilt to 54.7° at 0.2269. - In the ring it draws the towards-a-face member; the annotation covers that. - The member printed as lowest at 0.22, (0.9717, 0.2364, 0.0001), sits on the TINY cut-off, where height() reads up to about 2e-9 low (measured 3e-11 to 1.9e-9 at similar directions). That is the shortcut, not rounding.

E8. Minor. - "A smooth function": h is only twice differentiable (|x|³ terms at regime boundaries); that is what decides E1 and E4. - "Agrees with two million points to 0.0003" is four or more orders coarser than the claims; the real agreement is 6e-15 (section 4). - TINY puts a step of up to 1.9e-9 into height(), far above fatol and the 1e-12 margin. I found no quoted number changed by it.

4. What I verified and how

  • Closed form. I wrote my own (volume and first moment by inclusion–exclusion) in float, longdouble and 60-digit Decimal. It agrees with convex clipping to 2.6e-12 over 40 random cases, and with the author's height() to 6e-15 over about 300 random directions.
  • Physics. E = W·(z_G − z_B) after minimising over heave, and the heave stiffness is positive, so local minima of h over directions is the right criterion. Non-strict minima (the ring, the arc) are neutral. At face up it reproduces the metacentric condition 1/(12d) > (1 − d)/2.
  • Ring. By my own algebra, h = cos θ[(1 − d)/2 + tan²θ/(24d)], tan² = 12d(1 − d) − 2, h_min = √(12d(1 − d) − 1)/(12d), valid while tan θ(|cos φ| + |sin φ|) ≤ 2d. This gives 7d² − 6d + 1 = 0 towards an edge and d = 1/4 towards a face. Numerically the four azimuths at 0.22 are identical to 12 decimals.
  • Search. A grid of 17,424 directions at all 97 registered densities never goes below the author's lowest. A finer grid (step 0.004) plus 70 Nelder–Mead starts at 14 densities, including 0.2268, 0.2375, 0.238 and 0.2499, found nothing new.
  • Controls. Radius sweeps for both thresholds in my code and the author's; the rotated 36-point test at edge up; the sliver in Decimal against the author's doubles; the TINY error; the hexagon section.
  • Beam sentence. Right as far as I know: from memory the thresholds are (3 − √3)/6 and 9/32, mirrored above a half, and my algebra reproduces both and the tilted branch between.
