W1

The claim: Tiling: every weak tile of the cyclic group of order p^n q (or pqr, or p^2 q^2) is a tile.

  • Source: Kadir and Fan, 'Tiles and weak tiles in Z_pq', arXiv:2607.02149 (July 2026); the abstract (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02149)
  • "We prove that weak tiles and translational tiles are equivalent in this group."
  • Source: Kiss, Londner, Matolcsi and Somlai, 'Functional tilings and the Coven-Meyerowitz tiling conditions', arXiv:2411.03854; the abstract (https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03854)
  • "We show that the Coven-Meyerowitz tiling conditions do not necessarily hold in such generality."
  • Source: Kiss, Marshall, Matolcsi and Somlai, 'Nonnegative weak tilings and the Coven-Meyerowitz condition (T2)', arXiv:2611.13593 (10 November 2026); the abstract (https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13593)
  • "Using this method, we construct an explicit example in the group $\mathbb{Z}_{180}$."

W2

The claim: Brick bonds: English bond, or a named relative, is shown to be optimal among bonds with no straight joint inside the wall, by any stated measure of cost.

  • Source: A building-trade wiki, 'Types of brick bonding' (web page; no date in the text I saved) (https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Types_of_brick_bonding)
  • "English bond is a pattern formed by laying alternate courses of stretchers and headers. The joins between the stretchers are centred on the headers in the course below. This is one of the strongest bonds but requires more facing bricks than other bonds."

W3

The claim: Antipodes: for two continuous quantities on a sphere the number of opposite pairs with both equal is odd in general, so there may be many; or a count of such pairs from weather data.

  • Source: K. Abdelgalil, 'A perturbative multiplicity theorem for the Borsuk-Ulam setting', arXiv:2507.11556 (14 July 2025); the full text, five pages (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.11556)
  • "becomes finite and odd, and may exceed the classical lower bound of one antipodal coincidence"
  • "In particular, the number of such antipodal coincidences can increase from 1 (at ϵ = 0) to 3, 5, 7, or more"
  • Source: Lecture notes, 'Lectures on Differential Topology', arXiv:1907.10297; the section on the Borsuk-Ulam theorem (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.10297)
  • "have the same couple of temperature and pressure values."

W4

The claim: Trails: in a model of walkers who prefer worn ground, the trails between three or more points pass from direct paths to a shared system shaped like a Y.

  • Source: D. Helbing, J. Keltsch and P. Molnar, 'Modelling the evolution of human trail systems', Nature 388, 47-50 (1997); as arXiv:cond-mat/9805158 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/9805158)
  • "At the beginning, pedestrians take the direct ways to their respective destinations. However, after some time they begin to use already existing trails, since this is more comfortable"
  • "If κ is small, a direct way system develops (left), if κ is large, a minimal way system is formed, otherwise a compromise between both extremes will result"

W5

The claim: Snowdrops: in Germany the snowdrop's first flowering has moved earlier by about 10 days (8.5 to 12.5) between the mid and the late twentieth century or since, and follows late-winter temperature.

  • Source: Umweltbundesamt (Germany's environment agency), indicator BD-I-1, 'Phenological changes in wild plant species' (web page, undated in the text I saved); with the agency's chart of the start of snowdrop flowering, 1960 to 2025, from the weather service's data, which carries a linear trend and no figure (https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/en/monitoring-on-das/cluster/biodiversity/bd-i-1/indicator)
  • "Regarding the phenological seasons from pre-spring via early summer until early autumn, the two periods after 1981 started earlier than in the reference period of 1951 to 1980"
  • "Pre-spring is the only time when the statistical difference is not significant."
  • "Over the past 30 years, coltsfoot plants have flowered nearly a week earlier than in 1951-1980."
  • Source: S. Jochner and A. Menzel, 'Does flower phenology mirror the slowdown of global warming?', Ecology and Evolution 5 (11) (2015); the abstract on a university's publications page (https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/does-flower-phenology-mirror-the-slowdown-of-global-warming/)
  • "We explored in detail long-term flowering onset dates of snowdrop, cherry, and lime tree and relevant spring temperatures at three sites in Germany (1901-2012)"
  • "In the majority of cases analyzed, changes in temperatures were well mirrored by phenological changes."

W6

The claim: Leagues: in football leagues the simple count of points settles the title question as early as an exact method in nearly all cases, and is late mostly on other places in the table.

  • Source: C. C. Ribeiro and S. Urrutia, 'An application of integer programming to playoff elimination in football championships' (2003 preprint; later in International Transactions in Operational Research) (https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2003/10/754.pdf)
  • "We present two integer programming models which are able to detect in advance when a team is already qualiﬁed to or eliminated from the playoﬀs."
  • "but are also very useful to identify and correct wrong statements made by the press and team administrators."
  • Source: U. Gotzes and K. Hoppmann-Baum, 'Bounding the final rank during a round robin tournament with integer programming', Operational Research (2020) (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12351-020-00546-w)
  • "We solved 14,688 instances of the integer programming models defined above originating from the Bundesliga standings during seasons 2003/2004 – 2017/2018."

W7

The claim: Ice ages: the late terminations come at a fixed phase of the tilt cycle, two or three tilt cycles apart, and chance is rejected at about the 1% level or better.

  • Source: P. Huybers and C. Wunsch, 'Obliquity pacing of the late Pleistocene glacial terminations', Nature 434, 491-494 (2005) (https://phuybers.sites.fas.harvard.edu/Doc/Huybers_Nature2005.pdf)
  • "According to our analysis, the null hypothesis that glacial terminations are independent of obliquity can be rejected at the 5% significance level, whereas the corresponding null hypotheses for eccentricity and precession cannot be rejected."
  • "The simplest inference consistent with the test results is that the ice sheets terminated every second or third obliquity cycle at times of high obliquity, similar to the original proposal by Milankovitch"

W8

The claim: Elections: in real ranked-ballot elections in the United States a candidate who beats every rival exists in nearly all, and instant runoff elects that candidate in all but one or two (Burlington 2009 among them).

  • Source: A. Graham-Squire and D. McCune, 'An examination of ranked choice voting in the United States, 2004-2022', arXiv:2301.12075 (2023) (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.12075)
  • "In the American ranked-choice political elections in our database, RCV almost always selects the Condorcet winner"
  • "The authors found only one Condorcet failure, in a previously documented election from Burlington, VT"
  • "also found only one singlewinner election without a Condorcet winner"
  • "All other elections in the database contain a Condorcet winner."

W9

  • Source: A. Keller and L. B. Vosshall, 'Olfactory perception of chemically diverse molecules', BMC Neuroscience 17, 55 (2016) (https://bmcneurosci.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12868-016-0287-2)
  • "The presence of sulfur atoms biased molecules to be perceived as unpleasant."
  • "The number of sulfur atoms in a molecule was correlated with the odor quality descriptors “garlic,” “fish,” and “decayed,” and large and structurally complex molecules were perceived to be more pleasant."

W13

The claim: Ice blocks: a rectangular block of ice floats upright stably only if its width is more than about 0.75 of its height (0.64 to 0.86).

  • Source: J. C. Burton and nine others, 'Laboratory investigations of iceberg capsize dynamics, energy dissipation and tsunamigenesis', J. Geophys. Res. 117, F01007 (2012) (https://website.whoi.edu/gfd/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2018/10/Burton2012_146467.pdf)
  • "rectangular icebergs with aspect ratios larger than some critical value, ɛc, will not capsize unless an energy barrier is overcome by some external agent"
  • "which is in agreement with the expression derived by MacAyeal et al. [2003]. For icebergs in seawater, ɛc ≈ 0.75"
  • "Icebergs having aspect ratios close to ɛc have peculiar potential energy curves with shallow minima at q ≤ 15°; these icebergs are unstable at q = 0 but are able to rest at some other finite angle"

W16

  • Source: B. Jiang, D. R. Figueiredo, B. Ribeiro and D. Towsley, 'On the duration and intensity of competitions in nonlinear Polya urn processes with fitness' (2016) (https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/ribeirob/pdf/Jiang_et_al_2016.pdf)

  • Source: P. Kattuman and M. Lewis, 'A diagnostic for lock-in', Judge Business School working paper 20/2007; the full text (https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/fileadmin/user_upload/research/workingpapers/wp0720.pdf)

The rule, from the sealed sheet

"Found": a source dated before my study states the claim, and, where the claim below carries a number, gives a number within 15% of it or a range that contains it. "Found in part": a source states the claim's substance without its number, or for a neighbouring case (another country, another lattice, a narrower or broader class), or asserts it without showing it. "Not found": neither, within the budget.
