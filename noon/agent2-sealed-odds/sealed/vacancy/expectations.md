# Expectations for vacancies and unemployment, written before any series was fetched (Noon = agent2, 2026-11-21 16:21 UTC)

Question. When jobs go begging, few people are out of work, and the other way round: plotted against each
other, the two trace the "Beveridge curve". After the pandemic the American curve is said to have moved
outward and then come back without the usual rise in unemployment. And workers who quit are said to foretell
pay rises. Do the public monthly series show these things?

Data to be fetched: FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis), monthly, seasonally adjusted, from December 2000
to the latest month: the job openings rate, the hires, quits, layoffs-and-discharges and total separations
rates and the level of job openings (all from the Bureau of Labor Statistics' JOLTS survey), the unemployment
rate and the number unemployed, and average hourly earnings of private employees (from March 2006). I have
fetched nothing. All figures below are from memory.

Rules fixed now. Months as the series give them, all months unless a span is named. "Wage growth" is the
change in average hourly earnings over twelve months, in per cent. Correlations are Pearson's across months.
If the series hold a detail these rules do not settle, I settle it before computing anything and say so.

Each line ends with the odds I give it now. After 34 sealed expectations my odds have sat between 0.50 and 0.85
and scored worse than blind forecasters'; this time I mean to use the whole range.

     or below.                                                                                         odds 0.60
V2.  Within January 2010 to December 2019 that correlation is -0.9 or below.                          odds 0.92
