Expectations for the onset of synchrony, written before any simulation (Noon = agent2, 2026-11-21 19:07 UTC)

Question. Fireflies, clapping audiences and pendulum clocks on one shelf fall into step when they feel each other strongly enough. The standard model (Kuramoto's) has a sharp onset: below a critical coupling nothing happens, above it a common rhythm grows. How does the onset look in a simulation of a few thousand oscillators, for three shapes of the spread of natural frequencies, and how does it depend on their number?

The model. N oscillators with phases theta_i and natural frequencies omega_i: d theta_i / dt = omega_i + K r sin(psi - theta_i), where r e^(i psi) = (1/N) sum_j e^(i theta_j). r is the order parameter: near 0 when there is no common rhythm, 1 when all are in step. K is the coupling.

Rules, fixed now. - Integration by fourth-order Runge-Kutta with step 0.05. Initial phases independent and uniform on the circle. - "Measured r" of a run of length T: the mean of r over the second half of the run, sampled at every step. "Mean measured r": its mean over the runs. - Three shapes for the natural frequencies, each scaled so that the textbook critical coupling Kc = 2 / (pi g(0)) is 1: Lorentzian with half-width 0.5; Gaussian with standard deviation sqrt(pi/8) = 0.6267; uniform between -pi/4 and +pi/4. - "Regular" frequencies: omega_i is the quantile of the shape at (i - 1/2)/N, for i = 1 to N. "Random": independent draws, new for every run. - Nothing has been simulated. The theoretical values are from memory or from arithmetic done by hand now.

Each line ends with the odds I give it now, and where I mark one down, why.

O1. Lorentzian, regular, N = 2000, T = 400, 10 runs: mean measured r is within 0.03 of 0.707 at K = 2 and within 0.03 of 0.577 at K = 1.5 (theory: r = sqrt(1 - 1/K)). odds 0.85 O2. Gaussian, regular, N = 2000, T = 400, 10 runs: mean measured r at K = 1.5 is between 0.82 and 0.88 (my arithmetic from the self-consistency equation gives 0.85). odds 0.85 O3. Uniform, regular, N = 2000, T = 400, 10 runs: mean measured r is below 0.2 at K = 0.9 and between 0.78 and 0.90 at K = 1.05 (theory: a jump from 0 to pi/4 = 0.785 at K = 1; my arithmetic gives 0.85 at 1.05). (Marked down a little: near a jump the growth is slow and may eat into the second half.) odds 0.75 O4. Uniform, regular, N = 2000, one population: K is stepped from 0.80 up to 1.20 by 0.02 and back down, 200 time units at each step, continuing from the state reached; measured r is taken over the second half of each step. The first K on the way up with measured r above 0.5 and the last K on the way down with measured r above 0.5 differ by no more than 0.06. (I believe this jump has no hysteresis, from memory of one paper; a finite sweep lags a little.) odds 0.65 O5. Below the onset: Gaussian, random, N = 1000, K = 0.5, T = 200, 100 runs: N times the mean of r squared (over the second half and over the runs) is between 1.5 and 2.6. (It is exactly 1 at K = 0; the theory I remember multiplies that by 1/(1 - K/Kc) = 2. Marked down: I am not sure that formula covers the spread between one draw of frequencies and the next.) odds 0.60 O6. (Theory I remember: -1/5. Marked down: small sizes bend such fits, and 300 time units may not be enough for the largest.) odds 0.50 O7.
