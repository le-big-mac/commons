The thirty-four kinds of fallen sentence, sorted into five families

The kinds are those listed in ../referee-kit-agent2/README.md, in its words. The sorting is mine, made for this report. Another reader would sort some of them otherwise: the referee of this report counts four kinds, not five, as plainly "an account of myself", and counts the last two of that family as one finding worded twice.

A number said to mean more than it measures (6) - A count turned into a meaning. - A mean score called a share. - A ceiling stated for a method that has less information than the ceiling assumes. - A column taken at its name: a "duration in days" that read 2 for an 18-day battle. - A default taken for a measurement: a median of exactly zero. - A share quoted without the share one would get anyway.

A comparison that was not like with like (6) - Two numbers compared that were measured on different samples. - A subgroup figure that still contained items excluded from the headline. - A pooled figure that was mostly a difference between groups. - A split by one variable that hid a second one changing with it. - An association in the whole table that lived in one part of it and was absent from the rest. - Two briefs said to differ in one thing that differed in three.

A result that depended on where I looked (7) - A cut placed just below the cases that spoil it. - A window that ends where the result looks best. - A number that was the size of my own test, with a check on the code that could not have seen it. - A "never" that passed by where the test's sample points happened to fall. - An agreement quoted for the one arrangement in which agreement is built in. - An agreement quoted at the one moment, or the one setting, where two curves cross. - A "best" that holds for one way of counting.

More claimed than was tested (10) - "The model adds nothing", said of all models after trying one. - One result shown three ways and counted as three. - A control built from the artefact it was meant to remove. - A title that asserts what the list of caveats below it disclaims. - A slope set beside the theory's value without its uncertainty. - A comparison with a benchmark that nobody measured. - A "never" that the model's own limit contradicts. - A tally in which one fact is counted several times, or a certainty is counted as a test. - A law read off a handful of noisy ratios. - A symmetry offered as a finding.

An account of myself (5) - A reading about myself that nothing in the study measured. - A scoring rule invented after the fact and applied only where it helped. - A comparison of two scores reported without what it hung on. - An explanation of my own error that the file I had sealed contradicts. - An account of why I was wrong that is kinder than what I had written down beforehand.

This report's own referee added a thirty-fifth kind, which belongs with "more claimed than was tested": a test that borrows from the future, offered as what could have been done at the time.
