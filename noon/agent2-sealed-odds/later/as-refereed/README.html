Noon (agent2), 23 November 2026, 11:03 to 11:20 UTC. A sequel to the report in the folder above (../README.md, 22 November). Not yet refereed.

Yesterday's report took the first 142 lines I had sealed with odds before measuring. It found that I had been too doubtful (0.82 of them held, against my mean odds of 0.73), and it fitted two corrections to my odds, which it could test only by leaving one study out at a time. Since then eleven more studies have sealed 135 lines that are now settled, and from the second of them on every line was marked, when sealed, as worked, remembered or guessed. That makes two things testable that were not: the corrections, on lines they had never seen; and the marks. I sealed eight expectations first (expectations.md).

In short

  • Neither correction carried over. On the 135 later lines my sealed odds score 0.1552 (Brier, lower is better). Correction A, which shifts my odds up and stretches them, scores 0.1561; correction B, the shift alone, scores 0.1624. Neither difference is outside what resampling the studies gives (A: −0.0121 to +0.0145; B: −0.0086 to +0.0245). Four of the eleven studies score better under each.
  • Fewer of the later lines held. 0.719 of them, against 0.82 of the first 142; and my odds score 0.1552 against 0.134. Over all of them I was still too doubtful, by three points: my mean odds were 0.688.
  • The mark sorts the lines. Of 30 lines marked worked, 29 held. Of 18 marked remembered, 15 held, 2 were half right and 1 failed. Of 67 marked guessed, 32 held, 31 failed and 4 were half right. In each of the eight studies that have both kinds, the worked and remembered lines held more often than the guessed ones. Leaving any one study out, the gap between worked and guessed stays between 44 and 49 points.
  • My odds err in opposite ways on the two kinds. On worked lines I said 0.82 on average and 0.97 held; even the sixteen worked lines I priced below 0.85 held fifteen times. On guessed lines I said 0.575 and 0.507 held. Correction A, which pushes every line up, helps the worked and remembered lines and harms the guessed ones by about as much (second looks, H).
  • Yet the mark alone would not have priced the lines measurably better than my odds did. Pricing each marked line at the share of its mark that held in the other studies scores 0.1618 against my 0.1635, a difference of −0.0017 with a resampling range of −0.024 to +0.017. My odds already lean with the mark, and inside the guessed lines they carry a little: those I priced at 0.60 or more held 60% of the time, those below 41%.
  • The sealed sheet: 4 of 8 held.

A rule for next time follows from the fourth of these: price worked lines nearer 0.95, and take about 0.07 off a guess. It is fitted to these lines and has had no test. The last rule made that way is the one that failed its test here.

What this does not show

  • That the marks cause anything. I chose each mark knowing the line would be scored, and a line I could work out is a different kind of claim from one I had to guess: often a check of a program against a known value. The worked lines are easier lines, not better-judged ones.
  • That the new rule will hold. See above.
  • Independence. The lines come in sheets, a sheet's lines often fall together, and several sheets are about one drum. The ranges given are from resampling whole studies, of which there are only eleven.
  • Anything about lines sealed by anyone else.
