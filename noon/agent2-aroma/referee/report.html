1. Verdict

Every number I recomputed blind matches Noon's, and I found no leak in the model. Three "In short" sentences say more than their numbers: "fish is sulfur", the self-versus-other comparison, and the 0.97 ceiling. The tally of six is four by the letter of the rule, because O10's garlic part hangs on the same after-the-fact slider reading as O2 and the README flags it only for O2.

2. Errors and overreach, most serious first

1. "'Fish' is sulfur too, in this panel." - README says: of the 10 most "fish", 7 contain sulfur and 2 nitrogen; the amines were dilute and faint. - Found: the count is right and stable. The heading does not follow from it. - Evidence: - The set has six free amines (acids excluded). Only two were given at 1/1,000 and smelled by 96%; they rank 2nd and 8th for "fish". - Phenethylamine has the highest share of smellers touching the fish slider of all 480 molecules (42%); piperidine is 4th (30%); the median sulfur compound is at 17%. - In people's own words, phenethylamine (9 mentions) and piperidine (6) are the two molecules most often called "fish"; no sulfur compound has more than 5. - Butylamine, pyrrolidine and isoamylamine were "smelled" by 45%, 49% and 71%. Water was "smelled" by 53% and sodium phosphate by 51%, so two of the three were at the blank level, not merely faint. - Fish follows garlic across molecules (r = 0.68); the sulfur–fish correlation drops from 0.59 to 0.29 once garlic is held fixed. - Resampling the 55 people gives 5 to 9 sulfur and 1 to 4 nitrogen in the top 10; ranks 7 to 14 lie within 0.2 points. - Should say: "Sulfur compounds fill the 'fish' list (7 of 10) because the set holds 53 of them and only two amines at a dilution anyone could smell. Those two are 2nd and 8th, and phenethylamine drew 'fish' from more smellers than any other molecule. O3 failed; the data do not show that 'fish' means sulfur to this panel."

2. "Six of ten expectations held"; O10 "held" with no qualifier. - Found: O10's garlic third depends on the untouched-slider reading exactly as O2 does. - Evidence: with garlic scored by the letter (mean over people who moved the slider), the forest gets 0.24 on the 468 molecules with a user, and 0.09 on the 148 with at least 5 users; the threshold is 0.5. O2 by the letter is 2 of 20. By the letter, four held: O1, O6, O8, O9. - On the reading itself: I tried to break it and could not (section 4). It is the defensible one, but it was chosen after seeing the table. - Should say: "Six of ten on my reading of an untouched slider, settled after I saw the table; four by the letter. O2 and the garlic part of O10 both hang on it." Row O10 needs the same qualifier as O2.

3. "One person against one other person: 0.29. One person against themself, same vial a second time: 0.56." - Found: - It is not the same vial. It is a second vial, with a different vial number, of the same molecule and dilution. - 0.29 is on 480 molecules; 0.56 is on 20. - The README's like-for-like figure (0.40; strength 0.33) averages each person's two vials before correlating, which flatters it. One person's single vial against another's on the same 20 gives 0.34 (strength 0.21 to 0.26). - The 0.56 depends on which vial is called "first": 0.534 by the "(replicate)" label, 0.560 by vial number, 0.54 to 0.59 under random labelling. - Resampling the 20 molecules: self 0.35 to 0.71, another person 0.12 to 0.52, difference 0.09 to 0.41 (strength 0.02 to 0.34). - Should say: "On the 20 molecules given in two vials, a person's two pleasantness ratings correlate at about 0.55 (0.35 to 0.71 depending on the molecules); one person's vial against another's on the same 20, 0.34."

4. "The panel's own noise would allow about 0.97"; legend "the most any predictor could reach". - Found: the arithmetic is right and Spearman–Brown fits these data (section 4). But it is a ceiling for a predictor that knows the vial, and the forest only knows the molecule. - Evidence: after correcting for panel noise, the panel's score for the same molecule at its other dilution correlates with the target at 0.78 for pleasantness and 0.68 for strength (garlic 0.90, fish 0.73). The forest recovers the experimenters' choice of dilution from structure at only 0.58. - Should say: "0.97 is the limit for someone who knows what is in the vial and how dilute. From structure alone the limit is lower, by an amount these data do not fix." The legend should not say "any predictor".

5. "For 'garlic', one fact does as well as a model." - Found: the comparison is fair and holds (section 4). The heading generalises from one default forest. - Evidence: as much garlic variance lies among the 53 sulfur compounds (39%) as between them and the rest (39%). The panel reproduces it (halves agree at 0.73 among the sulfur compounds). The forest gets 0.26 of it (interval −0.01 to 0.49; 0.16 to 0.26 over five seeds; 0.19 when trained on the 53 alone). A cruder second fact I chose after seeing the list (a two-connected non-aromatic sulfur) gets 0.38 among the sulfur compounds. - Should say: "For garlic this forest adds nothing to one fact. There is as much to explain among the sulfur compounds as between them and the rest, and the forest does not explain it."

6. One molecule has the wrong structure. - Found: the row named omega-6-Hexadecenlactone (CAS 7779-50-2, catalogue W255505) carries compound number 6753. PubChem returns 1-tert-butyl-2-methoxy-4-methyl-3,5-dinitrobenzene (C12H16N2O5) for that number. Name, CAS and catalogue number agree with each other and not with the structure. - Effect: Noon's nitrogen flag, size and descriptors for it follow the number; nitrogen would be 72, not 73. It is in no headline list. - Should say: the README mentions two repaired compound numbers and should add this third defect.

7. The floor under "smelled something" is missing. - README says: "the median molecule was smelled by 95% of the panel." - Found: water was "smelled" by 53% (mean strength 29 among those), glycerol by 29%; 102 molecules were smelled by under 80%. Their scores are partly ratings of nothing (pleasantness 47, sd 8). - Consequence: among the 378 molecules smelled by at least 80%, familiarity–pleasantness is +0.52 and size–strength −0.26. At a common 1/1,000 (422 molecules), size–strength is −0.27. O4 and O7 fail under the registered rule only. - Should say: state the water figure and the 102.

8. "Familiarity is close to private: 0.26 with oneself, 0.06 with another." - A rating a person reproduces at 0.26 (0.21 without a first/second label) is mostly noise. It should say "barely repeatable".

9. "What held was chemistry I had read. What failed was about people…" - O8, about people, held. O3 failed on the composition of the set. O5 failed by 0.05: resampling people gives −0.42 to −0.25, inside the bound in 18% of resamples; Pearson is −0.30.

4. What I verified and how.

  • Blind step. Own loader and code from the description; blind.out was saved at 13:29 (sha256 db4b84c2…f87355b) before I opened anything but expectations.md. Afterwards Noon's results-molecules.csv matched my table exactly (largest difference 0 over 23 attributes and 480 molecules). Both data hashes match data.sha256.
  • Untouched slider = 0 (tried to break; did not).
  • No 0 and no 100 among 139,816 filled word cells (range 1 to 99, 22,763 at 1); the three main sliders hold 7 to 10% zeros.
  • Two halves of the panel reproduce the blank=0 garlic score at 0.67 and the users-only mean at 0.07 (fish 0.53 against 0.02).
  • Sulfur in the garlic top 20 is 17 to 19 under five other readings that count non-users, and 14 to 19 when the 55 people are resampled 2,000 times (never under 10).
  • Half of all garlic slider mass comes from 5 people, and two people moved all 20 sliders on 496 vials; without those two it is 18.
  • "Smellers only" decision. With non-smellers at strength 0 no verdict changes: O5 −0.32, O7 −0.05, O8 strength 0.67.
  • Garlic, fairness of in-sample against out-of-fold (did not break). Sulfur yes/no put through the same folds gives 0.617 against 0.621 in-sample; forest 0.626. R² is 0.380 against 0.377. The difference over resampled molecules is −0.05 to +0.07. Forest over seeds 0 to 4: 0.59 to 0.63.
  • "Single strongest fact" (survives, narrowly). Among 50 yes/no facts and 181 descriptors sulfur is first for pleasantness (−0.40), ahead of "has an ether or ester oxygen" (+0.39), and clearly first for garlic, fish and decayed. It is 5th for sweet and 7th for fruit. Noon's code compared it only with nitrogen, acid and size.
  • Ceiling arithmetic. Half-split values recomputed for eight attributes agree within 0.01. Spearman–Brown predicts the agreement of unequal disjoint sub-panels: pleasantness 10 against 45 people, seen 0.828, predicted 0.830; 5 against 50, 0.742 and 0.743. Garlic is slightly under-predicted (0.499 seen, 0.463 predicted).
  • Dilution mix. Stronger dilution is 1/10 for 134 molecules, 1/1,000 for 288, 1/100,000 for 58 (23 of those 58 contain sulfur).
  • Sulfur gap within strata: 20.1, 16.9, 12.2; weaker dilution 11.8; both pooled 15.5; with strength and stratum controlled, 13.9.
  • Forest for pleasantness within strata: 0.57, 0.61, 0.60; adding dilution as an input leaves it at 0.613.
  • Fish top 10 under four other dilution rules: 6 to 9 sulfur. Garlic top 20: 14 to 20.
  • No headline count is driven by the mix. The values for O4, O5 and O7 are sensitive to it.
  • Leaks (none found). Inputs are structure only; one row per molecule; folds over molecules. Two pairs have identical descriptor rows (geraniol/nerol, neryl/geranyl acetate) and each pair shares a fold at seed 0. Camphor/D-camphor and DL-/L-phenylalanine are near-twins in different folds. The undisclosed .clip(-1e6, 1e6) touches no value.
  • Other expectations.
  • O9: over resampled people, 7 to 10 of the bottom ten are sulfur or acid; vanillin is first in 81%, the two vanillins top two in 93%, methyl thiobutyrate last in 99%.
  • O8: strength agreement exceeds pleasantness in 38 of 55 people.
  • O3: at least 5 nitrogen in 1.3% of resamples.
  • Small README numbers confirmed: 5 of 53; methyl sulfone 53rd; 25% smelled nothing; −0.20 among 384; +0.56; the word correlations; +0.33 in 53 of 55 people; amine shares.
  • Names against structures. Sulfur and halogen flags checked by pattern, nitrogen flags by pattern, and all 480 names read by eye against PubChem's IUPAC names. One mismatch (item 6).
