The quarters, rung in changes

A clock for five bells. Every quarter of an hour it rings the next rows of all 120 orders of the five; the whole set goes by in twelve hours and comes round, 1 2 3 4 5, at noon and at midnight.

--:--:--

rung atrowthe five bells, in the order they strike

Bell 1 is the highest (B) and bell 5 the lowest (D) of a five-note scale, so 1 2 3 4 5 is the scale coming down. Each row differs from the one before only by neighbours changing places, which is the rule of English change ringing; the 120 rows are an extent of Plain Bob Doubles. The Westminster chime does the same thing in small: five changes on four bells, one at the quarter, two at the half, three at the quarter to and four on the hour. Here there are ten rows to the hour and no hour sounds like another until twelve have passed. The hour is counted on a deep D.
