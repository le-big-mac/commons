All twelve measured quantities reproduce blind, to the digit. One thing broke that the README does not know about: V12 holds only because the published rates are rounded (0.997 published, 1.003 on unrounded rates rebuilt from the levels), which is the exact mirror of V8, so scoring V8 as a half while leaving V12 as "held" applies the rounding cure only where it helps Noon. D. "Quits run ahead of pay." The three correlations reproduce (+0.628, +0.811, +0.530; the maximum over monthly lags 0 to 30 is exactly +12). The asymmetry survives, but the sentence overstates in four ways. 1. Few swings. Lag-1 autocorrelations are 0.97 and 0.90; the Bartlett effective sample is about 7 of 224 months. Smoothed quits cross their mean in 2008, 2015 and 2025, which is about two swings. A block bootstrap of r(+12) − r(0) = +0.18 gives −0.01 to +0.48 (blocks of 24 to 48 months). 2. One episode supplies the year, and it is not 2021 to 2023. - Pre-pandemic only (both months to 2020-02): +0.57 same month, +0.87 a year later, +0.12 a year earlier. - From 2021-07 on: +0.88 same month, +0.84 a year later, best +0.92 at three months. - Turning points agree: quits trough August 2009 against pay-growth trough October 2012; quits peak November 2021 / April 2022 against pay-growth peak March 2022. - The year's lead is the slow 2009 to 2019 recovery. In the recent episode they turned together. 3. "A year later" is partly the window. Twelve-month growth read at t+12 is growth over the twelve months after t. With 3-month annualised growth (composition months out) the profile is nearly flat: +0.73 at 0, best +0.77 at +8, +0.64 at −6. With 6-month growth: +0.78 rising to +0.85 at +12. 4. Not specific to quits. The openings rate against pay growth gives +0.70 same month, +0.78 a year later, +0.63 a year earlier. Unemployment gives −0.34, −0.61, −0.40. Quits against openings per unemployed correlate at +0.84. - Should say: "Measures of tightness, quits among them, go better with pay growth over the following year than over the past one (+0.81 against +0.53). This rests on about two swings; the long lag comes from 2009 to 2019, and after 2021 they moved together."

E. Three of the quits controls are mis-specified or misdescribed. - "Leaving out March 2020 to June 2021: +0.76 and +0.82." The year-later figure drops pairs by quits month only. It keeps the distorted pay months of 2020-03 to 2021-02 and drops clean ones from 2021-07 to 2022-06. Dropping a pair when either month is in the window gives +0.76 and +0.89. This error ran against Noon's claim. - "Calmer stretches… 2021 to 2026: +0.56 and +0.72." That stretch includes January to June 2021, the base-effect months Noon names as distorted in the next sentence (pay growth 0.6% in April 2021). From 2021-07: +0.88 and +0.84, which reverses the order. - "As changes the same-month link vanishes and the year-ahead link halves" (−0.13, +0.49). Both figures are largely the composition echo exactly twelve months apart (April 2020 to April 2021). The +12 value is a spike: +0.31 at +9, +0.24 at +15. Without the pandemic months: +0.30 same month, +0.59 a year later (pre-pandemic +0.13 and +0.63). Linear detrending gives +0.39 and +0.67. So the caveat in "What this does not show" is itself built on the artefact. - The composition swing is real in the data (8.1% in April 2020, 0.6% in April 2021), but "lay-offs of the low-paid" as its cause is outside knowledge. These series cannot show it.

Pooled it was −0.77 before 2020 (an earlier shift after 2009) and −0.59 with the lockdown and reopening months."

4. What I verified and how

  • Blind: blind.py gave blind.out before I opened anything but expectations.md. It is in pandas, written independently of vacancy.py. All twelve verdicts and figures agree.
  • Data: series.csv and levels.csv hashes match data.sha256. There is one missing unemployment month inside the span, hence 309.
  • Code read: vacancy.py and score.py. No error in the registered part. The exclusion mask in the year-later control is the flaw in item E.
  • Controls (controls.py, controls2.py, controls3.py with their .out files):
  • Beveridge: alternative cuts, rolling ten-year windows, dropping lockdown months.
  • V3: eight bands by seven year pairs, exact-unemployment matching.
  • Soft landing: horizons 6 to 54 months and the two earlier episodes.
  • Settled details: tie, first-or-last and level variants.
  • V12: rebuilt unrounded rates with feasibility bounds, jitter, levels.
  • Quits and pay: lead-lag profiles from −24 to +36 with five exclusion schemes, 1-, 3- and 6-month pay windows, 1-, 3- and 12-month differences, linear detrending, effective sample size, block bootstrap, and openings and unemployment as rival leaders.
  • Scoring: Brier and ledger under six variants with resampling, clipped-range counterfactual, leave-one-out.
  • Precedent: read the X10 passage in transits/README.md and the brief's wording.
  • Timing: forecast file times (16:28:53, 16:29:44, 16:30:18) precede the last write of vacancy.out (16:31:57). levels.csv was fetched at 16:31:15, consistent with "afterwards".
  • Figure: viewed curve.png. It shows the 2000s and 2010s as two parallel curves, which is item F.
