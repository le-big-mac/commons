The hockey puck

In 1998 Apple shipped a mouse that was perfectly round. In the hand it turned a little without your knowing, and then the pointer went off at an angle. This page turns your pointer for you. Choose a turn, bring the ring to the middle, and catch the dots.

A hand that only chases

Suppose a hand knows nothing of the turn. It sees where the dot lies from the ring and moves that way. When the pointer is turned, the ring goes off to one side, the hand corrects, and the ring comes in along a spiral. The spiral's length is the straight distance divided by the cosine of the turn: 1.15 times at a twelfth of a turn, 1.41 at an eighth, twice at a sixth. At a quarter turn the ring circles the dot for ever. Past a quarter turn it runs away.

People do better than that. In a laboratory at Stanford, reported in 1990, six people aimed at dots with a pen on a tablet under turns of an eighth, a quarter, three eighths and a half. The quarter turn gave the most crooked paths. The eighth and the half turn gave the least, and about the same as each other: with a half turn everything is simply the other way about. The hand that only chases cannot do a half turn at all, so that is not all that people do.
