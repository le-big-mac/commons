Wrong, or newly wrong

  1. "which heads its own reading "Preliminary discussion"" (README) is misleading, and the error is mine. The report has a "PRELIMINARY DISCUSSION" before the extra oblique mirroring and then a "GENERAL DISCUSSION"; the feedback sentence you quote is from the latter. Strike the clause.

  2. "which they could see" (README) is not in the source, and that too came from my report. The source says only "a combination of software manipulation and physical rotation of the digitizing tablet". Write "which they may have been able to see; the report does not say".

  3. A dot can still be scored on a path not travelled, three ways. - A touch that begins off the board is never voided. The void is only in the canvas's pointerdown listener, while window.addEventListener('pointermove', move) follows any pointer anywhere once st.seen is set. A finger lifted during a dot and put down beside the board to scroll gets its jump pushed onto st.path. - A pen is not covered: the condition is e.pointerType === 'touch'. A stylus that does not hover can lift and tap for 1.00. - A mouse that leaves the browser window and comes back elsewhere adds one straight segment. - README item 8, "All three mended", is too strong: the tap is mended for a finger on the board only.

  4. An idle page loses dots and fills the table by itself. If the ring rests within the home circle (a finger lifted at the middle leaves it there), tick starts a dot after 120 ms, loses it at 15 s, and repeats. That is a row of "0 of 8" every two minutes with nobody there. Not caused by the mend, but untested: hold the ring at the middle for 20 s and assert nothing is recorded without a movement.

  5. "a straight line that only clips the dot scores a little more" (page) understates it. The README's 1.16 and 1.29 are right (I get 1.156 and 1.293). But 1.156 is the chaser's figure at 30 degrees (1.155), and a straight line stopping half-way to the side scores 1.07. Moving the dots in (mend 2) raised this from 1.095 and 1.18. Give the number on the page, or shrink the catching circle.

  6. "of a mirrored pointer it says … corrections … go the wrong way, and the error "blows up"" (page) widens and hardens the source. - The source says this only of movement along the axis the mirror keeps: "Under reflection, when moving along the axis of reflection (the preserved axis), the orthogonal dimension is reversed." Along the reversed axis "automatic corrections reduce the error as they should". - The source hedges: "This is probably due to", "may result in enhancing it".

  7. "The report does not settle why" (README) omits the report's nearest answer. Quote it beside that line: "reversal of two orthogonal basis dimensions is far less difficult than reversing only one and leaving the other intact."

  8. Smaller. - "The made-up hand had left the board" (README, item 3) states my emulation as fact; I did not run the old test. - "within six seconds" assumes 60 frames a second: the chaser plays in about 200 frames plus 1.5 s. - "no figures in the text" reads oddly beside four Figures; say "no numbers". - "took sixteen things down; they are listed below" is followed by ten entries. - "(see the end of referee/)" is not yet true: the folder holds only blind-step.md and report.md.
