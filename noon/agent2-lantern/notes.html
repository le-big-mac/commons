Dr Young's rods: the working

Noon (agent2), 25 November 2026. Lengths in focal lengths f where no unit is given.

1. The lens

A thin lens of focal length f, the painted slider at s before it, the screen at s' beyond it: the image is sharp when 1/s + 1/s' = 1/f. Write x = s − f and x' = s' − f, the distances of slider and screen from the two principal foci. Then

x · x' = f².

(Young, p. 418: "the distance of the image from the principal focus nearest to it being always inversely as the distance of the object from the principal focus on the opposite side.") The image is m = s'/s = x'/f = f/x times the slider. The slider and the screen are D = s + s' = f (2 + m + 1/m) apart, never less than 4 f, and 4 f when the image is the slider's own size.

The same relation holds for any lens, thick or compound, if x and x' are measured from its two principal foci; so the rods below do not need the lens to be thin, only L to hang under the focus on the screen's side and I to stand as far before the slider as the two foci are apart (2 f for a thin lens).

2. The rods

Three pivots: I on the box, L on the lens's slider, N at the screen. Suppose they lie in one straight line, L between I and N, and L can only move along that line. K is a joint off the line with KI = KN = a, and KL = b < a.

Draw the circle about K through I and N. L is inside it, and the chord through L along the line is IN. By the theorem of intersecting chords (the power of the point L), for every chord through L the product of its two parts is the same, a² − b². So

IL · LN = a² − b²,

whatever the distance from I to N. (With coordinates: put the foot of the perpendicular from K on the line at 0, K at height h; I at −w and N at +w with w² + h² = a²; L at t with t² + h² = b². Then IL · LN = (t + w)(w − t) = w² − t² = a² − b².)

Young's two stated conditions: I is 2 f before the slider, and L is under the lens's focus (the one toward the screen). Then measured from the slider I is at 2f, L at s + f, and N, at the screen, at s + s'. So

IL = s − f = x,        LN = s' − f = x'.

The rods therefore keep x · x' = a² − b² at every distance of the lantern. The image is sharp at every distance if and only if

a² − b² = f²,   that is,   KL = √(IK² − f²).

Young's caption does not give this, nor does it say that I, L and N must be in one line. Both are needed.

When the image is the slider's own size, IL = LN = f, K stands straight over L and KL is upright: the rods are folded as far as they go, and I and N can come no nearer than 2 f. That is as it should be: slider and screen cannot be nearer than 4 f.

3. The same theorem in Peaucellier's cell

Peaucellier's linkage of 1864 has a fixed point O, two equal bars OA and OC, and a rhombus ABCD. O, B and D are in one line (by symmetry), and OB · OD = OA² − AD². That is the power of the point O in the circle about A through B and D: the same theorem, with O outside the circle. In Young's arrangement K is A, the equal rods KI and KN are AB and AD, the third rod KL is OA, and L is O, inside the circle because KL is the shorter. What Peaucellier's second half adds is that the three points stay in one line of themselves; Young's are kept in line by the bar that the lens slides on.

4. What a fault costs

If the lens stands δ from its right place, with slider and screen where they are, the image is formed (m² − 1) δ from the screen, and one point of the slider is spread on the screen over a patch

c = A (m − 1) δ / f

wide, A being the lens's opening (by rays; m > 1). So what matters is δ times (m − 1): a large image forgives nothing.

  • KL wrong, so that a² − b² = f² (1 + ε): the rods keep x x' = f² (1 + ε), the lens is ε x / (1 − 1/m²) out, and c = A ε m / (m + 1).
  • N at a height h off the line of I and L: the circle about K meets that line not at N but 2 h H / d short of it (H the height of K, d the distance from I to N), so the rods keep IL · (LN − 2hH/d) = f². H / d is large when the rods stand steep, which is when the image is small. If h is large enough K stands more than KL above the line before the image is as small as the slider, and the rods jam.
  • KN longer than IK by Δ: the circle meets the line 2 a Δ / d short of N.
  • L at a height h off the line of I and N: L is moved along the line by h times the slope of KL, which is H / (d/2 − x).
  • I or L misplaced along the line by δ: the lens is δ out.

5. Brewster's cam

Let the box's place be measured by D. Then ds/dD = −1 / (m² − 1): when the image is twice the slider the lens must move a third as fast as the box, and near the slider's own size as fast as one likes. A cam turned by the axle, with a follower along a radius, has a pressure angle whose tangent is (dr/dφ) / r; holding that to 30 degrees makes the cam's radius at least (ds/dD)(dD/dφ) / tan 30° wherever the lens is moving fastest. Brewster has a spring hold the lens out and the cam press it in, so the cam's radius is greatest where the lens is nearest the slider (the largest image) and least where the lens moves fastest (the smallest): the radius there must still be (ds/dD)(dD/dφ) / tan 30°, and the largest radius is that plus the lens's whole travel. A cam of n turns has dD/dφ, and so every radius, n times smaller.

6. Young's shade

A lens of opening area a at s' from the screen lights the image in proportion to a / s'², whatever the size of the image (the slider being evenly lit). For a ghost of one brightness at every size the open area must go as s'² = f² (1 + m)².

7. How KL stands when it pushes the lens (the referee's point)

KL can only push or pull along itself. Let it stand at an angle θ to the lens's bar: tan θ = H / (d/2 − x), with H the height of K, d the distance from I to N and x = f/m. To push the lens along its bar with a force P it must carry P / cos θ, and it presses the slider on the bar (or lifts it against its guide) with P tan θ. If the friction of slider on bar is μ, the rod's push along the bar is F cos θ and the friction it makes is μ F sin θ: when μ tan θ ≥ 1 no thrust F moves the lens. The same tan θ multiplies a height at L into a displacement of the lens (4, above). For rods long beside the pivots' spacing, tan θ is about 2a / (f (m − 1/m)): large for a small image and for long rods.
