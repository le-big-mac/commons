Dr Young's rods: what I expect, written before the sums

What this is about

In 1806 Thomas Young drew an arrangement for the phantasmagoria, the magic-lantern show in which a ghost grows and shrinks as the lantern rolls to and from a screen (Lectures, 1807, Plate XXVIII, Fig. 401). Three rods are to keep the ghost sharp of themselves: IK from the box up to a joint K, KN from K down to the screen, KL from K to the sliding lens. His caption gives two conditions and a third by the way: KN equal to IK; the pivot I twice the focal length before the painted slider; L "immediately under the focus of the lens". Brewster (Letters on Natural Magic, 1832) calls it "very ingenious and effective" and "clumsy", says the levers "would bend by the least strain" at long distances, and proposes a spiral cam on the front axle instead.

On paper, before this sheet: if I, L and N are in one straight line along which L slides, then IL x LN = KI^2 - KL^2 whatever the rods' slope (the power of the point L in the circle about K). With Young's conditions IL and LN are the distances of slider and screen from the lens's two principal foci, whose product must be f^2 for a sharp image, a relation Young states himself on p. 418. So the rods are exact if KI^2 - KL^2 = f^2, which the caption does not say, and if N is in the line of I and L, which it does not say either. lantern.py, run on the ideal arrangement, puts the lens where the lens equation wants it to 1 part in 10^14 at seven sizes of image from 1 to 20 times the slider. That is seen, and is no line of this sheet.

The lines

The rods are 11 focal lengths long (they reach a screen 24 focal lengths from the slider). A "patch" is the width of the spot that one point of the slider makes on the screen, by rays, as a fraction of the lens's opening. "Times" is the size of the image over the size of the slider.
