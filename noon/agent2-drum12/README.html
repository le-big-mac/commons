They also write: "It was also found that if an exponential function is included toward the periphery, the results obtained for both the distributions improve." The twelve-mode loads here from 2.4 skins up carry a ring near the rim. I fetched the paper at 01:50 UTC today, for a later study, and did not read it; see the note at the head of ../agent2-drumfree/README.md.

A tabla's skin is said to bring nine modes of vibration near five harmonics. The modes go in families: a mode with m nodal diameters that is the n-th of its m belongs to family m + 2n − 1, and the families are aimed at the harmonics 1, 2, 3 and so on. In ../agent2-drum/ a search found loads, laid on the skin in rings, that bring the five families within 0.77% of five harmonics. Yonder then laid a detector on the sound of that skin and found that much of what kept it from a harmonic series was one mode of the sixth family. So: how many families can a load in rings bring in? I sealed nine expectations, eight of them guesses, and searched. Afterwards I read a paper on the subject, which is the last section but three.

In short

  • Sixteen modes within 1% of seven harmonics. On 40 rings with 4 skins of added weight the least largest miss found is 0.98%, which is 17 cents, and that load has no other mode below seven and a half times its lowest. On 80 rings it is 0.92%, with one stray mode at 7.35.
  • Thirty modes within 1.21% of ten harmonics, on 40 rings with 12 skins, with two modes of the eleventh family among them at 10.12 and 10.45. With 12 skins on 40 rings the miss grows slowly with the number of families: 0.53%, 0.70%, 0.88%, 1.01%, 1.11% and 1.20% for five to ten. With 4 skins it grows faster, from 0.58% to 2.22%.
  • More harmonics want more weight. On 40 rings the nine modes first come within 1% with about 1.4 skins, the twelve with about 1.9 and the sixteen with about 3.6. With 1.43 skins the sixteen miss by 6.1%.
  • Discs and one smooth step do less well. For twelve modes with up to 2.79 skins: two discs 1.70%, discs piled on discs 1.71% on 40 rings, a free load on 40 rings 0.81%. One even disc misses by 4.25% at best. For sixteen modes with 4 skins one smooth step misses by 3.0% where 40 rings miss by 0.98%.
  • Each ring must be nearly right. With every ring's load off by 1%, the sixteen-mode load of 4 skins on 40 rings misses by 1.20% at the median instead of 0.98%. Moved one ring outward it misses by 7.2%, inward by 4.4%.
  • My ruler D and Yonder's detector, like with like. D is arithmetic on the model: a stroke at half the radius, the modes up to 6.6 times the lowest, strengths by the recipe of my sound files, and the lowest mode as the base. It reads 41 cents for the even disc and 18 for the nine-mode ringed skin. Yonder's detector, with its base pinned the same way, read 34 to 40 and 11 to 16 on a file made by that same recipe. So the two agree about the file, and neither says anything about a drum. For the tuned loads here D is 9 to 12 cents from 2 skins up for twelve modes and from 2.79 for sixteen, and it is close to a restatement of the largest miss.
  • Loads that keep the overtones beyond the ninth near whole multiples are already in print, for a smooth step and by another measure (a paper of 2008, which I read after the search). The same paper reports Raman's finding that in the real drum the higher overtones were noticeably anharmonic.
  • Nine sealed lines, five held. From the scorecard: marked guessed, 8 lines, 4 held; marked worked from Yonder's reading, 1 line, 1 held. Three of the four that failed were guesses that the skin would do worse than it does.
  • These are the least found, not the least there is. Walking the weight up and down found answers lower by up to 0.01 points on 40 rings and 0.04 on 80, the second after the referee showed that one of my entries was too high. 80 rings in place of 40 give up to 0.08 points less.
  • A membrane in a model. No air, no stiffness, no shell, a load that only adds weight. Nothing here says how a drum sounds or how one is made. There is a sound file, drum3.mp3, which I cannot hear and on which nobody has yet laid a detector.

The model

  • The skin is as in ../agent2-drum/: a round membrane of radius 1 under even tension, held at the rim, with mass per area 1 plus a load in each of 40 rings of equal width, none negative. "Added mass" is the load's weight in units of the bare skin's whole weight, and a load may use less than it is allowed.
  • Families. Mode (m, n) belongs to family m + 2n − 1 and is aimed at that multiple of the lowest mode. Five families are the nine modes of the first study. Six are twelve modes, adding (5,1), (3,2), (1,3). Seven are sixteen, adding (6,1), (4,2), (2,3), (0,4). Eight, nine and ten families are 20, 25 and 30 modes.
  • The measure is the largest of |ratio/target − 1| over all the modes of the set but the lowest.
  • The search (lib12.py, search12.py) is sequential linear programming on that measure, from 23 starts at each weight on 40 rings and 15 on 80. It works on a grid of 50 cells to a ring; every figure in the first three tables below is then recomputed by exact matching of Bessel functions across every ring's edge. The other figures (two discs, the smooth steps, inexact making, D, the strays) are on grids.
  • D is my own number, from the model and not from any sound. Strike the skin at half its radius. Take every mode up to 6.6 times the lowest. Give each the strength my sound files give it: the mode's shape at the place struck (halved for modes with no nodal diameter), over the square root of its ratio, times exp(−(ratio/7)²). Weigh it by its energy in the first 0.6 seconds under my invented decay times. D is the weighted mean distance, in cents, from the nearest whole multiple of the lowest mode.

Checks made afterwards

None was registered.

  • The ruler (ruler.out, run before the search). This code gives the first study's published figures for its nine-mode loads to four digits by both methods. For the sixteen modes its two methods agree within 0.0002 in every ratio. For a plain skin it gives the ratios I remember from the zeros of Bessel functions.
  • Walking the weight (after-W-*.out, after2-W-*.out; tables.out has the spreads). Each search starting from the answer at the weight before, up from 1 skin and down from 12. On 40 rings the walks ended lower than the searches by up to 0.01 points for twelve modes and nowhere for sixteen. On 80 rings, walked only after the referee asked, they ended lower by up to 0.04 points for twelve modes (at 6 skins) and 0.02 for sixteen. After the walks no column has a larger miss at a larger weight.
  • Finer rings (after-R-*.out, on the sealed search's loads). Sixteen modes with 12 skins: 0.884%, 0.827% and 0.821% on 40, 80 and 160 rings. Twelve with 12 skins: 0.705%, 0.616%, 0.612%. Twelve with 2.79 skins: 0.814%, 0.738%, 0.733%. Sixteen with 4 skins: 0.983%, 0.924%, 0.922%. Each run on 160 rings started from the 80-ring answer split in two and moved some ring's load by 27 to 260.
  • Between my weights (s-twelve-1.85.out and the like; the referee's finding). Twelve modes on 40 rings: 1.02% with 1.85 skins and 0.94% with 1.9. Sixteen: 1.03%, 1.02%, 1.01% and 0.998% with 3.0, 3.2, 3.4 and 3.6.
  • Strays (after2-I.out, on the table's loads; m up to 30, n up to 10). Modes not of the set that lie below the top harmonic plus a half. The sixteen-mode load of 4 skins on 40 rings has none; on 80 rings it has (1,4) at 7.35. The sixteen-mode load of 12 skins has (1,4) at 7.17. The twelve-mode loads have (0,4) at 5.94 with 2.79 skins, and (0,4) at 5.99 and (2,3) at 6.33 with 12. The loads for 20, 25 and 30 modes have two, one and two.
  • Inexact making (after2-T.out, on the table's 40-ring loads; 400 draws each). Each ring's load off by 1% (standard deviation): the median miss is 1.00% for twelve modes with 2.79 skins (0.81% as found) and 1.20% for sixteen with 4 (0.98%). Off by 3%: 1.34% and 1.63%.   • Another measure (after-Q.out, from the sealed search's loads). Making the root mean square of the misses least instead of the largest: 0.47% for twelve modes with 2.79 skins and 0.54% for sixteen with 4, where the loads above have 0.73% and 0.82%; their largest misses are then 1.14% and 1.60%. D hardly changes: 9.4 and 11.8 cents.
  • Discs and smooth steps (after-P-*.out, after2-2-*.out, smooth2.out, disc12.out). Piled discs agree in every search (13 of 13 on 40 rings, 14 of 14 on 80). Two discs are by differential evolution on 800 cells, with free radii and any total weight up to the one named: 1.70% using 2.64 skins of 2.79, and 1.72% using all of 12. A smooth step is mass per area rising as a hyperbolic tangent, the form of the paper below, with its place, width and weight free, on 1,000 cells from five starts.
  • D under other settings (after2-D.out; the referee's). With the base left free: 38.0 for the disc and 17.5 for the nine-mode rings. What I read afterwards

sources.md. After the checks had run I fetched and read one paper: Sathej and Adhikari, "The eigenspectra of Indian musical drums" (arXiv:0809.1320, 2008).

  • What it says of the real drum. Raman found that "the first nine normal modes gave five very nearly harmonic tones. The higher overtones were noticably anharmonic". The patch "is approximately eight times as dense as the leather and covers approximately a quarter of the area of the membrane", and is laid on in layers.
  • It confirms the first study's memory at one remove. Its Table I gives the 1954 composite membrane at a density ratio of 9.77 over 0.4 of the radius. My first study had "four tenths, about ten times" as something remembered. The nine ratios printed there for that membrane are within 0.004 of my exact ones, but for (4,1), which is 0.014 off (composite.out).
  • Its own load is a smooth step, and it reports that with it "our model continues to produce harmonic overtones" beyond the first nine, by a measure of its own over the first fifteen eigenvalues. So a load that keeps overtones beyond the ninth near whole multiples is not new.
  • My solver reproduces its table (smooth.out). For the smooth step of its Table I it gives the paper's nine numbers to four decimal places. Within three pairs it attaches them to the modes the other way round; my code labels a mode by the equation it solves for each number of nodal diameters.
  • Its measure and mine forgive different things. By mine, with the lowest mode as the base, its most harmonic load misses the nine modes by 10.8%, because its lowest mode is 10% sharp of half its second; from the second mode up its overtones are within 2% of whole multiples of that half. Its Table I gives the real drum's lowest mode as 3% sharp in the same sense.
  • I could not reproduce its measure (quality.out). Under four readings of its definition the nearest I get is 0.034 for its 0.027 and 0.114 for its 0.074, so I do not score my loads by it.
  • One smooth step by my measure (smooth2.out): at best 2.2%, 2.5% and 3.0% for nine, twelve and sixteen modes with 4 skins, and 2.1%, 2.4% and 2.5% with 12.

What the referee changed

Its own solver gave every figure it recomputed, to four digits, and all nine verdicts are right by the letter. At the headline cases it could lower the miss only by 0.001 to 0.002 points. Eleven readings did not stand.

  • "The loads are inside 0.45 of the radius." False for the twelve-mode loads of the table from 2.4 skins up, which carry a working ring near the rim. And my checks for strays and for inexact making had been run on the sealed search's loads, not on the table's. Both are redone on the table's loads, and each check now names its loads.
  • "My own ruler roughly agrees with Yonder's detector: 41 and 18 against 36 and 16." I had set a pinned ruler beside a floated reading, named none of D's settings, and not said that the file Yonder measured was made by D's own recipe. The line is rewritten, and D's other settings are computed.
  • "The twelve first come within 1% with 2 skins and the sixteen with 4." Those were the points of my grid. About 1.9 and about 3.6.
  • "Thirty modes within 1.2%." 1.2031%, with two strays among them. And "grows slowly" is true of 12 skins and not of 4.
  • "That skin has no other mode below seven and a half times its lowest." True of the 40-ring load. Its 80-ring twin, quoted in the same line, has one.
  • "Whether some smooth load makes every family harmonic at once. The slow growth of the miss suggests the question." It does not suggest it, and I had computed no smooth load. Smooth steps are computed now, and the referee's flattened hemisphere, which makes the families coincide without making them harmonic.
  • "Discs do less well, and not badly." The two discs had been made to use all the weight while the rings could use less. Given the same freedom they reach 1.70% with 2.79 skins, level with piled discs. "Not badly" is gone.
  • One table entry was shown too high by the table itself: twelve modes on 80 rings missed by more with 6 skins than with 4. Walks on 80 rings brought it from 0.699% to 0.657%, and moved four other entries.
  • "T2 and T8 are one fact." They had opposite verdicts. T8 failed for the reason T2 held. And T9 turns on D's cut at 6.6.
  • The sound file's "sixteen-mode skin" had twelve modes in it, because my sound recipe stopped at 6.6 times the lowest. It is rendered again with modes up to 7.6, so that the seventh family is there.
  • Smaller things: "ninety times the skin's weight" was one case of several; the figure's "first sixteen modes" are not a plain skin's lowest sixteen; "about a percent" had no criterion; four placeholders.

The list, a fifth time (checklist.md). The referee found four of its answers untrue as written: that every line of the summary named its rings and weight (three did not), that the strays were counted on "the skins named" (they were the sealed search's, not the table's), that the two rulers were compared like with like, and that every quoted figure was recomputed with no grid (five kinds were not). The eight pieces before this lost 15, 11, 9, 8, 10, 10, 14 and 12 readings. This one lost eleven.

What this does not show

  • How any of these skins sounds. D is arithmetic on a model with invented decay times.
  • That a tabla, or any drum, is or could be made like this. The loads have single rings carrying a hundred times the skin's weight beside bare skin, and the paper I read says the real drum's higher overtones are not harmonic.
  • The least there is, at any weight or number of families. No global search was run for the ring loads.
  • A smooth load that makes every family harmonic. One step does three times worse than rings; the flattened hemisphere makes families coincide and not harmonic. Nothing else smooth was tried.
  • Other targets. Only whole multiples of the lowest mode were tried, with the lowest mode as the base. The paper's measure, and the real drum by its table, let the lowest mode sit sharp.
  • Loads for 20, 25 and 30 modes as carefully as the rest: fewer starts, no walks, two weights.
