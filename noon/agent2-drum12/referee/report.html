REFEREE REPORT: drum, beyond the ninth mode

1. Verdict

Every figure I recomputed reproduces with my own solver, and all nine verdicts are right by the letter of the sheet. Several sentences say more than the numbers: one is plainly false ("inside 0.45 of the radius"), one check was run on a different load from the one in the tables, and the D line compares unlike quantities. At the headline cases I could lower the miss only by 0.001 to 0.002 points; at one table entry (twelve modes, 80 rings, 6 skins) I lowered it by 0.043.

4. What I verified and how

  • Solver. My own: radial linear finite elements for estimates and gradients, each root then polished by my own Bessel shooting across the rings (referee/sol.py). It gives the plain-skin Bessel-zero ratios to five decimals.
  • Blind step (blind.out), before opening the study: the three tasks. Sixteen and twelve modes had 60 starts each of sequential linear programming on the minimax; the disc had a full grid then Nelder-Mead.
  • Loads recomputed. Thirteen entries of best.json and all twelve after-N loads, each agreeing with the author's exact figure to four digits. The sixteen ratios in "Where the modes sit" match.
  • Strays. Counted to m = 40, n = 10 for 26 loads.
  • Scorecard. T3 and T4 on the sealed load; T6 blind; T7, T8 and T9 through my own D; the Brier arithmetic by hand; the four quotes against the sheet; the sheet's sha256.
  • Controls. Weights between the grid points; two discs with free weight; the tolerance draws (300); the fish-eye load; D under other strikes, cuts and weights; outer-ring seeds; 80-ring restarts.
  • Compute. About five minutes of wall time on up to four processes, no sub-agents. I went well over the twelve-minute aim.
