What I read, and when

Nothing was looked up before the sealed sheet was scored. After the checks had run, at 23:01 UTC on 22 November 2026, I fetched one paper and read it through, as text extracted from its PDF: G. Sathej and R. Adhikari, "The eigenspectra of Indian musical drums", arXiv:0809.1320v1, 8 September 2008 (https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.1320). Two searches by a tool led me to it. What follows is what the paper says, in its words where quoted.

  1. What Raman found. "Raman and coworkers obtained the eigenmodes and eigenvalues of the mridangam, showing that the first nine normal modes gave five very nearly harmonic tones. The higher overtones were noticably anharmonic, but Raman noted features in the construction of design to supress the higher overtones." Its Table I gives experimental ratios for the nine modes of the dayan, with the first overtone taken as 2: 1.03, 2.00, 3.00, 3.00, 4.00, 4.00, 5.04, 5.03, 5.08.
  2. The 1954 model. "Ramakrishna and Sondhi modelled the drum head as a composite membrane of two distinct densities", whose eigenvalues "agree with Raman's experimental values to within 10%". Table I is computed "for σ = 3.125 and k = 0.4", σ squared being the ratio of the two densities: 9.77 times the skin's weight over 0.4 of the radius. My first study had "four tenths, about ten times" from memory and could not confirm it; this confirms it at one remove (I have not read the 1954 paper).
  3. The real patch. "The sihai is approximately eight times as dense as the leather and covers approximately a quarter of the area of the membrane", is "applied in layers", and makers' values lie "typically in the range 2.5 < σ < 4 and 0.45 < k < 0.55".
  4. The paper's own model is a smooth step: mass per area 1 + (σ² − 1)/2 · (1 − tanh((r − k)/ξ)). Its measure Q is the sum, over the first 15 eigenvalues in rising order, of the squared distance from "the nearest integer multiple of the fundamental". It reports Q = 0.027 at σ = 2.57, k = 0.492, ξ = 0.091 against 0.074 for the composite membrane, and says: "Going beyond the first nine modes, we find that our model continues to produce harmonic overtones while the composite membrane model shows significant deviations from harmonicity." So a load that keeps overtones beyond the ninth near whole multiples is already in print, for a smooth step and by that measure.
  5. What it leaves out, by its own account: the air enclosed in the drum, which raises "modes with nodal circles ... the greatest being the fundamental", and damping.

The PDF and the text extracted from it are kept in my home folder and are not copied here; checkfacts.py reads the text to check three quoted phrases, and that one check will not run elsewhere.

What I did with it (smooth.py, smooth2.py, quality.py, composite.py):

  • For the 1954 composite membrane as the paper's Table I gives it (density ratio 9.77 over 0.4 of the radius), my exact solver's nine ratios are within 0.004 of the paper's "Composite" column, but for (4,1), which is 0.014 off (composite.out).
  • My solver gives the nine numbers of the paper's Table I, "Smooth" column, to four decimal places. Within three pairs it attaches them to the modes the other way round: the paper has (2,1) at 3.0393 and (0,2) at 3.0534 where I have (0,2) at 3.0394 and (2,1) at 3.0534, and likewise for (3,1) and (1,2), and for (4,1) and (2,2). My code solves a separate equation for each number of nodal diameters, so its labels are by construction; I have not asked the authors.
  • I could not reproduce the paper's two values of Q under any of four readings of its definition (each mode with nodal diameters counted once or twice; eigenvalues scaled by the lowest mode or by half the second). The nearest I get is 0.034 for its 0.027 and 0.114 for its 0.074. So I do not score my loads by Q.
  • The paper's measure and mine differ in what they forgive. By mine, with the lowest mode as the base, the paper's best load misses the nine modes by 10.8%, because its lowest mode is 10% sharp of half its second; its overtones from the second up are within 2% of whole multiples of that half.
