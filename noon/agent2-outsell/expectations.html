What I expect of two rival products, written at 20:57:16 UTC on 22 November 2026, before anything was computed

The model. Two products, A the better. Buyers come one at a time. When A has sold nA and B has sold nB, the next buyer takes A with probability

q (a + nA)^g / ( q (a + nA)^g + (a + nB)^g ).

q > 1 is A's edge: the odds with which a buyer who knows nothing of earlier sales prefers A. g is the strength of imitation: 0 is none (every buyer judges alone), 1 is in proportion to sales so far, above 1 is more than in proportion. a is the weight of a product that has sold nothing; a = 1 unless a line says otherwise. Both start at no sales. "P" after N sales is the chance that A has sold more than B, plus half the chance of a tie. It is to be computed exactly, step by step over all the ways the sales can go (no sampling). "Between" includes its ends.

What the lines rest on (all worked by hand; nothing has been run). Give each product its own clock: A's k-th sale takes an exponential time of mean 1/(q (a+k-1)^g), B's of mean 1/(a+k-1)^g, all independent; the order of the sales is then the model's (a construction I remember as Rubin's). So A is ahead after 2m-1 sales exactly when A's first m sales take less time than B's first m. For g = 1 and a = 1 the time of m sales is the largest of m exponentials, which gives line R4, and for large N: A is ahead when W_B < W_A^(1/q) (N/2)^(1 - 1/q), with W_A, W_B independent exponentials of mean 1. For g < 1 the share settles to x with x/(1-x) = q^(1/(1-g)), and its scatter is about sqrt(x(1-x)/((1-2g)N)) below g = 1/2, about sqrt(x(1-x) ln N / N) at g = 1/2. For g > 1 one product takes all but a few of the sales, and which one is settled early.
