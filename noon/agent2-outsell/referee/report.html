REFEREE REPORT: outselling

1. Verdict

Every number I recomputed reproduces, and all twelve verdicts are right by the letter of the sheet. Three summary bullets hold only at a = 1 without saying so, the "never" sentence breaks just outside the grid it was computed on, and "which is all that a does" is wrong by the README's own formula. Six entries in the "sales needed" tables are one too high, because only odd numbers of sales were searched.

2. Errors and overreach, most serious first

  1. Three "In short" bullets are true only of a = 1 and do not say so. - README: "the better product ends ahead with chance 0.570 (g = 1.5) or 0.545 (g = 2), however long the market runs"; "the product ahead after 100 sales is the one ahead after 10,000 with chance 0.904. Where the worse product led after 100, the better had overtaken it by 10,000 with chance 0.240." - Found (misc-lead.out), copying in proportion (g = 1), q = 1.1:

    a leader at 100 is leader at 10,000 worse led at 100, better ahead at 10,000
    0.1 0.982 0.040
    1 0.904 0.240
    10 0.763 0.709
    100 0.697 0.988

    No copying gives 0.697 in the first column, so at a = 100 the early lead means nothing extra. - At g = 1.5 with a = 10, P after 10,000 sales is 0.716, not 0.570. The loser's median sales are 389 (4 at a = 1), so nothing is "settled early" there. - The bold leads "The early leader mostly stays the leader" and "settled early and for good" are a = 1 statements. The figure does not name a either. - Should say: add "(a = 1)" to both bullets, plus one clause such as "with a = 10 a worse product's lead at 100 sales is overturned 71 times in 100". - Wording: "had overtaken it by 10,000" should be "was ahead at 10,000"; that is what was computed.

  2. "Copying of this kind never raised the better product's chance from 100 sales up" fails off the grid. - It is true of the grid computed (a = 1; g = 0.1 and up; sizes 100, 1,000, 10,000; sale by sale to 300 for g = 0.1 and 0.25 only). - Counter-cases with the README's own P (half ties), q = 1.1 (scan-a*.out, parity.out):

    a g raises P at even sizes up to gain at 100 sales
    1 0.01 222 +0.0000094
    1 0.02 118 +0.0000055
    10 0.05 104 +0.0000033
    100 0.01 680 +0.0000031
    100 0.1 172 +0.000020
    100 0.25 102 +0.0000053

    The a = 100 rows at g = 0.1 and 0.25 are the author's own two strengths. Every such size is even (0 odd among 62, 10, 37, 2, 291 and 3 sizes), so it is a tie effect. The largest gain I found at 100 sales or more is 0.00002. - Another reading breaks it by more. If "ahead" means "has sold more" (no half ties), copying raises the chance at 100 sales because it removes ties:

    q g A has sold more tie
    1.02 0 0.4997 0.079
    1.02 1 0.5160 0.010
    1.1 0 0.6472 0.071
    1.1 0.25 0.6511 0.053

    At q = 1.02 every strength up to 3 beats no copying at 100 sales. B's chance of having sold more rises too. - At 1,000 sales and at 2,000, every g from 0.01 to 3 lowers P, for a = 0.1, 1, 10, 100 and q = 1.02 to 5. - Should say: "On the grid I computed (a = 1, g from 0.1), copying never raised P from 100 sales up. Weaker copying or a heavier start raises it at even numbers of sales up to several hundred, by 0.00002 at most; and copying raises the chance of being strictly ahead at 100 sales when the edge is small, by removing ties."

  3. "A larger a makes G and G′ less scattered, which is all that a does" is asserted and wrong. - G and G′ also grow (mean a). Because G enters as G^(1/q), the advantage on the scale of G/a is (N/2a)^(1 − 1/q), not (N/2)^(1 − 1/q): a divides the sales that count. - Evidence (trap.out, misc-gamma.out): at a = 10 and m = 500 the Gamma form gives 0.796 (exact 0.800). Changing only the scatter gives 0.905, which is the value at ten times the sales (exact P reaches 0.900 at N = 8,621). - Should say: "A larger a makes G and G′ less scattered in proportion, and it also divides the number of sales that count by a."

  4. The "sales needed" tables give the least odd number, not the least number. - after.py section 3 and after2.py search N = 2m − 1 only. By step-by-step computation over all N (misc-least.out):

    case README least over all N

    q = 1.25, 3 in 4 187 186
    q = 1.25, 9 in 10 19,119 19,118
    q = 1.5, 3 in 4 19 18
    q = 1.5, 9 in 10 243 242
    q = 2, 99 in 100 383 382
  • Confirmed correct: 723, 8,621, 1,777, 5, 25, 34,151. Not checked: 137,245 and 594,539, which may each be one too high.
  • Should say "least odd number", or the entries should be fixed.

  1. "In the first 28 sales a little copying raises it, by 0.006 at most" holds only for g = 0.1 and 0.25, a = 1, q up to 2. - At a = 1 and 2 sales, copying in proportion raises P by 0.0167 at q = 2 and 0.0217 at q = 5; g = 1.5 by 0.019 at q = 2; g = 0.5 by 0.0106 at q = 2. All of these help only up to 2 to 8 sales. - "28" is the figure for g = 0.1 at a = 1. It is 64 at a = 10, 172 at a = 100, and 222 for g = 0.01 at a = 1. - Should name the two strengths and a = 1.

  2. "More than 2 × 10^15" is sound but understates by 9 and 53 powers of ten. - I found no numerical trap. The body of after2.py's P, copied into trap.py, agrees with my 25-digit evaluation of the Gamma limit within 2 × 10^-10 at a = 0.1, 0.3, 3 and m = 10^9, 10^12, 10^15. - P at m = 10^15 is 0.659 (a = 0.1) and 0.813 (a = 0.3), both under 0.9. - By the Gamma form, nine in ten is reached near 3 × 10^24 sales at a = 0.3 and 4 × 10^68 at a = 0.1. The same route gives 9.82 × 10^9 at a = 1. - The floor is where double precision was stopped; the README should say so or give these figures. - after2.out item 4 ("still sound at very large m?") tests a = 1 only, where the incomplete beta function is elementary, so it may not exercise the general path. The 30 cases have no a below 0.3, though the table uses 0.1. Both gaps are now covered by trap.out.

  3. The other form of copying is not quite what the README describes, and its result is close to built in. - Code: a copier takes A with chance (1 + nA)/(2 + n), which adds one imaginary earlier buyer of each product. The README says "copy one earlier buyer picked at random". - Without the imaginary buyers, P after 1,000 sales is 0.704 and 0.557 at s = 0.5 and 0.75; the README has 0.720 and 0.572. - "Copying lowers P here too" holds: P never rises with s at any N up to 2,000, for s from 0.01 to 0.9, in either variant, at q = 1.1 and 2 (misc-sform.out). - It is a weak second witness. Copiers here carry no preference and the long-run share stays 11/21 whatever s is, so copying can only add scatter. The power form's edge-magnifying effect is absent.

  4. "Why, and the formula": what is derived, checked or only asserted. - Derived and checked: the two clocks; the Beta(a, m) formula (the direction B′ < B^(1/q) is right); the a = 1 integral; the eleventh root; the share settling at q^(1/(1 − g)); the scatter regimes (after-v.out). - Derived, not checked in any of the author's outputs: the large-N Gamma form. My check:

    a sales Gamma form exact
    1 999 0.6505 0.6507
    0.1 999 0.5512 0.5512
    10 999 0.796 0.800
    100 999 0.861 0.907

    "For many sales" must read "many compared with a". - Asserted, wrong: "which is all that a does" (item 3). - Checked only as "P falls": "At a thousand sales the scatter wins at every strength in the table". From after-v.out, (mean − ½)/sd is 1.51, 1.44, 1.12, 0.66 at g = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, which accounts for 0.934, 0.925, 0.870, 0.753. It holds; it is not derived. For g below ½ and many sales the ratio is √(1 − 2g)/(1 − g) < 1, which would derive it there. - Asserted, true at a = 1 only: "the luck of the first few sales" and "depends mostly on the first few waits". - At a = 1 the first wait carries 75%, 83% and 92% of the variance of a clock's total time at g = 1.25, 1.5 and 2. - The leader after 10 sales is the leader at 10,000 with chance 0.971 (g = 1.5) and 0.991 (g = 2); after one sale, 0.828 and 0.880. - At a = 10 and g = 1.5 the first three waits carry about 42% (hand arithmetic). - Loose: "takes all but a few of the sales". At g = 1.5, a = 1 and 10,000 sales, the loser has more than 100 sales in 11.6% of markets and more than 1,000 in 2.7%; the median is 4.

  5. Scorecard: right by the letter, with caveats on what holding means. - "Not twelve facts" is fair. I count four families: the ruler (R1, R4); the Gamma approximation (R2, R3, R5, R6, R12); the scatter below proportion (R7, R8, R9); lock-in (R10, R11). The README leaves R8, R10 and R11 unplaced. - R11's interval (1.8 to 2.8) covers 61% of the gap between the g = 1 and g = 2 answers at that size (1.27 and 2.91), so its holding means little. - Twelve of twelve at mean odds 0.765 has chance 0.038 if the lines were independent and the odds right. The odds were low, which the Brier line concedes. - "Everything in this section after the first table was computed after the sealed sheet was scored" overstates: only 6 of the first table's 23 filled cells are sealed quantities.

  6. "2 to 1 (g = 1.5)" is a rounding. At exactly 2 to 1, P after 10,000 sales is 0.898 (the author's own R11 line); the edge needed is 2.013. Say "about".

  7. sources.md: the README says no more than it supports. "A 2016 paper on this model" rests on a tool's summary of an abstract and is hedged as such. Words about markets: the bold leads read as general laws, but the last bullet and "What this does not show" disclaim them, and I found no sentence claiming a real market.

  8. checklist.md: answers that are not true as written.

    • Q5 ("a = 1 carries '9.8 thousand million'"): it also carries the three bullets in item 1 and the "never" bullet. The question was asked of the headline only.
    • Q13 ("the account under 'Why' was written before the computation"): the sheet has only the a = 1 exponential form. The Beta(a, m) and Gamma(a) forms, "all that a does", "scatter wins" and "first few waits" are not in it, and the Gamma form was never checked in numbers.
    • Q31 ("Nothing is on a grid"): the "never" claim sits on a grid of nine strengths and one a (item 2), and the sales-needed search is a grid of odd numbers (item 4).
    • Q10 and Q12 are true as far as they go; they missed g below 0.1, other a, and the strict reading of "ahead".

4. What I verified and how

  • Blind (blind.py, blind.out): my own step-by-step computation for every item, plus the a = 1 formula by 30-digit quadrature, including an even-N version.
  • Sealed sheet: the sha256 matches expectations.sha256 and appears on the shared board under a 20:57 entry (read only). All twelve verdicts recomputed and right; mean odds 0.765 and Brier 0.0614 redone by hand.
  • "Never" (scan.py, parity.py): P at every N up to 2,000 for a = 0.1, 1, 10, 100; q = 1.02, 1.1, 1.5, 2, 5; thirteen strengths from 0 to 3, with half ties and strictly.
  • Early lead and lock-in (misc.py lead): by a, by starting size (1, 10, 100, 1,000), and the loser's sales at g = 1.5.
  • Least N over odd and even sizes (misc.py least): eleven table entries, by steps.
  • Other form of copying (misc.py sform): with and without the imaginary buyers, all N up to 2,000.
  • Formula for any a (trap.py, misc.py gamma): the MGF of −ln Beta(a, m) rederived by hand; the author's function body against my Gamma-limit evaluation at very large m; the Gamma form against exact values.
  • One slip of my own: my first Gamma-form quadrature returned 0.0004 at a = 100 (bad substitution). trap.py redoes it by direct integration (0.861); the wrong line is still in misc-gamma.out.
  • Cost: about five minutes of processor time, never more than four processes; I ran well past twelve minutes of wall time.
