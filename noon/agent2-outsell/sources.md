# What I looked up, and when

Everything below was looked up on 22 November 2026 between 21:03 and 21:06 UTC, after the sealed sheet had been scored and
most of the checks had run. I read no paper. Each entry is what a search tool, or a tool that summarises a page, reported
to me; where words are in quotation marks they are the tool's rendering of the source.

1. **The construction with two clocks.** Search: "Rubin exponential embedding generalized Polya urn feedback Davis 1990
   reinforced random walk monopoly". The tool reported sources that describe "Rubin's exponential embedding" as "an
   alternative construction of a generalized Pólya urn, built from independent birth processes". It did not confirm the
   paper by Davis (1990) in which I remember first seeing the construction credited to Rubin.
2. **The model itself.** Search: "Polya urn with fitness linear feedback probability fitter colour is ahead after n
   draws exact formula maximum of exponentials". The tool reported a paper by Jiang, Figueiredo, Ribeiro and Towsley,
   "On the Duration and Intensity of Competitions in Nonlinear Pólya Urn Processes with Fitness"
   (https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.02097, submitted 6 April 2016), whose model it gave as: the next ball has colour i with
   probability f_i X_i^β / (f_1 X_1^β + f_2 X_2^β), with r = f_1/f_2 "the fitness ratio". That is this study's model with
   q = r, g = β and counts that start at a. A page-summarising tool said of the abstract that the paper studies the time
   of the last tie and the number of ties, and "finds that fitness superiority shortens competitions in the sublinear
   regime but lengthens them in the superlinear regime". The search tool also said: "None of the results I retrieved
   gives a closed-form expression for P(fitter colour is ahead after n draws)". So I do not know whether the formula of
   line R4 is in print.
3. **Feedback without an edge.** Search: "Drinea Frieze Mitzenmacher balls and bins models with feedback probability
   leader biased bin". The tool reported a 2002 paper of that title in which "the probability a ball lands in a bin is
   proportional to the number of balls already there, raised to an exponent p", with "a phase transition at p = 1" and
   systems with p > 1 that "tend naturally toward monopoly"; and papers by Oliveira (https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0510415,
   https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0510648) and by Mitzenmacher, Oliveira and Spencer on the same family.
4. **The experiment I had in mind.** Search: "Salganik Dodds Watts 2006 Experimental study of inequality and
   unpredictability in an artificial cultural market findings". The tool reported an online market "with 14,341
   participants" who downloaded unknown songs with or without seeing earlier choices; that "increasing the strength of
   social influence increased both inequality and unpredictability of success"; and that "the best songs rarely did
   poorly, and the worst rarely did well, but any other result was possible". It also reported later papers that
   dispute how far the unpredictability is due to social influence as such. Nothing in this study tests that
   experiment: it has two products where the experiment, as I remember it, had 48 songs, and it has no data.
