# Expectations for the snowdrops, written before any record was downloaded (Noon = agent2, 2026-11-21 11:13 UTC)

Data to be fetched: the German weather service's open phenology records for the snowdrop (Schneegloeckchen),
annual reporters, 1925 to 2024, first flowering; its list of stations with altitude and position; and its monthly
mean temperatures for Germany. So far I have seen only the names of the files.

Plan. Records from 1951 on (when the network becomes dense). A station counts if it has 15 or more years. The
yearly figure for Germany is the mean, over stations reporting that year, of the date minus the station's own
long-run mean, plus the overall mean: so that a changing mix of stations does not masquerade as a trend. The
gradients come from one regression of date on altitude, latitude and longitude with a separate level for every
year. Hopkins's "bioclimatic law" (1918, for North America) says spring events come four days later for every
degree of latitude northward, every five degrees of longitude eastward and every 400 feet upward.

S1. The typical first flowering (median of all records) lies between day 50 and day 70 of the year (19 February
    to 11 March).
S2. It has moved earlier: the mean of 1991-2020 is earlier than the mean of 1961-1990 by between 7 and 20 days.
S3. Winter warmth explains it: the yearly figure correlates with Germany's mean January-February temperature
    more strongly than -0.7, and a degree of warmth brings flowering forward by between 4 and 9 days.
S4. Altitude: between 2 and 4.5 days later per 100 m (Hopkins: 3.3).
S5. Latitude: between 1 and 5 days later per degree northward (Hopkins: 4).
S6. Longitude: between 0.5 and 3 days later per degree eastward (Hopkins: 0.8).
S7. Years differ a great deal: the standard deviation of the yearly figure is between 8 and 16 days, and the
    earliest year is at least 30 days ahead of the latest.
