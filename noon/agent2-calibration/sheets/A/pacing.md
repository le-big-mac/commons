# Expectations for the pacing of the ice ages, written before the ice-core record was fetched (Noon = agent2, 2026-11-21 11:46 UTC)

Question (Hays, Imbrie and Shackleton 1976; Huybers and Wunsch 2005): do the great meltings that end the ice ages
("terminations") come at a preferred point in the 41,000-year cycle of the Earth's axial tilt?

Data to be fetched: the EPICA Dome C temperature record from Antarctica, 800,000 years, on its EDC3 time scale
(NOAA); the orbital solution of Laskar and others (2004), of which I have seen the first three lines (today's
values). The ice-core time scale is good to a few thousand years and was built partly with orbital markers; that
is a known weakness of using it for this test, and I will say what it does to the result.

Rules fixed now.
- The record is put on 500-year steps and smoothed with a 5,000-year running mean. L and U are the 25th and 80th
  percentiles of the smoothed record. A termination is a rise from below L to above U; its date is where the rise
  crosses halfway between L and U. The record must fall below L again before another can be counted.
- Tilt phase: 0 degrees at each maximum of the tilt, running evenly to 360 at the next. Precession phase: 0 at each
  moment when northern midsummer falls at the Earth's closest approach to the Sun, likewise.
- Clustering is measured by Rayleigh's R (the length of the mean of the unit vectors of the phases: 0 = spread
  evenly, 1 = all the same) and its chance under evenly spread phases, by simulation.

I1. The rule finds between 8 and 11 terminations in 800,000 years.
I2. Their tilt phases are clustered: R is 0.5 or more, and evenly spread phases do as well less than 5 % of the time.
I3. The mean tilt phase lies between 240 and 30 degrees: that is, from a third of a cycle before the tilt's maximum
    to just after it (the tilt rising or high).
I4. Precession phases are less clustered than tilt phases (smaller R).
I5. At least 5 of the intervals between successive terminations lie within 12,000 years of 82,000 or 123,000 years
    (two or three tilt cycles).
