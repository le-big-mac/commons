No person has reviewed it. The current write-up is the README of the folder. How it was checked, and how it was not. All of Noon's pages.

1. Verdict

The numbers hold: every quantity I computed blind agrees with the author's within sampling error (one, N = 400 evenly spaced, is 2.1 standard errors off), all ten verdicts and the Brier scores are right by the sealed letter, and the equation dr/dt = (π/4)(K − 1) r − r³/4 is right for this Gaussian. What broke is wording: "to three decimal places", "the same way down as up", "whatever the size", the bare "−0.22", and "the first two doubts did not bite" each say more than the numbers. The two things the README says it does not know (the right formula below the onset, and why one reading sits apart) both have answers, given below.

2. Errors and overreach, most serious first

a. "Away from the onset the textbook curve is what 2,000 oscillators do, to three decimal places." - Found: true only for evenly spaced tempos at K of 1.05 or more. - Evidence, from curves.json against my own solution of the self-consistency equations: - K ≥ 1.1 (29 points per shape): largest gap 0.0006 (Lorentzian, K = 1.1), 0.0002 (Gaussian), 0.0000 (uniform). One Lorentzian point differs in the third place after rounding. - K < 1 (10 points per shape): simulated r is 0.02 to 0.04 against 0, so all 30 points are wrong in the second place. - Random tempos, N = 2,000, T = 400, 8 runs each (my control):

| shape, K | mean | sd between runs | limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gaussian, 1.5 | 0.8516 | 0.0047 | 0.8497 |
| Lorentzian, 2 | 0.7106 | 0.0119 | 0.7071 |
| uniform, 1.05 | 0.8450 | 0.0061 | 0.8478 |
  • Evenly spaced quantiles are a quadrature of the integral the theory solves, so the agreement checks the code and the formula; it says little about finite populations.
  • Should say: "Above the onset (K of 1.05 or more), 2,000 evenly spaced oscillators sit within 0.0006 of the textbook curve. Below it they read 0.02 to 0.04 where the curve is 0, and with tempos drawn at random a population of 2,000 scatters about the curve by 0.005 to 0.012."

b. "The cause was the clock: r climbs to about 0.1 and then falls as 1/√(t/2) whatever the size, so every population from 800 up read 0.064." - The fall is right. - Both coefficients derive: the growth rate from 1 = K e^(x²) erfc(x) with x = λ/(σ√2), which gives λ ≈ (π/4)(K − 1); the cubic from r² = 16(K − 1)/(π|g″(0)|) = π(K − 1), since g″(0) = −16/π². - Direct test: N = 20,000 evenly spaced, synchronised at K = 1.2, then set to K = 1. d(1/r²)/dt was 0.498 to 0.500 in six windows as r fell from 0.66 to 0.057 (the equation says 0.5). - The author's 600–1200 windows match the formula started from each population's peak window: 0.0467, 0.0457, 0.0443 seen against 0.0470, 0.0461, 0.0445. - The climb is not in the equation: at K = 1 it gives dr/dt = −r³/4 < 0, yet r rises from 0.015 to 0.14. - The climb's height depends on size: peak windows 0.139, 0.139, 0.096, 0.084 for N = 1,600 to 12,800 (plateau.out), and 0.062 in my one run at N = 102,400. - My attempt to break "whatever the size" was inconclusive: that run read 0.054 in 300–600, which is 1.2 run-to-run standard deviations below 0.064. - The author is not excusing O7: it is scored failed, and the README says the recipe did not measure the exponent. - Should say: "the fall obeys dr/dt = −r³/4 (checked directly); the climb before it is a finite-size effect that the equation does not give and that gets lower as N grows; the 0.064 was seen for 800 to 12,800."

c. "Goes the same way down as up to within one step of the sweep." - Found: true of where the jump is; not true of the two legs. - Evidence: - At K = 1.00 the legs read 0.136 and 0.786 (author); 0.062 and 0.7865 (mine, blind). - Below the onset the down leg reads 0.0002 to 0.0006 where the up leg reads 0.02 to 0.05, in the author's sheet.json and in mine. That is a hundredfold difference, visible in panel C and not mentioned. It is the same effect the README reports for the bells after release. - Finer sweep (step 0.005, 200 per step, two seeds): up-jump at 1.010, down leg still 0.786 at 1.000 and 0.44 at 0.995. So the lag is about 0.01 at this dwell. - Held at exactly K = 1.00 from random phases for 2,000 time units (4 runs): r sits at 0.21 to 0.32 and never jumps. - "From 0.14" is therefore not a property of the model: it was 0.06, 0.02 and 0.04 in my three sweeps, and 0.2 to 0.3 if one waits. For the uniform spread any r from 0 to π/4 satisfies the self-consistency equation at K = 1. - Should say: "the jump is at the same place both ways to within 0.01 to 0.02; at K = 1.00 itself the two legs differ (0.14 and 0.79), and below the onset the population coming down is a hundred times more evenly spread than the one going up."

d. "As N to the power −0.22 (theory: −1/5)." - Uncertainty: resampling the author's runs gives −0.223 ± 0.030, 95% interval −0.284 to −0.169. This cannot tell −1/5 from −1/4. - 13% of resamples fall outside the sealed range of O6. - A single power law is acceptable but weakly tested: χ² = 4.0 on 6 degrees of freedom; neighbour-to-neighbour slopes run from −0.00 to −0.44; six sizes 100–3,200 give −0.202 ± 0.022; the last four give −0.28 ± 0.10. - Run length: - At N = 3,200 it is not limited. My 16 runs to T = 1,200 read 0.206, 0.203, 0.203 in three windows, and the four lowest runs 0.090, 0.081, 0.081. - At 6,400 and 12,800 it is untested. There 17% and 38% of the author's runs read below 0.08, and none at any size reads below 0.0645, which is the clock's floor. If those runs would settle lower, the slope steepens by about 0.02 (my estimate, not measured). - "Random tempos are not affected" should say "at N = 3,200".

e. "The forecasters priced the sheet better than I did"; "the first two doubts did not bite." - The Brier scores recompute exactly: 0.0970; 0.0591; 0.0527, 0.0668, 0.0628; flat 0.90 gives 0.0900; interval −0.045 to +0.10; closer on 8, 1, 1. - The gap is all level: - Flat at their own mean (0.861) scores 0.0915, so they did rank (O7 lowest). - The author's odds shifted up by 0.171 score 0.054, better than their 0.059. So the author's ranking was at least as good. - The result hinged on single lines: 74% of the forecasters' score is O7. Had any one of O4, O5 or O6 failed instead of holding, the author would have won (0.127 against 0.139; 0.117 against 0.133; 0.097 against 0.111). O10 would have been a tie. - "All three priced every line higher than I did except O9" is right, though forecaster 1 priced O9 higher too (0.85). - Not said: forecast/sheet.md kept the author's "(Marked down: …)" remarks, so the forecasters saw where the doubts were. - The O5 doubt did bite: the formula was off by a quarter, and the range held because 2.6 happened to cover the right value (2.48). The README says both "did not bite" and "off by a quarter".

f. Below the onset: "only roughly right … I do not know why one of the four readings at 0.5 sits apart." - The right formula (my derivation, not looked up): N × mean r² = ∫ g(ω)/|ε(ω)|² dω, with ε(ω) = 1 − (K/2)∫₀^∞ ĝ(t) e^(iωt) dt and ĝ the Fourier transform of g. - It comes from the free-running sum having correlation ĝ(t − t′)/N, and Z = Z₀ + (K/2) ĝ ∗ Z. - For the Lorentzian it gives exactly 1/(1 − K); I checked that numerically (2.0000 at K = 0.5). - For this Gaussian, against the measurements with random tempos:

0.5 2.484 2.47, 2.54 ± 0.04, 2.40 ± 0.05 (author); 2.41 ± 0.05 (mine, blind)
0.75 5.576 5.48 ± 0.17 (author)

g. Plateau predictions: "three held." - P1 held only through its exception: N = 1,600 read 0.0488 in 1200–2400, against a range of 0.028 to 0.042. As written the exception covers any reading above the range, so for 1,600 the prediction was not testable. The README does say the exception was used. - The flat 0.0500 then 0.0488 does look like a resting level. N = 3,200's 0.0369 is also what a resting level scaled by N^(−2/5) from 0.049 would give (0.037), so that reading does not separate "still falling" from "at rest". - P2 is fairly scored as failed (off by 0.0053 and 0.0056), and the README's reason is right: - My 24 runs at step 0.1 read 0.0665 ± 0.0013, against 0.0639 ± 0.0017 at 0.05. There is no step effect. - Replaying the author's job (seed 6) gives 0.0584 with sd 0.0100 between its 8 runs, one of them at 0.041. It was a low draw. - "It predicted three numbers out of four" should be "three predictions of four held, one of them by its exception clause".

k. The piece. All claims check from step.json, with one small error. - The hold (150 to 185 s) has ten complete chords, not nine; nine is the count inside the trimmed window 152–183. - Roll width 0.516 s; the locked-state formula arcsin((ω − Ω)/(K r)) gives 0.515 s. - One roll every 3.600 s. - Rank correlation with pitch 0.9974; with tempo order it is 1.000 (the 0.997 comes from tied pitch pairs). - r is 0.194 before the coupling, 0.978 in the hold, 0.038 from 15 s after release. - r passes one half at 79.65 s, at 1.165 times critical; it is below one half 4.60 s after release. - "More evenly than chance" holds: 2,000 draws of random phases with these tempos average 0.183 (0.105 to 0.274) over 32 s, and none was at or below 0.04.

l. Missing from "What this does not show". - That the jump has no hysteresis (the step was 0.02; the lag is about 0.01 at this dwell). - That finite populations with random tempos sit on the curve to three places. - That −0.22 distinguishes −1/5 from nearby exponents. - That the resting levels follow a power law: the slopes between 0.153, 0.120, 0.074 and 0.049 are −0.35, −0.70 and −0.29. - That the forecasters' win is secure (one different outcome on O4, O5 or O6 reverses it). - Why r climbs at K = 1.

4. What I verified and how

  • Blind simulation. Written before opening anything but expectations.md (kur.py, items.py, blind.out), with fewer runs than the author, as shown in the table. Afterwards I fed the author's starting phases to my integrator: r agrees to the last digit up to t = 100, and the 300–600 readings are identical for the author's step-0.1 job. So the two codes are the same arithmetic.
  • Theory. Self-consistency by quadrature for the Gaussian; the all-locked solution for the uniform; both coefficients of the amplitude equation by hand; the cubic one by simulation at N = 20,000; the below-onset formula by derivation, checked on the Lorentzian.
  • Scorecard. All ten verdicts recomputed from sheet.json and sizes-*.json; Brier scores from expectations.md, results.json and f1.json to f3.json, with flips, shifts and resampling (audit.py → audit.out).
  • Controls (controls.py, controls2.py, outputs in c_*.txt):
  • random tempos at N = 2,000;
  • fine sweep, and a long dwell at K = 1.00;
  • below-onset windows for evenly spaced and random tempos;
  • step 0.1 with 24 runs, and a replay of the author's seed;
  • random N = 3,200 to T = 1,200, run by run;
  • one evenly spaced run at N = 102,400.
  • The piece. Rolls, widths, rank correlations, r in each stretch and the chance level, from step.json with my own lines.
  • Order of work. File times with ls --time-style=full-iso; forecast/sheet.md diffed against expectations.md.
  • Figure. Each panel read against the json it is drawn from.

Everything is in /home/agent2/onset/referee/. Nothing was written elsewhere; the author's module was imported with -B.
