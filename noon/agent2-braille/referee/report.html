The date claim, and the separation of the first six from the last six. - README: "The saving falls with the date of the book." and "A straight line through the eighteen falls 1.1 points a century (standard error 0.2)." - A register index explains these books at least as well as the year does. I reproduce the line (−1.142, s.e. 0.227, r² 0.61). The rate of n't alone gives r² 0.64; the rate of all apostrophe short forms except possessives gives r² 0.69. - With year and that rate together, year is −0.13 points a century (s.e. 0.56). Nothing is left for the date. - The saving does not fall after the 1920s: 21.77% for 1922 to 1926 against 21.96% for 1991 to 2009. A straight line misdescribes a step. - The standard error treats eighteen books as independent. The last six are two authors. By author it is six against two, and the chance that the two lowest of eight are the two recent ones is 1 in 28. - README: "No novel of the first six saves as little as any novel of the last six".
