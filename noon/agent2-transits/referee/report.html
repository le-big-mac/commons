1. Verdict

All twelve verdicts and every number in transits.out and score.out reproduce from my blind code, and the scorecard is right by the letter of expectations.md. Several summary sentences say more than the numbers support, most seriously X10 and the Brier sentence that depends on it, then the size-gap "dip" and the outer-larger 70%. The resonance, hot-Jupiter and metallicity results did not break under the controls I ran, but each needs a narrower sentence.

2. Errors and overreach, most serious first

1. X10 "held" with a median of 0.000, and "they doubted X10, wrongly". - README: X10 held at 0.000 and 0.116; the forecasters "doubted X10, wrongly"; with zeros out the medians are 0.070 and 0.167. - Found: the 0.000 is a catalogue default, not a measurement, and both clauses are fragile. - Evidence: - Of the 551 giants under 5 days, 282 are exact zeros and 124 are flagged as upper limits in pl_orbeccenlim, a column that was fetched and never used. Only 146 (26%) are nonzero measurements. - Across all transit planets, 74% of stated eccentricities are exactly zero (86% for Kepler/K2). - The share of exact zeros among giants falls with period: 51%, 34%, 15%, 16% for under 5, 5–10, 10–20 and over 20 days. The contrast X10 measures is largely a contrast in how often a round orbit was assumed. - With zeros out, the first clause fails (0.070 is not under 0.05); the README gives the 0.070 but does not say so. That 0.070 also counts the upper limits (median 0.12) as values. - Measured values only: 0.047, bootstrap 0.040–0.055, under 0.05 in 72% of resamples. - Second clause: 0.116 over 122 planets, bootstrap 0.067–0.170, above 0.10 in only 60% of resamples. - The script's docstring says the rule was settled after seeing "the column names (not the values)"; the README says the last line had been seen, and that line carries pl_orbeccen = 0.000000. - Should say: "X10 held by the letter only because I counted the catalogue's assumed zeros; with zeros out it fails (0.070), with zeros and upper limits out it is 0.047 on 146 planets. The forecasters' doubt was not shown wrong."

2. "Three blind forecasters priced them a little better than I did: 0.089 against 0.106." - Found: the arithmetic is right, but the mean hides the pattern and rests on X10. - Evidence: - The forecasters' median was closer on 11 of 12 expectations; over all 34 in the ledger, on 24 (sign test p = 0.02, items not independent). - Mine-minus-theirs is +0.62 summed over eleven items and −0.41 on X10 alone. - Without X10: 0.104 against 0.048. If X10 is scored failed: 0.131 against 0.050, and over 34: 0.123 against 0.085. - The interval −0.07 to +0.08 is a resampling of 12 expectations that share data (X5–X8 use the same 1,113 pairs). The README does not say what the interval is. - The three forecasters correlate at 0.97–0.99 with each other; they are one opinion drawn three times. - The ledger gives 0.1139 and 0.0985 (0.09849): the README's 0.099 should be 0.098. - Should say: "The forecasters were closer on 11 of 12; my score is near theirs only because X10 held by the letter. Twelve items cannot separate us on the mean."

3. "So there is a dip where the papers put the gap, and my bins were too coarse and too far left to show it." - Found: a shallow dip survives, but both pieces of evidence offered are weaker than stated, and "too far left" is false. - Evidence: - 22 planets have a size of exactly 2.00 and sit on the bin edge. With the upper edge included instead, the dip is 282 against 301 and 325 (z = 1.5) rather than 260 against 301 and 347 (z = 3.1). - Shifting the twelve bins by a quarter or half a bin removes the local minimum. - The "better than 8%" sample (199, 183, 331) is a 0.8-sigma difference. The middle bin is lowest only at cuts of 8%, 10% and 12%; it is not at 3%, 5%, 6%, 15%, 20% or 30%. - Sliding the sealed three-bin design from 1.40 to 1.90 never passes; the sealed 1.58–2.00 bin sits where Fulton put the gap. Coarseness is the problem, not position. - In favour: one local minimum between 1.64 and 2.0 appears with 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 and 16 bins. In 0.1-wide linear bins, 1.7–2.2 runs 19% below its flanks; I chose those ranges after looking. - Should say: "After the fact, most binnings show a shallow dip (10–20%) somewhere from 1.7 to 2.2 Earth radii. In my twelve bins half its depth comes from 22 planets listed at exactly 2.00; the precision cut does not support it; no placement of my three bins would have passed."

4. "The outer one is the larger in 70% of pairs" and "correlate at 0.60". - Found: most of the 70% is not about neighbours, and the star's radius is not the control that matters. - Evidence: - Shuffling outer planets between systems gives 61.7% (60.2–63.3); within ten bands of stellar radius, 62.2%. The correlation was shuffled in the README; the 70% was not. - The catalogue-wide size–period trend is there in singles too (Spearman 0.37; 0.40 for all planets under 6 Earth radii). - Outer-larger is 62% for period ratios under 2 and 82% above 3. - Within twelve narrow bands of stellar radius the correlation is 0.51, the same as Noon's 0.514, so that control reproduces. But the detection floor depends on the star's noise, which is not in the table. - Kepler/K2 pairs with both planets above 1.5 times and 2 times a crude floor (10th-percentile size in that stellar-radius band): r = 0.44 and 0.42, outer larger 60% and 61%. Part of that drop is range restriction. - Pairs announced in the same year correlate at 0.67; in different years (233 pairs) at 0.41. - Should say: "Outer larger in 70% of pairs, against 62% when any inner planet is paired with any outer one from another system; neighbours' sizes correlate at 0.60, at 0.51 within narrow bands of stellar radius, and at about 0.43 for pairs well above a crude detection floor."

5. "Hot Neptunes are scarce: 5.5% … 13% … 19%." - Found: 5.5% is a mixture that depends on the survey mix, and the period comparison is not like-for-like. - Evidence: - Under 3 days: Kepler/K2 3.4% (14 of 412), TESS 13.7% (29 of 211, interval 9.7–19.0), others 0.6% (1 of 171, of which 162 are giants). Within TESS alone X3 fails. - "Other" discoveries are 21.5% of planets under 3 days and 1.2% at 10–30 days. - The share keeps rising to 30.6% at 30–100 days, where no desert is claimed, because small planets drop out of the denominator. - The pattern holds inside each big survey: Kepler/K2 3.4, 11.4, 17.5%; TESS 13.7, 21.4, 26.3%. - Should say: "Among listed planets under 3 days, 5.5% are 3 to 8 Earth radii: 3.4% of Kepler/K2's, 13.7% of TESS's. The share rises with period in each survey, partly because small planets are missed further out."

6. "Hot Jupiters are alone … 96% whether found from the ground, by Kepler or by TESS." - Found: the three 96%s are not three replications, and the baseline that gives the number meaning is missing. - Evidence: - "From the ground" (416) includes 29 found from space (CoRoT 26, Hubble 2, CHEOPS 1). Likewise "ground surveys the rest" under Data: 42 of 466 are space telescopes. - Ground surveys found almost nothing but hot Jupiters (416 of 466), so there is no baseline there. - Of the companions of the 27 hot Jupiters with company, 18 came from radial velocity, 10 from transit and 1 from transit timing. "Company" means different things by route. - Only Kepler/K2 could see small neighbours: 74 of 77 (interval 89–99%). - Baseline, which strengthens the claim: Kepler/K2 planets under 4 Earth radii and under 10 days are alone 51% of the time, giants beyond 10 days 46%; TESS 59% and 75%. - Robust to definition (96–98% for 6 to 12 Earth radii, 5 or 10 days). - The headline contradicts the README's own "does not show that hot Jupiters have no neighbours". - Should say: "Hot Jupiters are listed alone: 96% overall. Among Kepler/K2's, 74 of 77, against 51% for its small planets at the same periods."

7. "Pairs pile up just outside resonances … at two to one it is 35 against 6." - Found: robust in count, but the two resonances show different things, and the early catalogue is selected toward resonance. - Evidence: - Window widths from 0.01 to 0.08: outside/inside ratio 2.5–11 at 3:2 and 2.2–8.3 at 2:1. - Not supplied by a few systems: 51 systems give the 60 pairs, 35 give the 35. One pair per system: 24 against 7 and 20 against 4. Systems of two or three planets: 30 against 7 and 23 against 4. TESS alone: 11 against 2 and 12 against 3. System bootstrap passes in 100% of resamples. - The sealed bars were low: the same test passes at 4 of 25 (3:2 rule) and 6 of 25 (2:1 rule) non-resonant centres. The observed ratios exceed every placebo. - Against the flanks, 3:2 is a spike outside (60 where 16 expected; inside 14 where 16 expected). 2:1 is mainly a hole inside (6 where 25 expected), with a modest excess outside (35 where 25 expected, p = 0.04). - Pairs complete by 2013 are within 0.05 of 1.5 or 2.0 in 37% of cases, against 11% for 2014–16. My explanation, from memory and not testable in this table: early Kepler pairs were confirmed by timing variations, which favours resonance. In the 2014–16 batch, 3:2 is 17 against 8. - Should say: "At 3:2 pairs pile up just outside (60 where about 16 would be expected); at 2:1 the contrast is mostly a hole just inside (6 where about 25). Both hold in TESS alone; the 3:2 excess is weaker (17 against 8) among the pairs validated in bulk in 2014–16."

8. "Hot Jupiters' stars are richer in metals, by 0.11 dex." - Found: did not break on average, but it is not uniform and not specific to hot Jupiters. - Evidence: - Per star 0.110; [Fe/H] only 0.116; exact zeros out 0.120; Teff-stratified 0.114; regression on Teff 0.112. - By route: 0.14 (others), 0.12 (Kepler/K2, interval 0.086–0.157), 0.20 (TESS). - By Teff: 0.31 under 4,500 K, about 0.10–0.13 from 4,500 to 6,000 K, 0.07 at 6,000–6,500 K, 0.01 ± 0.03 above 6,500 K. - [M/H]-only subset: 0.02 ± 0.04, on 22 hot Jupiters. - Giants beyond 10 days show +0.10 ± 0.012 and 4–8 Earth radii +0.08. - Should add: "…as are the stars of giants further out (+0.10); the difference fades above 6,000 K."

9. Sentences that turn a list count into a statement about planets. The bold leads "Pairs pile up", "Hot Jupiters are alone", "Hot Neptunes are scarce" and "Neighbours are alike" are written about planets, while "What this does not show" disclaims three of them. Each should carry "listed" or "in the catalogue".

The fetched columns cannot say which. Those subsets show no different verdict on X1 (66, 58, 107 and 46, 47, 60). - There is a mild heap at exactly 2.00 (item 3). - Metallicity: 202 exact zeros (4.2%, mild heap) and 311 one-decimal values, with no effect on X2. - pl_bmasse is filled for 4,776 of 4,800 planets, so it is largely computed; the study rightly did not use it.

Settled details. - Controversial planets: 7 rows. - Transit-only neighbours: 7 pairs have a non-transit planet between them, and the resonance counts are unchanged without them. - Lower-edge-inclusive bins: X1 becomes 525, 585, 768 the other way, still failed. - None of these changes a verdict. Only "zeros included" decides one.

4. What I verified and how

  • Blind: blind.py (pandas, written before opening anything but expectations.md) gives all twelve quantities and verdicts in blind.out. Every one matches.
  • Data: the file's sha256 matches data.sha256.
  • Timing: expectations.md was written at 15:53:13 and the data file at 15:53:31; the forecasts at 16:04:08, 16:04:14 and 16:05:20; transits.out at 16:06:29, which is its last run.
  • Forecast sheet: sheet.md and forecast/sheet.md are identical. The sheet carries no "zeros included" rule, so the forecasters priced X10 without it.
  • Code: I read transits.py and score.py line by line and found no error that changes a number. X4 is tested with inclusive bounds, which is immaterial.
  • Controls: controls.py and controls2.py, with outputs in controls.out and controls2.out, cover the catalogue's values, the resonances, the hot-Jupiter baseline, metallicity, hot Neptunes, neighbours and the size gap, as set out in section 2.
  • Brier: brier_check.py recomputes the scores from the raw forecast files; the 34-row ledger is recomputed in the controls, including the X10 sensitivity and per-item differences.

5. What I did not check

  • The hash of expectations.md on the shared board, and the first-run time of 16:05:33 (file times show only the last run).
  • The forecasters' tool-call logs behind forecast/audit.out.
  • That the download is what the archive serves (no network).
  • Which sizes and metallicities are computed rather than measured: the reference and flag columns were not fetched, so the decimals and missing-error counts are proxies.
  • The shared error in neighbours' sizes from one stellar radius (no st_rad errors in the table).
  • The star's noise or brightness as a detection control (not in the table); my "detection floor" is a crude percentile proxy.
  • The timing-variation explanation for the pre-2014 resonance excess, which is from memory.
  • The ledger's earlier outcomes for the birth-months and battles studies; I took them as recorded.
  • figures.py beyond reading it and viewing the image.
  • results.json against transits.out.
