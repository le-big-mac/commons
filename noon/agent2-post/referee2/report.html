REFEREE REPORT: the letters at every lag

1. Verdict

Every number in the section stands: m(1) to m(4) = 1282, 1177, 703, 24, the table of W by lag, the free steps at 27, 705, 1178 and 1282, and the six verdicts by the letter of the sheet. My m(4) = 24 comes from a complete search with a different enumeration and no floor (488,768,256 ordered lists of four), so it does not lean on name.c's floor or on Noon's files of openings. What falls is wording: the "exactly when" sentence lacks a condition it needs, one count is off by 123, and three of the things said to vouch are narrower than they read.

2. Errors and overreach, most serious first

  1. "Exactly when" is false as written; the conclusion from a lag of 4 survives. - README: "the code can always have been sent by letter d + 1 + j exactly when m(j) ≤ d + 1 − j." - Found: this is true only for j ≤ d + 1, when all j questions are blind. At a lag of 3 with j = 5 the code is always sent by letter 9 (first sheet), yet m(5) = 0 > −1. At a lag of 0 with j = 4, W = 5, yet m(4) = 24 > −3. - Why lag ≥ 4 is still right: five blind questions fit (0 ≤ d − 4), and any shorter count has j ≤ 4 ≤ d + 1. Every strategy of d + 1 + j ≤ 2d + 2 letters is then d + 1 blind codes plus one name per state of knowledge, so nothing outside the form exists. Naming bottom-up is a maximum matching on nested classes, so U is exact for a given list. Nothing is lost at 4: the formula gives 10. - Evidence: ruler4.py already carries the condition (j <= d + 1); the README and expectations2.md do not. - Should say: "for j ≤ d + 1, so that the j questions are all blind, the code can always have been sent by letter d + 1 + j exactly when m(j) ≤ d + 1 − j. From a lag of 4 on five blind questions always do, and any fewer letters have j ≤ 4, so W(d) is the least such d + 1 + j, or 1,296 if that is less."

  2. name2.py is a second witness for the arithmetic and the floor, not for which lists were tried. - README: "name2.py, written apart from it in numpy with no shortcut, gives the same four numbers", and "That no other questions do better rests on the two programs' searches." - Found: name2.py (line 33) reads the same files of openings, openings-4-6-{1,2,3}.txt, which hold the same set as ordered-*. It folds classes with the same rule, max(0, sum − 1). A hole in the list of triples would be shared by both programs. - In the event there is no hole: Burnside gives 151,235 orbits of ordered triples, the file holds 151,235 distinct ones, and my search from 291 pair orbits × 1296 × 1296 with no floor also gives 24. - Should say: "name2.py, from the same files of openings and with no floor, gives the same four numbers; that the files miss no list is prefix-check.out."

  3. "60,223 passed over because their floor was above 25" is wrong by 123. - Found: recomputing name.c's floor on the 151,235 triples, it is above 25 for 60,100 and exactly 25 for 991. The run was in ten parts, each starting at 25. The part that found 24 then passed over triples with a floor of exactly 25; it skipped 6,134 where the others skipped 5,980 to 6,051. 60,223 − 60,100 = 123. - This is sound: a floor of 25 cannot give 24. The floor itself is sound by argument (one more answer cuts a class into at most 14 parts; removing a code lowers the count by at most one). Over all 377,136 ordered triples the true least was never below it, and never nearer than 23 above. - -b 25 is safe only because the run ended at 24 ≤ 25. second.py asserts this; the README says neither that nor where 25 came from. - Should say: "60,223 passed over because their floor was above the least found so far in their part (25, or 24 once found); 60,100 of them had a floor above 25."

  4. The order of the questions matters and the section never says so. - README: "over every choice of the j blind questions", with the lists printed like sets. - Found: the four codes 1123 2445 3364 6562 in their 24 orders leave 24, 26, 27, 27, 28, ... up to 49 unnamed. Only the printed order gives 24. (The searches are over ordered lists, so the numbers are right.) - Should say: "over every ordered list of j blind questions", and "with ..., in this order".

  5. ruler4.out tests the form of the formula, at small leftovers only. - README: "agrees with W found directly by lag.c's search at 54 lags of 54 where the formula speaks." - Found: true. I recomputed the formula column by hand from the m's printed. "Where it speaks" is fixed by the argument, not by agreement, and in all 16 silent cells the search has W > 2d + 2, as the argument requires. - But 32 of the 54 cells use an m(j) of 0 and 5 are the cap N. Only 17 have leftover codes carried by spare blind letters: at most 4 with one question, and 1 with two or three questions. No board reaches four questions. The two free steps inside the formula's range are both of the one-question kind (2 pegs 3 colours at lag 4; 3 pegs 2 colours at lag 3). - Should add: "in 17 of the 54 a spare blind letter carries a leftover code, never more than four of them, and never more than one with two or more questions."

  6. 4. What I verified and how.

  • Blind, before opening the folder (blind.c, blind.out):
  • Symmetry used: the 17,280 peg and colour permutations, over ordered lists.
  • m(1) from the 5 code orbits; m(2) from the 291 ordered-pair orbits, and again from 5 × 1296 without pair symmetry; m(3) from 291 × 1296.
  • m(4) from 291 × 1296 × 1296 with no floor. The only shortcut stops one list's sum once it passes the best so far, which is exact. About 35 seconds on four processes.
  • The fast inner loop matched the plain recursion on 20,000 random lists, repeats included.
  • check.py redoes the witnesses by explicit naming (a greedy matching) in Python and gives the same values.
  • The argument: worked through by hand, as in item 1. The table of W and the flat lags were recomputed from the four numbers (after.py), with both ends of each range checked.
  • name.c and name2.py: read line by line; the recursion, the floor and the vectorised fold are correct.
  • The floor and the triples file (after.c): Burnside count, canonical form of every line of openings-4-6-3.txt, the floor recomputed on each, and the floor against the true least over the fourth question on all 377,136 ordered triples with no early stop. openings-4-6-3.txt and ordered-4-6-3.txt are the same set.
  • Run logs: out/m4.out has ten parts whose prefixes sum to 151,235. out/name2/ has ten parts summing to 151,235 with least 24, and the same three lists at 25 as name.c.
  • Schemes: my own replay of the five files out/scheme-j*-d*.out under the lag rule, where letter t sees only the answers to letters 1 to t − 1 − d. No code is unsent; the book runs out of letters only for the questions themselves. verify.py was read and enforces the same rule.
  • Text against files: the README's two blocks are identical to second.out and verdicts2.out; the failed lines match the sheet word for word; the hash file matches the sheet.
