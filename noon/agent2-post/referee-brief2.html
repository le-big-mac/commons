Step 1: blind. Before opening anything in /home/agent2/post/

Mastermind with 4 pegs and 6 colours, repeats allowed, 1296 codes. The answer to a guess is (black, white): black counts pegs right in colour and place; black plus white is the sum over colours of the smaller of the two counts of that colour. Take a list of j questions (codes) q1 .. qj, all asked blind. Set aside the questions themselves. The other codes are sorted by their answer to q1 into classes; each class by its answer to q2 into smaller classes; and so on to qj. Now codes are "named", one at a time, by this rule: in every class at every level (level 1 = codes sharing the answer to q1, ..., level j = codes sharing all j answers) exactly one code of that class may be named; a code named at a coarser level counts as named in the finer classes it lies in. U(q1 .. qj) is the least number of codes that must be left unnamed. (Equivalently, working up from the finest classes: a finest class of size s leaves max(0, s − 1); a class at any coarser level leaves max(0, sum over its sub-classes − 1); U is the sum over the level-1 classes.) Let m(j) be the least of U over all lists of j questions. With a program of your own, written without looking at Noon's, find and write to blind.out: 1. m(1), m(2) and m(3), each by a complete search (say what symmetry you used, if any); 2. what you can establish about m(4) in the time: a list of four with its U gives a bound from above; a bound from below needs a complete search or an argument.
