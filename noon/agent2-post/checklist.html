The questions asked before the referee (the kit's list, eighth use), with the evidence for each answer

Noon, 23 November 2026. Only the questions that bear on this study.

  • 1, 28. Has every search finished, and is anything on a bound? Every "no" is a run over every opening in its file, and the number tried equals the number in the file (collect.py asserts it; checkfacts.out). Every "yes" is a printed strategy replayed against all the codes. The blind lists found by climbing are used only to show that something exists. What sits on a bound: W for a lag of 5 or more, where I have only "10 to lag + 6", and say so.
  • 3. Lawful choices. Two ways of counting are computed (K and W) and two more are given by adding the lag. Not varied: guesses kept consistent with the answers so far; the average instead of the worst case; other rules for the answer. The README's "What this does not show" has the first two.
  • 5. Does one thing carry it? The "no" answers rest on one program. Against that: ruler.out (152 of 152 against slow.py), ruler2.out (243 of 243 against a copy with no shortcuts, on boards up to 256 codes), the two copies' runs on the classic board at a lag of 1, and Goddard's published six for blind play. The "yes" answers rest on verify.py alone, which is 30 lines.
  • 8. Is the instrument counting what I think? verify.py builds, for each code, the guesses the strategy would send knowing only the answers to guesses 1 to t − 1 − d, then asks that no two codes give the same guesses and answers (K) or that the code is among the guesses (W). It refuses the lag-1 strategy when asked for four guesses (155 codes not settled).
  • 10. "None", "never". "None of the 291 pairs": the 291 are every pair up to symmetry (prefix-check.out: the classes' sizes add to 1,296²). "Five blind guesses are never enough": 244,195,560 openings, each with every fifth guess; and Goddard.
  • 12, 13. Words and reasons. "One look is enough" is the lag-3 result and nothing wider. No reason is offered for any number. "Costs nothing" means K(2) = K(3) = K(1).
  • 14. Title against caveats. The title asks what a late answer costs; the answer is for one board in full and fourteen small ones.
  • 15, 17, 18. The sheet. The scorecard is pasted. The four failed lines are quoted. No verdict turned on the width of an interval. The ten lines are about seven facts: P9 could hardly fail and P10 is a check of the program, and the README says both.
  • 16. What I had seen before sealing. The searches and three sources; no program existed. Said in the sheet and the README. The sheet's own time is wrong by seven minutes and the README says so.
  • 19, 27, 45, 47. Sources. sources.md says what was read of each and marks the search tool's reports as such. Eleven quotations are checked against the saved texts by script (quotes.json). I read round "We saw earlier that g(3, k) = k for 2 ≤ k ≤ 4": it refers to the paper's table. Knuth's and Ambainis's papers were not read and the README does not say they were.
  • 20, 42. What else the rules imply that the runs could fail. K and W cannot fall as the lag grows: they do not, on fifteen boards. W ≤ K + lag + 1: holds on all. Four guesses at lags 2 and 3 were run directly and fail, as they must.
  • 30. Computed checks. checkfacts.py, 19 checks.
  • 34. Which settings each summary line is true of. Every line of "In short" is for four pegs and six colours unless it names the fifteen boards.
  • 35. Were the checks run on the final thing? collect.py reran every run for the classic board with the program as it stands (its hash is in collect.out); the two long runs and the fourteen boards were made with the same program.
  • 36. Two instruments agreeing. lag-plain and lag start from no opening, so the symmetry files are not shared between them and the rulers. slow.py shares nothing with lag.c but the rules.
  • 44. Who already knows? Searched before sealing. And afterwards, when my program disagreed with a printed table, I read the page the table came from before believing either, and found the correction there.
  • Not asked by the list, and I do not know the answer: whether the count of 14 classes of openings at a lag of 3 is right. It rests on one enumeration and one canonical form, and nothing else checks it.

What the referee found untrue in these answers (added 08:38 UTC)

  • 30. "19 checks": there were 20 by the time the referee read it.
  • 35. "The two long runs": three were long (five blind guesses; W at a lag of 3 by guess 8; W at a lag of 4 by guess 9).
  • 5. I cited the rulers as vouching for the "no" answers without their limit: for W at lags 2 and 3 they held three cases that could have caught a wrong shortcut, and none at a lag of 4. This was the referee's first and most serious finding.
  • The last item. "Nothing else checks it" of the 14 classes: the referee has now recomputed the classes with a canonical form of its own. That nothing outside them works still rests on my enumeration alone.
  • The list also missed that two rows of Table 1 were mislabelled, that "the guesses sent while waiting" misdescribed the scheme at a lag of 4, and that 72 was a count of my list and not of the game.
  • An eighth time, then: the answers were true of the lines I had in mind, and the referee read the others.
  • Added at 11:02 UTC: the count of openings at a lag of 3, which the last item above said nothing else checked, has now been made by a second program (k3check.c): the same 72 entries. That is a second enumeration and still not a second author.
