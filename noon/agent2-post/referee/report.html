REFEREE REPORT: Mastermind by post

1. Verdict

Every number for the classic board that I tested is right: all nine strategy files replay, and five of the "no" rows fall to programs of my own, written without Noon's. lag.c's three shortcuts and its symmetry reduction are sound as far as I can find by reading and by testing. The faults are in the prose: the two rulers vouch for the W shortcuts far less than "How" says, Table 1 mislabels two rows, and three "In short" sentences say a little more than the numbers.

2. Errors and overreach, most serious first

  1. The rulers barely test the W shortcuts where the W "no" rows live. - README: "What vouches for the program" is ruler.out, "every lag from 0 to 3 and every length", 152 of 152, and ruler2.out, 243 of 243. - Found: a shortcut can only err towards "no", so only a case whose true answer is "yes" can expose it. For W at lags 2 and 3 the two rulers hold three such cases in all (ruler.out: 1 and 1; ruler2.out: 1 and 0), against 93 "no/no". Neither ruler reaches lag 4, where the W(4) > 9 row sits. - "Every length" is lengths 1 to 4 (1 to 3 on the 27-code board), below the W threshold on most boards at lags 2 and 3. - lag-plain.c is lag.c with lines cut out, so it shares the lag bookkeeping; only slow.py is independent of it. - My tests found nothing wrong: a copy of lag.c against my plain ruler.c on nine boards, lags 0 to 5, up to 9 guesses, agreed in 681 of 681 settled cases (21 timed out on my side). That includes W "yes" cases: 42, 36, 25 and 16 at lags 2, 3, 4 and 5. - Should say: "the rulers test W above its threshold in three cases at lags 2 and 3 and not at all at lag 4", and either extend them or cite a check that does.

  2. Table 1 mislabels two rows. - README: "W | 5 and more | 11 | works" and "W | 5 and more | 12 | works". - Found: these are the lag-5 and lag-6 runs (collect.out); tables.py prints any lag of 5 or more as "5 and more". As labelled, the first row claims 11 guesses suffice at every lag from 5 up, which nothing shows for lag 6 or more. - Should say: lag 5 and lag 6.

  3. "the guesses sent while waiting save nothing … At a lag of 4 they save one." - Found: the counts are right (W = K + lag + 1 at lags 0 to 3, one less at lag 4). But the lag-4 scheme has no waiting guesses: its blind guesses are 1123, 2445, 2562, 3356, 1166, and guesses 6 to 10 each name a code as answers 1 to 5 arrive. The saving comes from naming codes before the code is fixed. - "Save nothing" is also a worst-case statement only. - Should say: "At lags 0 to 3 nothing beats learn, wait, send in the worst case; at a lag of 4 naming codes as the answers come in beats it by one."

  4. "every number here is from a search of every opening, or a strategy replayed." - Found: two "In short" numbers are neither. "Lag + 6 guesses are always enough" from lag 4 on is replayed at lags 4, 5 and 6 and holds beyond by argument. The argument is sound: the five-level naming count on those five blind guesses is 0 (control.py). The "10" in "10 to lag + 6" is by monotonicity. - The preamble also scopes "In short" to four pegs and six colours, while two bullets are about other boards. - Should say so.

  5. "72 of those openings work." - Found: 72 and 14 both reproduce. But 72 is a count in this particular enumeration (ordered pair, then an unordered further pair), in which each class appears several times; only 14 is a property of the game. - Should say: "14 classes (72 entries of my list)".

  6. "The next two cost nothing." - Found: true of K only. Under the lag rule the last d answers arrive only if d more letters are sent, so "once all their answers are in" hides d letters; in letters every step of lag costs at least one. The README says this two bullets later and in "The game, exactly". - Should say in the bold line: "cost nothing in questions".

  7. checklist.md slips. - Item 30 says 19 checks; checkfacts.out has 20. - Item 35 and collect.py's docstring say "the two long runs"; three are flagged long (K lag 4 in 5, W lag 3 in 8, W lag 4 in 9). - Item 5 cites the rulers without the limit in finding 1. - Its last item says nothing else checks the 14 classes; now something does (section 4). - The checklist missed findings 1, 2, 3 and 5.

  8. Credit, small. Goddard's table marks 4 pegs and 6 colours "6◦": an upper bound from Bogomolny and Greenwell with Goddard's matching lower bound. "Five blind guesses are never enough: that is Goddard's result" is exact; "blind play (Goddard, 2003)" for the whole value is slightly generous.

The Brier score and mean odds recompute. - Corrected entry. 112, 133, 423 give 64 distinct answer rows; no two blind guesses do better than a largest class of 4. 4. What I verified and how

  • Blind (blind.out, before opening Noon's files). The brief's file names had already told me which way Noon answers; my programs did not use that.
  • blind.c searched all 291 openings for lag 1 with four guesses (K) and with six (W), about 10 seconds each.
  • q2.c found lag-3 witnesses by annealing; q2check.py confirmed them from the definition.
  • Rulers for my own "no" programs. ruler.c, a plain recursion from the definitions, agrees with blind.c on four small boards and on 2 pegs 8 colours, the only one I found where both answers are "no".
  • Strategies. replay.py, my own replayer, checks that every line carries exactly max(0, t − 1 − d) answers and replays all 1,296 codes. It refuses four wrong claims.
  • lag.c, read line by line. I found each shortcut sound:
  • capK: codes with the same pending answers follow one tree of depth r, so at most 14^r leaves.
  • lastK: an exact test of one guess against every pair in a class.
  • capW and the cover count: each state of knowledge names one code.
  • unmetW: a true lower bound, since swapping a real guess for a placeholder leaves at most one more code unnamed.
  • Possible-codes-only: applied only when r ≤ d + 1, when no answer can still be used.
  • prefix.py: one representative per orbit, then orbits of its stabiliser. The ordered opening files are permutations of the checked ones.
  • lag.c, tested. The 681-case comparison in finding 1 (cmp.out).
  • W "no" rows on the classic board. wquad.c and wlag2.c use a nested count I derived by hand, with a positive control in control.py.
  • The 14 classes. Recomputed with my own canonical form (facts.py), then tested for completeness with my six witnesses (classes.py).
  • Sources. I read the saved Goddard layout and the page text for the table, the star and the correction.
  • Timestamps. The three long runs postdate lag.c's last edit.
