Expectations for hawks and doves on a lattice, written at 16:34:50 UTC on 22 November, before any code was written

Question. In the hawk-dove game (also called the snowdrift game) a dove shares and a hawk takes. In a well-mixed crowd the two settle at a fixed mixture. The orthodox expectation, from the prisoner's dilemma, is that when players meet only their neighbours on a lattice the sharing kind does better, because it clusters. I remember a paper (Hauert and Doebeli, 2004) saying that for this game the lattice often does the opposite. I have looked nothing up. (From the words "hawk" and "orthodoxy".)

The model, fixed now. Payoff to the row player: dove against dove 1; dove against hawk 1 - r; hawk against dove 1 + r; hawk against hawk 0; with 0 < r < 1. Well mixed, the share of doves at rest is 1 - r. Lattice: 200 x 200 sites, edges joined round, each site a hawk or a dove; neighbours are the 4 nearest (and in one line the 8 nearest). A site's payoff is the sum of its games with all its neighbours. Updating, one site at a time: choose a site x at random and one of its neighbours y at random; if y's payoff is higher, x takes y's kind with probability (payoff of y - payoff of x) / (k (1 + r)), k being the number of neighbours. One generation is 40,000 such choices. Start: each site a dove with probability one half. Run 15,000 generations. "The dove share" is the mean over generations 10,001 to 15,000, read every 50; "at the end" is generation 15,000. The grid of r is 0.02, 0.04, ..., 0.98. The well-mixed control: the same updating, but x and y are any two sites and each one's payoff is from k opponents drawn afresh at random from the whole lattice.

Worked by hand beforehand, with four neighbours. A dove with n dove neighbours earns 4 - 4r + n r; a hawk with m dove neighbours earns m (1 + r). Two things follow that are certain and are not on the sheet: the last hawk can never be lost (alone among doves it earns most), and the last dove can be lost only if r > 0.6. A third is my reason for H2: a hawk with a hawk for a neighbour earns at most 3 + 3r, less than the 4 - r of a dove beside it when r < 1/4, so below 1/4 hawks cannot build on one another; they wander singly, sometimes as a pair, and merge when they meet.

"Between" includes its ends. Each line says whether it is worked, remembered or guessed.
