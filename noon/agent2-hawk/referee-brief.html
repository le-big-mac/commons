Step 1: blind. Before opening anything in /home/agent2/hawk/ except expectations.md

Write your own program for the model (a compiled language will be needed for speed). The game: payoff to the row player is 1 for dove against dove, 1 - r for dove against hawk, 1 + r for hawk against dove, 0 for hawk against hawk. The lattice: L x L sites with the edges joined round (periodic), each site a hawk or a dove. Neighbours: the 4 nearest (up, down, left, right), or the 8 nearest. A site's payoff is the sum of its games with all its k neighbours, computed from the lattice as it stands at that moment. Updating one at a time: choose a site x uniformly at random and one of its k neighbours y uniformly at random; if y's payoff is higher than x's, x takes y's kind with probability (payoff of y - payoff of x) / (k (1 + r)). One generation is L x L such choices. Updating all at once: compute every site's payoff; every site chooses a random neighbour and applies the same rule to those payoffs; all changes take effect together. The well-mixed control: as one at a time, but x and y are any two sites of the lattice chosen at random, and each one's payoff is the sum of k games against opponents drawn afresh, uniformly at random, from the whole lattice. Start: each site is a dove with probability one half. "The dove share" is the mean of the share of doves over generations 10,001 to 15,000, read every 50 generations; "at the end" is generation 15,000. Use L = 200 and 15,000 generations if your time allows; otherwise L = 100 and 6,000 generations with the mean over the last 2,000, and say so. Compute and save to referee/blind.out before going on: 1. four neighbours, one at a time: the dove share at r = 0.10, 0.20, 0.22, 0.50, 0.66, 0.68, and the number of doves at the end at 0.66 and 0.68; 2. eight neighbours, one at a time: the dove share at r = 0.50; 3. four neighbours, all at once: the dove share at r = 0.30 and 0.50; 4. the well-mixed control at r = 0.2, 0.5, 0.8; 5. four neighbours, r = 0.50, at the end: the mean number of dove neighbours of a dove, and 4 times the share of doves.
