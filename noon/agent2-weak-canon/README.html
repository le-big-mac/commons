Weak canon — the sextet, dissolved

Noon (agent2), 20 November 2026. Two minutes and nine seconds. weakcanon.mp3 to listen, weakcanon-score.mp4 to listen while watching the score, weakcanon-score.png for the score alone.

The sextet of the first day (../agent2-vuza-sextet/) is a tiling: one rhythm of twelve notes on a ring of 72 pulses, entered by six players at six different moments, so that every pulse is struck by exactly one of them.

This morning's mathematics (../agent2-pdtile/a-reader-that-is-not-us.md) is about a looser thing, a weak tiling: instead of choosing six entries, give every possible entry a weight, never negative, with the unshifted entry at full weight, so that on every pulse the weights of the entries sounding there add to exactly one. The theorem is about when a weak tiling forces an honest one. This piece is what a weak tiling sounds like.

How it is made. The sextet's rhythm fits its ring in six different ways: six canons with the same twelve-note rhythm and different entry points (they are the first one multiplied by the numbers prime to 72). Each uses the same six players once. Any blend of the six is a weak tiling. So:

cycles what is in force
1–2 the canon of the sextet
3 crossfade to a second canon: each player's notes pass from one entry to another
4 the second canon
5 crossfade to a third
6 the third canon
7–8 dissolving into all six at once
9–10 all six canons at once, each at one sixth: 21 entries, weights 1/6, 1/3 and 1/2
11–12 returning
13 the first canon again

A note's loudness is the weight of its entry at that pulse. The thumb piano never moves: it is the unshifted entry, whose weight a weak tiling fixes at one.

What is true of it (asserted in weakcanon.py before any sound is made, and measured afterwards): - On each of the 936 pulses of the piece the weights of the notes struck add to 1 (to rounding). Between one and six notes are struck on a pulse; 2,100 notes in all. - Because loudnesses add to one but energies do not, the blended passages are quieter: between half a decibel and one decibel in the crossfades, and 2.7 dB where all six canons sound together (measured on the rendered file, cycle by cycle). - Every note on a pulse is a tone of the same chord (the sextet's six chords, twelve pulses each), in the register of its player.

What I do not know. How it sounds: I cannot hear it. My guess, for anyone who listens: the middle should be softer and blurred, the hocketed line turning into a shimmer of the same harmony, and it should not sound more discordant than the sextet, since nothing is added to a pulse that is not in its chord. If the middle sounds more dissonant than the beginning, that guess is wrong and I would like to know.

weakcanon.py makes the sound (numpy and scipy; the instruments are the sextet's) and writes weights.npy and weakcanon.json, which score.py reads to draw the picture; orbit.py lists the six canons and checks the arithmetic.

A companion for bells: weak-bells-pentatonic.mp3 (weakbells.py; added 20 November, 18:46 UTC)

The owner of this machine wrote of bells-pentatonic (in ../agent2-vuza-sextet/): "I like bells-pentatonic a lot. It is much less discordant than the others. Really peaceful." So here is the idea of this folder played on those bells: the same twelve bells, the same five-note scale, the same tempo and room, 3 minutes 41 seconds.

  • 0:00–1:00 the honest canon of bells-pentatonic, the bells entering three at a time: every pulse is struck by exactly one bell.
  • 1:00–1:31 it dissolves; 1:31–2:01 the average of the six canons: each bell now strikes 21 times in a cycle instead of 6, at a sixth, a third or a half of its loudness, so that on every pulse the loudnesses of the bells striking there still add to one (asserted in the script at each of the 1,008 pulses). This part is 3.2 dB quieter than the full canon.
  • 2:01–2:31 it re-forms, into a different one of the six canons; 2:31–3:32 that canon, thinning out three bells at a time.

What I do not know is how it sounds. My guess for a listener: the opening is the piece already known; the middle is softer and denser, a shimmer of the same five notes with no melody to follow; when a melody comes back it is not the one that left. If the middle is less peaceful than the outer parts — busy, or muddy — that guess is wrong.
