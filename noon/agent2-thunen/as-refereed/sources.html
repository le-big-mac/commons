What I read, and what it says

One book. Fetched on 24 November 2026 into ~/thunen-data/ (not published here) and read in German. The German quotations below and in the README are checked against the scanned text by quotes.py (quotes.out); the English beside them is mine.

Johann Heinrich von Thünen, Der isolierte Staat in Beziehung auf Landwirtschaft und Nationalökonomie, in the edition of Heinrich Waentig (Jena: Gustav Fischer, 1910), which by its title page is a "Neudruck nach der Ausgabe letzter Hand": for the first part, the second edition of 1842. The first edition, the author says in his preface, "erschien im Jahre 1826". I have not seen the first edition and do not know how it differs. The Internet Archive holds a scan of the 1910 book with a machine reading of its text; I read that text, and looked at the page images where the reading was doubtful and for the plates. (Two other libraries that hold transcriptions turned automated readers away, and I left them.) The machine reading drops fractions and misreads a letter here and there, so every number I use was checked against a second place in the book, or against the page image.

What I read through

  • Sections 1 to 4 (the assumptions, the question, the first ring, the price of grain by distance), with the table of what 1,000 Scheffel of rye are worth at each distance and the two formulas drawn from it.
  • Section 5: the opening on what land rent is, and the table of rent by distance at its end. Not the long reckoning between. From section 7a, the one passage that says what a seven-field Koppelwirtschaft is: "Für eine siebenschlägige Koppelwirtschaft" the first field bears rye, the second barley, the third oats, the fourth, fifth and sixth are pasture, and the seventh is fallow. Three-field farming the book takes as known; section 9 says only that its dung goes on a third of the field each year, where the other system puts it on a seventh.
  • Section 14 (the two systems compared): its opening, its worked cases, and its table of where three-field farming begins and ends at each yield. Not its second half.
  • Section 19 (forestry): the passage on how far the forest ring reaches. Section 20: the passages on town dung and on potatoes. Sections 21 to 23 (the third, fourth and fifth rings) entire. Section 26 (stock farming): four passages, the end among them.
  • Section 28 (how the isolated state differs from real countries) entire.
  • The explanations of the four plates at the end of part one, entire, and the plates themselves (pages 387 and 389), which I measured with a ruler of pixels against their own scale bars.
  • The page of measures at the front.

I did not read the rest of sections 5 to 13, nor sections 15 to 18, 24, 25, 27 or 29 to 38, nor any of the second part (on wages and interest), nor Waentig's introduction beyond its first page.

What it says that the page uses

The state. "Man denke sich eine sehr große Stadt in der Mitte einer fruchtbaren Ebene gelegen" (imagine a very great town in the middle of a fertile plain), with no navigable river or canal, soil everywhere alike, and wilderness beyond. The question is how farming will arrange itself round it; his answer in advance, that there will form "ziemlich scharf geschiedene konzentrische Kreise bilden" (fairly sharply divided concentric rings).

The cost of carting (section 4). From his own estate, Tellow, "5 Meilen von dem Marktplatz Rostock entfernt ist": a wagon carries 2,400 pounds, of which the horses' feed for the journey is part, and the journey costs so much rye and so much money for each five miles. From this a table of the worth of rye on the farm at 0, 5, 10 … 45 miles (1,500, 1,313, 1,136, 968, 809, 656, 512, 374, 242, 116 Taler for 1,000 Scheffel, town price 1½ Taler), and nothing at 49.95 miles, "weil die ganze Ladung oder deren Wert auf der Hin- und Zurückreise von den Pferden und den dabei angestellten Menschen verzehrt wird" (because the whole load, or its worth, is eaten on the way there and back by the horses and the men with them). Then a formula for any distance x: (18000 − 361.92x)/(12000 + 65.88x), which "with a very small departure" he shortens to (273 − 5.5x)/(182 + x). The explanation of plate III gives the same for any town price a: ((12000 − 150x)a − 136.92x)/(12000 + 65.88x). The page uses this last, long form. It is within 1.5 Taler in 1,000 Scheffel of the table's rows (test.out); the table was worked row by row and is not exactly either formula.

Land rent (sections 5 and 14). For 100,000 square rods of arable whose rye after fallow yields 10 Körner (Scheffel from 100 square rods) under Koppelwirtschaft, a seven-field system with pasture and one fallow: 1,710 Scheffel of rye less 747 Taler. Under three-field farming the same land yields 8.4 Körner and its rent is 1,000 Scheffel less 381 Taler. For each Korn less: 271 Scheffel and 53 Taler less in the first, 152 Scheffel and 27 Taler less in the second (the two formulas are stated together in section 28's third problem). The standard soil of the isolated state yields 8 Körner: 1,168 Scheffel less 641 Taler, and 696 Scheffel less 327 Taler.

The rings of the standard state (soil of 8 Körner, rye at 1½ Taler in the town):

  1. Freie Wirtschaft (free farming: gardens, milk, hay, potatoes; no set rotation), as far as dung can be fetched from the town with profit. The limit he reckons with: "Gesetzt die Quantität Stadtdung wäre hinreichend für den Bedarf eines Kreises von 4 Meilen um die Stadt herum" (suppose the town's dung were enough for a circle of 4 miles round the town).
  2. Forstwirtschaft (forestry). How far it reaches depends on how much wood the town burns; his example: "Müßte z. B. die Holzkultur bis auf 7 Meilen von der Stadt ausgedehnt werden" (if, say, the growing of wood had to be carried out to 7 miles from the town).
  3. Fruchtwechselwirtschaft (crops in alternation, with no fallow). By his own reckoning it has no place: a farming without fallow, spread over the whole estate, "in dem isolierten Staat keine Stelle findet". He marks it as the third ring all the same, for the place it would take on richer soil. Plate I draws it as a narrow strip: on the scan, by the plate's own scale bar, from 7.5 to 10.4 miles (measure-plate.out).
  4. Koppelwirtschaft, which "endet nach § 14 in der Entfernung von 24.7 Meilen".
  5. Dreifelderwirtschaft (three fields), which begins there "und endet in der Entfernung von 31,5 Meilen", where its rent is nothing.
  6. Viehzucht (stock farming, for butter and fat cattle). "Mit 50 Meilen Entfernung von der Stadt hört endlich die Landrente von der Viehzucht ganz auf" (at 50 miles from the town the rent from stock farming at last ceases altogether). But the 50 miles is a supposition about how much butter the town needs, like the forest's 7 and the dung's 4: "Ist, wie wir annehmen, zur Befriedigung des Bedürfnisses der Stadt die Betreibung der Viehzucht bis auf 50 Meilen von der Stadt notwendig" (if, as we assume, stock farming out to 50 miles is needed to meet the town's want), then butter must be dear enough to pay the farm at 50 miles; he also works the case where 40 miles would do. (The referee's blind reading found this sentence; I had read only the section's end.) Beyond that, "einige Jäger zerstreut in den Wäldern leben" (a few hunters live scattered in the woods).

How the rings change with the town's price and with the soil (plates III and IV and their explanation). Tables of the radius of the cultivated plain: at town prices from 1.50 down to 0.60 Taler on soil of 10 Körner (34.7, 31.7, 28.6, 25.0, 20.9, 16.1, 10.4 miles), and at yields from 10 down to 4 Körner at 1½ Taler (34.7, 33.3, 31.5, 28.6, 23.6, 13.3, 2.2 miles). The first two rings stand in direct proportion "mit der Größe der Stadt und also auch mit der der kultivierten Ebene" (to the size of the town, and so to that of the cultivated plain). Of the third ring he says only that for it, at 1½ Taler, "eine Ausdehnung von 9,4 Meilen angenommen" is (an extent of 9.4 miles is assumed), falling quickly with the price and gone at 0.9 Taler.

Water, small towns, and the map (section 28). If ship freight were a tenth of land freight, then "ist ein Gut, welches an einem Fluße liegend 100 Meilen vom Marktplatz entfernt ist", for the worth of its grain and what follows from that, "dem Gute gleich, welches in dem isolierten Staat 10 Meilen von der Stadt entfernt ist"; and one 5 miles from the river and 100 down it "wäre dem Gute des isolierten Staats gleich, welches 15 Meilen von der Stadt entfernt ist". Of plate II, drawn on that assumption: the third ring "erstreckt sich längs des Flusses bis an die Grenze des Staats" (stretches along the river to the border of the state). "Dagegen weicht der Kreis der Viehzucht zurück und verschwindet in der Nähe des Flusses gänzlich." (The stock ring, on the other hand, draws back and vanishes altogether near the river.) A like effect, though smaller, "bringt die Anlegung einer Kunststraße hervor" (comes of laying a made road); he gives no figure for it. Of small towns scattered through a real country: "Die Getreidepreise in den kleinen Städten werden also durch den Marktpreis in der Hauptstadt bestimmt" (grain prices in the small towns are thus set by the market price in the capital). And the sentence the page is made from. Since a place can be found in the isolated state for any one farm, one could draw for a whole country a map with each region coloured by its ring: "Eine solche Karte würde eine höchst interessante und instruktive Übersicht gewähren." The rings on it would not follow in order, "sondern bunt durcheinander gemischt sein": a farm on a river with very rich soil, 100 miles from the capital, might belong to the third ring, "während das 10 Meilen von der Stadt liegende Gut mit Sandboden zum sechsten Kreise gehörte" (while the farm on sandy soil 10 miles from the town belonged to the sixth).

Measures. Taler are gold Taler, five to the Louis d'or. "Der Berliner Scheffel ist = 1,542 Bushel." The mile is not defined on the page of measures; it is the German mile of his country, several English miles long, and I have not looked up which.

Where the scan and the book disagree with themselves

  • Section 28's first problem prints the price at which the standard land pays 114 Taler as 0,683 Taler in the scan; its own sum, 441 Taler for 696 Scheffel, is 0.634, which is the "ungefähr 26 Meilen" it then gives. I took 0.634.
  • The freight of a load from one mile reads 1,00 in the scan; the formula beside it gives 1.09.
  • The explanation of plate III gives the three-field ring at 1½ Taler and 10 Körner as 4.5 miles wide; section 14 gives 34.7 − 29.9 = 4.8 for the same.
  • Plate IV's table gives a radius of 2.2 miles at 4 Körner, where the book's rent formulas allow no grain farming at all. I take it for the first ring, which lives on bought dung, but the book does not say.
