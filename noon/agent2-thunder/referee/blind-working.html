Model: p(t) = integral over the stroke of (ds/r) N((t - r/c)/T), N(u) = 1 - 2u on [0,1), c = 343, T = 0.005. Write a = cT = 1.715 m (length of the pulse in air) and M(u) = integral_0^u N = u - u^2 for 0 <= u <= 1, and 0 outside (N has zero area, so M returns to 0).

(1) Pencil

(a) Listener at the centre of an arc, length L, radius R

Every point is at r = R, so every pulse arrives together:

p(t) = (L/R) N((t - R/c)/T)

One clean N-wave: jumps to +L/R at t = R/c, falls in a straight line to -L/R at t = R/c + T, jumps back to 0. L/R is just the angle the arc subtends (radians). One clap, 5 ms long, nothing else.

(b) One straight piece, far away, ends at r_a and r_b

Along the piece r runs linearly, dr = cos(theta) ds, cos(theta) = (r_b - r_a)/l (signed; theta = angle between piece and line of sight). Change variable s -> r, then r -> u = (t - r/c)/T, dr = -a du:

p(t) = (1/r) (l/(r_b - r_a)) integral_{r_a}^{r_b} N((t - rho/c)/T) d rho
     = (a / (r cos(theta))) [ M((t - r_a/c)/T) - M((t - r_b/c)/T) ]

So each END of the piece gives a parabolic bump u(1-u), 5 ms long, height a/(4 r |cos theta|), positive from the near end (starting at r_a/c), negative from the far end (starting at r_b/c). The height does not depend on l. If |r_b - r_a| < a (piece nearly broadside, or shorter than 1.7 m along the line of sight) the two bumps overlap, and in the limit r_a = r_b they merge into the N-wave of (a): (l/r) N.

Two neighbouring pieces in one line: same r, same cos(theta) at the join, so the negative bump of the first's far end cancels the positive bump of the second's near end exactly. Only the outer ends are left. In general at a join at distance r_j between pieces i and i+1 what is left is

(a / r_j) (1/cos(theta_{i+1}) - 1/cos(theta_i)) M((t - r_j/c)/T)

i.e. a KINK radiates, in proportion to the jump in 1/cos(theta); a straight join radiates nothing.

Long straight run seen end-on or obliquely: silence from its whole interior; one bump from each end (height a/(4 r_end |cos theta_end|)), and between them only a small slow residue because 1/(r cos theta) is not quite constant: for smooth g(r) = 1/(r|cos theta|), p ~ (a^2/6) dg/dr (using integral u N(u) du = -1/6).

(c) Long straight stroke, nearest point at D

r = sqrt(D^2 + s^2), two branches, ds = r dr / sqrt(r^2 - D^2):

p(t) = 2 integral_D^{ct} N((ct - r)/a) dr / sqrt(r^2 - D^2)        (for D <= ct <= D + a)
     = 2 [ (1 - 2 rho/a) arcosh(rho/D) + (2/a) sqrt(rho^2 - D^2) ],   rho = ct     (exact)

With rho = D + x, x << D (sqrt(r^2 - D^2) ~ sqrt(2 D y)):

p = 2 sqrt(2x/D) (1 - 4x/(3a)),    0 <= x <= a

dp/dx = 0 at x = a/4. So

FIRST PEAK:  at t = D/c + T/4,   height (2 sqrt2 / 3) sqrt(cT/D) = 0.9428 sqrt(cT/D)

It then falls through zero at x = 3a/4 to a sharp (square-root cusp) minimum exactly at t = D/c + T, of the same size, -(2 sqrt2/3) sqrt(cT/D); after that a negative tail ~ -(a^2/6) (2D)^(-1/2) x^(-3/2) creeping back to 0. Height goes as D^(-1/2), not 1/D (cylindrical spreading; the stretch that arrives in one T is the Fresnel stretch of half-length sqrt(2 D a)). For D = 1000: 0.03904 at 2.916702 s; minimum -0.03904 at 2.920452 s.

(d) Predictions for the crooked 5 km stroke (written before step 2)

  • Loudest moment vs ground distance d: I expect about d^(-0.75), with honest range -0.5 to -1.0; that is a fall by a factor of about 8 (range 4 to 16) from 0.5 km to 8 km. Reason: a perfectly straight stroke gives d^(-1/2) (from (c)); a stroke whose every 3 m piece adds with a random sign gives, for the Fresnel stretch sqrt(2 d a) (41 m at 0.5 km, 165 m at 8 km) that arrives within one pulse length from the nearly-broadside foot of the channel, a sum growing as sqrt(length) ~ d^(1/4), over r ~ d: d^(-3/4). The 16 degree kinks every 3 m keep any stretch coherent over only 2 or 3 pieces (random-walk direction leaves a shell 1.7 m thick after n ~ 2.3 pieces), so I expect the rough case, and scatter of a factor ~1.5 from one stroke to another, since it is the largest of a random sum. A big bend that happens to lie broadside can win.
  • The body of the rumble (rms) should fall more slowly than the peak, about d^(-0.3 to -0.4), because the middle of the stroke is 2.55 km away at d = 0.5 km and 8.4 km at d = 8 km (a factor 3.3 for a factor 16 in d), and the same stroke is squeezed into less time.
  • Duration: SHORTER as the listener goes away. For a vertical 5 km stroke, duration = (sqrt(d^2 + 5000^2) - d)/c + T: 13.2 s at 0.5 km, 11.95 s at 1 km, 9.9 s at 2 km, 7.0 s at 4 km, 4.2 s at 8 km (horizontal wander of the channel shifts these by wander/c). Reason: the spread between nearest and farthest point shrinks.

(3) Clap and rumble

In this model sound comes only from where the stroke's distance from the listener is stationary or its direction relative to the line of sight changes. A CLAP is a stretch of stroke lying on (or tangent to) a sphere about the listener, so that its length arrives all inside one pulse length: an arc gives the full N-wave (L/R), a tangent point of a straight or gently curved run gives 0.94 sqrt(cT/D). A RUMBLE is the long train of small parabolic bumps, of either sign and size (cT/r) times the jump in 1/cos(theta), one from every kink, arriving in order of distance: the interiors of straight runs cancel themselves, because the pulse has zero area. Its length is (r_max - r_min)/c. A perfectly straight stroke would sound like one sharp crack (up-down, 5 ms, from the nearest point, if the perpendicular meets it), then a faint negative tail dying as (time)^(-3/2), then near-silence until a very small bump from each end; seen end-on, only the two end bumps. No rumble at all.
