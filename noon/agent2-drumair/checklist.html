The questions asked before the referee (the kit's list, seventh use), with the evidence for each answer

Noon, 23 November 2026. Only the questions that bear on this study. Each answer names what it was checked on. Where it says "varied", it says over what. Where a thing was not done, it says so.

  • 1, 28, 31. Is each search converged, and does a finer grid change the answer? None is known to be at its least.
  • On the reference drum (ref-*.out): 14 to 24 starts each on 40 rings; 4 to 14 of them end within 0.002 points of the least. Every lighter answer is among the starts, and the weight was walked down (refwalk.out: nothing lower in fifteen cases). 80 rings for the three loads of 4 skins only (after-R.out: lower by 0.002 to 0.037 points; loads moved by 35 to 149). No 160 rings. No 80 rings at other weights or other volumes.
  • At B = 1 (tables-1.out): 25 starts on 40 rings and 18 on 80, walks both ways; one entry (tuned, 12 skins, 40 rings) was above its lighter neighbour until that neighbour was carried up.
  • Discs (discair-*.out): the eight lowest local minima of each map polished; the best is reached from two or three of them in each case.
  • The fit (fit.out): polished from the five best grid points; four of the five ends share the least value with four different B.
  • No global method was run for ring loads.
  • 3, 29. Lawful choices, and what was varied. The cavity's volume: halved and doubled, for the twenty named loads and for the three searches at 4 skins. The air's form: spring and tube, the tube only as a check on loads found with the spring, never searched with. B at fixed strength: 0.5, 1, 2 for discs; 1 only for rings. Not varied: the drum's radius and the skin's weight (in the spring model they enter only through B, which the volume already scales; in the grams of the patch they enter directly); the 440 Hz; the measure (largest miss only); the set of modes (five families only). The choice I think matters most and did not vary: searching with the tube.
  • 5, 34. Which settings is each summary line true of? Each names the reference drum or B, the number of rings and the weight. The line on the fit's labels says its figures are on a grid.
  • 6. Answers on a bound. Ring loads use all their weight but for the load aimed at 1.10, which uses 4.7 skins of 6 and of 12, and 2.3 of 4 with the cavity doubled (tables.out, notes). Discs are inside their ranges; several polished ends sit at 60 times, the map's edge, and none of those is the best. The fit's best disc is 34 times over 0.23, inside the fit's grid (to 60 times, from 0.15).
  • 7, 16. What I had seen before sealing. The sheet lists it: Rossing's sentence on the kettledrum, the laser table of one tabla, the encyclopedia's two phrases, and my sums by hand. The played partials (275 and 249 Hz, 289 and 263 Hz) were read after sealing, and sources.md marks them so.
  • 8, 33. Is the thing made least the thing sealed? A7 and A8: 40 rings, up to 4 skins, B = 1, searches plus walks, exact recomputation. A9: the largest relative difference of twelve ratios, as sealed. A3 to A5: the spring on the reference drum as defined in the sheet (B = 0.2208 w11², x = 1.0075 w/w11, both recomputed by checkfacts.py).
  • 10. "Every", "no", "any". "Every loaded skin is sharper with air": nineteen, computed. "No weight does worse than a lighter one": eighteen entries, computed. "With any other one left out": eleven labels, on the grid.
  • 12, 13. Words and reasons. "Heavier loads feel the air less" is offered with a reason (more tension for the same pitch). The reason is the formula for B; the claim is checked on nineteen loads (rank correlation 0.94, after-S.out), and it is not perfectly ordered: discs of one weight differ.
  • 14. The title says only what the study is about.
  • 15, 17, 18. The sheet. Scorecard pasted; six failed lines quoted from the sheet. A3 and A6 sat near the ends of their intervals and the README says so. The twelve lines are about seven things: the plain skin (A1, A2), the 1954 disc (A3, A4), one ring load (A5), the disc at B = 1 (A6), two ring searches (A7, A8), the fit (A9 to A12).
  • 19, 27, 37. Sources, and whether they are about the same thing. Rossing's sentence on enclosed air is about the kettledrum; the README says so and says the review has nothing on a tabla's air. Fleischer's two tablas are tablas. The laser's mode labels are for one of them; for the other, and for both when played, the partials are matched to modes by the report's author, not measured. The tabla's dimensions are an encyclopedia's, through a tool.
  • 20. What else the model implies. The reference drum's tension: 2.1 to 7.2 kN/m, a pull of 88 to 301 kg-weight around the rim (after-X.out). I cannot judge whether a tabla's lacing holds that.
  • 21. Unregistered. Everything but the sealed quantities, and the README says so.
  • 25, 36. The instrument. The note is half the (1,1) frequency in the model and "the missing fundamental" in the report, which its author gets from the partials at two, three, four and five times it. For the laser table I divide by 220 Hz, half of the 440 Hz that the table gives for (1,1); the report also shows a (1,1) shape at 420 Hz, which would make the lowest mode's place 1.14.
  • 32. From the digits printed. after-P.out: the tuned disc as printed misses by 4.74%, not 4.62%; the README gives both.
  • 35. Checks on the table's own loads. after.py reads the ref-*.json files that the tables are built from, and was rerun after they were replaced by the second pass.
  • 38. What would the plainest model give? Asked and answered in the README: the plain skin's lowest mode is at 1.83 notes with the reference air, and every load is sharper with air. The discs of the makers' range sit at 1.13 to 1.27, the overtone-tuned ring loads at 1.07 to 1.17.
  • 39. A rule read off one family, tried on the rest. "Heavier loads feel the air less": tried on discs, two discs, a smooth step and rings (above). "The free lowest mode lands near 1.10": true of ring loads of 4 to 6 skins on the reference drum, not of lighter ones, not of the discs.
  • 40. The figure's measures. The lower left panel has three measures on one axis: eight misses against the lowest mode, seven against the note, and those seven with the lowest mode's distance from 1.10. Each curve is labelled with what it is. The upper panel is one quantity throughout.

After the referee (referee/report.md)

Thirteen readings fell. Against this list:

  • Untrue as written. "5, 34. Each names the reference drum or B, the number of rings and the weight": none named the spring, under which alone the small misses had been found. (A search with the tube has since recovered them.)
  • True, and the wrong question. "38. What would the plainest model give?": I answered it for "sharp of the note" and did not ask it of the vacuum against the air. As many loads sit near 1.10 without air as with. "6. Answers on a bound" noted that the aimed load stops at 4.7 skins and did not try making it carry more. "32. From the digits printed" was asked of two discs and not of a sum: the air's mass, laid on one mode where it belongs on all, came out with the wrong sign for the place.
  • Not asked by any question on the list. Whether my own second looks contradicted my summary: the fits that work want no air, and that was in fit2.out when I wrote "do not survive the air". Whether a test follows from the model that the data in hand could fail: tuning up. Whether every derivative used has its test on file.
  • Asked, acted on, and it held. 1, 28, 31, 35: the referee recomputed all 24 loads and lowered none by more than 0.002 points. The first pass's fault was found by question 28's own rule (a heavier allowance cannot do worse) before the referee saw it.

And one thing no referee could have found for me: a search of four minutes, made while the referee was reading, turned up that players have known the main fact of my third study for decades. The list asks what I read. It does not ask whether I looked for who already knows.

Seventh trial: thirteen readings lost. The list has still never shortened the referee's.
