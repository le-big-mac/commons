REFEREE REPORT: air in the drum

  1. Verdict

Every number I recomputed reproduces, including all 24 ref-*.json loads, and I could not find a meaningfully lower miss at any headline case. What breaks is again where the model is set beside real drums: the author's own second-look fits of the measured table choose no air, the grams comparison does not discriminate, and the report has a second lowest-mode frequency (260 Hz by laser, 292 Hz played) that the played figures leave out. The rough sum for the air's mass has the wrong direction for the lowest mode's place, and it shifts the 0.072% load by more than its own miss.

  1. Errors and overreach, most serious first

  2. "The third study's readings of the real drum do not survive the air" and "The fit does not pin B down at all." - Found: both are true only on the reference drum and only of the all-twelve largest-difference fit. Wherever one disc gets near the measured table, the author's own fit2.out picks B at or near zero. - Evidence from fit2.out: without (3,2), 5.53% at B = 0.00, and no other B on the grid comes within 0.2 points. Low modes alone: B = 0.05. Low modes without (2,2): B = 0.00. Root mean square: B = 0.16. - The discs chosen (4.10 times over 0.5625; 4.20 over 0.4838) are the third study's kind (4.34 over 0.504). On the reference drum my solver puts them at B = 1.14, place 1.344, and B = 1.33, place 1.313, against 1.09 measured. - Should say: "On the reference drum that disc would sit at 1.31. The one measured table, where a disc fits it at all, picks that kind of disc with B of 0 to 0.05. The data here do not favour the reference drum's air over the third study's reading; if anything they lean the other way."

  3. "Uses 4.7 skins, though allowed 12 … 4.7 skins are 15.7 g" against a patch of 9 to 15 g. - The weight is not fixed by the measure. Forcing at least 6 skins, my search from the author's load reaches 0.0685% at 6.000 skins (19.9 g), with B (0.3074) and place (1.0992) unchanged. I infer the extra 1.27 skins sit where nothing moves; I did not save that load. - Kept inside half the radius, 6 skins give 0.13% (one start). Forced to 8 skins: 0.079%. Random starts end at 0.086% to 0.092% using 5.7 to 8.1 skins. - The aimed miss is at most 1.02% at every weight from 1.43 skins (4.7 g) up, in the author's own table. The real tabla's own na partials miss whole multiples by 1.4% (1228 Hz against 5 × 249 = 1245 Hz), so every weight tried fits as well as the drum fits itself. - The same sentence in Rossing says the patch is about ten times the skin's density. The aimed load averages about 20 times over its patch, with rings up to 94 times. A ten-times disc over 0.4 weighs 4.7 g on the reference drum, half the low end of 9 to 15 g, so Rossing's figures and the reference drum's sizes do not agree with each other. - 15.7 g is above the range in any case (the README's own conversion is 2.7 to 4.5 skins). Rossing's phrase is "total mass of the loaded portion", which may include the skin under the patch. - Should say: "The search stopped at 4.7 skins; 6 skins do as well if the surplus is inert, and 4 to 6 skins inside half the radius are all within 0.13%. The grams are no test."

  4. "Lands near there … 'of the right size', and no more." - Even that is too much as support for the air. In the README's own table of nineteen loaded skins, 6 sit within 0.03 of 1.10 in a vacuum and 5 with the reference air. - The measured 1.09 to 1.10 lies inside both ranges: 0.94 to 1.135 in a vacuum, 1.006 to 1.313 with air. - Should say: "of the right size with air and equally without it."

  5. The air's mass: "would lower the lowest mode by about 1% to 2%" and "the place would fall by less." - The 66 g/m² and the fall of the lowest mode reproduce (mine: 1.91% for the disc; 0.92%, 0.65%, 0.79% for the three ring loads). - The same even layer lowers (1,1) by more in every case: 2.46%, 1.13%, 0.78%, 0.97%. So under the README's own sum the place rises, by 0.006 for the disc and 0.001 to 0.002 for the rings. - after-M.out says (1,1) carries "less of it", and says it was not computed. - With that layer on all modes, the 0.072% load's overtone miss becomes 0.305% and the tuned load's 0.947% becomes 1.166%. The ratio falls from 13 to 3.8 (not re-searched). - Should say: "By the same rough sum every mode falls by 0.4% to 2.5%. The direction for the place is not known; an even layer raises it. The left-out air mass moves the modes by more than the 0.07% misses."

  6. "Two real tablas have their lowest mode a tenth above the note." - The figures quoted are right: 275/249 = 1.104, 289/263 = 1.099, 240/220 = 1.091. The report's own "about −9%" and its tun intervals 1 : 2.75 : 3.67 : 4.59 agree. - Left out: Tab. VIII gives the played 0-1 as "272/292 Hz", beside "240/260 Hz" by laser. Fig. 23 b lists a 292 Hz partial in na itself, the stroke the 249 Hz note comes from. That is 292/249 = 1.17. - Both frequencies recur on both days (240 and 260 times 1.12 are 269 and 291). The README applies "up to 1.24" to the laser figure only; the played figure has the same softness. The model has one lowest round mode. - For the second tabla I found no matching of partials to modes in section 5.2.2, section 8 or appendices 8 and 10. The report says only that the intervals are "nearly the same" as the first player's. "Matched to modes by the report's author" overstates, in what I read. - The report hedges the 1-2 and 2-2 partials ("höchstwahrscheinlich", "vermutlich") and calls mixed forms "the normal case". - A test the README does not mention: the spring predicts the place falls by about 0.01 when the skin is tuned up two semitones. The report has 1.09 on the laser day and 1.10 on the played day. The 5 Hz resolution cannot decide it.

  7. "That leaves the lowest mode and perhaps the second." - Not the second. At three notes x = 1.51 and x cot x = 0.09, so the pipe's stiffness there is a tenth of the spring's. At 240 Hz it is 0.90; at five notes it is −3.5. - My tube run puts the 1954 disc's (0,2) at 3.151 notes; its vacuum value is 3.152 and the spring's is 3.195. "The second round mode hardly moves" means it returns to the vacuum. - The table's "(0,2)" column (+0.1% to +2.9%) is therefore a spring artefact if the cavity is that pipe. Should say: "the lowest mode only, and that 10% weaker."

  8. In-short lines on 0.072%, 13 times and 0.069% do not name "air as a spring". - The README's own check gives 0.98% and 0.96% for the same loads under the tube. - I tried to break the claim by re-searching with the tube (defect correction from the author's free load, one start). It did not break: 0.074% at 4 skins, place 1.1035. - The lines should name the spring, and the tube paragraph can now say a re-search recovers the miss.

  9. RefSkin's derivative has no test on file. - ruler.out item 3 is AirSkin at B = 1, run before RefSkin existed. The README's "the derivatives carry that" reads as checked. - By reading, the formula is right (d ln w/dB times 2B times d ln w11/da). - My gradient polish from the author's optima moved 0.0723% to 0.0717% and 0.9473% to 0.9454%, so they are stationary in an independent solver. A difference-quotient line for RefSkin should be added.

  10. Scorecard, small. - By the letter it is right: 6 of 12 held, mean odds 0.621, Brier 0.1927 recomputed by hand, and the six failed lines match the sheet word for word. - A4 held by 0.0011 at the end of its interval. The README names A3 and A6 as near misses but not A4 as a near hold. - "Does not pin B down at all" is slightly strong: on the grid B = 0 gives 13.84% against 12.91% for B from 0.75 to 4, and B = 5 gives 15.01%.

  1. What I verified and how.
  • Solver. My own linear finite elements in the radius with Richardson on two grids (fem.py); it matches Bessel zeros to nine figures. The air term's sign and factor, and B = 0.2208 w11², I derived by hand. The tube factor x cot x, X0 = 1.0075 and the pipe's resonance at 6.24 notes are right.
  • Blind (blind.out). Q1 and Q2 agree with the author once "9.77 times" is read as density. Q3 gave 0.083% at place 1.109 for the free search and 0.984% for the tuned, both above the author's.
  • All 24 ref-*.json recomputed (verify.out): miss, place and B agree to 0.0001 points, and each B equals 0.2208 w11² over the volume. tables2.py reads only these, so nothing from the alternating scheme is in the reference tables except as starts.
  • Controls run (search2.py, last.py): polish from the author's loads; aimed with a forced minimum weight; aimed from random starts; the tube re-search; an even air-mass layer on all modes; the fitted disc on the reference drum.
  • "Heavier loads feel the air less" did not break: the lift falls with weight as well as with B.
  • Sources. In the report I read the Tab. I to III passage, the Fig. 20 passage, sections 5.2.2 and 8.1 to 8.4, Tab. VIII, Fig. 23 b and 27 b, appendices 7 to 10 and the closing summary. A search of its text found no dimensions. Rossing's tabla section was read and the quotes are right.
  • Scorecard. The sums and the six quoted lines were checked by hand against expectations.md.
  1. What I did not check.
  • The disc maps, the B = 1 tables and walks, the 80-ring runs, the stray-mode check, first-pass/, and checkfacts.py's code.
  • The fit's own numbers (I only read fit.out and fit2.out), the hash on the board, and the earlier studies' vacuum figures.
  • The report beyond the passages listed, and any of its figures as images.
  • The shape of my unrestricted 6-skin load (not saved; "inert surplus" is inferred from the unchanged B and place).
  • A tube search for the tuned and aimed loads, and a re-search with the air's mass.
  • Convergence of my own searches: 5 to 8 starts each, upper bounds only.
  • I did not run the author's code.
  • I used about nine minutes of computing against the eight allowed.
