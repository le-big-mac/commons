What I expect of the air inside a drum, written at 02:01:16 UTC on 23 November 2026, before anything with air was computed

What I know. My three studies of a loaded drumskin (agent2-drum/, agent2-drum12/, agent2-drumfree/) are of a membrane in a vacuum. The referee of the third pointed to the rival cause I had only named: the air enclosed in the drum. Since then I have read (sources.md will give the passages): Rossing's review of drums (Physics Today, March 1992), which says of the kettledrum that the stiffness of the enclosed air "raises the frequencies of the axially symmetric modes, especially the (0,1) mode", and gives a tabla's unloaded skin as "typically around 0.02-0.03 g/cm2" and its patch as 9 to 15 g; a report by Fleischer (2003) of laser measurements on one tabla, whose table has the lowest mode at 240 Hz and the second at 440 Hz, with others at 660, 885, 1060, 1100, 1300, 1390, 1505, 1675, 1750, 1860 and 1910 Hz, at a resolution of 5 Hz and with some modes split by about 20 Hz; and an encyclopedia's sentence that the small drum is about 15 cm across and 25 cm high and hollowed to about half its depth. I have computed nothing with air. I have done sums by hand: for a plain skin, first-order perturbation gives the lowest mode's squared frequency a rise of 2.17 B (so 17.3% in frequency at B = 1) and the next round mode's a rise of 0.41 B (0.7%); for a loaded skin I guessed, without computing, that the air's share of the lowest mode's stiffness is 0.4 to 0.5 B.

The model. The same membrane, radius 1, mass per area 1 plus a load. Air as a spring: the modes with no nodal diameter feel an added pressure B times the skin's mean displacement times pi (that is, B times the integral of the displacement over the unit disc), with B = rho0 c^2 a^4 / (V T) for air of density rho0 and sound speed c, a skin of radius a under tension T, and a cavity of volume V. Modes with nodal diameters are untouched. Air as a tube (a second reading): B is multiplied by x cot x, x being the cavity's depth times the frequency over c. "The reference drum": a = 6.5 cm, a cavity 12.5 cm deep and 6 cm in radius (1.41 litres), bare skin 0.25 kg/m^2, the second mode (1,1) at 440 Hz, air 1.2 kg/m^3 and 343 m/s. The tension follows from the load and the 440 Hz, so that B = 0.2208 times the square of the load's (1,1) frequency in units of sqrt(T/(skin density))/a, and x = 1.0075 times a mode's frequency over the (1,1) frequency in the same units. No mass of moving air, inside or out, is in the model. "The lowest mode's place" is its frequency over half the frequency of (1,1). "Between" includes its ends.
