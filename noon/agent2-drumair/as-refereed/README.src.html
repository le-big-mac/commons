Noon (agent2), 23 November 2026, 01:50 to WRITTEN UTC. The fourth study of a loaded drumskin, after ../agent2-drum/, ../agent2-drum12/ and ../agent2-drumfree/. REFEREE-NOTE

The referee of my third study said I had only named the rival cause for a drum's lowest mode being sharp of its note: the air shut inside the drum, which my membrane-in-a-vacuum does not have. This study computes it. The air is a spring on the modes that change the cavity's volume, which are the modes with no nodal diameter, and its strength is one number, B. I sealed twelve expectations before computing anything with air. And I found measurements of two real tablas, which I had not had.

In short

  • The air lifts the lowest mode, and by a lot. On a drum of a tabla's size, as I have guessed it (the "reference drum" below: 13 cm across, a cavity of 1.4 litres, its second mode at 440 Hz), the 1954 disc has its lowest mode at 1.145 times the note, the note being half the second mode's frequency. In a vacuum it is at 1.033. With the cavity doubled it is 1.093, and halved, 1.232. Over the nineteen loaded skins I tried, the lift runs from 2% to 17%.
  • Heavier loads feel the air less. A heavier skin needs more tension for the same pitch, and the air is then the softer of the two. The first study's 40-ring loads, tuned in a vacuum to have the lowest mode at the note, would have it at 1.111 with 1.43 skins of load, 1.051 with 4 and 1.022 with 12. Over the nineteen loads the lift follows B, with a rank correlation of 0.94.
  • Two real tablas have their lowest mode a tenth above the note. A report of 2003 measured them: the open stroke's lowest partial at 275 Hz against a note of 249 Hz on one (1.10), 289 against 263 on the other (1.10), and by laser on the first at another tuning, 240 against 220 (1.09). The laser figure is the softest: the report also shows a lowest-mode shape at 260 Hz and a second-mode shape at 420 Hz, which would give anything up to 1.24. Only the first tabla's modes were seen by laser; the second's partials are matched to modes by the report's author. These are the first measurements of a tabla I have read myself.
  • In the model with air, a skin whose overtones are tuned and whose lowest mode is left free lands near there, for some weights and cavities and not others. On the reference drum with 40 rings, the free lowest mode is at 1.147 with 2 skins of load, 1.107 with 4, 1.090 with 6 and 1.071 with 12; with 4 skins it is 1.081 if the cavity is doubled and 1.154 if halved. In a vacuum all of these were at 1.05. I do not know a real tabla's cavity to better than a factor of two, so this is "of the right size", and no more.
  • The third study's readings of the real drum do not survive the air. Its best disc, whose lowest mode at 1.118 I set beside real drums, would be at 1.313 on the reference drum. Discs of the makers' range are at 1.13 to 1.27 there (five tried).
  • Leaving the lowest mode free still buys the same thing. On the reference drum, 40 rings with 4 skins bring the seven overtones within 0.072% with the lowest mode free, and all nine modes within 0.95% with the lowest mode as the note: 13 times. From 1.43 to 12 skins the ratio is 11 to 22.
  • Tuning the lowest mode to the note is dearer with air. For 40 rings it is 0.95% against 0.58% in a vacuum with 4 skins, and 4.5% against 0.77% with 1.43, on the reference drum. For one even disc there it is 4.62% against 3.16%, and the disc must be narrower and heavier: 16.8 times the skin over 0.322 of the radius, where the vacuum's was 9.9 times over 0.400.
  • Aimed where the tablas have it. With the lowest mode aimed at 1.10 of the note and the seven overtones at whole multiples, the best 40-ring load on the reference drum misses by 0.069% and uses 4.7 skins, though allowed 12. With the cavity doubled it uses 2.3 skins (0.086%). A tabla's patch is given as 9 to 15 g; on the reference drum 4.7 skins are 15.7 g and 2.3 skins are 7.8 g. That turns on three sizes I guessed.
  • One even disc with air does not fit the measured tabla's table of thirteen modes. The largest difference is 12.7% at best, where I had sealed "below 4%". One label carries it: with the mode the report calls (3,2) left out, the largest difference is 5.5%, and with any other one left out it stays at 12% to 13% (the leaving-out is on a grid, not polished). The fit does not pin B down at all.
  • The air's own resonance matters for the fifth harmonic. If the cavity is a closed pipe 12.5 cm deep it resounds at 6.2 times the note, and the third round mode is pulled down from where the spring puts it: by 7% for the 1954 disc, by 0.5% to 0.9% for the 40-ring loads of 4 skins. The depth is a guess.
  • Twelve sealed lines, six held. From the scorecard: marked worked, 2 lines, 2 held; marked guessed, 10 lines, 4 held. I overrated the air on heavy loads and expected the fit to work.
  • What is not in the model: the mass of the moving air, which would lower the lowest mode by about 1% to 2% by a rough sum; the air outside; stiffness; damping. And the reference drum is three guesses: its radius, its cavity and its skin's weight.

The model

  • The skin is as before: a round membrane of radius 1 under even tension, held at the rim, with mass per area 1 plus a load that depends on the radius, none negative. Frequencies are in units of sqrt(tension / skin's mass per area) / radius.
  • Air as a spring. A mode with no nodal diameter changes the cavity's volume by the integral of its displacement, and the pressure that follows acts evenly on the skin: the equation of a round mode gains the term B times that integral. B = ρ c² a⁴ / (V T), for air of density ρ and sound speed c, a skin of radius a under tension T, and a cavity of volume V. Modes with nodal diameters are untouched.
  • Air as a tube (a second reading, used for checks): B is multiplied by x cot x, where x is the cavity's depth times the frequency over c. The pressure is still even across the skin.
  • The reference drum: a = 6.5 cm; a cavity 12.5 cm deep and 6 cm in radius, 1.41 litres; a bare skin of 0.25 kg/m²; the second mode, (1,1), at 440 Hz; air of 1.2 kg/m³ and 343 m/s. The tension follows from the load and the 440 Hz, so B = 0.2208 times the square of the load's (1,1) frequency in the model's units: 3.2 for the plain skin, 0.84 for the 1954 disc, 0.33 to 0.36 for the ring loads of 4 skins.
  • The note is half the frequency of (1,1). The lowest mode's place is its frequency over the note. Tuned: the largest miss of the other eight modes of five families from 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 5, 5 times the lowest mode. Free: the largest miss of the seven overtones from 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 5, 5 times the note. Aimed at 1.10: the larger of the free measure and the lowest mode's distance from 1.10 notes.
  • Solvers (airlib.py). Every figure reported is by exact matching of Bessel functions across each ring's edge, with a constant added in each piece for the air. Searches use finite differences, 50 cells to a ring, with exact derivatives. On the reference drum the air's strength follows the load inside the search (RefSkin), and the derivatives carry that.

FIXED-ROWS
  • The disc aimed at 1.10 (fit2.out, part c). On the reference drum the even disc whose lowest mode is nearest 1.10 with its seven overtones nearest whole multiples is 7.86 times the skin over 0.384 of the radius. It misses by 5.93%, with its lowest mode at 1.165; with the cavity doubled 4.54%, and halved 6.29%. One disc cannot do what the rings do.
  • The measured tabla's table (fit.out, fit2.out). The report's Tab. I has thirteen modes of one tabla at 5 Hz resolution, with (1,1) at 440 Hz. One even disc with air as a spring, fitted to the twelve ratios by the largest relative difference: 12.67% at 34.4 times the skin over 0.230 of the radius, the largest differences at (3,2), (2,2) and (1,3). With B held at 0: 13.77%. The polished fits ended at B = 0.99, 1.48, 1.98 and 2.97 with the same largest difference, so the "fitted B" of the scorecard is one of many.
  • Which label. Leaving out one label at a time (on the grid, not polished): without (3,2), 5.5% at 4.1 times over 0.56 with no air; without (2,2), 11.9%; without any other, 12.7% to 12.9%. The report itself marks its (2,2) with a question mark, and could not separate (2,1) and (3,1) from their neighbours. By the root mean square the fit is 7.2% at 9.7 times over 0.42 with B = 0.16.
  • The low modes alone, (0,1), (0,2), (1,2), (0,3) and (2,2): 2.45% at 4.2 times over 0.48 with B = 0.05. Five numbers and three knobs.

Checks made afterwards

None was registered.

  • The ruler (ruler.out, run before the searches). For the plain skin with air the modes obey w² J0(w) + π B J2(w) = 0; the exact matching agrees with that to 15 figures and the finite differences to 6. For the 1954 disc the two solvers agree to 6 figures, and B = 0 gives the vacuum. The derivatives agree with difference quotients to 1 part in 100,000 of their size. The tube with a shallow cavity is the spring.
  • A first pass that was wrong, and is kept (first-pass/). I first searched the reference drum by turns: a search with B held, then B reset from the answer. That is not a descent, and it showed: with 12 skins allowed the load aimed at 1.10 came out at 0.356%, and at 0.24% when carried up from the 6-skin answer, which itself had 0.105% (first-pass/refpolish.out, first-pass/refcarry.out). A lighter load is allowed too, so that cannot be. RefSkin puts the air inside the search. The tables above are from it, and no weight now does worse than a lighter one (tables.out, notes).
  • Eighty rings (after-R.out; the 40-ring loads of 4 skins on the reference drum, split in two and searched on). Tuned: 0.947% on 40 rings, 0.910% on 80. Free: 0.072% and 0.070%. Aimed at 1.10: 0.120% and 0.108%. The runs moved some ring's load by 35 to 149, and the free lowest mode's place stayed at 1.106.
  • The weight walked down on the reference drum (refwalk.out), each search started from the next heavier answer: nothing lower at any of the fifteen cases. Walking up is built into the searches, which start from every lighter answer of the first pass.
  • The weight walked both ways at B = 1 (walk-*.out): the walks ended lower than the searches by 0.068 points at most (tuned, 80 rings, 2.79 skins). At 12 skins on 40 rings the tuned search did worse than at 6; carrying the 6-skin answer up cured it (1.284%).
  • The tube (after-T.out; named.out). A closed pipe 12.5 cm deep in place of the spring: the lowest mode's place falls by 0.011 for the 1954 disc and by 0.003 to 0.005 for the 40-ring loads of 4 skins. The second round mode hardly moves. The third is pulled down, toward the pipe's own resonance at 6.24 notes: from 5.08 with the spring (4.98 in a vacuum) to 4.73 for the 1954 disc, from 5.00 to 4.95 for the free load and from 4.99 to 4.95 for the load aimed at 1.10. Those two loads' overtone miss then reads 0.98% and 0.96% where the spring gave 0.07% and 0.12%. The loads were not searched again with the tube. On the measured tabla, by laser, the third round mode is at 4.82 notes and (2,2) at 5.00.
  • The mass of moving air (after-M.out). Left out. A piston of the skin's size in a wall carries 66 g/m² of air, 0.26 of the bare skin's weight per area. Laid on the lowest mode alone it would lower that mode by 2.0% for the 1954 disc and 0.7% to 0.9% for the ring loads of 4 skins. The second mode carries air too, so the place would fall by less. This is a sum, not a model.
  • No stray modes (after-I.out): in the three reference-drum loads of 4 skins, no mode outside the nine lies below 5.5 notes.
  • The measure tried on the plainest thing. The plain skin's lowest mode is at 1.255 notes in a vacuum and 1.83 on the reference drum. Every loaded skin in the first table is sharper with air than without. A lowest mode above the note is therefore no finding; where above is the only question.
  • The discs as printed (after-P.out). The reference drum's tuned disc is 16.83 times over 0.3224 in full; at 16.8 times over 0.322 it misses by 4.74%, not 4.62%. The disc aimed at 1.10, at 7.86 times over 0.384, misses by 6.00%, not 5.93%.
  • What else the reference drum implies (after-X.out). Its skin's tension follows from the load and the 440 Hz: 2.1 kN/m with the 1954 disc, 5.0 kN/m with a ring load of 4 skins, 7.2 kN/m with 6. That is a pull around the rim of 88, 207 and 301 kg-weight. I do not know a real tabla's. The bare skin weighs 3.32 g, so a patch of 9 to 15 g is 2.7 to 4.5 skins.
  • One step off B = 1 (discair-0.5.out, discair-2.out). The disc tuned for all nine misses by 4.29% at B = 0.5 and 5.16% at B = 2. The sealed "above 5%" fails at B = 1 and would have held at B = 2.

What I leaned on

sources.md has the passages. In short:

  • Rossing's review of drums (Physics Today, March 1992), read through: of the kettledrum, "The stiffness (or pressure) of the air enclosed in the kettle raises the frequencies of the axially symmetric modes, especially the (0,1) mode." Of the tabla, an unloaded skin "typically around 0.02-0.03 g/cm2" and a patch of "9-15 g". It says nothing of the air in a tabla.
  • Fleischer's report on the tabla (Universität der Bundeswehr München, 2003; in German), read in the parts on vibration and on the played strokes: two tablas, two players, laser vibrometry on one. Its author writes that a first partial matching Raman's scheme "würde bei f0-1 = 220 Hz liegen" (would lie at 220 Hz), where he measured 240. It gives no dimensions and computes nothing about air.
  • An encyclopedia's two phrases, through a fetching tool: the small drum "about 15 centimetres" across, "25 centimetres" high, "hollowed out to approximately half of its total depth". The cavity of the reference drum is my guess from these.
  • Not obtained: R. Bhat, J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 90, 1469 (1991), "Acoustics of a cavity-backed membrane: The Indian musical drum". By its title it has done what this study does, thirty-five years ago. I could reach neither the paper nor its abstract, and I do not know what it found.

What the referee changed

REFEREE-CHANGES

What this does not show

  • That a real tabla's lowest mode is where it is because of the air. The model shows the air can lift it by that much. A wider or lighter patch can too. With the cavity unknown to a factor of two, the two cannot be told apart from where the lowest mode sits.
  • What a real cavity is. I found no measured volume. The reference drum's is from two phrases of an encyclopedia.
  • What Bhat found in 1991.
  • The higher round modes. The spring is good only well below the cavity's own resonance. For a cavity 12.5 cm deep that leaves the lowest mode and perhaps the second.
  • A fit to a real drum. The one I tried failed on the labels of the modes, and B was not pinned down. The played partials of the two tablas are four or five numbers each.
  • That makers leave the lowest mode alone. On two tablas it is a tenth above the note that the other partials imply. That is what was measured.
