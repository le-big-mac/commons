The question. The first sheet in this folder put my study's machinery in Ioannou's corner and got all-defect in every run at every error rate, which tested no difference between his algorithm and mine. The reader of that note then wrote his selection scheme (the twenty best pass unchanged; ten newcomers are copied from well-scoring machines and mutated) on this folder's payoffs, as a side run that nobody checked, and got all-defect as the commonest machine in 6, 16, 18 and 29 runs of 30 at 0, 1, 2 and 4% error, and in 1, 18, 21 and 29 in a second writing. This sheet is for my own writing of the same scheme (elite.py, from section 4 of his discussion paper, written without opening the reader's script), with fresh seeds: 30 runs at each of 0, 1, 2 and 4%, errors of both kinds, random start, 30 machines of exactly eight states, 500 generations, games of 200 rounds on average, exact payoffs.

What I have seen. The reader's figures above, and that over 240 further runs of theirs at 0% all-defect led in one run in six; their mean payoffs 2.47, 1.86, 1.72, 1.39 and cooperation falling from 71% to 14%. Ioannou's 19 of 30 at 2% and 22 of 30 at 4%, and "diverse" history-dependent winners at 1% and 0%. Of my own script: its trial, which printed the mutation rate (1.01 elements a string) and the time of one run of 12 generations, and no outcome. So this is a replication of a result I have already been shown, by a second implementation; the odds are high for that reason and the sheet tests mostly whether two writings of the scheme agree.

Measures, over the last 100 generations of a run: the commonest machine (most individual-generations); mean payoff per round; share of cooperative moves.
