## Claim by claim

### P1. 92.70%, centred a little west of Berlin (2020)

In print, in date order:

- **Johnson and Preparata, "The densest hemisphere problem", Theoretical Computer Science 6 (1978) 93–107.** This is the problem in the abstract, with a weighted variant. I know it by title and a search summary of its abstract; I did not read it.
- **Rankin, "Your Hemisphere", 2015** (`radicalcartography.net/your-hemisphere.html`). The map marks "max: 92.9%" in western Switzerland, "min: 8.1% (antipodes is.)" and "south pole: 12.2%", and is signed "calculated using GRUMP v.1". The page says:
  > "For every point on earth, I've calculated the percent of all humans that live on the half of the globe centered on that point. ... On land, the values range from a high of 92.9% (the Human Hemisphere) to a low of 8.1% on the Antipodes Islands off the coast of New Zealand."

  > "... a good chunk of Europe is above 92 percent. The center of the Human Hemisphere is a unique point, but there are plenty of places nearby that are almost as special. Given the distribution of land and people around the world, this large plateau of high values makes perfect sense ..."

  > "Data from GRUMP, the Global Rural-Urban Mapping Project. Calculations done with ArcGIS."
- **Rankin, "Human Hemisphere", 2015** (`radicalcartography.net/human-hemisphere.html`):
  > "The hemisphere shown here — the Human Hemisphere — contained 92.9% of all humans in the year 2000, the most recent year with global data available. Its center is in western Switzerland, at roughly 46.5°N, 7°E. (A more exact value isn't possible, given the imprecision of population data from around the world.) Not surprisingly, the Human Hemisphere is not so different from the Land Hemisphere (the hemisphere with the most land); the main difference is that the Human Hemisphere contains more of Southeast Asia and less of South America."

  The same page: "Just under 88 percent of humanity lives in the Northern Hemisphere; 82 percent lives in the Eastern Hemisphere."
- **Brilliant Maps** (27 September 2015 by Wikipedia's citation). Your quotation is exact. It gives no year and no data, and it misreports Rankin's minimum as 7.1%.
- **World Economic Forum reprint of a Big Think piece, March 2016.** Not read; the site refused.
- **Wikipedia.** The talk page of "Land and water hemispheres" (10 December 2016) repeats Brilliant Maps. The article has said since 12 December 2016: "This hemisphere is almost identical to the hemisphere containing the greatest human population."

I found no other computation in English, French or German.

- **How yours differs:** same object and in substance the same method; different data, a different year, a finer search, and a centre 771 km from his.
- **Do the numbers agree:** yes, for the shared year. In your grid for 2000 a half centred on his point holds 92.84% and the best holds 92.85%; he gives 92.9%.
- **Hard on the claim:** Rankin declined to place the centre more closely than "roughly". You name it to a tenth of a degree. In your 2020 grid the Alps trail Berlin by 0.057 points, and the two datasets differ by about that much at one point for 2000. The ranking has not been tested on a second dataset.
- **Verdict:** the half, its share of about 93% and its Alpine centre for 2000 are known (Rankin, 2015). The 92.70% for 2020 and a centre in Germany are in nothing I found: an easy update, probably unrecorded, about five chances in six.

### P2. The centre moved; the best share fell from 93.4% to 92.7%

Nothing in print computes this half for more than one year or says the centre has moved. Three things bear on it:

- Rankin's map is of 2000, and your 2000 grid has the Alps ahead of Berlin by 0.13 points. "Right for its time" can now be said with the date.
- The ridge is already drawn. On his map a thin unlabelled contour, which I take for the 92% line, encloses a band from the south-east of France through Germany to southern Sweden and Finland.
- If Boggs's 94% for 1945 is right, the fall in the best share continues an older decline. That is one rough estimate, second-hand.

Work on drift over time (Grasland and Madelin, Grether and Mathys, Quah, Balsa-Barreiro) concerns the mean position of mankind, a different object. I know it by title only.

The dataset's own documentation (GHSL Data Package 2023, pp. 45–47) says every grid is built the same way:

> "The population estimates at polygon level for each mapped epoch (5-year interval between 1975 and 2030) are obtained by applying for each unit the population growth rate computed by CIESIN within the census available dates ... The estimates are then rescaled to match ... (b) the total population time series at country level provided by the UN World Population Prospects 2022" ... "(medium variant)".

Its table of inputs gives the census behind each country:

- 2010: Philippines, Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand, Brazil, Ecuador, Paraguay.
- 2012: Bolivia.
- 2007: Peru.
- 2005: Colombia.

So the 2020 grid is those censuses carried forward and scaled to the UN's country totals. The censuses of the 2020 round are not in it below the level of the country.

- **Hard on the claim:** "every grid up to 2010" is four of the eight grids the dataset has to that year. "From 2020" is one modelled year and two projections. The direction of the move is sturdy; its date is the dataset's.
- **Verdict:** not found; probably unrecorded (about four in five), not hard, and standing on one modelled dataset.

### P4. Who is left out; the land half holds 88.5% of people

- **Boggs, "This Hemisphere", Journal of Geography 44 (9), 1945.** A user on Wikipedia's talk page (13 June 2018) quotes the caption of his figure 1: "About 6 per cent of the world's population lives here [water hemisphere] on 12 per cent of the world's land area (excluding Antarctica)." That is about 94% in the land half centred on Nantes. The publisher answered with a bot check; I have not seen the paper.
- **Rankin and Wikipedia** call the two halves "not so different" and "almost identical".
- I found no count by continent of who lies outside, and no figure for the share of mankind living opposite other people.

My check confirms 219.95 and 352.74 million outside, and one cell of 347.5 people at Tristan da Cunha between 30° W and 60° E. The island's own page (18 November 2026) counts "242 people currently living on Tristan da Cunha or visiting the surrounding islands". Your wording "the grid puts" is right; the grid has about a hundred too many.

The land half's share depends on the summit: 88.5% at the Catalan summit, 91.1% centred on Nantes. The public page gives only the first.

- **Verdict:** the count of who is left out is a by-product nobody would have printed, and 88.5% is probably unrecorded (about nine in ten). But the question was answered in 1945 with about 94%, for another year and the other summit.

### W1. About 83% of 239 species, centred in Gascony

Nothing of the kind is in print for woodpeckers, birds or any group. What bears on it:

- **Wikipedia:** "about 240 species arranged in 35 genera".
- **Ilsøe and others, Journal of Biogeography 44 (2017) 1824–1835,** on worldwide woodpecker richness. I read only the abstract. A search summary adds 254 species and hotspots in Southeast Asia and the eastern Andes.
- **Mikusiński (2006)** was not obtainable (404 at both addresses).

- **How yours differs:** the published work counts species per place; yours spreads each species over boxes of records and asks which half holds most.
- **Hard on the claim:** the share moves from 83.1% to 84.5% and the centre by 300 km with the edge treatment, and range maps, the usual instrument, were not tried.
- **Verdict:** an easy and probably unrecorded exercise (about nine in ten), whose number belongs to its definition.

### W2. "What lies opposite South America is the Malay Archipelago, not mainland Asia"

Wikipedia, "Antipodes on Earth":

> "The largest antipodal land masses are the Malay Archipelago, antipodal to the Amazon basin and adjoining Andean ranges; east China and Mongolia, and small sections of southeast Russia, antipodal to Argentina and Chile; ..."

and "The largest landmass with antipodes entirely on land is the island of Borneo, whose antipodes are in the Amazon rainforest." I did not find the remark applied to woodpeckers or to any hemisphere of species.

- **Hard on the claim:** the same sentence says what yours leaves out. Opposite Argentina and Chile lie China and Mongolia, which is mainland Asia. By my arithmetic Lima is opposite Cambodia and Santiago opposite Shaanxi.
- **"China and Indochina are all within 90°" is not so:** Hong Kong (91.6°), Guangzhou (90.4°), Fuzhou (91.8°), Hainan (90.9°), Da Nang (92.4°), Phnom Penh (93.4°) and Ho Chi Minh City (95.1°) are outside the best woodpecker half.
- **The explanation survives corrected:** the best half keeps tropical South America and gives up what lies opposite it (14% of the total); it keeps China and gives up Argentina and Chile (3%).
- **Verdict:** the geography is commonplace; its use here is probably unrecorded (about nine in ten); as worded in the README it is partly wrong.

### Outside the six claims: the circle holding half of mankind
