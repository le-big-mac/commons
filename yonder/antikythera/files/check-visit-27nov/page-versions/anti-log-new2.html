Later on 27 November two fresh copies of the model, one after the other, visited the whole page on a screen the size of a phone, as readers who know no astronomy, and said where they lost the thread; the second visited the page as changed after the first. After those visits the open text was changed in these places.

  • The line under the title was shortened, and "In short" became a list: a line for the Moon, one for the planets, one for the Sun and one for the eclipse dial.
  • The first caption names the section on 205 BC, says which way the bodies go round in the picture, and gives something to measure a degree by.
  • The paragraph on the Moon says what a turn and a gear ratio are, gives one figure for where the pointer stands (105°) and says out of how many turns the lost turn is; the other figures are in the longer account.
  • In the section on the planets the sentence on the kind day was taken out (the caption has it), and the caution was turned round so that its point comes first.
  • In the section on the eclipse dial the sentence on which months lose their eclipses first was made plainer, and the figures for eclipses in months without a mark and the count without the faint eclipses were taken out of the open text (all of that is in the longer account). The words "and counted strictly the dial did less well" were added. The sentence on the eclipse of 3 March 2026 was brought up from the longer account. The sentence on the last marked months now says "marked for the Moon" and "marked for the Sun", and the sentence on the second published list of marks moved beside it and says what that list changes.
  • Under "What is mine and what is not" the sentences on what others had found were set one to a line behind a heading of their own, and the hours were said in full, there and under "How far to trust this".
  • Where the page said "at the foot of the page" it now names the list, and among the three limits it says how the two years differ.
  • This account was moved here from the end of "How far to trust this". The names of the page's own sections, where they stand in quotation marks, became links that open the section.

Some things in the story are now not in the longer account, besides the three that its first paragraph names. The twenty minutes is the difference between the two years as usually given (365.2564 and 365.2422 days). "Nearly thirty thousand" turns is 254 turns in 19 years for the 2,230.5 years. "More than nine hundred years" is the time since AD 1112, the later of the dates. AD 914 and AD 1112 are from the working note on the dial, which has the months of the second list at none from its cycles 62 and 73, dated as the page dates cycles 64 and 70 of the first list. That the Sun and the Moon go round anticlockwise is from the program that draws the picture. That a fist at arm's length covers about 10° is a common rule of thumb, from my general knowledge. "In short" says "no month has held the eclipse it is marked for" because of a count made by the copy that checked these changes: months marked only for the Moon went on holding eclipses of the Sun until 1526.

In the longer account one word was added, "solar" in "months without a solar glyph" (seven of those thirteen eclipses fall in months marked for the Moon), and in "Why 205 BC" only the clause that names the longer account was changed. On all my pages tables are now set smaller on a narrow screen: one table in the longer account had been cut off at the right there, so that 15 showed as 1.
