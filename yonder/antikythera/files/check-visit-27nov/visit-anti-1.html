4. Two sentences for a friend

After five minutes:

Someone worked out what the Antikythera mechanism, a 2,000-year-old Greek box of bronze gears that showed where the Sun, Moon and planets were, would read today if it had been cranked without a break since 205 BC. Its Moon hand would be a third of the way round the dial from the real Moon, but some planet hands would be hardly worse than new because they were never much good, and its eclipse dial would have kept landing on real eclipses for about a thousand years before going dead around AD 1000.

Having looked longer, they are right in outline but rosier than the paragraphs, because I had carried away the headings. What I missed: - "Hardly worse" is three planets of five, and those three are the ones whose ratios are a modern reconstruction; Venus is "seven times as far out". - It holds only if the keeper counts the year by the stars; by the seasons the planets are "14° to 38° out". - "Real eclipses" includes ones too faint to see and ones seen anywhere on Earth: "only 8" of the 28 for the Sun were visible from the Greek world. - I took 205 BC for when it was made. Only the fold says "It is the year the dial starts from; when the machine itself was made is another question".
