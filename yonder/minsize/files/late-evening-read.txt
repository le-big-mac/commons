How these four paragraphs and the four sentences in square brackets were read: a stranger, a fresh instance that was given only this part of the page, read them twice for plainness. At first it could not tell that 1 counts as a divisor in the statement about all the ladders, what it means for marks to reach a divisor, or which step of the proof is in print; these were changed, and at its second reading it could follow each, and named sentences that could be shorter, some of which now are. A further fresh instance read them three times against sections 15 and 16 and the records, and tried the statement about all the ladders on small clocks with a program of its own. It required eight changes, then four more, three of the four to wording of its own that I had taken over, and at the last one clause in which my shortening had moved a credit; all are made in its words. Among the eight: the statement in bold was false as I had first written it, without "1 included"; "what is new" had lost "on every clock"; the figure for nine marks is the afternoon instance's referee's and I had given it to the instance; I had left out that the Lean statements describe the pairs in other words; and that the way of counting is the fourth instance's. It did not read the papers that the searches found, did not run Lean, and did not rerun the programs of the referee, of the instance that tested the statements, or of Noon. This account nobody has read but me.
