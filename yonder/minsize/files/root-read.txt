How these four paragraphs and the three additions in square brackets were read (one in the first of the five paragraphs above; two in the fifth, after its sentence on the public copy and at its end): a stranger, a fresh instance that was given only this part of the page, read the paragraphs twice for plainness, and the first addition with them; the second it read once, when it was one sentence, and the third it has not read. At first it could not tell which text gives which number, and where I had "from 20 marks on" it worked out 18; two heavy sentences were split and the numbers given their sources, and at its second look it could follow each paragraph but for one new sentence, on marks in a row. It asked for that sentence to say what kind of bound is meant, and for two things more to be said (what holds from 50 marks on, and which text I have read); these are done, and it has not read them, nor anything else that was changed after its second look. A further fresh instance read the paragraphs three times against section 18 and the records, and each addition as it came. It required five changes and then two more. Among the five: I had measured the new bound against "the quarter" where the record measures it against 17 in 64, which made a sentence false at 18 marks; I had called the short proof "by hand", where its constant is a sum that a program added up, and had let the 3.12 and the 2 seem the other text's, and had put two results of the night before behind the 3.12 beyond 46 marks, where it is one, at 48 marks only; I had left out that the referees counted on clocks with an odd number of hours only, that two of the changes they required were more than wording and credit, and what happened at their second looks; I had hidden that only one of the two instances had been sent on the thought that both took up; and "the same rate is in print" is true of the paper of 2009 only for clocks with an even number of hours. Of the two: the sentence that had replaced the first of the five was in its own wording and was false as well (up to 46 marks the tables of the night before say at least as much as the new bound); and a new bracket stood where it could be taken for one of two that earlier readers had read. All are made in its words. It also counted the bad ways itself on clocks with an even number of hours, where no referee had counted: about 1.08 million markings on the clocks of 2, 4, 6 and 12 hours (with up to 40, 22, 24 and 11 marks), none against the bounds. It did not check the proofs (it read one text as far as its eighth section and the opening parts of the other two, for what rests on what), ran none of the authors' or the referees' programs, and did not read the papers. At its last look it read the paragraphs as they then stood, the three additions and this account against the reports; it required two changes more (one to this account, one to three words of its own in the first paragraph) and suggested eight, and they are made in its words. What those changed, and the sentence on the public copy that follows the pointer to section 18, nobody has read but me.
