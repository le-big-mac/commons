A. Must change

1. A threshold that is false against the page's own "quarter".

  • As it stands: "the new bound says more than the half from 17 marks on, for odd numbers of marks, and more than the quarter from 20 marks on, for even numbers."
  • The record: section 18 compares with "at least 17/64 for even m ≥ 10", not with a quarter: "more for every odd m ≥ 17 and for every even m ≥ 20".
  • Recomputed: at 18 marks the new bound gives at least 0.2646, which is more than a quarter (0.25) and less than 17 in 64 (0.2656). Putting "the quarter" for 17/64 made the sentence false at 18 marks.
  • Replacement: "the new bound says more than the half from 17 marks on, for odd numbers of marks; for even numbers it says more than the night before's proofs from 20 marks on (for ten or more even marks those give at least 17 ways in 64, a little over a quarter; at 18 marks the new bound is above a quarter and below 17 in 64)."

2. The sentence on the constants is wrong in three clauses.

  • As it stands: "The shorter proof is by hand and gives 5.8 divided by √m on every clock; the constants 3.12 and 2 rest on sums computed by program, and the 3.12 also on the night before: for up to 46 marks on its tables, beyond on two of its results about the divisors 3, 9, 27 and so on."
  • "is by hand" says more than the record. The argument is by hand, but the constant is a sum over a table of counts made by program.
  • root/one/result.md line 181: "With the exact terms for N ≤ 61".
  • root/report-referee-one.md line 33: "C = 2.893 rests on … the table cnt(N) for N ≤ 61"; the referee listed 11,465,710 patterns to confirm it.
  • Section 18's own table row has "computed from a table of counts up to N = 61 and a tail bound".
  • "The shorter proof" reads as one of the two proofs, with the 3.12 and the 2 belonging to the other. By section 18's table the 5.8 and the 3.12 are both the first text's (Theorems 1 and 5). The 2 is in both texts; the third text has it from its own sums, without the night before's tables.
  • "beyond on two of its results" is not what the first text shows (root/one/result.md §7, lines 328–345).
  • For the 1.56, beyond 46 marks, one result is needed, the one for even numbers of marks, and "at m = 48 only".
  • The other result, for odd numbers of marks, serves that text's own "fewer than 1" for 47 to 83 marks. The 1.56 does not need it: for odd m ≥ 47 that text has 1.1576 without the night before (line 202).
  • Section 18's table row lumps both results under Theorem 5; "beyond" is the plain text's addition.
  • Replacement: "The shortest form of the proof, about a page in one of the two texts, gives 5.8 divided by √m on every clock; its argument is by hand, and its constant is a sum that a program added up from a table of counts. The 3.12 is from the same text, and the 2 is proved in both. They rest on sums computed by program (the close cases in exact fractions, the rest in rounded numbers, by the referees' reports with room to spare). The 3.12 rests also on the night before: for up to 46 marks on its tables, and at 48 marks on one of its results about the divisors 3, 9, 27 and so on. The other text proves the 2 from its own sums, without those tables; without them it has 3.39 in place of 3.12."
  • The 3.39 is twice the third text's 1.6914, which holds "without Theorem Q".

B. Suggestions

  1. The bracket. It is true, conditioned by its paragraph ("on every clock") and points to the right place. Two weaknesses: "at most 3.12 divided by the square root of the number of marks of all the ways" is hard to parse, and standing straight after "it is not known exactly" it can be read as answering that. Unlike the earlier brackets it does not say "on paper". Suggested: "[Later on 28 November, proved on paper: for many marks nearly every way is good. The least share is still not known exactly from nine marks on, but the share of bad ways is at most 3.12 divided by the square root of the number of marks. See the paragraphs added later that day, below.]"

  2. "The third instance". On the page it does not mislead: it is told third, and by root/launch-time.txt it also came back third (reports saved 02:47:28, 02:56:58, 03:00:07). A reader who follows the pointer finds "the second text" for it in section 18 and "the third text" for a complete proof. Add: "(section 18 calls its text the second, and the two complete ones the first and the third)".

  3. The partial result is told weaker than it is. root/two/result.md Corollary 3 has at most 1/√m on a clock whose number of hours is a power of one odd prime, for every m ≥ 1, which section 18 calls "the open statement (a) below on those moduli"; the 2/√m is only the doubled form. Suggested: "on clocks whose number of hours is a power of one odd prime the bad ways are at most 1 divided by √m of all, whatever the number of marks (the very statement that is told below as not proved, on those clocks); and so at most 2 divided by √m on clocks of twice, four times, eight times … such a number of hours."

  4. "Not proved". The sentence is true as it stands. Three additions from section 18: - Even numbers below ten are proved too (0.707, 0.75, 0.842, 0.862 times 1/√m for 2, 4, 6, 8 marks). - The 0.94 is for clocks with an odd number of hours. - It was found by the first text, not by the brief (the brief's largest was 0.93). - Suggested parenthesis: "(for odd numbers of marks, and for two, four, six and eight marks, it is proved; the largest that I know on such clocks for even numbers of marks is about 0.94 divided by √m, in a family that one of the instances found)".

  5. How far 3.12 is from what is seen. Section 18 has 1.7616/√n for one mark on each hour of the clock of 30 hours. By my own count, 24 marks on the clock of 6 hours (1, 8, 7, 8 marks on hours 0, 1, 3, 5) have 1,550,159 bad ways of 4,194,304, which is 1.81/√24. A sentence such as "On clocks with an even number of hours there are markings with about 1.8 divided by √m, so the 3.12 could at best come down to that" would stop a reader applying the 0.94 to every clock.

  6. "The rate cannot be bettered". This is true on every clock: weights all at one point balance only when pluses and minuses are equal in number. For an odd number of marks that example has no bad way at all, so it shows nothing for the "2". The record has the odd example (brief line 23; section 17). Add: "(for an odd number of marks there are none; but on the clock of 3 hours, all the marks but two on one hour and one on each of the other two give about 0.4 divided by √m)". My count on the clock of 3 hours agrees: 63/512 at 11 marks.

  7. Something above that is better than the new bound. For 20 or more even marks, nothing on the page. For odd marks, the parenthesis in the third paragraph added late on 27 November ("more than 760 in 1,000" on clocks without a factor 3) says more than the new bound for odd numbers from 17 up to 69; the new bound passes it at 71. Nothing is false, but a half-clause could say so.

  8. Missing: how far you read. The night-before paragraph tells it; this one does not. From section 18: "Of the three texts I have myself read the first as far as its short proof, and of the other two only the opening parts; I have not read their programs. The search read no paper in full, and I have read none of them again for this."

  9. Missing: what the referees did not check. From section 18: none computed the night before's tables again, on which the 3.12 rests, and none read the proof of the statement about all the ladders. The page's own night-before paragraph says those tables were computed again by that night's referees.

  10. A further piece in print. By the search's own derivation, for one mark on each of the hours 0, 1, 2, … in a row, wound round a clock of any size, a bound that holds on every clock is already in print (section 18: "the like for roots of unity of any order"). "One constant serves for every clock and every marking" stays true, but this is the nearest printed thing to it.

  11. Small wordings in the first paragraph. "for any m marks" could be "for any number m of marks, from one on"; "of them" could be "of all the ways". For nine marks or fewer the 3.12 bound is more than the whole and says nothing.

  12. "How this page was rebuilt". "to the first of those five" could be taken for the five paragraphs of the evening of 27 November, named one sentence earlier. Write "to the first of the five paragraphs of 28 November".
