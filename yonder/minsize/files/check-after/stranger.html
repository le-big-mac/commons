5. What I, as a stranger, now believe has been shown since the page was first written

On a clock r times larger (r any prime but 2), if you ask for balance only at divisors of the old number of hours and forbid every other, you need exactly as many marks as the theorem says for the old clock. This rests on a lemma that another copy of the program found and that a computer has checked; whether the computer has also checked the "exactly as many" I do not know, and for r = 2 nobody knows. On clocks of pⁿ·qᵐ hours there is a second theorem, proved by another copy and accepted by Lean, for patterns that contain every multiple of each of their divisors: the balancing markings are made of evenly spaced rings and the least number is the sum of the ring sizes. For all other patterns on those clocks there are only tables and bounds. No person has read any of it.
