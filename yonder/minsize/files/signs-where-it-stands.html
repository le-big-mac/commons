In short

A multiset of elements of Z_n is given signs ±1, one for each element (a signing); the signing is bad if the signed sum Σ ± X^b is divisible by a cyclotomic polynomial Φ_d for some d | n, d = 1 included, and good otherwise. The next section says it exactly.

  1. On every Z_n, every nonempty multiset has a good signing, except certain pairs, which have none. In Lean.
  2. The bad signings on Z_n are at most twice the bad signings of the same multiset reduced modulo the odd part of n. On paper, refereed; in Lean in the form with exponents.
  3. From three elements on, at least a quarter of the signings are good on every Z_n, and at least a half when the number of elements is odd. On paper, with exact computations; refereed.
  4. With m elements the share of bad signings is at most 3.12/√m on every Z_n, and 1/√m is the true order. On odd moduli the largest share of bad signings, β(m), is below 1/√m for every odd m and for every sufficiently large even m (how large is not made explicit); β(m)·√m has a limit along even m and a limit along odd m, and neither limit is known. On paper, with computations; refereed (for the large even m: two texts each refereed, and a step of mine that joins them, read by one of the two referees).
  5. and 6. In Lean besides 1 and 2: Erdős's bound for one value with the bounds that follow from it and 2, and one lemma of the proof that the limits exist.

"Refereed", "in Lean" and "the form with exponents" have fixed meanings here, given at the end of the next section.

The setting

n ≥ 1, Z_n = Z/nZ, and B a finite multiset of elements of Z_n with m elements, counted with multiplicity. A signing ε gives each of the m elements (each copy) a sign ±1; its signed mask is S_ε(X) = Σ_(b ∈ B) ε_b X^b, a polynomial modulo X^n − 1. The signing is bad at d, for d | n with d = 1 included, if the cyclotomic polynomial Φ_d divides S_ε (that is, S_ε vanishes at the roots of unity of order d; at d = 1: the signs add up to 0); it is bad if it is bad at some d | n, and good otherwise. "Share" means the share of the 2^m signings. 2^k is the highest power of 2 dividing n. β(m) is the supremum, over all ODD moduli n′ and all multisets of m elements of Z_(n′), of the share of bad signings, and c_m = C(m, ⌊m/2⌋)/2^m (a binomial coefficient over 2^m); c_m·√m tends to √(2/π) = 0.79788…, so c_m is about 0.80/√m for large m.

"Refereed" always means: by a fresh AI instance, not by a person; the referees were briefed to referee hard, and those of statements 2, 3 and 4 to assume that the text may be wrong. The word does not say what the verdict was: "passed" stands where the referee's last report says it, and otherwise the verdict is given.

The form with exponents. Statement 1 is in Lean for multisets on Z_n, given by their multiplicities. Statements 2, 5 and 6 are in Lean in another form: the m elements are given by natural numbers e_1, …, e_m, which need not be reduced modulo anything, and a signing ε is bad at d ≥ 1 if Φ_d divides Σ ε_i X^(e_i) in Z[X], and it is bad on Z_n if it is bad at some d | n; a multiset on Z_n is the case of exponents below n, and that the two forms then say the same is a short lemma that is not in the library (statement 2 tells of it).

What is proved

1. Good signings exist (in Lean). For every n ≥ 1, every nonempty multiset on Z_n has a good signing, unless it consists of two elements a, b with a − b not divisible by 2^k (so there is no exception for odd n, and two equal elements are no exception); such a pair has none.

  • Status. In Lean: exists_signs_all and not_exists_signs_of_exceptionalPair in Pnq/EvenFinal.lean; for odd n, exists_signs_odd in Pnq/SignFinal.lean. In Lean the exception reads "two marks whose difference has even additive order" (marks: elements). This is the same (the order of c in Z_n is n/gcd(n, c), which is even exactly when 2^k ∤ c), but that is not a statement of the Lean library; Noon checked it by program for n ≤ 2,048. 2. Twice (on paper; in Lean). Let n = 2^k·n′ with n′ odd, and B′ the reduction of B modulo n′ (a signing of B is a signing of B′, element by element). The signings of B that are bad on Z_n number at most twice the signings of B′ that are bad on Z_(n′). So every multiset of m elements on every Z_n, even or odd, has a share of good signings of at least 1 − 2·β(m).

3. At least a quarter (on paper, with exact computations). β(m) ≤ 3/8 for every m ≥ 3, with equality only for m = 4. More exactly: β(3), …, β(8) = 1/4, 3/8, 1/4, 11/32, 7/32, 39/128; β(m) ≤ 1/4 for every odd m ≥ 3; β(m) ≤ 47/128 for even m ≥ 10. Combined with statement 2, on every Z_n: the share of good signings is at least 1/4 for m ≥ 3 and at least 1/2 for odd m ≥ 3; the least share is exactly 1/2, 1/4, 1/2, 5/16, 9/16, 25/64 for m = 3, …, 8; and the quarter is attained by four elements ({0, 0, 0, 1} on Z_2, for example) and by no other number of elements.

4. The order 1/√m; below 1/√m for every odd m and for every sufficiently large even m; the limits (on paper, with computations).

  • For every m. β(m) ≤ A/√m for every m ≥ 1, with a constant A, in section 18:

    • A = 2.893 in its first text, by a short argument (the constant is a sum computed from a table of counts);
    • A = 1.6914 in its third text (the largest value, at m = 14, of √m times an explicit finite sum);
    • A = 1.56 in its first text again, with the tables of bounds for β(m), m ≤ 46, of statement 3's first and second texts (recomputed by their own referees, by their reports) and two results of statement 3's first text on the powers of 3.

    Combined with statement 2, on every Z_n the share of good signings is at least 1 − 3.12/√m. The order 1/√m cannot be improved: for even m, m copies of one element have a share of bad signings of exactly c_m, about 0.80/√m (for odd m, see the limits below).

  • Below 1/√m. For every odd m, β(m) < 1/√m (section 18: the third text, from its own sums; the first, with the tables just named); so on every Z_n the share of good signings is more than 1 − 2/√m for odd m. For even m, β(m) < 1/√m holds for m = 2 (the share is exactly 1/2), for m = 4, 6, 8 (by the exact values of statement 3), and for every sufficiently large even m (section 21). How large is not made explicit, so for each given even m ≥ 10 the inequality β(m) ≤ 1/√m is open; what is proved for all even m at once remains the 1.56/√m above (for a given m the tables and the explicit sums of the texts give that or less).

  • The limits. β(m)/c_m tends to a constant K_even along even m and to a constant K_odd along odd m (Theorem L of section 20), and each constant is a supremum over all finite multisets D on odd moduli ("decorations"). For a decoration D of r elements on Z_L, L odd (the heavy element being 0), and a signing of D, take the signed sums Q(ζ) = Σ_(b ∈ D) ± ζ^b at all the L-th roots of unity ζ, and count the different integers v with Q(ζ) = −v for some ζ: those with v ≡ r (mod 2) for K_even, those with v ≢ r for K_odd. The expected number of such v, over the signings of D, is what the supremum is taken of (section 20). What the two constants are is not known. By section 21, 9876481/8388608 = 1.177368… ≤ K_even ≤ 1.248953 < √(π/2); and 86549/131072 = 0.660316… ≤ K_odd (section 21) and K_odd < 1.0131 (section 20). So the limit of β(m)·√m along even m lies between 0.9394 and 0.9966, and the limit along odd m between 0.5268 and 0.8084.
  • Restricted moduli. If the supremum that defines β(m) is taken only over moduli n′ = p^a that are powers of one odd prime, it is at most 1/√m for every m ≥ 1 (the second text of section 18). Taken over decorations on powers of 3 alone, the supremum that defines K_even is exactly 7/6 and is not attained (section 21). For squarefree odd moduli, the second text of section 21 proves by itself that the limsup of the largest share·√m along even m is below 0.98741 (its Corollary B′, from its bound 1.2375342 for the expected number on squarefree moduli; by its author's account the corollary uses one section of the proof of Theorem L as the author read it, and its referee had one displayed number of it corrected). The first text of section 21 has a smaller bound for the expected number for even m on squarefree moduli, 1.1767; nobody has put that number through the second text's corollary (by the arithmetic of this page's first reader it would give 0.9389), and it is not claimed here.
  • The shape for large m. Asymptotically nothing is worse than one heavy element (many copies of one element) with a bounded decoration of further elements, in the sense of section 20's Addendum: bounded for each δ > 0; up to δ·c_m, so nothing is said of multisets whose share is far below c_m; and compared with a number, not with the share of an actual multiset; the heavy shape's share is that number up to a factor 1 − o(1), uniformly for a bounded decoration. For a family with one heavy element and a fixed set of further elements the share of bad signings is K·√(2/(πm))·(1 + o(1)), with K the expected number defined under "The limits", for the parity of m; so these give the lower bounds √(2/π)·K. The largest K known are 9876481/8388608 for even m (a decoration of 24 elements on Z_127575, found by the third text of section 21 and recomputed by its referee with an exact count of its own) and 86549/131072 for odd m (a decoration of 22 elements on Z_1155, mine, recomputed by the second text's referee over all 2^22 signings). Programs, none of them Lean: lean-lift/even/root/one/, lean-lift/even/asym/ and lean-lift/even/kconst/.

5. In Lean: the one-value form of Erdős's bound, and bounds for the signings that are bad at some divisor. For nonzero real or complex numbers a_1, …, a_m and any value v, at most C(m, ⌊m/2⌋) of the 2^m signed sums Σ ± a_i are equal to v (the form for one value of Erdős's bound for the problem of Littlewood and Offord). From it and statement 2, in the form with exponents: for every d ≥ 1 at most C(m, ⌊m/2⌋) signings are bad at d; on moduli 2^k no signing is bad if m is odd, and for every m at most 2·C(m, ⌊m/2⌋) signings are bad; and for n′ odd at most 2·d(n′)·C(m, ⌊m/2⌋) signings are bad on Z_(2^k·n′), where d(n′) is the number of divisors of n′.
