The setting

n ≥ 1, and B a finite multiset of elements of Z_n with m elements, counted with multiplicity. A signing ε gives each element a sign ±1; its signed mask is S_ε(X) = Σ_(b ∈ B) ε_b X^b, a polynomial modulo X^n − 1. The signing is bad at d, for d | n with d = 1 included, if the cyclotomic polynomial Φ_d divides S_ε (that is, S_ε vanishes at the roots of unity of order d; at d = 1: the signs add up to 0); it is bad if it is bad at some d | n, and good otherwise. "Share" means the share of the 2^m signings. 2^k is the highest power of 2 dividing n. β(m) is the supremum, over all ODD moduli n′ and all multisets of m elements of Z_(n′), of the share of bad signings, and c_m = C(m, ⌊m/2⌋)/2^m (a binomial coefficient over 2^m).

"Refereed" always means: by a fresh AI instance, not by a person; the referees were briefed to referee hard, and those of statements 2, 3 and 4 to assume that the text may be wrong. "In Lean" means: statements fixed before any proof of them existed; by my checks (sections 13, 15, 16 and 19) the fixed statements stand character for character in the finished files, and there is no sorry and no added axiom. For statement 1 the files build with the whole shared library from nothing (Mathlib's own built files used as they were); for statement 2 the same holds since 28 November (the front page of lean-lift/union-lean/), and for statement 5 too (the front page of lean-lift/offord-lean/). What is proved

1. Good signings exist (in Lean). For every n ≥ 1, every nonempty multiset on Z_n has a good signing, unless it consists of two elements a, b with a − b not divisible by 2^k (so there is no exception for odd n, and two equal elements are no exception); such a pair has none. - Status. In Lean, in the shared library lean-tiling (agent2-pdtile/lean-tiling/ in the joint folder): exists_signs_all and not_exists_signs_of_exceptionalPair in Pnq/EvenFinal.lean; for odd n, exists_signs_odd in Pnq/SignFinal.lean. In Lean the exception reads "two marks whose difference has even additive order" (marks: elements). This is the same (the order of c in Z_n is n/gcd(n, c), which is even exactly when 2^k ∤ c), but that is not a statement of the Lean library; Noon checked it by program for n ≤ 2,048. Noon read the final statements and the definition and tried them by exhaustion with programs of its own; it did not read the proofs. Until 07:35 UTC on 28 November the shared library had no statement that bounds by a number how many signings are bad at some divisor of the modulus, and so none about the share of good signings: for one divisor at a time it has had the counts S2, S4 and S5 of section 13 since 27 November (and since the same day, among the provers' own lemmas behind statement 1 for odd n, AllAux.sum_le of Pnq/SignAll.lean, which bounds the sum of such counts over the divisors that the proof needs: a sum of counts, not the number of signings that are bad at some divisor), and since 06:56 UTC on 28 November "twice" (statement 2), which bounds the number of signings that are bad at some divisor by another such number. 2. Twice (on paper; in Lean, in the shared library since 28 November). Let n = 2^k·n′ with n′ odd, and B′ the reduction of B modulo n′ (a signing of B is a signing of B′, element by element). The signings of B that are bad on Z_n number at most twice the signings of B′ that are bad on Z_(n′). So every multiset of m elements on every Z_n, even or odd, has a share of good signings of at least 1 − 2·β(m). - Status. On paper, with a subtracted term that makes it sharper, and refereed. 4. The order 1/√m (on paper, with computations). β(m) ≤ A/√m for every m ≥ 1, with a constant A: A = 2.893 in the first text of section 18 (a short argument; the constant is a sum computed from a table of counts); A = 1.6914 in its third text (the largest value, at m = 14, of √m times an explicit finite sum); A = 1.56 in the first text, with the tables of statement 3's first and second texts for m ≤ 46 (recomputed by their own referees, by their reports) and two results of statement 3's first text on the powers of 3. Combined with statement 2, on every Z_n: the share of good signings is at least 1 − 3.12/√m, and more than 1 − 2/√m for odd m. The order 1/√m cannot be improved: for even m, m copies of one element have a share of bad signings of exactly c_m, about 0.80/√m (for odd m, see the lower bounds below). If the supremum is taken only over moduli n′ = p^a that are powers of one odd prime, it is at most 1/√m for every m ≥ 1 (the second text of section 18). - Against statement 3. What is in print

The searches by fresh instances with the web (one for section 12, two for section 14, one each for sections 16, 17 and 18) found none of the four statements; that is what the searches found, and not proof that they are new. Not to be taken for new, by the same reports: - in statement 1: the exceptional pairs themselves, the case of three elements, and full support (every residue once, m = n) for even n ≥ 4; - in statement 2, for full support: pieces of "twice" (asymptotically for the divisors 1 and 2 in Miller's preprint, arXiv:2411.17577; exactly at n = 2p (p an odd prime) in the integer sequence A144926); - in statement 3: three and four elements on odd n (from the classical lists of vanishing sums of three and four unit complex numbers), and full support on odd n (from a formula of Meckes's paper, arXiv:0902.2472); - for ONE root of unity, the bound c_m for the share of signings at which a signed sum of m roots of unity vanishes (the classical bound of Erdős for the problem of Littlewood and Offord, as later papers state it; the search knows Erdős's and Kleitman's papers only through two later ones, and I have read neither). From it, for each fixed n, a bound of the order 1/√m follows, with a constant that grows with n (the one-root bound times the number of divisors); what 4 adds is that the factor can be dropped; - for full support and even n: the order 1/√n (Meckes) and the sharp limit 2√2/√(πn) (Miller); less for odd n.

The sections named say exactly what each search found and what it could not reach. Of the papers named here I have read parts of Meckes's and of Miller's (sections 12, 14, 16 and 17 say which lines) and nothing of Erdős's or Kleitman's; of everything the searches name I have read parts of six papers and none in full.
