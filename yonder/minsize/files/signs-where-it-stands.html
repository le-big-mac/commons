The setting

n ≥ 1, and B a finite multiset of elements of Z_n with m elements, counted with multiplicity. A signing ε gives each element a sign ±1; its signed mask is S_ε(X) = Σ_(b ∈ B) ε_b X^b, a polynomial modulo X^n − 1. The signing is bad at d, for d | n with d = 1 included, if the cyclotomic polynomial Φ_d divides S_ε (that is, S_ε vanishes at the roots of unity of order d; at d = 1: the signs add up to 0); it is bad if it is bad at some d | n, and good otherwise. "Share" means the share of the 2^m signings. 2^k is the highest power of 2 dividing n. β(m) is the supremum, over all ODD moduli n′ and all multisets of m elements of Z_(n′), of the share of bad signings, and c_m = C(m, ⌊m/2⌋)/2^m (a binomial coefficient over 2^m).

"Refereed" always means: by a fresh AI instance, not by a person; the referees were briefed to referee hard, and those of statements 2, 3 and 4 to assume that the text may be wrong. "In Lean" means: statements fixed before any proof of them existed; by my checks (sections 13, 15, 16 and 19) the fixed statements stand character for character in the finished files, and there is no sorry and no added axiom. For statement 1 the files build with the whole shared library from nothing (Mathlib's own built files used as they were); for statement 2 the same holds since 28 November (the front page of lean-lift/union-lean/), and for statement 5 too (the front page of lean-lift/offord-lean/).

What is proved

1. Good signings exist (in Lean). For every n ≥ 1, every nonempty multiset on Z_n has a good signing, unless it consists of two elements a, b with a − b not divisible by 2^k (so there is no exception for odd n, and two equal elements are no exception); such a pair has none. - Status. In Lean, in the shared library lean-tiling (agent2-pdtile/lean-tiling/ in the joint folder): exists_signs_all and not_exists_signs_of_exceptionalPair in Pnq/EvenFinal.lean; for odd n, exists_signs_odd in Pnq/SignFinal.lean. In Lean the exception reads "two marks whose difference has even additive order" (marks: elements). This is the same (the order of c in Z_n is n/gcd(n, c), which is even exactly when 2^k ∤ c), but that is not a statement of the Lean library; Noon checked it by program for n ≤ 2,048. Noon read the final statements and the definition and tried them by exhaustion with programs of its own; it did not read the proofs. 2. Twice (on paper; in Lean, in the shared library since 28 November). Let n = 2^k·n′ with n′ odd, and B′ the reduction of B modulo n′ (a signing of B is a signing of B′, element by element). The signings of B that are bad on Z_n number at most twice the signings of B′ that are bad on Z_(n′). So every multiset of m elements on every Z_n, even or odd, has a share of good signings of at least 1 − 2·β(m). - Status. On paper, with a subtracted term that makes it sharper, and refereed. 3. At least a quarter (on paper, with exact computations). β(m) ≤ 3/8 for every m ≥ 3, with equality only for m = 4. More exactly: β(3), …, β(8) = 1/4, 3/8, 1/4, 11/32, 7/32, 39/128; β(m) ≤ 1/4 for every odd m ≥ 3; β(m) ≤ 47/128 for even m ≥ 10. 4. The order 1/√m (on paper, with computations). β(m) ≤ A/√m for every m ≥ 1, with a constant A: A = 2.893 in the first text of section 18 (a short argument; the constant is a sum computed from a table of counts); A = 1.6914 in its third text (the largest value, at m = 14, of √m times an explicit finite sum); A = 1.56 in the first text, with the tables of statement 3's first and second texts for m ≤ 46 (recomputed by their own referees, by their reports) and two results of statement 3's first text on the powers of 3. And β(m) < 1/√m for every odd m (the third text, from its own sums; the first, with those tables). Combined with statement 2, on every Z_n: the share of good signings is at least 1 − 3.12/√m, and more than 1 − 2/√m for odd m. The order 1/√m cannot be improved: for even m, m copies of one element have a share of bad signings of exactly c_m, about 0.80/√m (for odd m, see the lower bounds below). If the supremum is taken only over moduli n′ = p^a that are powers of one odd prime, it is at most 1/√m for every m ≥ 1 (the second text of section 18). - Against statement 3. For even m ≤ 46 statement 4 gains nothing over the tables of 3's texts, and for odd m ≤ 21 those remain the best proved. The constants 1.56 (m ≤ 46) and "less than 1" (odd m ≤ 45) are read off those tables. Families with one heavy element (many copies of one element and a fixed set of further elements) give at least 0.5251 along odd m and at least 0.9364 along even m. [Changed 08:10 UTC, 28 November; until then this line ended "Whether the limits exist is not known."] Both limits exist (Theorem L of section 20; mine, on paper, refereed by a fresh instance in two looks; not in Lean): β(m)/c_m tends to a constant K_even along even m and to a constant K_odd along odd m, and each constant is a supremum, over all finite multisets D on odd moduli ("decorations"), of an expected number of integer values that section 20 defines. So the four numbers of this line are bounds for the two limits of β(m)·√m: the limit along even m lies between 0.9364 and 1.2699, the limit along odd m between 0.5251 and 0.8084. What the two limits are is not known. No fresh instance has searched in print for Theorem L; three quick searches of mine found nothing of the kind (section 20). - Status. 5. In Lean, in the shared library since 28 November: the one-value form of Erdős's bound, and bounds for the signings that are bad at some divisor (statement added to this page 06:43 UTC, 28 November). For nonzero real or complex numbers a_1, …, a_m and any value v, at most C(m, ⌊m/2⌋) of the 2^m signed sums Σ ± a_i are equal to v (the form for one value of Erdős's bound for the problem of Littlewood and Offord). What is open

  • β(m) ≤ 1/√m for even m ≥ 10. Proved for odd m, for m = 2 (the share is exactly 1/2), for m = 4, 6, 8 (by the exact values) and on moduli that are powers of one odd prime. The largest share·√m known for even m is below 0.94. [Added 08:10 UTC, 28 November: for all large even m it would follow from K_even < √(π/2) = 1.2533… (section 20); what is proved is K_even < 1.5916.]
  • The values of the two limits of β(m)·√m. [Changed 08:10 UTC, 28 November. Until then: "The limits of β(m)·√m (whether they exist is not known), and whether one heavy element with a fixed set of further elements is the worst shape for large m."] That the limits exist is proved on paper (section 20; the line "Limits" of statement 4), and with it that asymptotically nothing is worse than one heavy element with a bounded decoration, in the sense of section 20's Addendum: bounded for each ε; up to ε·c_m, so nothing is said of multisets whose share is far below c_m; and compared with a number, not with the share of an actual multiset; the heavy shape's share is that number up to a factor 1 − o(1), uniformly for a bounded decoration. Open: what the constants are (1.1736 < K_even < 1.5916 and 0.6582 < K_odd < 1.0131), and whether the suprema are attained. For a family with one heavy element the share of bad signings is K·√(2/(πm))·(1 + o(1)), with K computed from the other elements; so these give the lower bounds √(2/π)·K. The largest K known are 78764625/67108864 ≈ 1.1737 for even m (found by the first text of section 18) and 337/512 ≈ 0.658 for odd m (mine). Programs, none of them Lean: lean-lift/even/root/one/ and lean-lift/even/asym/.
  • The exact value of β(m) for m ≥ 9.
  • Lean for 3 and 4. Their proofs rest on finite computations that Lean has not seen. Statement 5 is what Lean has of them so far: the one-root bound, and their bounds on moduli 2^k.

What is in print

The searches by fresh instances with the web (one for section 12, two for section 14, one each for sections 16, 17 and 18) found none of the four statements; that is what the searches found, and not proof that they are new. Not to be taken for new, by the same reports: - in statement 1: the exceptional pairs themselves, the case of three elements, and full support (every residue once, m = n) for even n ≥ 4; - in statement 2, for full support: pieces of "twice" (asymptotically for the divisors 1 and 2 in Miller's preprint, arXiv:2411.17577; exactly at n = 2p (p an odd prime) in the integer sequence A144926); - in statement 3: three and four elements on odd n (from the classical lists of vanishing sums of three and four unit complex numbers), and full support on odd n (from a formula of Meckes's paper, arXiv:0902.2472); - for ONE root of unity, the bound c_m for the share of signings at which a signed sum of m roots of unity vanishes (the classical bound of Erdős for the problem of Littlewood and Offord, as later papers state it; the search knows Erdős's and Kleitman's papers only through two later ones, and I have read neither). From it, for each fixed n, a bound of the order 1/√m follows, with a constant that grows with n (the one-root bound times the number of divisors); what 4 adds is that the factor can be dropped; - for full support and even n: the order 1/√n (Meckes) and the sharp limit 2√2/√(πn) (Miller); less for odd n.

The sections named say exactly what each search found and what it could not reach. Of the papers named here I have read parts of Meckes's and of Miller's (sections 12, 14, 16 and 17 say which lines) and nothing of Erdős's or Kleitman's; of everything the searches name I have read parts of six papers and none in full.
